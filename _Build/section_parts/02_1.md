## Overview: two years dominated by GRAIL and a product transition

Illumina's 2022–2023 financial record is hard to read at face value. Over the two years the company reported net losses of about $5.6 billion. Most of that came from non-cash and legal charges tied to GRAIL, the cancer-screening company Illumina bought in August 2021, not from the core sequencing business.

The underlying business tells a quieter but still important story. Core Illumina, meaning everything except GRAIL, grew revenue only 1% in 2022 and then shrank 3% in 2023. Its gross margin fell from 70.7% in 2021 to 64.4% in 2023. Gross margin is the share of revenue left after paying the direct cost of making products.

Three forces drove the slowdown:

- **Macroeconomic caution.** Customers held less inventory and delayed lab expansions and capital purchases.
- **A platform changeover.** Customers moved from the NovaSeq 6000 to the NovaSeq X, launched in Q1 2023. That hurt both instrument margins and consumables volumes in the meantime.
- **Weakness in China.** The company cited competitive pressure there.

All figures below come from Illumina's SEC filings: the 10-Q quarterly reports for Q1 2022 through Q3 2023, and the 10-K annual reports for FY2022 and FY2023. Illumina's fiscal years end on the Sunday closest to December 31, so FY2022 ended January 1, 2023 and FY2023 ended December 31, 2023.

### Revenue by product type

Illumina sells three kinds of things:

- **Instruments:** the sequencers and array scanners themselves.
- **Consumables:** the reagent kits customers must buy for every run.

## 2022: flat growth, collapsing margins and the GRAIL charges

### Q1 2022: the last "normal" quarter

Revenue grew 12% to $1,223 million, up from $1,093 million in Q1 2021 [Q1 2022 10-Q]. Products made up $1,070 million (+12%):

- Sequencing consumables: $778 million, +13%, from a larger installed base. The installed base is the number of Illumina machines in customers' labs.
- Sequencing instruments: $211 million, +20%, on NovaSeq shipments.
- Microarray consumables: $75 million, down from $79 million.

Service and other revenue was $153 million, including $10 million from GRAIL.

By region:

- Americas: $648 million, +15%.
- EMEA: $316 million, +4%.
- Greater China: flat at $127 million.
- Asia-Pacific: $132 million, +33%.

Gross margin fell 330 basis points (3.3 percentage points) to 66.6%. Core margin held at 69.7% despite higher freight costs. GRAIL posted a $29 million gross loss, driven by $34 million of amortization. Amortization is the gradual expensing of intangible assets recorded at acquisition, such as technology and brand. (One extract summary refers to a "30 basis point" decline; the filing's numbers show 330.)

On expenses:

- **R&D** rose 64% to $323 million. Core R&D was $238 million (+21%) and GRAIL added $85 million.
- **SG&A** (selling, general and administrative expense) fell 18% to $308 million. Q1 2021 had included $105 million of "Continuation Payments," cash advanced to GRAIL during regulatory review. Q1 2022 also benefited from a $49 million gain from cutting the estimated value of the GRAIL earn-out.

Operating income fell 5% to $184 million. Net income fell 41% to $86 million because the effective tax rate jumped to 38.3% from 13.1%. Two factors drove the higher rate:

- $24 million from GRAIL's pre-acquisition losses interacting with GILTI, a US minimum tax on foreign earnings.
- $4 million from a new rule forcing R&D costs to be capitalized and spread over years rather than deducted at once.

Management estimated the R&D rule would add about $103 million to 2022 tax payments [Q1 2022 10-Q]. By Q2 it raised that estimate to about $142 million [Q2 2022 10-Q].

Operating cash flow was $172 million, down from $282 million. Free cash flow was $111 million, down from $240 million.

### Q2 2022: $609 million of legal accruals

Revenue rose 3% to $1,162 million [Q2 2022 10-Q]:

- Core consumables: $818 million, +5%.
- Core instruments: flat at $193 million.
- GRAIL: $12 million.
- Americas: $639 million, up from $589 million. EMEA fell to $308 million and Greater China to $118 million. Asia-Pacific rose to $97 million.
- Foreign exchange (FX) cut first-half revenue by $33 million.

Gross margin fell to 66.0% from 71.2%. Management cited less fixed-cost leverage (fewer units to spread factory overhead across), higher freight costs, and GRAIL's $29 million gross loss, partly offset by a better product mix.

The quarter swung to a $579 million operating loss because of $609 million in legal charges:

- **EU fine accrual: $453 million.** This was 10% of 2021 revenue, the maximum fine for closing GRAIL before EU clearance. The European Commission (EC) issued its Statement of Objections, a formal charge, on July 19, 2022.
- **BGI patent dispute: $156 million.** This was an estimate of past damages owed under a settlement signed July 14, 2022.

The net loss was $535 million, or $(3.40) per share, compared with a profit of $185 million ($1.26) a year earlier. Core R&D rose 23% to $249 million. Core SG&A fell 18% to $339 million, despite a $38 million charge from raising the earn-out estimate.

Leadership changed as well. CFO Sam Samad left, and on June 9, 2022 strategy chief Joydeep Goswami became interim CFO.

### Q3 2022: the $3.9 billion GRAIL write-down

Revenue was essentially flat at $1,115 million, up 1% [Q3 2022 10-Q]:

- Sequencing consumables: flat at $720 million. Management said installed-base growth was offset by "challenging macroeconomic factors on customers."
- FX cost $27 million, partly offset by $16 million of hedge gains.
- Sequencing instruments: down 9% to $162 million, as fewer NovaSeq 6000s shipped.
- Core service revenue: +11% to $142 million.
- By region, EMEA fell 7% and Greater China rose 9%.

Core gross margin fell to 67.9% from 70.7%. The consolidated figure was 64.3%.

The headline was a **$3,914 million goodwill impairment** on GRAIL. Goodwill is the premium paid over the book value of an acquired company's assets. The triggers were:

- the EU General Court ruling on July 13, 2022 that the EC had jurisdiction;
- the EC's September 6, 2022 prohibition of the deal;
- the fall in Illumina's stock price.

The discount rate used to value GRAIL rose to 22.0% from 16.0%. (The discount rate is the annual return investors would demand to fund a risky business; a higher rate means a lower present value.) At the May 2022 test, GRAIL's value had exceeded its book value by $700 million. Core Illumina's value exceeded its book value by more than $30 billion.

A $218 million gain from cutting the GRAIL earn-out estimate shrank consolidated SG&A to $146 million. The BGI settlement netted a small $11 million gain in the quarter.

The net loss was $3,816 million, or $(24.26) per share. In Q3 2021 the company had earned $317 million, helped by a one-time $899 million gain on its prior GRAIL stake.

Management issued its first numeric 2022 guidance: revenue "flat to 1% higher" than 2021 [Q3 2022 10-Q].

### Q4 and full-year 2022

Full-year revenue was $4,584 million, +1% [FY2022 10-K]. That landed at the top of the "flat to 1%" guidance.

By product:

- Sequencing consumables: $2,919 million, +$26 million.
- Instruments: $728 million, −3%. Customers deferred purchases of high-throughput machines while waiting for the NovaSeq X, though NextSeq 1000/2000 shipments rose.
- Core service: +6%.
- GRAIL: $55 million.

By region:

- Americas: $2,479 million, of which the US was $2,290 million.
- EMEA: $1,215 million, down from $1,289 million.
- Greater China: $472 million, down from $502 million.
- Asia-Pacific: $418 million, up from $377 million.

(The 10-K extract describes the Americas as "down," but the figures show it rose from $2,358 million.) FX cost Core revenue $84 million, offset by $53 million of hedge gains. International sales were 50% of revenue, down from 52%.

Gross margin fell 490 basis points to 64.8%. GRAIL's gross loss widened to $117 million on $134 million of amortization.

On expenses:

- **Core R&D:** $1,004 million, +13%.
- **Core SG&A:** $1,003 million, −33%. It benefited from a $205 million earn-out gain and the end of the 2021 Continuation Payments ($245 million). Q4 restructuring charges were $24 million in SG&A and $6 million in R&D, mostly severance and a lease write-down.

The EU fine accrual was raised to $458 million, now 10% of 2022 revenue. Total legal charges for the year were $619 million.

The tax provision was $68 million despite a $4,336 million pre-tax loss. The non-deductible impairment added $822 million of tax effect and the fine added $96 million. R&D capitalization added $87 million and GRAIL's losses $60 million.

Operating cash flow was $392 million and capex $286 million. The year included a $180 million cash payment recorded as a licence asset from the BGI deal.

## 2023: decline, restructuring and the move to divest GRAIL

### Q1 2023: NovaSeq X launch hits revenue and margin

Revenue fell 11% to $1,087 million [Q1 2023 10-Q]:

- **Core consumables:** $770 million, −10%. Sequencing consumables fell $92 million because of "lower NovaSeq 6000 consumables pull-through as some of our high throughput customers transition to NovaSeq X," plus tighter customer inventory. Pull-through is consumables spending per installed instrument.
- **Core instruments:** $160 million, −27%. The first NovaSeq X shipments only partly offset this.
- FX cost $24 million.

Illumina reorganized its sales regions in Q1 2023:

- Americas: $616 million, −5%.
- Europe: $261 million, −9%.
- Greater China: $91 million, −28%.
- AMEA (Asia-Pacific, Middle East and Africa): $119 million, −27%.

Core gross margin fell to 63.8% from 69.7%. Management said that is "typical with a new platform introduction until we scale manufacturing." The consolidated operating loss was $64 million. Interest expense rose to $20 million on the new notes, and interest income was $17 million.

A 103.9% effective tax rate produced an $81 million tax benefit, so net income came to $3 million ($0.02 per share). Operating cash flow was only $10 million, largely because the company paid income taxes during the quarter.

On guidance, management said "we continue to expect revenue to grow 7% to 10% in 2023" and planned to cut "annualized run rate expenses by more than $100 million beginning later in 2023" [Q1 2023 10-Q].

### Q2 2023: guidance cut and restructuring begins

Revenue was $1,176 million, +1%, and first-half revenue was down 5% [Q2 2023 10-Q]:

- Sequencing consumables: $734 million, −1%.
- Instruments: $193 million, +2%, on NovaSeq X shipments.
- Service and other: $175 million, +12%.
- Europe rose 11% to $303 million. AMEA fell 10%.

Gross margin was 62.2%. Higher field-service and installation costs added to the earlier pressures.

Restructuring charges totalled $33 million: $25 million of severance and $7 million of asset write-downs. They included a plan to exit the i3 campus in San Diego. SG&A also absorbed $25 million of proxy-contest costs. (A proxy contest is a shareholder campaign to replace directors; it was led by Icahn.)

The net loss was $234 million, or $(1.48) per share. The tax charge was $145 million on a pre-tax loss of $89 million.

Full-year 2023 revenue guidance was cut to about 1% growth, down from 7–10%. Management cited macroeconomic conditions and competition in China.

On July 12, 2023, the EC imposed the €432 million fine. The accrual stood at $471 million. (One extract wrongly says the fine was "substantially accrued in Q2 2022"; the accrual began then, but the fine itself was imposed in July 2023.)

### Q3 2023: second GRAIL impairment

Revenue was $1,119 million, flat, and down 3% year to date [Q3 2023 10-Q]. Greater China fell 26% and AMEA 22%, while the Americas rose 11% to $663 million. Gross margin was 61.1%, helped somewhat by lower freight costs.

A falling share price triggered another $821 million GRAIL charge: $712 million on goodwill and $109 million on in-process R&D. The GRAIL discount rate was 24.0%.

Restructuring added $58 million, mostly write-downs from the i3 campus exit. The net loss was $754 million, or $(4.77) per share.

### Full-year 2023 and the outlook for 2024

Revenue was $4,504 million, −2% [FY2023 10-K], slightly below the roughly 1% growth guided in Q2:

- Core consumables: −$140 million.
- Core instruments: −$23 million.
- Core service: +8%, to $626 million.
- GRAIL: $93 million.

Core operating income rose 15% to $552 million despite a gross margin of 64.4%. SG&A grew 24%, as the earn-out gain shrank to $24 million from $205 million. Restructuring added $94 million and proxy costs $32 million.

The order backlog fell to $653 million from $1,030 million. Backlog means firm orders not yet shipped.

For 2024, management guided Core revenue to "remain flat" and operating expenses to "slightly increase."

## Balance sheet, debt, cash flow and buybacks

### Cash and debt

Cash moved sharply over the two years:

- From $1,232 million at the start of 2022 it fell to $1,000 million by Q3 2022.
- It rose to $2,011 million at year-end 2022 after Illumina issued $1 billion of new notes on December 13, 2022. These were $500 million at 5.800% due 2025 and $500 million at 5.750% due 2027.
- It then fell to $1,048 million by end-2023. Illumina repaid the $500 million 0.550% notes on March 23, 2023 and the $750 million 0% convertible notes on August 15, 2023, both in cash and with no shares issued.

Debt at end-2023 was $1.5 billion of term notes: the 2025 and 2027 notes plus $500 million at 2.550% due 2031. Annual interest expense on debt rose to $74 million from $21 million.

The company also has a $750 million revolving credit facility, a standing loan line. It was replaced on January 4, 2023 with a facility running to January 2028 and was never drawn. Its key covenant caps debt at 3.5 times EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization).

### Shareholders' equity

Equity fell from $10,740 million to $6,599 million at end-2022, and to $5,898 million by Q3 2023. The share count stayed around 157–159 million.

### Buybacks

There were no buybacks in 2022 or 2023. Only $15 million remained under the $750 million programme authorized on February 5, 2020. Management stated it had no intention to repurchase shares in fiscal 2022, 2023, or 2024.

### GRAIL-related liabilities

- **Earn-out.** The fair value of the contingent value rights fell from $615 million to $412 million in 2022, then to $387 million in 2023. These rights pay former GRAIL holders 2.5% of the first $1 billion of annual GRAIL revenue and 9% above that, for 12 years.
- **EU fine.** The accrual was $484 million by end-2023, including interest at 5.5%. In October 2023, Illumina provided guarantees in place of cash while it appealed.
- **Divestment funding.** The EC's October 12, 2023 divestment order requires GRAIL to be funded for 2.5 years at sale.
- **Tax on a sale.** Estimates of Illumina's tax basis in GRAIL vary by filing: $500 million–$1 billion in the FY2022 and Q2 2023 filings, and zero to $500 million in Q3 2023. A lower basis means a bigger taxable gain on any sale.

### The GRAIL purchase price

Sources describe the GRAIL purchase price differently:

- $4,986 million of stock [Q1 2022 10-Q];
- about $7.1 billion [Q2 2022 and Q3 2022 10-Qs];
- $9,745 million of total accounting consideration, including cash, stock, the earn-out and the prior 12% stake [FY2022 and FY2023 10-Ks].

These figures measure different things and should not be compared as if they were the same.

### December 2023

On December 17, 2023, Illumina announced it would divest GRAIL, aiming to finalize terms by the end of Q2 2024. This followed a December 15, 2023 Fifth Circuit ruling that vacated the FTC's divestiture order and sent it back to the agency.
