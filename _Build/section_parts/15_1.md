## What this section rests on, and what it lacks

MGI Tech is a Shenzhen-headquartered company listed on Shanghai's STAR Market (ticker 688114.SS; 688114 CH on Bloomberg). It is the main Chinese maker of DNA sequencers (machines that read genetic code) and Illumina's most direct competitor in China.

**There are no MGI earnings-call or conference transcripts in this research folder.** Everything below comes from four short sell-side notes by a single broker, UBS. The notes were written by Henry Liu, CFA (lead analyst) and Chen Chen, PhD of UBS Securities Co. Limited, in UBS's China "Advanced Medical Devices" coverage. Judy Xie (associate) joined from April 2026, and Jun Deng is thanked for contributions. The four notes are:

- A "Fast Take" (a brief reaction note) on preliminary 2025 results [UBS, 2026-01-26].
- A Fast Take on an acquisition [UBS, 2026-02-02].
- A full review of 2025 and Q1 2026 results [UBS, 2026-04-29].
- A "First Read" (a quick note issued right after results) on H1 2026 [UBS, 2026-08-24].

Two limits follow from this. First, management statements appear only second-hand, as UBS reports them; they are tagged "[MGI mgmt via UBS]" below. Second, **none of the four notes names Illumina or any other competitor.** None of them discusses China's trade restrictions on Illumina directly. The China-competition picture here therefore has to be read through indirect evidence: MGI's share of public tenders, "import substitution" (Chinese buyers replacing foreign products with domestic ones), and MGI's moves to reduce its own geopolitical exposure in the US. For Illumina's side of the China story, see the Illumina sections of the Core Notes.

## Business overview

### What MGI does

UBS describes MGI as "China and the world's leading genome sequencer and lab automation equipment producer." It focuses on research and development and on producing and selling life-science and biotech instruments and reagents [UBS, all four notes]. MGI reports four revenue lines:

- **"Seq-all" (genome sequencing).** This covers sequencers and the reagents they consume. Reagents are the consumables, the chemical kits customers must buy for every sequencing run. Seq-all is the major revenue contributor, and UBS says it "showcases the company's competitive edge." It was Rmb2,203m of 2025's Rmb2,780m revenue, or about 79%.
- **Generative Lab Intelligence (GLI).** This is lab automation, meaning robotic sample-handling equipment. It complements sequencing and brought in Rmb254m in 2025.
- **Omics, also called the "new business."** This covers new products and services exploring "digitalisation opportunities in life sciences," including spatial omics. It brought in Rmb279m in 2025.
- **Other.** Rmb43–44m in 2025.

### Technology: short reads, long reads and spatial

MGI's core platform is short-read sequencing, also called NGS (next-generation sequencing). It reads DNA in many small fragments, which is the same approach Illumina uses. The notes mention two MGI sequencing chemistries by name, CoolMPS and StandardMPS, in the context of licensing deals (below).

On 1 February 2026, MGI announced it would buy nanopore-sequencing and spatial-omics platforms from BGI Tech for Rmb365.7m.

- **Nanopore sequencing** reads DNA by threading a molecule through a tiny pore. It produces "long reads," meaning long continuous stretches of sequence.
- **Spatial omics** measures genes or proteins while preserving where they sit in a tissue.

The notes do not describe BGI Tech's relationship to MGI. The seller committed to earnings targets for the bought platforms: over 2028–30 for nanopore and over 2026–28 for spatial [UBS, 2026-04-29]. The notes give no figures for the assets' revenue or profit.

UBS's view on the deal [UBS, 2026-02-02]:

- It expects "limited direct financial impact near term."
- It calls the deal "key for strengthening MGI's product (NGS+nanopore-sequencing) and tech barrier (short +long-read)." A tech barrier is a technical edge that rivals find hard to copy.
- It adds: "MGI is the only supplier in China and one of two globally with such capability," meaning the ability to offer both short- and long-read sequencing. UBS does not name the other global supplier.

By H1 2026, management credited "synergies between short-read and long-read sequencing and spatial omics" for part of the omics segment's growth [MGI mgmt via UBS, 2026-08-24].

On the long-run technology question, UBS believes high-throughput short-read sequencing "will remain the mainstream technology for the foreseeable future," based on cost, stability and range of applications [UBS, 2026-04-29].

### Position in China: dominance through import substitution

The clearest signs of MGI's domestic strength are management claims relayed by UBS:

- **Tender share.** MGI said its share of public bids (government and hospital tenders) in China's sequencing market rose to about 70% in 2025 [MGI mgmt via UBS, 2026-04-29].
- **Import-substitution orders.** By the end of 2025, MGI had shipped about 60% of the prior year's orders tied to China import-substitution demand [MGI mgmt via UBS, 2026-04-29].
- **How competition is shifting.** In August, management said that as import substitution rises, competition in China has shifted "from product pricing to broader competitive capabilities" [MGI mgmt via UBS, 2026-08-24].

These claims sit alongside weak numbers. China seq-all revenue fell 11% in 2025, and UBS repeatedly cites "weak market demand and intensified competition," pricing pressure and gross-margin erosion [UBS, 2026-04-29]. Domestic demand for lab automation also "remained under pressure" in H1 2026 [UBS, 2026-08-24].

*Interpretation, not stated in the sources:* the import-substitution orders and the 70% tender share are consistent with Chinese public buyers turning away from foreign sequencers such as Illumina's. The notes themselves never draw that link.

### Position abroad: growth in the Americas while exiting US ownership

MGI's international business is mixed:

- **2025 seq-all revenue by region:** Asia-Pacific (APAC) fell 29%; Europe, Middle East and Africa (EMEA) rose 4%; the Americas rose 21% [UBS, 2026-04-29].
- **H1 2026:** Americas seq-all revenue jumped 126% year on year, APAC rose 8.5%, and EMEA was flat [UBS, 2026-08-24].

UBS argues MGI has gained share globally "even amid rising geopolitical tensions," thanks to product competitiveness and an overseas sales network. It adds that with "lacklustre demand and geopolitical uncertainties, the process could take some time" [UBS, 2026-04-29].

At the same time, MGI has been restructuring its Western business into licensing deals. This is an important geopolitical signal.

1. **CoolMPS licence, 13 October 2025.** MGI out-licensed its CoolMPS sequencing chemistry outside APAC and Greater China to Swiss Rockets. Out-licensing means letting another company use a technology for fees. The terms are a US$20m upfront payment, a US$20m milestone payment, and tiered royalties (a percentage of future sales).
   - As of H1 2026, MGI had not yet recognized the US$20m upfront payment as revenue.
   - Management estimated the technology transfer was about 50% complete [UBS, 2026-08-24].
2. **Complete Genomics sale and StandardMPS licence, 24 February 2026.** MGI announced the sale of its US subsidiary, Complete Genomics Inc, for US$50m. It also out-licensed StandardMPS in the US and Canada to Swiss Rockets for future sales royalties.
   - UBS assumes the sale completes in early 2027 and books a milestone payment and an asset-disposal gain that year [UBS, 2026-04-29].

UBS's reading is that these deals, together with the nanopore acquisition, should:

- raise product competitiveness,
- reduce MGI's exposure to geopolitical risk, and
- improve long-term profitability, partly by removing a loss-making US subsidiary [UBS, 2026-04-29].

In effect, MGI is shifting from selling directly in North America to collecting royalties there through a partner.

## Results

All figures are in Chinese yuan (Rmb). MGI's revenue peaked at Rmb4,231m in 2022, when it was profitable (net earnings Rmb239m). Revenue then fell about 31% to Rmb2,911m in 2023 and has stayed near Rmb3bn since. MGI posted large losses in 2023–24. Its net cash (cash minus debt) fell from Rmb6,348m at end-2022 to Rmb4,417m at end-2023 and Rmb1,601m at end-2024, reflecting cash burn. It recovered to Rmb1,974m at end-2025.

### Full-year 2025: preliminary range, then final

**Preliminary figures.** In January, MGI guided to a 2025 net loss attributable to the parent of Rmb221–273m [UBS, 2026-01-26]. "Attributable to the parent" means the loss belonging to the listed company's shareholders, excluding minority interests.

- This was much smaller than the 2024 loss of Rmb601m.
- But it was clearly worse than UBS's own estimate of a Rmb126m loss.
- UBS's explanation: "Cost savings, FX gain and tax reversal helped narrow losses YoY, though partially offset by impairment loss in our view." An FX gain is a profit from currency moves. An impairment is a write-down of an asset's value. UBS did not size any of these items.

**Final figures** were "largely in line with the preliminary results" [UBS, 2026-04-29]:

- **Revenue:** Rmb2.78bn, down 7.73% year on year. UBS had forecast Rmb2,998m before results, so revenue also came in below its expectations.
- **Net loss to parent:** Rmb222m, versus Rmb601m in 2024.
- **Recurring net loss:** Rmb390m, versus Rmb653m in 2024. This measure excludes one-off items. The gap between the two loss measures shows how much the one-offs flattered 2025.
- **Gross margin:** 52.9%, down from 55.5% in 2024 and 59.5% in 2023. Gross margin is the share of revenue left after direct production costs.
- **EBIT:** a loss of Rmb237m (–8.5% margin), versus a Rmb460m loss in 2024. EBIT is operating profit before interest and tax.
- **EBITDA:** turned positive at Rmb123m. EBITDA is EBIT with depreciation and amortization added back.
- **Operating cash flow:** Rmb199m.

**Seq-all detail for 2025** [UBS, 2026-04-29]:

- Revenue was Rmb2.20bn, down 8.04%.
- Instrument revenue fell 17% and reagent revenue fell 6%.
- Sequencer unit sales nonetheless rose to more than 1,470 from more than 1,260 in 2024. UBS attributes the gap between rising units and falling revenue to a lower average selling price (ASP), caused by pricing pressure and a shift toward cheaper, lower-throughput machines.

**Other segments in 2025:** GLI revenue fell 32%. UBS says omics "grew a rapid 48%" in "2025 and Q1 2026." However, UBS's own division table shows omics revenue falling from Rmb400m in 2024 to Rmb279m in 2025, while GLI rose from Rmb209m to Rmb254m. **The text and table contradict each other.** The 48% figure most plausibly refers to Q1 2026 only, and the GLI figures look swapped or mislabelled. Treat the 2025 segment growth rates with caution.

**Minor inconsistencies across UBS's tables:**

- UBS's model shows 2024 net earnings as a loss of Rmb596m, versus the Rmb601m loss to parent.
- It shows 2025 net earnings as a loss of Rmb213m, versus Rmb222m to parent.
- Reported 2025 EPS was –Rmb0.51 (EPS is earnings per share). UBS had forecast –Rmb0.30 before results.

### Q1 2026: growth from a low base, but a miss

- **Revenue:** up 24.8% year on year to Rmb585m, from a weak Q1 2025.
- **Recurring net loss:** narrowed to Rmb88.5m from Rmb144m. UBS credits higher gross margin and cost control.
- **Seq-all:** grew about 23%. Instrument revenue fell 4% while reagent revenue rose 23%.
- **GLI:** up 10%.
- **Omics:** up 48%.

Results still "missed our forecasts" [UBS, 2026-04-29]. The miss was against UBS's own numbers, not consensus, and UBS did not size it. Despite the miss, UBS subtitled the note: fundamentals "may have bottomed."

### H1 2026: sequential improvement and a reagent shift

UBS called H1 results "largely in line" [UBS, 2026-08-24].

- **Revenue:** Rmb1.37bn, up 18.9% year on year.
- **Q2 revenue:** up 33% over Q1. That implies roughly Rmb785m in Q2 (derived from the figures, not stated). Losses also narrowed from Q1 to Q2.
- **Net loss to parent:** Rmb158m, versus Rmb168m in H1 2025.
- **Recurring net loss:** Rmb161m, versus Rmb203m.
- **UBS's reasons for the improvement:** growth across all segments and regions; gross-margin expansion from a higher mix of reagent revenue; and cost control. No H1 gross-margin figure was given.

**Segment detail for H1 2026:**

- **Seq-all:** Rmb1.1bn, up 17.6%.
  - By region: China +8.5%, APAC +8.5%, EMEA flat, Americas +126%.
  - By product: instruments –6.4%, reagents +16.3%. UBS calls this "an accelerated conversion of the installed base into recurring higher-margin reagent revenues." In other words, machines already placed with customers are generating more repeat consumable sales.
  - By customer: clinical customers (hospitals and diagnostic labs) drove the growth, with revenue up 36.6%, while research revenue was broadly flat [MGI mgmt via UBS].
- **GLI:** Rmb116m, up 4.1%, mostly from customized projects in EMEA.
- **Omics:** Rmb162m, up 44.9%. Drivers were the formal commercial launch of spatial omics, short-/long-read/spatial synergies, and AI-driven demand for multi-omics datasets. Multi-omics data combines several molecular data types, and large language models (LLMs) are creating demand for such datasets.

**Outlook.** UBS said guidance was "largely unchanged" but did not quote it. Management expects industry fundamentals to improve in 2027, possibly to teens-percentage growth. It cited industry reports and competitors' guidance [MGI mgmt via UBS, 2026-08-24].

After the Q1 miss, UBS lowered its 2026–28 operating assumptions "to reflect weak market demand and intensified competition" [UBS, 2026-04-29]. It still expects:

- teens revenue growth, led by seq-all and omics;
- net-profit breakeven in 2026;
- margin expansion from a richer reagent mix and continued cost cutting;

This is UBS's outlook; no company guidance is quoted.

## Analyst views (UBS)

### The thesis in brief

UBS's stance is "good company, fairly priced" [UBS, 2026-04-29]:

- **Bull case.** MGI can keep gaining share in China and abroad thanks to product competitiveness, an overseas sales network, and capabilities in product and technology integration, ecosystem operations and global commercialisation [UBS, 2026-08-24]. Short-read NGS will remain the mainstream technology.
- **What holds UBS back.** Demand is weak, competition is squeezing prices and margins, and geopolitics slows the overseas push.
- **What is priced in.** MGI underperformed China's SW Medtech index in early 2026 as consensus cut growth and margin forecasts. UBS concluded MGI's "higher tech barrier and stronger competitive positioning are fairly priced in." It also expects consensus to take time to absorb the effects of the acquisition and the US divestment.
- **After the August rally,** the stock trades about 22% above UBS's unchanged target, yet the rating stayed Neutral.

### Risks UBS lists

UBS lists the same risks in every note. The downside risks are:

1. intellectual-property (IP) litigation and geopolitical tensions;
2. shifts in the technology roadmap;
3. macro pressure on research and clinical funding;
4. slower downstream adoption of sequencing;
5. tighter life-science regulation;
6. more competition from new entrants.

The upside risks are the mirror image: easing geopolitics, faster product iteration, macro stimulus, wider adoption, looser regulation and less new competition. IP litigation and geopolitics heading the list is the closest the notes come to referencing the Illumina–MGI patent history and US–China tensions.

### Short-term scores
