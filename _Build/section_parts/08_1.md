## Background: Bloomberg Intelligence's standing view of the business

BI's business profile and M&A note, first written on 13 November 2025 and refreshed on 11 March 2026, frame the long-term debate that every later note builds on.

### Position, customers and products

BI calls Illumina's reach in genomic tools "unparalleled for more than a decade" [BI, 2025-11-13]. Genomic tools are the instruments and consumables used to read DNA. Consumables are the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run. Illumina sells to research and clinical customers worldwide with a broadening lineup meant to offer "a solution for every setting." BI says competition is rising from venture-backed entrants Element Biosciences and Ultima Genomics, and that Roche's return to sequencing is "on the horizon."

The customer base is diverse by geography and application. BI notes that sequencing is spreading into commercial markets such as oncology (cancer), agriculture and reproductive health. Most sales are still in the US, but overseas demand is growing as more countries fund genomics.

Government funding makes up almost half of sales. BI's example of the risk is 2012, when US budget sequestration (automatic across-the-board spending cuts) and a federal shutdown temporarily capped growth. During Covid-19, lab reopening timelines were expected to set the pace of recovery. Instead, 2021 brought record instrument demand.

In its March 2026 refresh, BI added that growth is shifting toward clinical end-markets. It said consumables now make up 60% of sequencing revenue, driven by NovaSeq X volume and oncology, but gave no prior-year comparison [BI, 2026-03-11].

BI describes the product ladder as follows:

- **NovaSeq X.** The flagship, launched at the 2022 Genomics Forum at Illumina's lowest cost per gigabase (one billion DNA letters read). It has 2.5x the throughput of earlier systems, and BI calls it "a critical catalyst."
- **iSeq and MiSeq.** Small-footprint, competitively priced machines for smaller labs.
- **NextSeq.** The mid-throughput line, whose demand drives the push into clinical work.
- **HiSeq X Ten.** Launched in 2014, it broke the $1,000-per-genome barrier.
- **XLEAP-SBS.** An upgrade to SBS (sequencing by synthesis, Illumina's core chemistry) that BI projects will push costs below $200 per genome. This is BI's projection, not a company result.

### Market opportunity

BI relays Illumina's view that its addressable markets total more than $125 billion in two categories. Illumina meaningfully penetrates only research, with an estimated 34% captured by 2027. That figure is a company estimate from the 2022 investor day, so it is dated [BI, 2025-11-13].

Clinical penetration depends on more biomarker profiling (testing patients for specific molecular markers) and on physicians using downstream sequencing applications. BI cites liquid biopsy (blood tests that detect cancer from circulating DNA) as an $800 million market where Illumina "hoped to compete via Grail." Competitors there include Guardant Health, Tempus AI and Exact Sciences.

Ameriprise later cited the same $125 billion figure for next-generation sequencing (NGS) and added that only about $10 billion of it is served today [Ameriprise, 2026-02-06].

### M&A history

BI argues that Illumina's dominance normally rules out large acquisitions, so it tends to buy early-stage technologies that extend its sequencing workflow [BI, 2025-11-13]. BI's view of each deal:

- **Solexa.** BI says the $480 million purchase "may go down as one of the best health-care deals in history." Illumina's market value rose from $1.8 billion when the deal was announced to a peak of $76 billion in 3Q21, and BI says almost all of that value comes from sequencing.
- **Helix.** A consumer-genomics venture announced on 18 August 2015 and funded with $100 million from two venture investors. The company's press release promised individuals "an unprecedented amount of genetic information." Helix was deconsolidated (removed from Illumina's reported results) in 2019 after years of weighing on profits. BI called this "a prudent step."
- **Context.** BI notes the research market is maturing, and each new instrument generation brings smaller performance gains.
- **PacBio.** The deal, now terminated, would have added long-read technology, which reads much longer stretches of DNA. Cash questions mounted until the failed 2020 PacBio deal, and the cash then went to Grail [BI, 2026-03-11].
- **Grail.** Illumina formed Grail in 2016 to commercialize a blood-based cancer screen. It closed the acquisition in August 2021 without EU approval and, after an arduous dispute, was forced to divest. The spin-off took place on 24 June 2024.

BI lists the Grail aftermath as a $476 million fine, $35–50 million of incremental spin-off charges, a retained 14.5% stake and a re-established royalty agreement. BI says Illumina "must now devise a new strategy" for cancer screening, and that the divestiture plus a fresh CEO help refocus the company on its core.

### Capital allocation

The Grail deal paused buybacks, which resumed modestly in the second half of 2024 [BI, 2025-11-11]. Buybacks historically just offset employee-option dilution. Illumina bought none in 2022 or 2023 and spent $115 million in 2024.

Year-end 2024 cash was $1.2 billion, and 2024 free cash flow (cash left after capital spending) was $695 million. The repurchase authorization is $1.5 billion. It had $684 million left at 3Q25 and $643 million at 4Q25. Year-end 2025 cash reached $1.6 billion on free cash flow of $931 million, but BI says it will take time to regain pre-Grail flexibility [BI, 2026-03-11].

### Peers

BI says Illumina's offerings "tower over peers" such as Thermo Fisher. Its operating margins matched peers until 2023, when Grail, the delayed NovaSeq X ramp and the Icahn proxy fight hurt performance. BI's chart titles summarize its view: "Margin Setback a 2023 Hiccup, Ambitious Goals Ahead" and "Amid Choppy Water Post-Pandemic, Stability on the Horizon" [BI, 2026-03-11].

## 3Q25 results and BI's post-quarter outlook (late October to November 2025)

### The quarter

BI's highlights panel records the third quarter of 2025 as a beat:

- Revenue was $1.08 billion, 2% above consensus (the average analyst forecast).
- EPS was $1.34, 17 cents above consensus.
- Core sales (Illumina excluding Grail) were flat.
- Sequencing instruments and sequencing consumables each grew 3%, while sequencing service fell 2%.
- Core operating margin (operating profit as a share of revenue) was 24.5%, up 190 basis points from a year earlier. A basis point is 0.01 percentage point.
- Illumina raised its 2025 EPS guidance to $4.65–4.75 from $4.45–4.55.

### BI's thesis at that point

In its equity outlook, BI said NovaSeq X and its updated chemistry should help Illumina hold its position, while Roche's return "adds a wrinkle" [BI, 2025-10-31]. It called the extended transition to NovaSeq X "financially painful": the machine ramped while demand weakened and competition in mid-throughput systems (mid-sized sequencers) accelerated.

BI's other points:

- Management's early 2026 view pointed to constrained research customers, with tariffs starting to ease.
- China's sanctions had halved that market's contribution, with no clear timing for a resolution.
- Illumina was gaining "a small degree of momentum," with a stable revenue outlook and cost cuts delivering margins ahead of expectations.
- The "specter of Roche's launch in 2026" made a sharp recovery unlikely, though "some green shoots are emerging."

BI judged that sequencing consumables had reached an inflection. NovaSeq X volume growth was now enough to offset the price cuts customers get when they move to the new machine. That gave better visibility on Illumina's 26% operating-margin target for 2027, especially if sales growth could move toward the high-single-digit goal (excluding China). BI expected 2026 end markets to look like the second half of 2025, with coming multiomics launches as an extra growth vector. Multiomics means measuring several kinds of molecules, such as DNA, RNA and proteins, together.

### BI's record of 2025 guidance

At this stage, BI cited 2025 guidance of a 0.5–1.5% decline in core revenue. Instruments were guided down 4–6% and consumables up 2.5–3% excluding China (printed as "2.53%," apparently a typo). Consensus was more cautious, at +1.5% for consumables and −11% for instruments. Core operating margin was guided to 22.75–23%, up 145–170 basis points from 21.3% in 2024.

BI explained the path to this point:

- The hoped-for 2025 turnaround "quickly dimmed."
- China's "unreliable entity" designation (a Chinese government listing that restricts a foreign company) and tightening US research budgets forced the guidance cut.
- Only half of the planned mitigation (pricing, supply-chain moves and cost cuts) was expected to land in 2025.

BI's key-points box adds more figures. 2024 core revenue fell 3% and 2024 core EPS was $4.16. The 2025 gross tariff cost was estimated at $85 million and 2025 China revenue was expected at $220 million. The box also shows a 2025 revenue decline of "0.5–2.1%," which conflicts with the 0.5–1.5% in BI's own prose, and an EPS range of $4.45–4.55 that predates the 3Q raise.

For history, BI notes core sales grew 40% in 2021, only 1% in 2022, and fell in 2023 and 2024. Consensus then expected a modest rebound in 2026.

## 4Q25 results and the first 2026 guidance (5–10 February 2026)

### What Illumina reported

Illumina reported full fourth-quarter results on 5 February 2026.

**Headline numbers.**
- Revenue was $1,159 million, up 5.0% reported from $1,104 million a year earlier. It rose 4% in constant currency and 7% excluding China, against consensus of about $1.10 billion [Ameriprise, 2026-02-06; JPM, 2026-02-06]. Barclays' table shows $1,155 million and a 284-basis-point beat on cc growth (4.0% versus 1.1% expected) [Barclays, 2026-02-05].
- Adjusted EPS was $1.35, above the preannounced $1.27–1.30, the Street's $1.21 and the guidance midpoint of $1.20. Ameriprise also cites a beat versus internal guidance of $1.29. It was up 42.1% from core EPS of $0.95 a year earlier [Ameriprise; JPM]. Barclays had forecast $1.22.

**Margins and costs.**
- Adjusted operating margin was 23.7%, up 400 basis points year on year. That compares with the Street's 22.5% and an implied guide of about 22.8%, which was also Barclays' estimate [JPM; Barclays].
- Adjusted gross margin fell 40 basis points to 67.0%, missing the Street's 68.1% (68.2% in another JPM passage) and Barclays' 68.9%. Tariffs cost 205 basis points. Excluding tariffs, gross margin rose 165 basis points from the third quarter.
- Core operating expenses fell $24 million to $502 million. JPM credited multiyear cost programs for the improved operating leverage. The tax rate was 19.5%.

**Instrument placements.** Illumina placed more than 100 NovaSeq X systems, above the ">95" it had preannounced and the second-highest quarter since launch. The installed base reached about 890 units, and over 60% of the quarter's placements went to clinical customers [JPM; Ameriprise].

**UBS: Neutral, target raised to $135 from $120 (5 February).** UBS called the results "mixed" for its thesis, which "balances a muted sales growth outlook (lesser than peers') with outsized margin expansion opportunity." It found the 20% clinical growth and the mid-teens 2026 clinical guide "encouraging to the upside." The +1–3% organic guide, while matching UBS's own forecast, "compares unfavorably" with peers.

UBS forecast only 1.0% upside to its target, an excess return of −7.4% against its 8.5% market assumption. This sits inside a widened ±15% Neutral band that UBS applies because the stock is volatile. All of UBS's short-term scores were neutral (3 of 5), with "No Catalyst" expected in the next three months.

**JPM: Neutral, target cut to $120 from $130 (6 February).** JPM wrote that "ongoing competitive concerns and pressured pricing dynamics" keep it "on the sidelines until there is more visibility." **Barclays: Underweight, $110 unchanged (5 February).** In a note titled "Getting Ready for the Main Event – Hulk Hogan vs. The Big Show This Year at AGBT," Sergott said Illumina "keeps up the trend" of a soft first-quarter guide. That creates a second-half-weighted ramp just as competition arrives.

Barclays acknowledged "plenty of momentum" in clinical, with close to 20% growth driven by liquid biopsy and NGS oncology assays and showing little sign of slowing. Its own thinking was that consumables should grow at least mid-single digits, so a normal reading would see "a healthy dose of conservatism" in the guide.

But Barclays said investors were reading the guide as a market freeze ahead of competitor launches, or a clinical slowdown. Investors have "little risk appetite for a 2H-weighted guide," Barclays said, and while Illumina will likely hit guidance, Barclays struggles to see the upside "needed at these valuations." The target applied 21x to new FY26 EPS of $5.14 (previously 22x on $5.00), implying about 17.7% downside. The upside case is $150, if the high-single-digit framework is delivered. The downside case is $60, if competitors erode the moat. Barclays named two risks to its bear view: innovation that widens Illumina's lead, and China fears not materializing.

He called the guidance "solid" and ahead of consensus. Ameriprise wrote: "For more aggressive investors with multi-year time horizons, we view the recent weakness as an opportunity to accumulate the stock." Its risk graphic shows quantitative risk as High and analyst-assessed risk as Moderate.

Market data in the note: trailing P/E 28.0x, price-to-book 8.6x, price-to-sales 4.8x, price to operating cash flow 19.3x, beta 1.37 (more volatile than the market), book value per share $15.56 and no dividend. Grail stock closed at $94.06.

**Freedom Broker: Buy, target raised to $140 from $130 (10 February).** Zubkov's "Steady Plans for 2026" argues revenue is "back on a growth trajectory" after declines through 1H25, led by clinical consumables. He sees instruments stabilizing amid a more predictable regulatory backdrop, and services diversifying revenue. He called the guidance constructive after three years of falling core revenue.

## Competitive shocks before and at AGBT (February 2026)

### Element's VITARI (19 February)

UBS published a "Fast Take" titled "Competitive Announcement Keeps the Pressure on Illumina." It reacted to Element Biosciences' VITARI sequencer, measured against Illumina's NovaSeq X Plus [UBS, 2026-02-19]. The extracted text is partly garbled. The readable comparisons are:

- The instrument costs about 40% less than the X Plus's $689,000 list price.
- Consumables cost about one-tenth as much per run, roughly $3,000 versus $32,000.
- Cost per gigabase is about half.
- Output per run is about one-fifth (3T versus 16T, apparently terabases).
- Shipping appears planned for the second half of 2026.

UBS kept Neutral and $135. At $120.09 that implied 12.4% upside, an excess return of 3.9%. Its EPS of $5.07 and $5.74 sat slightly below consensus of $5.13 and $5.82.

### Roche prices Axelios 1 (26 February)

At its AGBT workshop, Roche priced sequencing consumables for its Axelios 1 instrument, which uses SBX technology, at a list price of $150 per genome. The price includes synthesis reagents but excludes library prep (the step that prepares DNA samples). It also assumes the sensor module, Roche's equivalent of a flow cell, is reused 20 times. JPM compared this with about $200 per genome for NovaSeq X, making Roche's price about 25% lower [JPM, 2026-02-26].

JPM said most investors had expected about $150, and some as low as $100. Roche's other figures:

- Duplex mode reads both DNA strands for accuracy. It runs 16 genomes per run and 64 per day, about $2,400 per run.
- Simplex mode produces more than 40 billion reads per run and about 160 billion per day, at about $0.06 per million reads.
- A cell-free DNA (cfDNA) panel of about 1.1 Mb at 2,000x coverage and 96 samples per run would cost $25 per sample.
- On reuse, Roche showed stable performance over more than 20 uses within one week. 15 of 16 modules reached 20 runs, and the product is designed for up to 20 uses.

JPM kept Neutral and $120, again citing "competitive concerns and pressured pricing dynamics."

UBS's "First Read" the same day called the news "relatively benign" and said the "competitive overhang could soften" [UBS, 2026-02-26]. It gave two reasons:

1. Large Illumina customers likely already pay similar or better prices.
2. Illumina's roadmap, announced earlier that week, implies about $150 per genome on NovaSeq X Plus. That assumes the coming 35B flow cell is priced like the current 25B flow cell. The labels refer to billions of reads.

UBS saw more vulnerability in "counting" applications such as RNAseq (measuring gene activity). These are priced per million reads, where Roche's $0.06 compares with Illumina's $0.68 list on the 25B flow cell. The decimals are reconstructed from garbled text. UBS roughly estimates counting at about 40% of Illumina's research usage, or about 16% of total sequencing consumables sales.

Early researcher checks were "skeptical" that anyone would switch. Researchers saw speed as Axelios' main advantage, and speed matters little in research. They questioned whether simplex quality is good enough for the cell barcodes and UMIs (molecular tags used to count molecules) in 10x Genomics single-cell RNAseq. They also saw 20x reuse as a possible clinical issue. UBS kept $135, implying 10.8% upside at $121.87.

### Expert feedback (26 February)

A second UBS "First Read" that evening reported feedback from four experts plus a "Doc Debates" call. One expert was very negative, one very positive and two indifferent. UBS judged that a fair picture of the research community and repeated that the update "should soften the competitive overhang" [UBS, 2026-02-26].

**Positives.**
- Yields of 40 billion reads per run and 16 genomes per chip, up from a previously disclosed 7, beat expectations.
- Reuse data showed no carryover between runs. Chips can probably be reused "well more than 20 times," with a life of "months, not weeks."
- $150 per genome looks cheap "by a wide margin" against Illumina's per-lane pricing, especially per million reads.

**Negatives.**
- Library prep is "very complicated." Duplex needs two adapters and two preps, and Roche will not share adapter sequences.
- Software will require going "through hoops… like in the early days of Illumina."
- One expert already pays $175 per genome after Illumina discounts. They expect the 35B flow cell to reach $150, believe large users pay $125, and had expected Roche at $100.
- Half of that expert's clinical users accept reuse if validated. The pathologists call it "a nonstarter," especially for MRD.
- The summer shipment target "seems like a stretch," because the platform is "nowhere near ready." The expert estimated six to eight more months.

UBS planned another call on 6 March 2026. At $127.40, its $135 target implied 6.0% upside and an excess return of −2.5%.

## BI's spring update and 1Q26 preview (March–April 2026)

BI's refreshed thesis said management frames 2026 as steady, clinically led growth with cautious research assumptions [BI, 2026-02-06; 2026-03-11]. BI's verdict was that the 2026 setup looks better than a year earlier, but the story needs support beyond clinical to feel durable [BI, 2026-04-24].

BI summarized 2026 against 2025 as follows:
- Core growth of 2–4% versus 2% actual.
- Core EPS of $5.05–5.20 versus $4.84.
- Core margin of 23.3–23.5% versus 23.1%, including a 100-basis-point SomaLogic drag.

Upside would come from looser NIH funding and from mitigation actions taking hold through 2026.

In its 24 April preview, analyst Adeline Zandi called 4Q25 "a step in the right direction." She asked whether momentum could hold in a stepped-down first quarter, with research pressured and instruments normalizing. BI said NovaSeq X utilization, the remaining pace of transition and any easing of research pricing matter more than the quarter's numbers. It said it is likely too early to see Roche Axelios' impact.

Consensus for the first quarter was $1.07 billion of revenue and $1.05 EPS. The preview has two internal problems:
- It cites a 1Q core operating margin of 20.5% versus "20.4% in 4Q25," which conflicts with the 23.7% reported.
- It gives 2026 growth of 1–3% where other sections say 2–4%. This likely mixes total organic and ex-China figures.

## The bull and bear debate as of April 2026

**Bull case.** The bulls are Ameriprise, Freedom and, in part, BI and UBS.
- Clinical sequencing is a durable engine: 20% growth exiting 2025 and a double-digit to mid-teens guide.
- The NovaSeq X transition is nearly done, which should end research price erosion in the second half of 2026.
- Cost cuts have restored margins above 23%, with a credible path to 26% in 2027.
- Net cash is building.
- Multiomics, SomaLogic and BioInsight add new revenue from 2027.
- The valuation is far below history.

**Bear case.** The bears are Barclays, with JPM's concerns leaning the same way.
- Organic growth of 1–3% trails peers.
- The guide relies on a second-half ramp just as Element (second half of 2026) and Roche launch.
- Research funding remains fragile and China is half its former size.
- Roche is a well-capitalized rival able to push another round of price cuts in 2027 and beyond.
- Counting applications, about 16% of consumables, look exposed.
- At 24–26x 2026 earnings, there is little upside left.

**Middle ground.** UBS's channel checks and expert calls suggest Roche's pricing roughly matches what large Illumina customers already pay. They also point to real workflow, software and clinical-reuse hurdles that could slow switching.
