## Why 10x Genomics matters for Illumina

10x Genomics (ticker TXG) makes instruments and reagent kits for two fast-growing corners of biology research. The first is **single-cell analysis** (measuring gene activity cell by cell instead of averaging across a ground-up tissue sample), sold under the **Chromium** brand. The second is **spatial biology** (measuring gene activity while keeping track of where in a tissue each molecule sits), sold under the **Xenium** and **Visium** brands and, from late 2026, a new platform called **Atera**.

10x matters to Illumina in three ways. None of them is addressed directly in the two sources summarized here: neither the Q1 2026 call nor the BofA conference contained any Illumina-specific commentary. The links below are therefore partly background context rather than sourced claims.

### Single cell is a sequencing customer

A Chromium single-cell experiment prepares samples that must then be read on a DNA sequencer. Strong 10x single-cell volumes therefore pull through sequencing demand, and sequencing cost is a gating factor for 10x's largest projects.

CEO Serge Saxonov addressed this directly, but he named Illumina's competitors rather than Illumina. He said that with both **Ultima** and **Roche**, "the price per read has dropped like hugely," which is "definitely a huge enabler" [BofA, 2026-05-13]. Very large single-cell projects that sequencing cost would have constrained a few years ago are now "absolutely possible." He called this "a massive change in the ecosystem, which is something we absolutely love."

BofA analyst Michael Ryskin asked whether whole-genome pricing of $80 to $100 per genome is therefore no longer a problem. Saxonov said no. He added that single-cell work can use simplex reads (a simpler read format than whole-genome sequencing needs), so the effective cost is "even cheaper than the whole genome price would indicate" [BofA, 2026-05-13].

For Illumina, the takeaway is twofold:
- 10x's demand growth benefits sequencing in general.
- The 10x CEO publicly credits lower-cost rivals, not Illumina, as the enabler of mega-scale projects.

### Spatial is shifting toward imaging

Spatial products split into two kinds:
- **Sequencing-based readout**, such as Visium, which feeds a sequencer.
- **Imaging-based readout**, such as Xenium and Atera, which reads molecules directly in the tissue under a microscope-like instrument and does not need a sequencer.

Saxonov said the market trend is toward imaging-based readout, "especially Xenium," and that Atera should reinforce it [Q1 2026 call]. He also said "spatial will increasingly become the default approach for analyzing biological samples" [Q1 2026 call].

If that happens, a growing share of tissue-level gene measurement could bypass sequencers entirely. Atera's roadmap also includes in situ sequencing ("base-by-base" sequencing inside the tissue, used to detect mutations and variants in place). That would move 10x into territory that today requires a separate sequencer.

### Same customers, same budgets

10x sells into the same academic and biopharma labs as Illumina. Its commentary on government research funding and on capital-equipment budgets is therefore a read-across to Illumina's own instrument demand (see "Funding and capital-equipment backdrop" below).

## Funding and capital-equipment backdrop

On the Q1 2026 call, CFO Adam Taich looked back one year. On the Q1 2025 call, the company faced a highly uncertain environment after "drastic changes to government research funding" and withdrew its full-year guidance. Since then, he said, 10x has "consistently executed, improved our operating profile and have grown our cash balance by over $100 million. The business is in a meaningfully stronger position today" [Q1 2026 call].

Saxonov described the same period more vividly at BofA. He said single-cell volumes grew double digits "despite the fact that the end markets have been just dropping out from under us, especially going back to the first half of last year with the academic kind of disruption" [BofA, 2026-05-13].

On the current environment, both executives described capital-equipment spending as constrained but stable.

**Taich [Q1 2026 call]:**
- The macro backdrop for capex (capital expenditure, meaning spending on instruments rather than consumables) is "not meaningfully different" from before, "but I'd be remiss if I didn't say it's still fairly constrained."
- Saxonov likewise called it "a challenging capital equipment environment."
- Taich said that even so, "customers are finding CapEx. We've got orders." A partly garbled passage is best paraphrased as "this is the type of launch where folks find money."

**Taich [BofA, 2026-05-13]:**
- When 10x reinstated full-year guidance in February 2026, management noted the capex market had not "tremendously recovered."

On timing and budgets, 10x said it timed the Atera launch to fit customer budget cycles, including institutions with fall fiscal year-ends and grant submission cycles. The aim was to let customers earmark funds for Atera in their 2027 budgets [Q1 2026 call].

Saxonov also noted that existing products keep running on established grant budgets [Q1 2026 call]. Looking to 2027, he said "at the very least, we will have good compares" (easy prior-year comparisons). If conditions improve, "we should see an acceleration to all the other trends" [Q1 2026 call].

Saxonov also argued that the era of roughly ten years in which "every single lab" bought its own instrument is not coming back. That is why Atera was designed as a "somewhat more centralized system" [BofA, 2026-05-13]. This view on how labs buy instruments is relevant to Illumina's own placement strategy.

## AI and mega-projects as a demand driver

Management's central growth argument is that AI models of biology need vastly more data of exactly the kind 10x produces.

**Saxonov's framing [Q1 2026 call]:**
- He called AI "a significant and structural tailwind."
- He said there is "increasing evidence that scaling laws apply in biology just like they do in other domains." In plain terms, bigger models trained on more data keep getting better.
- He said "many orders of magnitude more data" are needed.
- He said "as AI models improve, we expect an exponential increase in the demand for the kind of data our technologies produce."

**William Blair asks for numbers.** William Blair's Matthew Larew noted that AI had been a relatively low share of revenue the prior quarter and asked how large the AI TAM (total addressable market, the total revenue opportunity) could become. Saxonov gave no number. He said AI is "really pervasive" across customers, and that there is no large project where AI is not a big driver, "if not the biggest." He added that even small experiments are often designed to feed AI models later. The AI wave "is really lifting all of it," in many ways "by design" [Q1 2026 call].

**How early it is [BofA, 2026-05-13]:**
- Very large projects are running and protein-protein-interaction (PPI) models are being published, but it is "very much early days."
- Every model's conclusion is that the next step needs 10x as much data.
- Saxonov said "everything that we're seeing is in the sort of early stage of that exponential." He said AI's lesson over ten years is an "insatiable hunger for data."

**Named partnerships over the past year [Q1 2026 call]:**
- **Chan Zuckerberg Initiative:** the Billion Cells Project.
- **Arc Institute:** the Virtual Cell Challenge.
- **Xaira Therapeutics:** building the largest perturbation dataset ever reported. Perturbation data shows how cells respond when genes are switched off or altered.
- **Bioptimus (announced April 2026):** an AI-focused biotech running STELA. STELA aims to profile up to 100,000 patient tissue specimens across three continents to build the data backbone for a "world model of biology." It starts on Xenium and plans to move to Atera over time.
- **Chan Zuckerberg Biohub (announced the week before May 7, 2026):** a new $500 million Virtual Biology Initiative to build AI models that simulate cells and tissues in silico (by computer). Saxonov compared it to the Human Genome Project in catalyzing a new era.

Ryskin also raised large-data efforts such as a Million Cell Atlas initiative at BofA.

## Atera: the new spatial platform

### What it is and why management calls it transformative

10x launched Atera in April 2026 at AACR, a major cancer research conference. Saxonov said at BofA on May 13 that the launch was about three weeks earlier. He called it "the most significant product introduction in our history" [Q1 2026 call] and "the biggest launch in our company's history" [BofA, 2026-05-13].

He said it is "poised to become the large-scale discovery engine, whether for basic research, AI-driven science, or translational applications" [Q1 2026 call]. At BofA he said it is "reshaping the industry and really opening up Spatial in a huge way."

**Design rationale.** Saxonov's argument is that biology tools have been fragmented: one set of tools for tissues, another for cells, another for molecules. Spatial biology is the convergence: "measuring molecules, cells, and tissues together, preserving a full context at massive scale and incredibly high resolution."

Until now, researchers had to trade off five things: throughput (how many samples can be run), specificity (measuring the right molecule), sensitivity (catching faint signals), scale and performance. Atera was built to remove those trade-offs [Q1 2026 call]. He said Visium and Xenium, despite their success, face "fundamental throttles" on the market, and Atera addresses "every single one of them." He added: "No one really quite expected what we were going to announce" [BofA, 2026-05-13].

**Development history.** The program draws on technology going back "almost the very beginning of the company." It formally began around the time Xenium launched. Saxonov said the needed technology did not exist about five years earlier, so 10x invented across several fields to get there, guided by years of customer feedback on what kept users constrained or on the sidelines [BofA, 2026-05-13].

### Capabilities

Management described the following features across the two events.

**Whole transcriptome with single-cell-level sensitivity.**
- "Whole transcriptome" means measuring essentially all expressed genes. Xenium instead uses targeted panels, which are pre-selected gene lists.
- Choosing a panel can be "quite a project" and limits unbiased discovery (looking broadly without presupposing which genes matter). With whole transcriptome, "you don't have to choose" [BofA].
- Sensitivity is comparable to **Flex Apex**, which Saxonov called "the gold standard for single cell analysis" [Q1 2026 call].
- Previously, raising plex (the number of genes measured at once) cost throughput and sensitivity. Saxonov said Atera delivers all three, "something that no one had thought possible before," and "we actually increased sensitivity in a massive way" [BofA].
- This matters for skeptics who compared spatial data to FISH (an established method that labels specific genes in tissue) and did not see the genes they expected. "You see these cells with just massive numbers of transcripts lighting up and it resonates with the biology they expect" [BofA].

**Specificity.** Refined probe chemistry delivers data researchers can trust, which Saxonov called non-negotiable [Q1 2026 call].

**Throughput.**
- One instrument can process up to 800 whole-transcriptome samples of 1 cm² per year, or more than 3,000 using the targeted **Atera Select** panels [Q1 2026 call].
- That is a "massive increase" over Xenium, whether measured by sample count or tissue area [BofA].
- Saxonov also cited a "billion-cell-per-year capability" [Q1 2026 call].
- Jefferies asked about roughly 3,000 core biopsies per year for drug trials. Saxonov clarified that the counts refer to 1 cm² areas and that more samples can fit per slide (for example, with tissue microarrays), so further scaling is possible.

**Standard glass slides.**
- Atera runs on off-the-shelf glass slides. Saxonov said this "feels like a minor detail" but is often what enables large translational studies [BofA].
- It also enables distributed sample collection and access to archived samples in biobanks (stored tissue collections) [Q1 2026 call].
- It uses a universal sample preparation that works across tissue types, including mixed sample types on one slide [Q1 2026 call].

**Workflow and compute.**
- Atera builds on the proven Xenium workflow.
- Image processing, the most computing-intensive step, runs on the instrument using onboard GPUs (graphics processors), so data is processed in real time.
- Customers can analyze data on their own servers or on the 10x Cloud [Q1 2026 call].

**Roadmap** [Q1 2026 call; BofA]:
- Full "sample-in, data-out" automation, for which the instrument is "really well suited."
- Protein multiomics (measuring proteins alongside RNA). Saxonov: "I don't think anyone has quite got proteins right yet."
- In situ, base-by-base sequencing for spatial mutation and variant detection.
- Continued software, compute and AI work, including multi-sample analysis on the 10x Cloud. Saxonov called the ability to turn large data into insight for all customers "absolutely game changing."

### Customer response, orders and buyers

**Reception.**
- Saxonov said early response "has been extraordinary," exceeding already high expectations [Q1 2026 call]. At BofA he called it "really phenomenal."
- One customer called it the largest technology leap of their career; another called it "the holy grail."
- He said conversations center "on how to incorporate Atera into their research programs, not whether to buy it," often moving quickly to multi-year programs and large cohort studies [Q1 2026 call].

**Orders.**
- Pre-order flow is "incredibly encouraging, right out of the gate, not really anything like we've seen before" [BofA].
- Saxonov declined to give order numbers, saying "It's not our policy." He said investors can expect "some directional kind of indication of the velocity of demand" [BofA].

**Who gets instruments first.**
- Interest is "coming from everywhere"; Saxonov could not name a segment without interest [Q1 2026 call; BofA].
- 10x is prioritizing customers who can run at scale immediately. That favors large existing Xenium users, service providers and core labs (shared facilities serving other researchers), plus "evangelizers" who will validate and promote the platform.
- Demand gives 10x "the luxury of prioritizing." It will broaden to more new customers later [BofA].
- There is also "quite intense interest" from labs new to spatial, or new to 10x spatial, who can now "step off from the sidelines" [BofA].

**Utilization.** Atera has a "substantially higher ceiling" per instrument than Xenium. Management expects "materially higher utilization rate pretty quickly out of the gate," but gave no figure [BofA].

**New markets.** Saxonov said Atera expands the addressable market in three segments [Q1 2026 call]:
1. **Discovery research**, such as mapping whole organs for the Human Cell Atlas.
2. **Translational research**, meaning characterizing clinical patient cohorts. He cited work by collaborator Carl June presented at AACR.
3. **Large AI models**, "an entirely new market."

### Supply, ramp and margins

**Shipment plan.**
- Pre-orders are being taken. About **40 Atera units** are expected to sell across Q3 and Q4 2026, mostly in Q4 [Q1 2026 call, Taich].
- Saxonov said supply, not demand, will set that number through the rest of 2026 and into 2027: "supply is going to be the constraint" [BofA].
- Most customers ordering now "probably won't be getting their instruments until well into 2027" [Q1 2026 call].

**Production ramp.**
- Asked by BofA about bottlenecks, Saxonov named none. He called Atera "a really sophisticated instrument" and said 10x will be "very smart and measured around how we build it and how we test it" [Q1 2026 call].
- No 2027 unit capacity was given.
- Taich said the capacity investment is "not a significant investment relative to sort of where we are in manufacturing capacity" because it leverages Xenium experience [BofA].
- There is "some dual capability" at the Pleasanton headquarters and the Singapore plant; the final supply location is still being decided.
- 10x is buying long-lead-time parts ahead and has begun procuring for the first half of 2027. Taich said management feels "really confident" in about 40 units for 2026 [BofA].

**Margins.** Gross margin is revenue minus the direct cost of goods, as a share of revenue. Taich gave the following outlook [Q1 2026 call; BofA]:
- Atera instrument margins in Q4 2026 should be similar to Xenium instrument margins, which are "lower than the corporate average for sure." This causes "a little bit of margin pressure," especially in Q4.
- Atera consumable margins are designed to be in the same range as Chromium's.
- As the installed base grows and runs at high utilization, Atera as a platform will be "margin accretive" (it will raise company-wide margin). The mix "should actually get better than where our corporate averages have been."

## Effect on Xenium and Visium (cannibalization)

Atera's announcement caused customers to pause purchases of the existing spatial instruments.

**Why spatial instruments fell in Q1.** In Q1 2026, spatial instrument revenue fell 32% year over year. Taich attributed it mainly to "fairly significant anticipation" of the launch rather than a worse macro backdrop [Q1 2026 call]:
- 10x ran a campaign at AGBT (a February genomics conference), word spread, and some early-access customers were working under NDA.
- Customers who would have added Xenium units decided to wait.

Saxonov added at BofA that 10x signaled something was coming in mid-Q1. Instrument purchases are "always back-end loaded" (concentrated late in a quarter), and many customers expected a new Xenium version. Ryskin noted that Q1 spatial instruments were lighter than BofA expected.

**What happens to the older products.** Analysts from Barclays, Leerink and Deutsche Bank pressed on cannibalization. Management's answers:
- Atera "is going to put pressure on both Spatial platforms," Xenium and Visium, and this is in the guidance [Q1 2026 call, Saxonov to Leerink].
- Customers keep running existing systems because of ongoing projects, workflows, familiar data, grants and collaborations. "You're always surprised by how sticky things are" [BofA].
- Xenium will keep selling at "a much reduced number, but … continued number," helped by long-cycle deals already in the pipeline. Consumables especially should hold up [BofA; Taich, Q1 2026 call].
- In the long run, "things will just consolidate onto Atera," improving margin, turnaround and scale [BofA].
- Visium "has its place." Customers like its consistency across large sample numbers, and 10x will keep supporting it [Q1 2026 call, to Deutsche Bank].
- Over time, migration to Atera would be "an amazingly great thing for us" [Q1 2026 call, to Barclays].

**Pricing.** Saxonov did not give Atera prices. He said pricing is set to enable a large expansion of the market. Whole transcriptome removes panel customization, throughput allows entire cohorts, and standard slides unlock biobanks [Q1 2026 call]. He said the "biggest launch" label was intentional and that he is mindful of the company's history with its earlier Single Cell product transition.

**Does Atera threaten single cell?** Stifel asked whether Flex volume could migrate to Atera. Saxonov said it is "not something that we're expecting to be an issue in the near term nor is it anything that we're hearing from customers." Configuration, pricing and workloads are "sufficiently different" [Q1 2026 call].

At BofA he said Apex enables the next order of magnitude of single-cell scale. Only at "2 logs of scaling" (about 100 times larger) do some future massive projects become candidates for Atera.

## Chromium single cell: price versus volume

The single-cell story has turned on falling prices offset by rising usage.

**The debate.** Saxonov recalled that about two years earlier (around 2024), investors debated whether single cell had any market left. 10x believed there was "way more volume than people were appreciating" [BofA, 2026-05-13]. He laid out three goals:
1. **Show consistent volume growth.** This has been achieved, with double-digit reaction growth for "quite a number of quarters running."
2. **Show pricing declines can be absorbed.** The last couple of quarters reached "that ballpark."
3. **Let pricing equilibrate so volume growth becomes revenue growth.** "We're getting closer to that point as we think especially towards 2027."

**Q1 2026 numbers.** Single-cell consumables revenue grew 6% year over year, while reaction volumes (the count of single-cell runs, a usage measure) grew double digits [Q1 2026 call]. Revenue per reaction therefore fell. Saxonov said Flex Apex pricing and mix were "similar to what we saw in Q4" [Q1 2026 call] and that "the pricing declines were more than made up with the volume growth" [BofA].

Guggenheim asked for the Q2 pricing headwind; none was given. Stifel asked for volume growth excluding Flex Apex; none was given. Saxonov noted Apex was only a little over one quarter into its launch, with "really large scaling up of volumes."

**Where volume comes from.**
- **Flex**, and newer **Flex Apex** (Chromium assays that work well on preserved clinical tissue), drove growth. Flex was the most popular assay by volume [Q1 2026 call].
- Big projects and AI projects are "unquestionably" incremental. They "would not have happened at like previous price points" [BofA].
- Studies are getting larger. Single cell is moving from "an esoteric technique where you pick and choose your samples" to one where "the entire cohort runs it." "Flex and especially Apex has been sort of a game changer" [BofA].
- Saxonov said new products "with more accessible pricing have been unlocking new ways of Single Cell demand" [Q1 2026 call].
- Flex Apex works well on FFPE samples (formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue, the standard preserved form of clinical specimens), which suits large translational studies [Q1 2026 call].

**2027 view.** Saxonov said Flex Apex will by then be widely adopted and single-cell pricing "will have stabilized," so volume growth should translate into revenue growth [Q1 2026 call].

## Translational research and clinical diagnostics

**Translational demand.** Saxonov said translational demand is growing in academia and biopharma [Q1 2026 call]. Translational research means turning lab findings into patient-relevant drug targets and biomarkers (measurable signals that predict disease course or treatment response). Customers want these because many therapies only work in subsets of patients.

He said Xenium is robust on clinical samples and Atera extends this with larger studies and more information per sample. Biopharma conversations on Apex and Atera are "really encouraging," and he expects biopharma to be "a big driver, especially as we kind of think about next year and beyond." He did not detail feedback on readiness for Phase 2 or Phase 3 drug trials, which Jefferies asked about.

**The path to diagnostics.** The largest opportunity is in later-stage translational work, where biomarkers explain patient response and toxicity, pointing toward eventual diagnostics. The next step is clinical evidence on large patient cohorts, pursued on two tracks [Q1 2026 call]:
1. Supporting customers' studies and partnering for later clinical deployment.
2. 10x's own work in two announced areas: tissue-based spatial profiling in oncology, and blood-based single-cell monitoring in autoimmune disease.

At BofA, Saxonov said the diagnostics opportunity has "come into much sharper focus" as Atera and Chromium make it possible to "generate clinical evidence at scale." He said 10x is "leaning in very intensely," but "it's not necessarily a story quite yet in terms of revenue for the near term." Longer term, "there's potential for really massive businesses to grow out of that."
