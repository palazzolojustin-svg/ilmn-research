## Why Thermo Fisher Matters to Illumina, and What These Sources Do and Don't Cover

Thermo Fisher Scientific (TMO) is the largest company in life-science tools. It sells lab instruments, consumables (supplies and reagents customers keep re-buying), and services to drug makers, universities, hospitals and industrial customers. It touches Illumina in three ways. It owns Ion Torrent, a rival DNA sequencing platform. It owns Olink, a proteomics business (measuring proteins rather than DNA) that competes with Illumina's SomaLogic. It also serves the same customers Illumina depends on: biotech companies, pharma, academic labs and government labs.

**The most important caveat is that neither source covered here discusses Illumina, DNA sequencing, Ion Torrent, clinical NGS (next-generation sequencing used in medical testing), or any sequencing competitor.** That covers the Q2 2026 earnings call [Q2 2026 call, 2026-07-23] and CEO Marc Casper's appearance at the Morgan Stanley Global Healthcare Conference [MS conf, 2026-09-15]. The Morgan Stanley talk also did not cover diagnostics, pricing, or competitors by name. So nothing here tells us how Ion Torrent is doing or how Thermo views Illumina. This is a coverage gap.

The value of this section for Illumina research is as a read-across, meaning what Thermo's experience implies about conditions Illumina also faces. Thermo is the broadest bellwether in the industry. Its view of biotech funding, U.S. academic and government spending, China, and AI-driven lab demand bears directly on Illumina's own customers.

## Read-Across for Illumina

### Biotech funding is recovering, and Thermo explains how it reaches tool suppliers

Casper said biotech is "in a much better part of the cycle" because funding and confidence are improving [MS conf, 2026-09-15]. He described the timeline:

- In 2025, emerging biotechs sounded more upbeat even though funding was not yet strong.
- Funding improved through 2025 and stepped up further in 2026. Two things helped: industry M&A (large companies buying biotechs, which returns cash to venture investors) and IPOs.
- Funding typically takes 2 to 3 quarters to turn into spending, depending on the business.

He laid out the order in which new funding reaches Thermo's businesses [MS conf, 2026-09-15]:

1. **Clinical research comes first.** Clinical trials get a company to its next milestone and funding round. This already shows up as very strong growth in authorizations (newly contracted work) and in revenue.
2. **Lab supplies come next.** These flow through the research and safety channel, Fisher Scientific, Thermo's distribution business. Spending there rises as companies refill research pipelines, and Casper said this was "starting now" in September.
3. **High-tech reagents come last.** These are specialized research consumables, and he expected them to strengthen "over the next couple of quarters."

On the July call, Casper added a second lag. Thermo's biotech authorizations had been strong "for a few quarters," and they take roughly six months to become revenue [Q2 2026 call]. He said the pickup in biotech was "not surprising." It fit the framework CFO Jim Meyer set out at the May 2026 Investor Day, in which the two big drivers of faster growth are recovery in biotech and recovery in academic and government spending.

**Why this matters for Illumina.** Sequencing consumables used in research are probably closer to "high-tech reagents" than to clinical trial services in this sequence. If Casper's order of events is right, the biotech recovery should reach research reagent buyers, including sequencing customers, from roughly late 2026 into 2027. That timing is an inference from Thermo's framework, not a statement Thermo made about sequencing.

### U.S. academic and government spending is stabilizing, but nobody is calling a trend yet

Academic and government customers returned to low-single-digit growth in Q2 2026 [Q2 2026 call; MS conf]. Here is how it broke down by region:

- **Europe:** very strong.
- **U.S.:** "slightly positive," a return to growth.
- **China:** academic spending remained "quite muted." It was neither worse nor better, though the Morgan Stanley talk called it "a better quarter."

Growth was driven by the chromatography and mass spectrometry business and by adoption of high-end instruments, including new product launches [Q2 2026 call; MS conf].

Casper was careful not to over-read this. In July he said "we're not calling a new trend based on Q2" and that he wanted to see broader-based activity first [Q2 2026 call]. He said the market is "going through a stabilization period," so Thermo left its academic and government outlook unchanged. Sustained improvement would be upside.

He was encouraged by U.S. government support for research. He said "it's less about the headlines than it is actually about funding flow and the funding flow is improving," which gave him confidence in "slow stabilization." By September his framing was firmer: "Funding has been okay, right, but anxiety has been high" [MS conf]. In 2025, many announcements about policy changes made customers cautious. In 2026 the market is "stabilizing but still muted," and he expects a return to growth in 2027. He called the anxiety "literally only a U.S. dynamic." Europe has fewer policy issues, and China is muted because its government has restrained academic spending.

**Why this matters for Illumina.** U.S. academic and government labs are a large customer group for Illumina's research sequencing. Thermo is seeing the same thing Illumina-watchers want to see, money flowing again despite noisy headlines, but neither firm's evidence yet shows a durable recovery.

One nuance is useful. Casper said "irrespective of funding environments, if you have really relevant innovation, customers get money" [Q2 2026 call, on mass spectrometry adoption by academics]. New, clearly better instruments can win budget even when overall budgets are tight. That is relevant to any Illumina instrument launch cycle.

### China: a gradual recovery, but academic demand stays weak

China is about 7.5% of Thermo's revenue [Q2 2026 call]. It grew at a low-single-digit rate in Q2 2026, which was a return to growth, driven by pharma/biotech and industrial customers. Casper said China had been a drag on industry growth for several years. Thermo had generally done better than peers but was still "flattish to down" [MS conf]. In July he said China was "not accretive to our organic growth as a company yet," meaning it was not yet growing faster than the company overall, but Thermo was positioning itself [Q2 2026 call].

After his August 2026 trip (his second of the year), Casper said: "I actually came away more bullish on China" [MS conf]. His reasons:

- Innovative Chinese biotechs are investing heavily in R&D.
- They want to work with Western partners to derisk licensing deals. Having Thermo's name behind a product or trial data shows buyers it works.

He believes China can over time grow at least at the company average, if not better. Academic and government spending there will stay weak until Beijing changes fiscal policy, but "I don't think that matters all that much." Thermo said it has been in China for over 40 years. It does not need China to contribute meaningfully in order to hit its targets. China is a possible upside because Thermo's assumptions there are modest [MS conf].

**Why this matters for Illumina.** Illumina's China position differs sharply from Thermo's, and its China academic exposure has been especially affected. Thermo's experience suggests the improving part of China is commercial biotech, while government-funded science stays weak.

### AI is driving demand for reagents rather than displacing it

Casper was asked whether pharma spending on AI might crowd out spending on traditional tools. He argued the opposite [MS conf, 2026-09-15]. Investment is driven by the return on drug development, and better returns from AI will lead customers to fund bigger pipelines. In practice:

- More drug uses will be tested in parallel in clinical trials.
- Better understanding of biology will guide research spending.
- Early-research demand is rising because of large-scale lab experiments that generate data to train foundational biological models (large AI models of how biology works). These high-throughput labs "consume many reagents."

He is "quite bullish that AI will actually be a facilitator of industry growth." He sees it as "another tailwind" that strengthens Thermo's competitive position, given that Thermo has the most data and customer insight. Pharma customers expect AI to improve drug-development returns, which fuels an investment cycle. Internally, AI is accelerating Thermo's PPI Business System, its continuous-improvement operating method [Q2 2026 call].

**Why this matters for Illumina.** Large-scale data generation to train biology models is a key part of the case for sequencing and proteomics demand. A major peer independently reports it is seeing this demand.

### Proteomics and multiomics: Olink and mass spectrometry

The only Olink item in these sources is a strategic collaboration with Precision Health Research, Singapore [Q2 2026 call]. It combines Olink proteomics technology with Thermo's Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometer (an instrument that identifies molecules by their mass) for a population health study.

Population-scale proteomics is a field where Illumina's SomaLogic competes. Thermo's pitch pairs Olink with its own mass spectrometry rather than with sequencing. Neither source mentions SomaLogic or compares the platforms.

Thermo also launched two mass spectrometers at the American Society for Mass Spectrometry (ASMS) conference in June 2026 [Q2 2026 call]:

- **Orbitrap Tribrid Apex:** aimed at multiomics (combining several molecular data types), structural biology, biopharma characterization and small molecules.
- **Orbitrap Excedion:** pitched as reducing drug-development risk and speeding time to market.

The launches came with AI-driven software, including proteomics workflows.

### Other product launches relevant to genomics labs

- **Applied Biosystems PowerFlex Thermal Cycler.** This is a next-generation PCR platform. PCR is the technique for copying DNA, and a thermal cycler is the instrument that runs it [Q2 2026 call]. PCR is a standard step in many sequencing workflows.
- **Vanquish Amplify UHPLC.** This is a liquid chromatography system (it separates mixtures into components) with less sample loss and better reproducibility. Casper called it "an important addition to our liquid chromatography offering" that "bodes well for the future" [Q2 2026 call].

## Other Business Drivers

### Pharma and biotech mood

Casper said large-pharma executives are upbeat, citing a breakfast meeting the day before the July call [Q2 2026 call]. They are excited about their pipelines and focused on speeding up innovation productively. Companies facing large patent cliffs (when key drugs lose exclusivity) are "really working their pipeline." He said customers "have their arms around the macro," meaning they have absorbed the main policy risks:

- the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA), the 2022 U.S. law that lets Medicare negotiate drug prices;
- most-favored-nation (MFN) pricing, U.S. proposals to tie drug prices to the lowest prices paid abroad;
- tariffs.

He did not say whether guidance leaves room for upside. In July he described Q2 as a "clean quarter," meaning no unusual one-off effects, and expected the second half for pharma and biotech to be "a little better" [Q2 2026 call, to Operon's Jack Meehan].

**Personalized mRNA cancer vaccines.** These are therapies tailored to an individual's specific tumor. Casper said the opportunity is "less about the Moderna and Merck specific spending in two important customers but more about the interest in the area, which then spurs a reinvestment cycle" [MS conf]. He likened it to how capital flowed into antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs, antibodies that deliver a toxic drug to cancer cells) and called it "yet another leg to drive growth in the industry."

### Reshoring of drug manufacturing to the U.S.

U.S. policy rewards domestic production. Casper described three one-time waves of benefit to Thermo [MS conf, 2026-09-15]:

1. **CDMO contracts.** A CDMO is a contract development and manufacturing organization, which makes drugs for other companies. Customers can meet U.S. requirements by contracting U.S. production rather than building factories. Thermo has won several large contracts. President Trump visited Thermo's Cincinnati oral solid dose (pill) facility in March 2026. Casper calls this the first phase.
2. **Expansions of existing facilities.** Customers buy equipment and stock labs. Activity is picking up, probably stepping up in 2027.
3. **Greenfield (brand-new) plants.** These account for most of the announcements and dollars and bring one-time equipment and lab fit-out purchases, with Fisher Scientific supplying labs. Expected 2027–2028.

Because total drug volume is unchanged, this is a one-time boost "additive to how we've thought about our growth outlook" that will later normalize. As Casper put it, "we'll take it." Many U.S. medicines are currently made in Western Europe (innovative drugs) or Asia Pacific, with generics mostly made in India.

### Bioproduction

Bioproduction (supplying the tools to manufacture biologic drugs) is growing faster than the market, according to the interviewer's data [MS conf]. Casper credits a long record of share gains and customers buying best-in-class technology to lower production costs. Its strengths, as he described them [Q2 2026 call; MS conf]:

- leadership in cell culture media (the nutrient broth cells grow in) and single-use technologies (disposable bioprocessing equipment);
- the DynaDrive single-use bioreactor, which he says is becoming standard across contract manufacturers and adopted by innovative pharma, "quickly becoming the favorite technology," which bodes well for follow-on consumable sales;
- a fast-growing purification resin business, where wins on drug molecules from 5 to 7 years ago are now turning into revenue.

When TD Cowen noted a large peer had seen customer delays, Casper reported no delays of his own. On competition he said there are "a number of fine players" [Q2 2026 call].

The filtration and separation business was acquired from Solventum (formerly part of 3M). Its view evolved between the two events:

- **July:** demand was strong and capacity was being added, but it did not yet show in organic growth. Integration was going well with positive customer feedback. The legacy 3M technology is well respected, and Thermo's reach and reputation for reliable supply are generating many customer trials [Q2 2026 call].
- **September:** having passed its one-year anniversary, it now counts in organic growth. The technology was strong but its commercial reach had been weak. Casper expects mid- to high-single-digit growth. That is slightly dilutive to bioproduction's growth rate but accretive to the company's, and "highly accretive" to EPS. He expects it to reach the bioproduction growth rate as capacity expands [MS conf].

Long-term bioproduction growth is expected at high single to low double digits, as it was through 2019. That followed a pandemic surge and an industry "hangover" [MS conf].

### Pharma Services (Patheon) and clinical research (PPD, Clario)

**Pharma Services** grew modestly in the first half, at low single digits, as signaled in January. A "nice step-up" in the second half is expected, based on contracts and shipment schedules, and the business has "the best visibility of any of our businesses" [Q2 2026 call; MS conf]. Meyer cautioned this "doesn't necessarily imply that the fourth quarter grows over the third quarter." Reshoring contract wins, visible in drug product and somewhat in drug substance, were already in the outlook.

The business has three parts:

- **Clinical trial packaging and logistics.** Thermo physically touches about 40% of all experimental medicines.
- **Drug product.** Mainly sterile fill-finish, putting drugs into their final vials or syringes, where capacity has been expanded.
- **Drug substance.** Thermo is one of the largest producers of biologics using single-use technology.

Casper expects Pharma Services to be a "nice growth business" in 2027 and 2028 [MS conf].

**Clinical research** (Thermo is a CRO, a contract research organization that runs trials for drug companies) had an excellent Q2. Organic growth and authorizations were strong, with authorizations growing "well in excess of our revenue" [Q2 2026 call; MS conf]. The combined Patheon/PPD development offering cuts time to market, which matters because failing fast is cheaper and earlier launches lengthen patent exclusivity. It is called "Accelerated drug development" in the July transcript and "Accelerator Drug Development" in the September one. Demand is "incredibly strong," especially from biotech, and AI is being used "quite aggressively."

The CRO's view of early-stage pipelines shows heavy interest in neurology, continued oncology strength, and adoption of new modalities (new drug types) [MS conf].

**Clario** is a $9B acquisition that closed in March 2026. It is a platform that generates and manages trial endpoints, the measurements that decide whether a trial succeeds. It leads in three of the four major endpoint types, and Thermo's central lab covers the fourth. The goal is to become the standard AI-enabled platform whoever runs the trial. Q2 was its first full quarter, with very strong authorizations, revenue and earnings and smooth integration. Customers who do not use Thermo's CRO are also showing interest. Casper said the first six months "have gone great" [Q2 2026 call; MS conf].

### Industrial and channels

**Electron microscopy** was very strong, driven by semiconductors [Q2 2026 call; MS conf]. Electron microscopes show chip structures at the atomic level. They are increasingly used for yield ramp (raising the share of good chips in a new fab), helped by automated near-line testing. "Orders are far outstripping revenue," and new fab construction should sustain demand.

**Chemical analysis**, the smallest of the three instrument businesses, returned to growth. Two factors drove it: high commodity prices boosting spectroscopy demand, and safety and security spending (radiation and explosive detectors) amid global conflict. "Market conditions are getting better" [Q2 2026 call].

**Channels.** The health care channel had specific Q1 headwinds and an "incredibly strong" Q2. Casper said to "take the average of the 2 quarters" as the run-rate. The research and safety channel grew on share gains and account wins with pharma and biotech, plus broad market improvement. Better academic demand helped but was not the main driver [Q2 2026 call].

## Strategy and Capital Deployment

Casper describes three strategic pillars: high-impact innovation, being a trusted partner to customers, and an "unparalleled commercial engine." In April 2026 Thermo opened a flagship U.S. Bioprocess Design Center in Massachusetts for collaborative work with customers [Q2 2026 call]. Its updated sustainability reporting cited a net-zero roadmap, more zero-waste sites, and STEM programs reaching over 185,000 students a year.

M&A remains the top use of capital, alongside returning cash to shareholders. Casper cited "an active pipeline of M&A opportunities in our highly fragmented industry" while maintaining a strong investment-grade credit rating [Q2 2026 call; MS conf]. Activity in 2026 through September:

- $9B of M&A.
- $4B of buybacks.
- A 10% dividend increase.

**Microbiology divestiture.** The sale was announced in late April 2026, expected in July to close in Q3, and confirmed in September as closed in August 2026. Casper said it "reflects our active management of the company" and frees capital for higher-value areas.

The business had $645M of 2025 revenue. About a quarter of that was sold through Thermo's own channels, which Thermo keeps. The sale cuts 2026 revenue by about $200M (net of those retained sales) and 2026 adjusted EPS by $0.05. It is about $0.15 dilutive in the first full year after close. Thermo spent the expected proceeds on $1B of Q2 buybacks ahead of closing, "based on an assessment of our valuation at that time" [Q2 2026 call, Meyer].

Casper closed the July call saying Thermo is "on track to deliver a strong year" and "well positioned to deliver a great 2026 and build an even brighter future for our company." In September he said 2026 "is setting up to be really an outstanding year."
