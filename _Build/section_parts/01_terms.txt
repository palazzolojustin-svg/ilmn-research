DNA sequencing :: Reading the order of the four chemical letters (bases) that make up DNA.
genome :: The complete set of DNA instructions in an organism, about three billion letters in humans.
exome :: The roughly 2% of the genome that codes for proteins, often sequenced as a cheaper alternative to the whole genome.
next-generation sequencing (NGS) :: Sequencing that reads millions of short DNA fragments in parallel and reassembles them with software.
gigabase (Gb) :: One billion DNA letters read, the standard unit of sequencing output.
terabase :: One thousand gigabases.
sequencing by synthesis (SBS) :: Illumina's core chemistry, which reads DNA by recording each tagged letter as a copy of the strand is built.
reversible terminator :: A chemically capped DNA letter that lets the machine add and read one base at a time.
XLEAP-SBS :: Illumina's latest, faster and more robust version of its SBS chemistry.
flow cell :: The glass slide on which DNA fragments are read inside a sequencer, sold as a consumable.
throughput :: How much sequencing data an instrument produces per run or per year.
high-throughput (HT) :: Illumina's largest production sequencers, the NovaSeq X and NovaSeq 6000.
installed base :: The number of Illumina instruments in active use at customers.
consumables :: Reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every sequencing run.
library prep :: Preparing DNA samples so they can be loaded onto a sequencer.
pull-through :: Average annual consumables revenue generated per installed instrument.
reagent rental :: An arrangement where an instrument is placed with a customer who pays through reagent purchases.
recurring revenue :: Revenue that repeats each year, defined by Illumina as consumables plus service and other.
microarray :: A chip that detects known DNA variations rather than reading every letter.
genotyping :: Identifying which known genetic variants a sample carries.
multiomics :: Measuring several biological layers, such as DNA, RNA, proteins and DNA marks, from the same samples.
proteomics :: Large-scale measurement of proteins.
SOMAmer :: SomaLogic's protein-binding reagent used to measure thousands of proteins.
methylome :: The pattern of methyl chemical marks on DNA that help switch genes on and off.
single-cell sequencing :: Measuring gene activity one cell at a time rather than averaged across tissue.
spatial genomics :: Mapping gene activity to its location within a tissue.
Perturb-Seq :: An experiment that switches off genes one by one and sequences the effects to map gene function.
foundation model :: A large AI model trained on broad data that can be adapted to many tasks.
BioInsight :: Illumina's data and AI business launched in October 2025.
DRAGEN :: Illumina's software for fast analysis of raw sequencing data.
structural variant :: A large-scale rearrangement, deletion or duplication of DNA.
phasing :: Determining which DNA variants sit on the same copy of a chromosome.
Research & Applied :: Illumina's customer group of academic, government, pharma, consumer-genomics and agricultural users.
Clinical :: Illumina's customer group of hospitals and diagnostic labs testing patients.
agrigenomics :: Genomic analysis used in crop and livestock breeding.
comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP) :: Broad tumor DNA testing to guide cancer treatment.
minimal residual disease (MRD) :: Blood testing for traces of cancer remaining after treatment.
multi-cancer early detection (MCED) :: Blood tests that screen for many cancers before symptoms.
liquid biopsy :: A blood test that detects disease, such as cancer, from DNA circulating in the blood.
biomarker profiling :: Testing patients for specific molecular markers to guide care.
noninvasive prenatal testing (NIPT) :: A blood test on a pregnant woman that screens fetal DNA for chromosomal conditions.
Research Use Only (RUO) :: A label meaning a product is not FDA-cleared for diagnosing patients.
laboratory-developed test (LDT) :: A diagnostic test designed and run within a single lab.
510(k) :: The shorter FDA clearance route showing a device is equivalent to an existing one.
premarket approval (PMA) :: The longer, costlier FDA approval route requiring clinical evidence.
IVDR :: The EU regulation governing in-vitro diagnostic products.
National Institutes of Health (NIH) :: The main U.S. government funder of biomedical research.
budget sequestration :: Automatic across-the-board U.S. federal spending cuts.
unreliable entities list (UEL) :: A Chinese government sanctions list that can restrict a foreign company's trade in China.
IEEPA :: The International Emergency Economic Powers Act, under which U.S. tariffs were imposed and later ruled improper.
tariff refund :: Money returned to importers after courts ruled the IEEPA tariffs improper.
Greater China :: Illumina's region covering mainland China, Taiwan and Hong Kong.
rest of world (ROW) :: Illumina's term for everything outside Greater China.
USCAN / EMEALA / APAC :: Illumina's 2026 regions: U.S. and Canada; Europe, Middle East, Africa and Latin America; Asia Pacific excluding Greater China.
AMEA :: Illumina's former region covering Asia Pacific, Middle East and Africa.
constant currency (CC) :: Growth with exchange-rate effects removed.
organic growth :: Growth excluding currency effects and acquisitions.
year-over-year (YoY) :: Compared with the same period a year earlier.
sequential :: Compared with the immediately preceding quarter.
GAAP :: Standard U.S. accounting rules for reported results.
non-GAAP :: Company-adjusted results that exclude items such as amortization, acquisition costs and investment gains.
Core Illumina :: Illumina's results excluding GRAIL.
consolidated :: Results including all businesses the company controlled during the period.
single reportable segment :: Reporting the whole company as one business for accounting purposes.
chief operating decision maker :: The executive, here the CEO, who reviews results to allocate resources.
gross margin :: Revenue left after direct product costs, as a percentage of revenue.
operating margin :: Operating profit as a percentage of revenue.
basis point (bp) :: One hundredth of a percentage point.
EPS :: Earnings per share, net income divided by shares outstanding.
free cash flow :: Operating cash flow minus capital spending.
capital expenditures (capex) :: Spending on property, plant and equipment.
EBITDA :: Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a measure of operating earnings.
gross leverage :: Gross debt divided by trailing-twelve-month EBITDA.
investment-grade rating :: A credit rating indicating low default risk.
return on invested capital (ROIC) :: Operating profit relative to the capital invested in the business.
backlog :: Orders received but not yet shipped.
market capitalization :: The total stock-market value of a company.
goodwill :: The part of an acquisition price above the value of identifiable assets.
intangible assets :: Non-physical assets such as technology, brands and customer relationships.
impairment :: A write-down when an asset is worth less than its book value.
amortization :: Spreading the cost of an intangible asset over its useful life.
purchase accounting :: Valuing an acquired company's assets and liabilities at fair value when the deal closes.
contingent consideration :: Additional acquisition payments owed only if future targets are met.
milestone payment :: A contingent payment triggered by hitting a specific target.
royalty :: A payment calculated as a share of revenue from a product.
contingent value right (CVR) :: A right to future payments tied to an acquired business's results.
deconsolidation :: Removing a business from a company's consolidated financial statements.
spin-off :: Separating a business by distributing its shares to existing shareholders.
Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) :: The U.S. law requiring antitrust review of large acquisitions.
derivative lawsuit :: A suit brought by shareholders on the company's behalf against its directors.
securities class action :: A lawsuit by a group of investors alleging misleading statements.
class certification :: A court order allowing a lawsuit to proceed on behalf of a group.
share repurchase (buyback) :: A company buying back its own stock.
restructuring :: Reorganizing operations, usually with layoffs and facility charges.
ERP :: Enterprise resource planning software that runs a company's core finance and operations.
tax holiday :: A period of reduced or zero tax granted by a government to attract investment.
long-read sequencing :: Sequencing that reads much longer continuous stretches of DNA than standard short-read methods.
addressable market :: The total revenue opportunity available to a product or company.
high-single-digit (HSD) :: Growth of roughly 7–9%.
low-/mid-single-digit (LSD/MSD) :: Growth of roughly 1–3% or 4–6%.
