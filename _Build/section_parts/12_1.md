## Why Roche matters to Illumina

Roche is the world's largest in vitro diagnostics company. In vitro diagnostics (IVD) means tests run on samples such as blood or tissue outside the body. Until mid-2026 Roche was not a seller of DNA sequencers. That changed on 29 June 2026, when Roche launched AXELIOS 1, a next-generation sequencing (NGS) instrument. NGS means reading millions or billions of DNA fragments in parallel, which is Illumina's core business. AXELIOS 1 is built on a new chemistry Roche calls sequencing by expansion (SBX).

Roche is therefore a direct new entrant in Illumina's market. It brings a large diagnostics sales force, a presence in more than 100 countries, an owned cancer-testing lab (Foundation Medicine), and a stated ambition for sequencing of more than $1 billion in sales. This section explains the technology, launch, pricing and early traction first. It then covers the diagnostics division's results and outlook, and finally what sell-side analysts (bank research analysts writing for investors) say.

A note on naming. Roche writes the product as "AXELIOS" (and "AXELIOS 1" for the launched model). J.P. Morgan writes "Axelios 1". Barclays' September 2026 note spells it "ALEXIOS", which appears to be a typo for the same product.

## Sequencing strategy: SBX and AXELIOS

### Origins and timeline

The SBX technology came from Roche's 2020 acquisition of Stratos Genomics [H1 2026 call, 2026-07-23]. Group CEO Thomas Schinecker said Roche's work with NVIDIA, which began in 2016, started partly because the sequencer needed computer chips able to handle its data flow. Schinecker described that flow as "three high-definition movies per second," with speeds set to rise further, and said no earlier chip could cope [Roche DX Day, 2026-05-12].

Roche first unveiled SBX at the AGBT conference (Advances in Genome Biology and Technology, the main annual sequencing-technology meeting) in February 2025 [Roche DX Day, 2026-05-12; H1 2026 call]. Early-access sites before launch included the Hartwig Medical Foundation in Europe and Broad Clinical Labs in the US, as well as Foundation Medicine [Roche DX Day]. In April 2026 Roche hosted a few hundred prospective US and European customers. Palani Kumaresan (Head of Roche Diagnostic Solutions) reported "tremendously positive feedback" on speed, throughput, accuracy, flexibility and the $0.06 per million reads price [Roche DX Day].

At the London Diagnostics Investor Day on 12 May 2026, Roche said the global launch would come in summer 2026. It made the 12 May event's "special focus" the "upcoming global launch of our truly transformative next-generation DNA sequencing technology" [Bruno Eschli, Head of IR, Roche DX Day]. AXELIOS 1 then launched on 29 June 2026 [H1 2026 call].

Schinecker framed the launch as a promise kept. In May he said he had been asked for years when Roche would launch, and "I always said we are going to launch, and we will have a very competitive solution" [Roche DX Day]. In July he said, "I've always promised that we will deliver, and we did deliver on the sequencing with really a differentiated product" [H1 2026 call]. He attended the May Diagnostics Day, despite now alternating years between Pharma Day and Diagnostics Day, specifically because of the sequencing launch and the PathAI deal.

### How SBX works

Most NGS today, including Illumina's, uses sequencing by synthesis (SBS). In SBS, a DNA copy is built one base at a time and each step is imaged. Matthew Sause, CEO of Roche Diagnostics, called SBX "truly differentiated chemistry… a move away from sequencing by synthesis to sequencing by expansion" [Roche DX Day].

Josh Lauer (Molecular Labs) described three parts of the system [Roche DX Day]:

1. **Synthesis instrument.** This converts each DNA molecule into an "Xpandomer," a stretched-out surrogate molecule. Lauer said the Xpandomer improves the signal-to-noise ratio, which is what makes single-molecule sequencing at scale possible.
2. **Sequencing instrument.** This passes Xpandomers through an 8-million-well, reusable CMOS sensor array (CMOS is the semiconductor chip technology used in camera sensors). It produces very high data rates.
3. **Analysis module.** This is a high-performance computing stack that processes reads in real time. Data processing is therefore almost complete when the run ends, with no waiting for flow cycles to finish each read.

Lauer added several operating points [Roche DX Day]:

- **Flexible loading.** Customers can load one to four sample pools per run. Lauer said "mid-throughput users can actually access the same economics as high throughput users."
- **Familiar library prep.** Library preparation (the lab steps that ready DNA for the sequencer) is similar to existing methods. It has been automated on Roche, Beckman and Hamilton liquid-handling robots.
- **Sensor reuse.** The sensor module can be reused up to 20 times.
- **Low carryover.** Carryover (leftover material contaminating the next run) is lower than in traditional systems, because Xpandomers are more delicate than DNA and wash out easily.

### Run modes and performance

AXELIOS has two run modes: SBX-Duplex, which emphasizes accuracy, and SBX-Simplex, which emphasizes raw read count. Sause said together they give "flexibility, speed and scalability that is unprecedented" and "differentiated cost effectiveness" [H1 2026 call]. The figures were described slightly differently in May and July. The table below lines them up so the reader can see what was claimed and when.

| Metric | May 2026 description [Roche DX Day, Lauer] | July 2026 description [H1 2026 call, Sause] |
|---|---|---|
| Duplex output | "Roughly a couple of terabases" per run | 1.8 terabases in 4 hours of sequencing |
| Duplex genomes per run | Always 16 or more genomes | About 16 whole human genomes |
| Duplex accuracy | Q38 or higher | Q38 (about 99.98% accuracy) |
| Duplex insert length | Longer than the traditional 150 base pairs | Not stated |
| Simplex reads | Over 40 billion short reads in 4 hours (over 10 billion an hour) | 40 billion reads averaging 175 base pairs in a 4-hour run |
| Simplex long reads | 500 to 1,000 base pairs, still over 10 billion reads in 4 hours | Up to 1,500 bases, enabling isoform and structural-variant detection |

Some terms in the table need a word of explanation:

- A **terabase** is a trillion DNA letters.
- A **Q score** is a sequencing-quality scale. Q30 means one error in 1,000 bases; Q38 means roughly one in 6,000.
- **Isoforms** are alternative versions of a gene's RNA message.
- **Structural variants** are large rearrangements of DNA.

The main difference between the two dates is the long-read ceiling, which rose from about 1,000 to 1,500 bases. The Duplex figure was also made more precise, at 1.8 terabases.

Lauer said the AGBT statistics were what Roche expected at the summer launch. He said Simplex output means "the sequencing world has never seen output like this," and that a new technology matching or beating incumbents on accuracy, cost and performance "out of the gate" is "a rare thing." He added that customers keep stressing "speed, speed, speed" [Roche DX Day].

Lauer gave two speed examples:

- **Throughput.** A fully loaded instrument with four queued runs produces 64 genomes in under two days. Lauer said traditional technologies need "significantly more than 2 days" of on-board sequencing time alone.
- **Rapid diagnosis.** In a world-record neonatal intensive care (NICU) run using an amplification-free workflow, Roche went from sample to a finished variant file (VCF, the standard list of genetic differences found) in under four hours. The Broad Institute and Boston Children's Hospital showed same-day results.

**Target enrichment.** Target enrichment means pulling out only selected genes before sequencing, as in cancer panels. After AGBT, analysts asked how this works on SBX, because Roche had shown only Simplex data using UMIs. UMIs (unique molecular identifiers) are molecular barcodes that let several reads of the same molecule be stacked into a consensus.

Lauer said SBX errors are largely random, so stacking reads can push accuracy beyond Q38. Roche has shown Q50 and does not yet know the ceiling. Enrichment also works in Duplex mode through a different workflow. That workflow is traditional, with PCR steps at the end that extend at a hairpin to make a Duplex construct. It suits applications where two reads per molecule are enough [Roche DX Day].

### Pricing

Roche disclosed list prices at the May Investor Day [Lauer, Roche DX Day].

**Whole genome.** The list price is $150 per human genome, with "no volume-based discounting. Everyone can achieve this pricing." Lauer said this is competitive with high-throughput platforms. He called it "way more than competitive" for lower-throughput labs, which normally pay much more per genome because they cannot use the largest instruments' economics.

**Simplex reads.** The price is $0.06 per million reads. Lauer called this "multiples lower than the nearest competing technology."

**Liquid biopsy panels.** A liquid biopsy is a cancer test on DNA shed into the blood. For a commonly used 1.1-megabase panel (1.1 million DNA letters) sequenced to normal clinical depth, the cost is about $25 per sample. Lauer called this "multiples lower than what you can achieve with other technologies."

These are Roche's own claims. None of the source documents contains independent price checks.

### Applications, ecosystem and openness

Lauer called AXELIOS Roche's "first-gen RUO platform." RUO means research use only: it is not yet cleared for diagnosing patients. Roche has demonstrated the following uses [Roche DX Day]:

- **Core applications:** germline sequencing (inherited DNA) and oncology, with high accuracy.
- **Sample types:** fresh-frozen and FFPE tissue. FFPE (formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded) is the standard way hospitals preserve biopsies.
- **MRD testing:** shown by Foundation Medicine. MRD (minimal residual disease) testing looks for trace cancer DNA after treatment.
- **Long tail of research uses:** single-cell sequencing, Perturb-seq CRISPR screens (gene-editing experiments read out by sequencing), spatial biology, proteomics (protein measurement, via collaborations with Olink, Pixelgen and Alamar), and novel isoform detection.

Roche describes AXELIOS as an open platform. It has data with Watchmaker and other vendors, a partnership with 10x Genomics, and says it will engage the wider NGS ecosystem. Kumaresan said Roche will introduce many workflows itself, and customers will build more with Roche's library-prep and bioinformatics support. Long term, Roche wants a clinical platform with end-to-end workflows, automation and library prep, used for multi-omics beyond DNA.

Lauer said Roche "intend[s] to invest in the next 20 years of improvements on SBX." The planned improvements are scaling the platform up and down, longer reads, better chemistry accuracy and more sensor reuse [Roche DX Day].

**Clinical evidence so far.** Foundation Medicine presented MRD data at AACR 2026 (the American Association for Cancer Research meeting). It said AXELIOS detected more MRD-positive cases than a competing technology and did better in earlier-stage disease [Lauer, Roche DX Day]. Freenome, a cancer early-detection company, is evaluating AXELIOS for its multi-cancer early detection (MCED) platform. MCED means a blood test screening for many cancers at once. Sause said whether AXELIOS is built into Freenome's workflow depends on the evaluation's outcome [Roche DX Day].

### Launch targets, traction and the $1 billion ambition

**Ambition.** Asked about market share at the May Investor Day, Sause declined to give a number [Roche DX Day]. He said AXELIOS would be "an accelerant" and capture "significant market share." Its differentiation would come from technology, applications and channel, for example selling through Foundation Medicine. Schinecker called it a "blockbuster potential" product, which he defined as "[$1 billion] plus," with no timeline. Sause echoed: "Blockbuster."

Schinecker later grouped three recent diagnostics launches together: sequencing, mass spectrometry and continuous glucose monitoring. He said each could be a $1 billion-plus opportunity and that "even for Pharma terms, [each] would be a blockbuster" [H1 2026 call; JPM CEO call, 2026-09-08]. He said he did not think any other diagnostics company had launched three such instruments in so short a period [Roche DX Day]. Sause reaffirmed the "more than CHF 1bn" sales ambition for sequencing in late September [Barclays, 2026-09-28]. The currency shifts between sources: management said dollars, and Barclays reported francs.

**Year-one target.** In May, Sause said a successful first year would be "ideally… 100 of these systems in the first year, thereabouts" [Roche DX Day, answering TD Cowen]. He added that early orders came from the more than 100 countries where Roche operates, and that first orders had already arrived from around the world.

**Progress against that target.** The two September updates use different measures:

- On 8 September 2026, Schinecker said roughly one-third of the approximately 100 first-year placements had already been made [JPM CEO call, 2026-09-08]. A placement is an instrument installed at a customer. He said these were all with external customers and involved no "teaser offers," meaning no promotional or discounted come-on deals.
- Barclays reported Roche "progressing well" in the first 60 days against its "initial order target of 100 systems in 12 months" [Barclays, 2026-09-28].

Barclays thus frames the 100 as orders while J.P. Morgan frames it as placements. On either reading, the early pace is ahead of a straight-line one-twelfth per month.

**Demand and recurring revenue.** Schinecker cited positive early customer feedback. He expects reagent revenue (the consumable kits that must be bought for every run, usually the most profitable part of a sequencing business) to build over time as customers migrate existing assays onto AXELIOS [JPM CEO call]. Sause said early demand comes from both clinical laboratories and academic centers. It is supported by long-read capability, clinical accuracy and competitive economics. Roche plans to present more data at ASHG (the American Society of Human Genetics meeting, in autumn 2026) and wants further progress to be "data-driven" [Barclays, 2026-09-28].

**Who the customers are.** Lauer expects academic centers to drive early momentum. Community oncology practices, which often want to bring testing in-house for reimbursement reasons, could be served by "a capable, economic solution." Centralized and decentralized testing models will coexist [Roche DX Day, answering Jefferies].

**Profitability.** Asked by Canaccord whether sequencing can be profitable at scale, Lauer said there is no reason it "can't be massively profitable," even at blockbuster scale [Roche DX Day]. He noted molecular diagnostics are high-margin, and said Roche has "every technical entitlement to be just as cost efficient, if not more" than public sequencing peers. As per-genome costs fall, Lauer said, cost shifts to library prep and sample collection. Kumaresan added that this assumes continued R&D investment.

### Market sizing

In May, Roche put the sequencing market at CHF 7 billion growing about 7% a year. That compares with the PCR molecular market at CHF 9 billion, with the molecular lab overall growing 4% [Roche DX Day]. In July, Sause cited an NGS market of about USD 7.3 billion, assessed in 2025, growing as clinical applications become routine [H1 2026 call]. The two figures are roughly consistent once currency is allowed for. Sause said Roche is entering sequencing "very ambitiously."

### Sequencing within Roche's oncology ecosystem

Roche's argument is that AXELIOS will not be sold as a standalone box. It sits inside an end-to-end cancer-testing portfolio that Sause said lets Roche "shape the future of the Oncology testing market in a way that no other company can" [Roche DX Day]. The pieces are:

- **AXELIOS** for molecular oncology. Sause called it "the core of that backbone" for oncology and translational research [Roche DX Day, answering Kepler].
- **Foundation Medicine (FMI)** for comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP, a large-panel tumor DNA test that guides therapy choice).
- **SAGA Diagnostics** for ultrasensitive MRD. At the May Investor Day this was a pending "definitive merger agreement." By July Roche described it as acquired.
- **Freenome partnership** for early cancer detection.
- **VENTANA tissue staining plus PathAI** digital pathology.
- **Digital LightCycler**, Roche's digital PCR system for low-cost repeat monitoring.

**How SAGA uses AXELIOS.** Schinecker described the MRD workflow as follows [H1 2026 call]. A patient's tumor is sequenced on AXELIOS to find its mutations. The patient is then monitored with tumor-specific primers on digital PCR, which is cost-effective and which Roche already makes. He said Roche is "extremely well positioned here to really scale this on a global level, much better than some of the other players." He also pointedly referred to the market capitalizations of other MRD companies.

Sause said MRD "is going to be one of the biggest drivers of the diagnostics industry" [Roche DX Day]. Roche sees the MRD-driven disease-monitoring market growing 32% a year to CHF 5 billion by 2030.

**Foundation Medicine as a channel.** FMI's CEO, Dan Malarek, said FMI is an AXELIOS early-access site [Roche DX Day]. It expects shorter turnaround and a lower cost base from the platform, and plans to bring its installation-and-services partner sites onto AXELIOS later. Malarek said AXELIOS in clinical workflows will also enhance FMI's biopharma services.

For Illumina, this matters because FMI is a large-volume clinical sequencing customer. Key FMI facts [Roche DX Day]:

- Founded in 2010 out of the Broad Institute.
- First to launch a CGP test.
- More than 1.5 million CGPs delivered.
- More than 40% of US oncologists ordered from FMI in the last 12 months.
- More than 100 approved companion diagnostics, "more than the entire rest of the industry combined." A companion diagnostic is a test required to pick patients for a specific drug.
- Works with the top 80 biopharma companies and supports more than 300 active trials.

The FMI and SAGA product plan around AXELIOS:

- **Pathlight MRD** (from SAGA). It detected breast cancer recurrence more than 13 months before CT/PET imaging in early-stage disease. It has Medicare coverage for stage II and III breast cancer across all subtypes, over 6 years and 12 time points. The plan is to use AXELIOS for a whole-genome tissue baseline and Digital LightCycler for multi-timepoint monitoring.
- **SAGA pipeline.** Breast and colorectal assays are launched, ovarian studies are underway and lung is next. Malarek would say no more until the deal closed.
- **F1 Monitor.** This is a blood-only test for metastatic patients. It detects progression with roughly half the lead time of CT/PET imaging and reports resistance mutations.
- **Flexomics platform.** This extends FMI into neurodegenerative, metabolic and autoimmune disease.

Malarek also made several market and quality points [Roche DX Day]:

- **Adoption gap.** One large community oncology network, which treats about 20% of US cancer patients, gets CGP for only about 50% of metastatic patients. Malarek said this should be above 90%.
- **Market growth.** He expects oncology testing across biopharma, therapy selection and monitoring to "essentially double over the next 5 years." The drivers are new drug types (bispecifics, antibody-drug conjugates, molecular glues), CGP adoption and monitoring. About 50 million people live with cancer, a number that has nearly doubled in 10 years.
- **Quality example.** MTAP loss is present in about 10% of cancers and is a biomarker for MAT2A and PRMT5 inhibitor trials. A Sarah Cannon Research Institute study of three labs found FMI detected it at published rates, while the other labs found 40% to 60% less. FMI is the enrollment assay for 15 MTAP trials.
- **Copy-number loss.** FoundationOne CDx is the only FDA-approved tissue NGS test validated to detect a copy-number loss (PTEN).
- **Delivery models.** FMI runs centralized testing in US and German labs, with samples from more than 50 countries. Roche Diagnostics sells FMI outside the US, and Chugai sells it in Japan. Installation-and-services sites include the UK (where FMI won 50% of the NHS liquid-biopsy tender), Gustave Roussy in Paris and University Hospital Zurich. FMI also sells decentralized AVENIO kits.
- **Software integration.** VENTANA staining and navify Clinical Hub (trial matching, dynamic NCCN guidelines, AI patient education) are being built into the FMI ordering portal.

**Competitive differentiation and regulatory debate.** Asked by Guggenheim how FMI differs from peers, Malarek gave two answers [Roche DX Day]. Clinicians and pharma still see FMI as best-in-class, and FMI gains scale through Roche, particularly outside the US.

Asked by JPMorgan about a recent FDA advisory committee (ODAC) that rejected earlier molecular testing over imaging-based progression for lack of data, Malarek called it "a setback in a specific case, but not a setback overall." He argued pharma needs surrogate endpoints, meaning early measures that stand in for survival, and ultrasensitive MRD can supply them [Roche DX Day].

Sause's closing argument was that MRD-enabled surrogate endpoints will make possible trials "that cannot run today," benefiting both Diagnostics and Pharma [Roche DX Day].

**Market sizes for the oncology ecosystem.** Roche's view of oncology testing to 2030 [Roche DX Day]:

- **Early detection (MCED).** Growing at a 35% compound annual growth rate (CAGR, the average yearly growth rate) to CHF 5.3 billion by 2030. Sause later said the 35% is global, "early days," and potentially higher.
- **Therapy selection.** CHF 17 billion, growing 9%. This covers companion diagnostics, digital pathology and CGP.
- **Monitoring.** Growing 32% to CHF 5 billion, driven by MRD.

## PathAI and digital pathology

Roche announced a definitive merger agreement to acquire PathAI, a Boston AI-pathology company, around 8 May 2026. Eschli said it was announced "just last Friday" before the 12 May event. The deal is subject to regulatory approval [Roche DX Day]. Roche's July call referred to the agreement being signed "earlier in July 2026" [H1 2026 call]. The sources disagree on the date; the May transcript is the more contemporaneous.

**Rationale.** Schinecker's case is that cancer cases and diagnostic complexity are rising while the number of pathologists is falling [Roche DX Day]. Sause called PathAI "wholly complementary to our Ventana business" [H1 2026 call]. He said it gives earlier engagement with pharma on companion diagnostics.

**What Roche plans.** Moritz Hartmann (Roche Information Solutions) said Roche plans to pair its scanners and algorithms with a next-generation AI-centric image management system. Roche will also build on companion-diagnostic algorithm partnerships and offer AI services to biopharma for trial efficiency and biomarker discovery [Roche DX Day].

**PathAI's view of the field.** CEO Andrew Beck said PathAI was founded 10 years ago with "one foot in biopharma and one in diagnostics." Only a minority of pathology is digital today. Beck expects it to be routine and AI-assisted by about 2035, with AI companion diagnostics accompanying most new drug approvals [Roche DX Day].

**PathAI products** [Roche DX Day]:

- PathExplore and IHC Explore, for translational research.
- PathAssist Derm, which received FDA breakthrough device designation in Q1 2026.
- AIM-MASH AI-Assist, the first AI algorithm qualified by both the EMA and FDA for MASH liver-disease trials, in 2025.
- AISight Dx image management, which gained CE-IVDR and FDA clearance in 2025 and was the first such system with a predetermined change control plan.

**Existing partnership.** PathAI has partnered with Roche since 2024, including an AI companion-diagnostic deal with VENTANA.

**Case study.** Beck cited Genentech work presented at AACR 2023. Deep learning on standard tissue slides from the OAK lung cancer trial found a "CP1" signature (more lymphocytes and plasma cells, fewer fibroblasts) that predicted the best response to anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy. This held even in patients negative on the standard PD-L1 test. In the held-out IMpower150 dataset, the hazard ratio was 0.56 in PD-L1-negative patients versus about 0.7 in the broader evaluable population; a hazard ratio below 1 means lower risk of death or progression. Beck framed this as a way to widen immunotherapy eligibility.

Asked by Citi about PathAI's contracts with third-party pharma companies, Beck said PathAI has operated independently. He expects the combination with Roche to increase the value PathAI can offer those partners [Roche DX Day].

## Diagnostics division: strategy, results and outlook

### Strategy and market position

Sause said Roche Diagnostics' strategy has been unchanged for six years [Roche DX Day]. It has three pillars:

1. Innovate and deliver high-medical-value tests.
2. Deliver clinical decision-support tools.
3. Combine the two into solutions across the patient's care journey.

The four goals are to expand leadership in the clinical lab, lead in decentralized testing, differentiate in the highest-burden disease areas, and help patients navigate the oncology and neurology "continuum."

Roche claims more than 50% of FDA diagnostics approvals over the past six years without spending more than half the industry's R&D budget. It says it delivered 31 billion tests, which is 3 to 4 per person on Earth [Schinecker, Roche DX Day]. Roche has more than 48,000 Elecsys immunoassay instruments installed and a direct presence in more than 150 countries [Gillieron, Roche DX Day].

**Market growth benchmark.** Sause said the IVD market grows a little above 3%, so mid-to-high single-digit growth is up to double the market. About 5% would be at least 66% above market [Roche DX Day].

**Customer areas.** The table below shows Roche's four customer areas, the market sizes and growth rates Roche presented in May, and Roche's stated position in each. It shows that sequencing is Roche's fastest-growing lab market among the large ones, while Core Lab is the biggest but slowest.

| Area | Market size / growth (as presented, May 2026) | Roche position |
|---|---|---|
| Core Lab (routine chemistry, immunoassays, mass spec) | CHF 40 billion; growing 3% | No. 1; largest unit |
| Molecular Lab: PCR | CHF 9 billion; molecular lab growing 4% | No. 1 in lab molecular diagnostics |
| Molecular Lab: sequencing | CHF 7 billion; growing 7% | New entrant via AXELIOS |
| Pathology Lab | Growing 8% | No. 1 in precision oncology diagnostics |
| Near Patient Care | Growing 3% (CGM within it growing 14%) | Diabetes management largest segment |

**Core Lab growth cut.** Roche's Core Lab five-year growth assumption fell from 4% a year earlier to 3% in May 2026. Sause blamed China, probably the second-largest immunoassay market globally, where reimbursement cuts and healthcare reform have shrunk the market [Roche DX Day, answering Citi].

**Disease-burden framing.** Roche's focus areas account for about 60% of the global health burden. Roche projects mortality increases to 2040 of 50% in oncology, 67% in neurology, 40% in cardiometabolic disease and 29% in infectious disease [Roche DX Day].

**Other 2030 market opportunities Roche cited** [Roche DX Day]:

- **Neurology.** Early detection CHF 480 million; therapy selection growing 17%.
- **Cardiometabolic.** Early detection CHF 6.9 billion; acute cardiovascular diagnosis CHF 4.2 billion.
- **Infectious disease.** Diagnosis CHF 25 billion; viral-load monitoring CHF 1.6 billion, a market Roche says it "shaped and built… over several decades."

Instrument placements across the three lab customer areas grew double digits in 2025. Roche plans more than 140 new tests [Roche DX Day].

Excluding China's healthcare pricing reform, growth was 6%. A weak Northern Hemisphere respiratory season cost a further CHF 50 million. For context, the Pharma division grew 6% and the group 6% at CER.

By region:

- North America +8%
- EMEA +3%
- Latin America +10%
- Asia-Pacific −5%, made up of China −15% and Asia-Pacific excluding China +6%

The customer-area table below shows that Pathology was the strongest area and Near Patient Care the weakest. Core Lab would have grown 8% without the China effect.

|---|---|---|

The figure was helped by CHF 126 million of extraordinary patent-licensing income. ### Outlook: growth, margins and China

**Growth.** The long-term ambition is mid-to-high single-digit sales growth, outgrowing the market, with core operating profit growing faster than sales [Sause and Schinecker, Roche DX Day]. For 2026 the ambition is mid-single-digit growth with a smaller China impact. For 2027, Roche aims to return to mid-to-high single digits [H1 2026 call]. Schinecker repeated his confidence in mid-to-high single-digit growth from the base business plus launches in September [JPM CEO call, 2026-09-08].

**Margins.** The 2026 Diagnostics core margin is meant to be "broadly stable" at CER versus 14.4% in 2025, absorbing China and tariffs, with a negative currency effect also noted [H1 2026 call]. In May, Sause said all headwinds, including oil-linked costs, were built into the ambition [Roche DX Day]. BNP's Peter Verdult had noted peers citing about 100 basis points of oil impact with oil above $100. Sause said an operational-improvement program begun in 2025 creates room in the P&L to fund launches.

The longer-term margin target is notably higher. In September, Schinecker said Diagnostics should return to a margin of "at least 20%" over time, after two years of significant China price cuts [JPM CEO call, 2026-09-08]. Barclays heard the same near-term message: stable FY26 margins, supported by efficiency programs [Barclays, 2026-09-28].

**China timing has slipped slightly across sources.**

- In May, Sause said China should stabilize "next year," return to growth after that, and only then would Roche consider raising its ambition [Roche DX Day, answering UBS].
- By late September, Sause told Barclays the reforms will still weigh in FY27, with a return to single-digit growth in FY28. He added that Roche's in-market volume in China is already growing, that pricing headwinds are manageable and in guidance, and that Roche has no plan to shrink its China presence [Barclays, 2026-09-28].
- The July target of returning to mid-to-high single-digit division growth in 2027 implies the rest of the business offsets China.

**Rest of world.** Underlying market growth outside China is low-to-mid single digits. Roche says it keeps outperforming competitors despite a weak respiratory season [Barclays, 2026-09-28].

**Acquisitions.** Sause expects "significant tailwind" from acquisitions and launches [Roche DX Day]. He added that, as the largest IVD company, Roche's room for inorganic growth is limited by its size [answering Wolfe].

**US reimbursement.** PAMA (the Protecting Access to Medicare Act, which sets Medicare lab-test prices) was the subject of questions. In May, Sause said Roche lobbies through ACLA and AdvaMed, including for the RESULTS Act. He said the year-end outcome is impossible to call, but even a non-extension would not be "really material" to Roche [Roche DX Day]. After CMS announced proposed PAMA cuts on 21 September 2026, Barclays said it believes Roche is insulated because it sells to labs, which are the ones reimbursed by Medicare. That is Barclays' view, not management's [Barclays, 2026-09-28].

**Launch progress.** By mid-2026, Roche had achieved 7 of its 11 key Diagnostics launches for the year [H1 2026 call].

### Other launches (non-sequencing)

**Mass spectrometry (cobas i 601)** [Roche DX Day]. Mass spectrometry identifies molecules by their mass.

- **Market.** It addresses a CHF 3 billion segment that is largely manual and based on laboratory-developed tests.
- **Launch.** Roche launched a fully automated IVD version a little over a year before May 2026. Placements are in Asia, Europe and North America, including LabCorp, and some customers have issued press releases about buying it.
- **Menu.** There are 39 assays in CE-mark countries, with 4 more in 2026. In the US, the instrument plus vitamin D and six steroid tests are approved for use in CLIA labs (labs certified under US federal lab-quality rules). The remaining tests go through 510(k) FDA clearance from late 2026 into 2027. Drugs of abuse are the next wave.
- **Target.** Roche aims for an installed base of 100 instruments by end-2026. In September Roche still held this as a "stretch target." It reported strong interest from large reference labs despite longer-than-expected validation cycles [Barclays, 2026-09-28].

**Continuous glucose monitoring (Accu-Chek SmartGuide).** CGM means a wearable sensor that tracks blood sugar continuously.

- **Product.** It is AI-powered, with Glucose Predict and Night Low Predict algorithms.
- **Roll-out.** Controlled launch is now in 45 markets, up from 4 to 5 at the previous update. Roche has 11 automated manufacturing facilities, and its mySugr app (more than 7 million downloads) is built in.
- **Next steps.** Roche aims for approval of factory calibration in H2 2026, which removes the initial finger-prick, and a pediatric claim. A next-generation patch is coming with a smaller inserter, a different battery and fewer manufacturing steps.
- **Strategy.** Roche wants to upgrade its 30 million blood-glucose-meter users (11 million on insulin, 20 million not) to CGM.

Sause said CGM can revive a franchise whose glucose-meter business declined for years [Roche DX Day, Amann-Zalan and Sause].

**Alzheimer's blood test (Elecsys pTau217).** This received a CE mark on 12 May 2026 as the first blood-based rule-in/rule-out Alzheimer's test intended for primary care [Roche DX Day; H1 2026 call].

- **Performance.** It uses two cutoffs. Sensitivity and specificity are all above 93%. In primary care, NPV (the chance a negative result is truly negative) is 98.6% and PPV is close to 80%. Fewer than 20% of patients land in the intermediate zone [Gillieron, Roche DX Day].
- **Market need.** In Alzheimer's, 75% of patients are undiagnosed, diagnosis takes about 3 to 3.5 years, and 98% are untreated. Roche expects the market to quadruple in 10 years.
- **Competitive position.** Schinecker claims a "significant head start versus our other major competitors," especially combined with Roche's Alzheimer's drug trontinemab [H1 2026 call].
- **Limits.** An annual screening test for people without symptoms is not available from anyone. Kumaresan called it "a pretty tough problem" [Roche DX Day].
- **Related tests.** The NfL test for multiple sclerosis progression got a CE mark in Q1 2026. Roche's Alzheimer's portfolio has seven solutions, including pTau181 and ApoE4.

**Tuberculosis test (Elecsys IGRA TB).** IGRA (interferon-gamma release assay) is a blood test for latent TB.

- **Approval.** It received a CE mark in July 2026.
- **Validation.** It was validated in close to 2,000 patients, with 91.1% positive and 94.6% negative agreement versus standard of care.
- **Throughput.** Up to 400 samples an hour on four e 801 modules (100 an hour per module).
- **Market.** About 30 million IGRA tests are run annually. Gillieron sees a market above CHF 1 billion by 2034 [Roche DX Day].
- **US status.** US trials are progressing [Kumaresan, Roche DX Day], but in September Sause gave no US timing. He conceded that without a gold-standard reference test Roche cannot prove superiority over incumbents, and is generating more data. Tender-driven European contracts are slow to validate [Barclays, 2026-09-28].

**Point-of-care PCR (Liat).** Roche says it has an installed base of "around one thousand" Liat systems this year. New placements are largely additive, with no meaningful competitive impact so far [Barclays, 2026-09-28]. Flu demand remained weak. Declining vaccination and rising Flu-A activity can signal a severe season, but its size is hard to predict.

**Other instruments and assays** [Roche DX Day]:

- **cobas smart e.** A low-volume immunoanalyzer for a CHF 1 billion market growing about 5%. It succeeds the e 411, which has more than 18,000 units installed, with CE launch planned for late 2026.
- **cobas ultra lab automation.** Controlled launch in 2026; omni transport with Hitachi to follow.
- **Next-generation staining platform.** Being developed with Hitachi.
- **cobas sense.** A benchtop immunoanalyzer launching in 2027 with high-sensitivity troponin T, with 0.995 correlation to the lab assay.
- **cobas pulse.** More than 24,000 placements, with a US launch later in 2026.
- **LumiraDx.** A portable point-of-care platform, available in more than 43 countries and approved in more than 75.
- **Blood screening.** The MPX-E multiplex assay adds hepatitis E, for a donor-screening market of CHF 1.8 billion.
- **Hepatitis D.** The first fully automated hepatitis D RNA test is coming.
- **HPV genotyping.** A test covering 14 high-risk types, plus a self-collection kit, is planned next year.
- **TROP2.** This is set to be Roche's first computational-pathology companion diagnostic, launching in lung cancer in 2027.

### Digital

Hartmann said Roche has converted its analyzer base into the "largest connected digital software footprint globally" [Roche DX Day]. It plans an AI-native lab operating system in three phases:

1. **End of 2026:** AI on operational data, such as demand forecasting and quality-control anomaly detection.
2. **Second phase:** lab clinical intelligence, adding lab results to a trained large language model.
3. **Third phase:** a "precision engine" that adds selected electronic medical record data.

## Group context (where it bears on Diagnostics)

Diagnostics is about 25% of Roche's sales and Pharma about 75% [UBS, 2026-09-14]. Group performance shapes how much room Diagnostics has to invest.

**H1 2026 group results.** Sales grew 6% at CER, core operating profit 10% and core EPS 9% (core earnings per share) [H1 2026 call]. In Swiss francs, sales fell 2% because of an 8-point currency headwind. A weak flu season cost about 1% of group sales (more than CHF 200 million).

**2026 guidance confirmed.** Roche expects mid-single-digit sales growth, high-single-digit core EPS growth and a higher dividend. It cut its expected 2026 loss-of-exclusivity hit to about CHF 600 million. Loss of exclusivity (LOE) is the sales lost when patents expire and cheaper copies arrive.

**Pipeline.** Schinecker says pipeline value is up 63% to 93% versus end-2022: 63% in May, 93% in July. Up to 19 new medicines could launch by 2030. He says the marketed Pharma portfolio will keep growing, with no near-term patent cliff [Roche DX Day; H1 2026 call; JPM CEO call]. Pharma margins are guided "at least stable." Roche says it has "significant firepower" for M&A [JPM CEO call].

**Productivity.** About CHF 1 to 1.3 billion of R&D spending has been reallocated. Flatiron, which lost about EUR/CHF 250 million a year three years ago, turned profitable in Q1 2026 [Roche DX Day; H1 2026 call].

**AI.** AI is embedded in all large-molecule drug discovery. Roche says it has the highest compute power in pharma, and it is a founding member of the SAIL Foundation, alongside companies such as Anthropic, Microsoft and IBM [Roche DX Day].

**Key pharma catalysts analysts track:**

- Giredestrant breast-cancer FDA decisions: 30 November 2026 (lidERA) and 18 December 2026 (evERA).
- Fenebrutinib MS filing.
- Full data for the competing remibrutinib at MSToronto, 21–23 October 2026.
- Obesity drug enicepatide (CT-388), which Roche calls best-in-class.
- Pharma Day, 28 September 2026.

## What analysts say

**Questions at the May Investor Day.** Analysts focused on four things:

1. How acquisitions change the growth and margin model (UBS).
2. AXELIOS market share and year-one placements (BNP Paribas, TD Cowen).
3. Sequencing profitability (Canaccord).
4. How Roche would integrate SAGA and Freenome (Jefferies).

Management gave no share target, offered "about 100" first-year systems, and asserted that sequencing can be "massively profitable" [Roche DX Day].

**J.P. Morgan (Neutral).** J.P. Morgan rates Roche Neutral, meaning it expects performance in line with the analyst's coverage [JPM, 2026-09-15]. The share price was CHF 361.10 on 14 September 2026. The last published target is CHF 325 (12 March 2026). J.P. Morgan was Underweight from October 2023 to July 2025, then moved to Neutral at a CHF 350 target on 8 December 2025.

The note relays Schinecker's points without its own estimates or valuation. For sequencing, those points are roughly one-third of about 100 first-year AXELIOS placements, no teaser pricing, reagent revenue building as assays migrate, and the "at least 20%" Diagnostics margin goal. J.P. Morgan is a market maker in Roche and has banking ties to the company.

**UBS (Buy).** UBS rates Roche Buy with a CHF 376 target, unchanged since 27 April 2026, against a CHF 344.20 share price on 11 September 2026 [UBS, 2026-09-14]. That implies a 12.2% forecast total return (9.2% price gain plus a 2.9% dividend yield), or 6.9% more than UBS's 5.2% market assumption.

UBS values Roche on a multiple of 2026–27 earnings per share, cross-checked with a product-by-product net present value. UBS's EPS forecasts sit below consensus (the average of analysts' forecasts):

| Year | UBS EPS (CHF) | Consensus EPS (CHF) |
|---|---|---|
| 2026E | 19.61 | 20.43 |
| 2027E | 20.77 | 21.97 |

UBS forecasts group revenue rising from CHF 61.5 billion in 2025 to CHF 72.2 billion in 2030. Over the same period it sees the operating margin rising from 35.5% to 40.5%, and net debt of CHF 16.2 billion turning into net cash by 2028.

UBS's takeaways were mostly pharma: Roche says it can sustain mid-single-digit growth to 2030 without new Phase 3 readouts, and is confident in about CHF 500 million of 2027 giredestrant sales. For diagnostics, UBS lists "Diagnostics sales growth ahead of expectations on new technology launches (NGS, CGM, mass spec)" as an upside risk to its view.

**Barclays (Overweight).** Barclays reiterated Overweight with a CHF 430 target, against a CHF 363.40 share price on 24 September 2026, implying 18.3% upside [Barclays, 2026-09-28]. The target rose from CHF 260 in October 2023 through CHF 390 and 410 in January 2026 to CHF 430 on 7 September 2026. It is set at 18 times 2028 core EPS, about a 34% premium to the roughly 14x sector multiple.

Barclays justifies the premium on three grounds:

1. Faster 2028–2032 EPS growth: 5.1% a year versus a 4.0% sector median.
2. Limited LOE exposure.
3. Diagnostics' durability, since it "isn't subject to LOEs in the same way a Pharma business is."

Its Overweight case also cites giredestrant and margin expansion. Barclays calls the near-term Diagnostics outlook "balanced." AXELIOS and mass spec launches look strong but early, there is little visibility on the US TB launch, China weighs until 2028, and flu is weak. Barclays is positive on the long term, expecting NGS and mass spec to become "large, differentiated revenue drivers." It also expects Roche to become the preferred development partner for labs and pharma through its end-to-end oncology offering, combining Digital LightCycler, AXELIOS, SAGA, PathAI and Elecsys.

**What this means for Illumina.** None of the analyst notes quantifies AXELIOS revenue or discusses Illumina directly. The open questions they leave are:

- whether early placements convert into reagent pull-through;
- how the $150 genome and $0.06 per million reads prices hold up against incumbents;
- whether clinical demand, especially through FMI and SAGA's MRD workflow, materializes as Roche claims.

ASHG data in autumn 2026 is the next disclosed checkpoint.
