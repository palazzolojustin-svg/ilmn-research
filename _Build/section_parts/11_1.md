## Business overview

### What PacBio does

Pacific Biosciences of California (PacBio, ticker PACB) makes long-read DNA sequencers. These are instruments that read long, continuous stretches of DNA, often tens of thousands of letters at a time. Illumina's short-read machines instead read fragments of a few hundred letters and stitch them together. PacBio was founded in 2000 and is headquartered in Menlo Park, California. It reports three revenue lines: instruments (the sequencers), consumables (the reagent kits and chips customers must buy for every sequencing run, which recur), and service and other [UBS, 2026-02-13].

PacBio's signature data type is **HiFi** (high-fidelity) sequencing, which produces long reads that are also highly accurate. Sequencing happens on a disposable chip called a **SMRT Cell**, using a chemistry kit. The current product line is:

- **Revio:** the high-throughput system. Throughput means how much DNA data a machine produces per run. Revio's average selling price (ASP) was about $482k in Q4 2025 [UBS, 2026-02-13]. Cumulative shipments reached 346 by Q1 2026 and 366 by Q2 2026.
- **Vega:** the lower-priced benchtop long-read system, launched in late 2024. It is aimed at smaller labs and new customers. Cumulative shipments were 147 at end-2025, 174 after Q1 2026 and 200 after Q2 2026.
- **SPRQ and SPRQ-Nx chemistries:** SPRQ is the earlier reagent set. SPRQ-Nx is the new one. It raises SMRT Cell yield and, with the **multi-use SMRT Cell**, lets one chip be run up to three times. It launched commercially on Revio at the end of May 2026 and on Vega in August 2026.
- **Other products:** **Kinnex** (RNA sequencing kits), **PureTarget** (targeted panels used for carrier screening), and **DeepConsensus** (an AI-powered accuracy algorithm developed with Google). An older line, Sequel II/IIe, is still used clinically in China.
- **A high-throughput (HT) platform:** an unnamed next-generation, higher-capacity sequencer, targeted for launch in 2027.

PacBio also had a short-read sequencer, **Onso**. In January 2026 it closed the sale of its high-throughput short-read intellectual property and assets **to Illumina** for about $48.1M in net cash [Q1 2026 call]. The short-read business had been meant to address "the piece of oncology that long reads can't really go after very well" [Van Oene, Morgan Stanley conf., 2026-09-15]. In Q1 2026 PacBio also resolved litigation with Personal Genomics of Taiwan. Then-CEO Christian Henry said these steps "sharpen our focus" on long reads, which the company calls its true competitive advantage.

A key metric throughout this section is **pull-through**: the annual consumables revenue generated per installed Revio. Management targeted "low-to-mid $200k" in 2025 and $225k–$250k for 2026. In August 2026 it cut the 2026 range to $200k–$225k.

### How PacBio competes with Illumina

The core trade-off is quality versus cost. Barclays put it this way: long reads are clearly better than short reads on quality, but "from a cost, speed, and ease-of-use perspective they have never been on par," and "it [takes] a lot to dethrone the entrenched technologies" [Barclays, 2025-11-05]. UBS made the same point. It said PacBio's "unique capabilities" struggle "in the face of persistently unfavorable relative cost and throughput differentials vs. Illumina," which "will remain a challenge" [UBS, 2026-02-13].

**Cost per genome is the main battleground.** In August 2025, Barclays argued that "the true Dx opportunity unlocks when PACB gets the cost of sequencing down close to ILMN's $200" per genome. Dx means diagnostics. At that price, "the economics work for larger scale vols of HiFi + SBS for a Dx test." SBS is sequencing by synthesis, Illumina's chemistry. Barclays called this "not a biochemistry issue" but "an engineering problem around the chip and the supporting system." The team had been working on it for two to three years, and "engineering issues tend to be more easily solved than chemistry issues." This was Barclays' own opinion, not a management statement [Barclays, 2025-08-07].

SPRQ-Nx is the answer to that problem. Management first said it would lower the cost of a human whole genome (WGS, reading a person's entire DNA) to below $300 at scale [Q3 2025 call]. Barclays then described it as $300 per genome, on par with high-throughput peers. At launch, the U.S. list price for a 20x HiFi human genome fell 30%, to **$345**. "20x" means each DNA position is read about 20 times on average [Q2 2026 call].

**PacBio's clinical argument** is that HiFi is "the only commercially available sequencing technology that we believe can comprehensively characterize substantially all classes of variants in a single assay." Short-read approaches need multiple tests to get a similar result, so HiFi should give a faster answer at lower total testing cost [Henry, Q1 2026 call]. Barclays framed PacBio as a "substitution model for an already existing market." It said gaining just 2% market share "would essentially double revenues from here" [Barclays, 2025-11-05].

**Illumina is now a long-read competitor.** Illumina tried to acquire PacBio in 2020, but the FTC blocked the deal. Barclays calls Illumina's TruPath launch "the third iteration/name change of ILMN's push into long-read sequencing." Given that mixed history, Barclays wants more community data before concluding Illumina will win share. In the near term, though, "ILMN's launch could freeze part of the long-read market," and Barclays expects it to draw more investor attention than Roche [Barclays, 2026-03-06].

**Roche** is coming with Axelios, "a relatively long-ish read technology that may freeze some of the market for PACB" [Barclays, 2026-02-12]. Axelios reads are longer than Illumina's SBS reads "but no where close to PACB." Even so, budget-constrained researchers who use both technologies may spend scarce capital on Axelios. Barclays expects a modest headwind to Revio in 2026 [Barclays, 2026-03-06].

Notably, PacBio management did not name Illumina or other competitors on the Q2 2026 call or at the Morgan Stanley conference. The only competitive language was that SPRQ-Nx lets PacBio "compete for substantially larger projects where competitive economics are crucial to winning" [Van Oene, Q2 2026 call].

### Customers and end markets

Demand comes from three main groups:

- **Academic and government (A&G) labs:** historically the core market, especially for plant, animal and other non-human research. U.S. A&G demand has been weak throughout 2025–2026 because of NIH (National Institutes of Health) funding uncertainty.
- **Clinical and hospital customers:** the growth engine. They use HiFi for rare disease diagnosis, **carrier screening** (testing healthy prospective parents for gene variants they could pass on), newborn screening and pharmacogenomics.
- **Newer buyers:** population-scale programs, commercial service providers, biopharma, and AI companies building **foundation models** (large AI models trained broadly on biological data).

Management increasingly frames the AI and biopharma groups as replacing A&G. Van Oene expects "AI therapeutic companies and nontraditional customers" to "likely replace academic and government in many ways" over three to five years [Morgan Stanley conf., 2026-09-15].

### Q2 2025 (reported early August 2025)

Revenue of about $40M beat the Street's $37M by 8.9%. The beat came from instruments ($14M versus $12M, +20.0%) and service ($7M versus $5M, +34.2%). Consumables were a slight miss ($19M versus $20M, -4.2%). Placements also beat Barclays' forecasts: Revio 15 versus 13 and Vega 38 versus 24. Gross margin of 38.2% beat the Street's 34.5% by 375 basis points (bps, hundredths of a percentage point; 100 bps equals one point). EPS (earnings per share, here a loss) was -$0.13, versus -$0.16 expected by Barclays and -$0.17 by consensus [Barclays, 2025-08-07].

Asia-Pacific (APAC) and Europe, Middle East and Africa (EMEA) together grew 45% year over year and drove the beat. U.S. A&G demand was soft, as expected and consistent with peers.

Guidance changes:
- FY2025 revenue narrowed to $155–165M with an unchanged midpoint. Consensus was $156M; Barclays was at $154M.
- Gross margin guidance rose 100 bps to 37–40%, against consensus of 37.3%.
- Consumables were guided to grow mid-teens percent and instruments to decline mid-teens percent.
- Opex was lowered to $235–240M. Interest and other expense was raised to $6–8M (from $5–7M). The share count was trimmed to 298M (from 299M).
- Q3 2025 revenue was guided flat both sequentially and year over year, about $40M. APAC would rise year over year but fall sequentially because demand was pulled forward into the first half.
- The exit gross margin above 40% and positive cash flow exiting 2027 were maintained.

Barclays' summary was: "Trends consistent with rest of peers on US A&G side. Strength in APAC/EMEA drove the beat. Strong demand for Vega highlights the interest in HiFi. Dx and translational demand starting to pick up." Translational means research aimed at clinical use. Barclays read Vega as bringing HiFi to the same budget-constrained customers "just at a more approachable price point." It called clinical traction "the most notable update on the print." Its diagnostics lab checks and a WGS non-deal roadshow (an investor marketing trip) turned up more long-read use in carrier screening and targeted panels, citing an ataxia panel with Quest Diagnostics. Barclays tied this to the many large long-read projects completed in recent years. Management highlighted a large global ALS study. Barclays came away "incrementally more positive," calling the quarter "relatively strong" given the macro environment [Barclays, 2025-08-07].

### Q3 2025 (reported 5 November 2025)

Barclays headlined the note "For Every 2 Steps Forward in Consumables, Instruments Take a Step Back." Revenue of $38.4M missed the Street's $40.2M by 4.4%. The details:

- Instruments: $11.3M versus $13.7M, -17.5%.
- Service: $5.8M versus $6.2M, -7.0%.
- Consumables beat: $21.3M versus $20.0M, +6.3%.
- Revio placements were in line at 13. Vega missed at 32 versus Barclays' 42, a sequential decline in Vega's first launch year.
- Gross margin was 42.1% versus the Street's 37.8%, a 430 bps beat driven by the high-margin consumables mix.
- EPS of -$0.12 beat -$0.15 from both Barclays and consensus.

Management gave several explanations [Q3 2025 call, via Barclays]:
- Vega missed mainly in Europe, where procurement ran past quarter-end. Some delayed purchase orders had already arrived for Q4.
- One Revio with a "much higher ASP" failed installation testing, so its revenue shifted to Q4.
- About 75% of Revio shipments and about 60% of Vega placements went to new customers.
- Instrument ASPs dipped because of discounted **strategic placements** at key institutions meant to drive consumable use. Management expected ASPs to recover.
- Revio pull-through of $236k was at the high end of the range.

By region, EMEA grew 18% on 50% consumables growth. The Americas fell 10% but grew 2% sequentially. APAC fell 11%, although China beat management's expectations.

FY2025 revenue guidance fell to $155–160M, cutting $5M from the top. Management guided Q4 revenue up about 10% sequentially, with both Revio and Vega placements at yearly highs. It gave no formal 2026 guidance but said revenue should grow on a larger installed base, a better funnel, steady placements and strong utilization. Cash burn for 2025 was reiterated at $115M, and positive cash flow by end-2027 was reiterated. SPRQ-Nx drew interest from more than 100 customers for a paid beta that began in November 2025, with early access planned for early 2026 and broad rollout in 2026.

Barclays credited the pull-through to "all the market education and marketing the team has done over the past 2 years." It cited an EU consortium paper showing HiFi is "much better than standard testing technologies for carrier screening and hard to sequence genes," giving PacBio "a good foothold in the clinical market." The remaining hurdles, in Barclays' view, were up-front workflows and costs and simplifying data analysis. Its conclusion: "we are still at the early days and the story will take time to play out." Barclays expected margin to keep improving from the low-40s and possibly expand materially "when vols reach critical capacity utilization" [Barclays, 2025-11-05].

On 15 December 2025, with the stock at $2.17, Barclays raised its target to $2.00 from $1.50.

### Q4 2025 and initial 2026 guidance (reported 12 February 2026)

PacBio had pre-announced a beat in January. Revenue was $44.6M versus the Street's $42.1M, a 6.0% beat. Instruments drove it at $17.3M versus $14.3M (+20.8%), helped by Vega shipments in Europe that had slipped from Q3. Consumables set a record at $21.6M, in line with the Street. Service missed slightly at $5.7M versus $6.0M. Revio placements of 21 beat Barclays' 13, and Vega's 42 matched its estimate. Gross margin of 39.9% missed the Street's 40.7% by 81 bps [Barclays, 2026-02-12; UBS, 2026-02-13].

Other details from the quarter:
- Hospital and clinical customers' consumables sales grew 55% in 2025.
- About 65% of 2025 Vega placements went to new customers. Vega ASPs were flat.
- Revio was again discounted into key accounts expected to generate "above-average consumable pull-through."
- UBS cited full-year Revio pull-through of about $242k, slightly below the prior year. The note labeled this "2026," but it most likely refers to 2025.
- Management de-emphasized the academic/government channel and highlighted clinical momentum, especially in EMEA.
- SPRQ-Nx was moving from beta (about 4–5 users) to early access (about 15), expanding to EMEA "in several weeks," then APAC, with full launch later in 2026.

2026 guidance and the analyst reaction:
- **Revenue** guidance of $165–180M sat against consensus of $178M. Barclays was already modeling the high end ($178M in its model, $175M in its guidance table, which the note did not reconcile). UBS modeled $173.3M.
- **Gross margin** guidance was 41–44%, against consensus of 42.4%.
- **Placements and pull-through:** Revio placements flat to slightly up, Revio pull-through $225–250k, Vega placements up, and Vega pull-through $25–40k.
- **Costs:** cash burn to "slightly tick up" and opex below $230M.
- **Q1 2026:** revenue up year over year but below Q4, with gross margin about 41% at the low end. Instrument seasonality is stronger than consumables seasonality.
- **Growth drivers:** clinical HiFi adoption and consumables, with SPRQ-Nx and multi-use cells driving clinical growth and, eventually, margins.

Barclays called Vega "the big headline in the Q," opening doors that "could eventually help drive broader HiFi adoption w/in the clinic," since "the more researcher's hands HiFi is in, the better chances of making the shift to clinical applications." It said SPRQ-Nx "resets long-read economics at $300/genome," with launch "by 2H." It was "not expecting the funding floodgates to open" and predicted "another knife-fight of a 1H," but praised management for continuing to "keep hammering away at the opportunities." It planned channel checks at AGBT (the Advances in Genome Biology and Technology conference) to see whether momentum could become "an inflection point over the next 12-18mos" [Barclays, 2026-02-12].

Barclays also listed clinical adoption examples:
- **Rare disease and oncology:**
  - Berry Genomics won approval from China's regulator (the NMPA) for Sequel II thalassemia testing, with possible expansion to fragile X, spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) and Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD).
  - Children's Mercy adopted HiFi as a first-line diagnostic.
  - Radboud UMC consolidated several standard-of-care tests into one HiFi test.
  - Stanford uses Revio for pharmacogenomics.
  - Ambry Genetics is validating improved diagnostic yields.
  - The HiFi Solves consortium passed 15 members.
- **Carrier screening:** HiFi assays are being validated alongside short-read tests, with launches expected in 2026.
- **Population sequencing:** HiFi is used in All of Us (first data published October 2025), the Long Life Family Study (about 8,000 samples) and the Asian Pan-Genome Consortium (more than 10,000 samples). Pipeline projects are planning for hundreds of thousands of samples.

UBS called the print "incrementally positive but [it does] not change our view." It said the guide "seems conservative but faces an unfavorable academic/government end market." UBS flagged uncertainty over whether PacBio has the capacity to meet SPRQ-Nx volume, even though the product should add to sales growth and **incremental margins** (profit on each extra dollar of sales) [UBS, 2026-02-13].

### The March 2026 Barclays sector note and the PacBio downgrade

On 6 March 2026, Barclays published a sector note titled "The Bullet Train to the Singularity is Broken – Grab the Tools Train to Reality Instead." It downgraded PacBio to Underweight from Equal Weight and cut its target to $1.50 from $2.00. "Underweight" means the stock is expected to underperform Barclays' coverage over 12 months. The broader note is covered in its own section below.

PacBio fell 26% between Anthropic's Claude clinical-research plug-in announcement on 3 February and 5 March, versus a 2% drop in the S&P 500. Barclays attributed the fall to "competition beginning to enter on long-read," not to AI.

The reasoning:
- Barclays saw a potential demand pause from competitive launches (Roche's Axelios and Illumina's TruPath).
- It expected an "air pocket" in consumables in the first half as customers wound down work ahead of SPRQ-Nx. SPRQ-Nx alone "could freeze PACB's market near term until customers burn off inventories."
- Lower cost could be a "meaningful shift/inflection." Barclays heard less concern than in prior years about the multi-wash step in research, but it could be a bigger hurdle clinically, where PacBio must win to unlock a larger **TAM** (total addressable market).
- Population-sequencing wins would not reach the P&L until later years.
- Barclays "debated a lot internally," but concluded "an illiquid stock with a 2H-weighted catalyst path is not likely to garner much investor interest" [Barclays, 2026-03-06].

The new target applied 4x EV/revenue to FY2026E revenue of $167M, down from 5x on $178M. EV/revenue is enterprise value (equity value plus net debt) divided by sales. FY2026 EPS moved to -$0.50 from -$0.53. On 14 April 2026, with the stock at $1.45, Barclays cut its target further to $1.00.

### Q1 2026 (reported 7 May 2026)

Revenue was $37.2M, flat year over year and $2.80M (6.7%) below consensus of $39.8M. EPS of -$0.12 beat consensus by $0.01 according to Seeking Alpha. Barclays recorded -$0.13 against its -$0.12 and consensus of -$0.14. The line items:

- **Instruments:** $9.7M, down 12% and 16.2% below the Street's $11.6M.
- **Consumables:** a third straight record at $21.8M, up 9%, but 2.4% below the Street's $22.3M.
- **Service:** $5.6M, down 7%.
- **Placements:** Revio shipments of 15 (up from 12) beat Barclays' 11. Vega shipments of 27 (versus 28) missed Barclays' 36.
- **Gross margin:** the non-GAAP margin fell to 37% from 40%, 400 bps below the Street's 41%. Non-GAAP figures adjust out certain items, though PacBio keeps stock compensation in them.

CFO Jim Gibson cited three reasons for the margin drop: higher memory (DRAM) and computing component costs, a one-time Vega promotion, and one-time inventory and warranty charges [Q1 2026 call; Barclays, 2026-05-08].

Management explained the instrument weakness as follows:
- U.S. academic funding pressure weighed on demand.
- A limited-time Q1 Vega promotion lowered ASPs. A "substantial portion" of the 27 Vegas were sold under it, mostly in price-sensitive APAC. Few U.S. buyers took it, which management read as a sign of funding constraints.
- More than 85% of Vega placements went to new customers.
- Shipments to the Middle East were blocked by the regional conflict. Management did not quantify this, but Barclays put it at $1.5M of hardware, noting "$1.5m moves the needle" on a roughly $40M quarter.
- Half of Revio placements went to new customers, and clinical accounts kept placing multi-system orders.

Clinical shipments grew more than 100% and reached a mid-teens share of consumable shipments, double the year-ago share. Some customers, including the largest Chinese accounts, delayed orders ahead of SPRQ-Nx. Barclays said the launch "froze some of the market on Revio consumables."

By region:
- **EMEA:** $10.8M, up 17%, driven by clinical customers moving from pilots to production.
- **Americas:** $16.7M, up 2%. The largest accounts were commercial service providers and clinical labs.
- **APAC:** $9.7M, down 16%.

Operating figures: opex fell 19% to $49.9M, with stock compensation of $3.8M versus $8.0M a year earlier. The non-GAAP net loss was $35.9M, versus $44.4M. Cash was about $276M, versus $280M at year-end, helped by the Illumina proceeds. Headcount was 492, versus 485.

Guidance changed as follows. The top of the FY2026 revenue range fell $5M, to $165–175M, a 1.4% cut at the midpoint, against consensus of $174M. The guide assumes no meaningful A&G funding recovery, especially in the Americas. Gross margin improvement was now expected toward the low end of the +100 to +400 bps range because compute costs would "temper the pace." Barclays recorded the 41–44% range as maintained, with consensus at 42.2%. Opex guidance moved to $220–225M. Gross margin was expected to improve in Q2, and Vega ASPs were expected to normalize, with no further discounting planned.

Henry said Revio and Vega trends should "somewhat balance out," so the company still expected to be within its February range. On the long run, Henry said "perhaps as much as more than half of our consumable revenue over time will be clinically driven," without giving a date. Most of the consumables guide depended on the existing installed base, with SPRQ-Nx "the one variable."

Henry described three platforms (Vega, Revio and the coming ultra-high-throughput system) serving different customer scales. Mid-throughput customers would stay on Revio, and large clinical accounts would move to the new system. Henry did not address whether Revio orders might slow ahead of its launch.

Clinical programs cited on the call:
- Ambry's ONCE study of 1,000 patients.
- n-Lorem and EspeRare on ultra-rare diseases.
- A University of Washington study of sudden unexplained death in childhood across 200 families.
- A DNAstack collaboration, announced in late February, to federate HiFi genomes through HiFi Solves. **Federated** data stay at their home sites but can be analyzed together. The consortium now has nearly 30 institutions in 15 countries and more than 10,000 genomes connected or committed.
- The Babies in Focus newborn-screening study led by Eurofins Genomics UK, covering at least 2,000 samples, with 1,000 to be sequenced on PacBio between April and September 2026. Henry called it foundational evidence for possible inclusion of long reads in a UK national newborn screening program.
- About 300 million people live with a rare disease, and Henry said the market is in "early innings."

**The Basecamp Research deal** was the quarter's strategic headline. It was first press-released in mid-March 2026. PacBio will deeply sequence about **100,000 metagenomic samples** (mixed environmental DNA, mostly soil bacteria). Henry called it the largest HiFi project in company history and the first scaled use of HiFi to build a biological foundation model.

The samples feed Basecamp's "Trillion Gene Atlas," which aims to grow its dataset from 10 billion to 1 trillion genes and to expand known genetic diversity up to 100-fold by sequencing up to 100-plus million species. The other partners are Anthropic and Ultima Genomics (a short-read rival), on NVIDIA infrastructure. The data train Basecamp's EDEN model, which designs therapeutics such as gene-insertion systems, antimicrobial peptides and cell therapies.

Barclays called it "the biggest deal in the company's history." It estimated $10–20M of revenue over 18 months (100,000 samples at $100–200 each), mainly in 2027. It said it had "clearly underappreciated" the March release and saw possible future deals in which AI companies get discounted sequencing in exchange for shared downstream economics: "the frontier is open." Its caution: "it is still very early... we look for more proof points before diving in both feet." Barclays kept its Underweight rating but raised its target 50%, to $1.50, applying 4x to 2027E revenue of $187M (versus 3x on $190M before), "on the AI foundational biology model opportunities." Outside Basecamp, Barclays called the quarter "unsurprisingly difficult," "a waiting game" [Barclays, 2026-05-08].

### Q2 2026 and the CEO change (reported 5 August 2026)

On 5 August 2026, Christian Henry stepped down as President and CEO after nearly six years. Henry stays on the board and advises the new CEO. Chief Operating Officer **Mark Van Oene**, who joined in 2021 and led the R&D teams that built Revio, Vega and SPRQ-Nx before running commercial operations, became CEO effective immediately.

Van Oene's priorities are to scale the EMEA clinical model globally, drive SPRQ-Nx adoption, and enable more HiFi throughput and data access. His summary line was to "sharpen our execution, and run a leaner, more focused organization." Analysts praised Henry. Canaccord's Kyle Mikson said "There's been the highs and the lows, but it's been overall a really impressive job." Barclays said Henry's tenure "wasn't always smooth sailing, but he always made it fun" [Q2 2026 call; Barclays, 2026-08-05].

A reorganization begun in late July cut about 40 jobs. It folded marketing into the commercial organization and reduced management layers, with "none of our key R&D platform projects" affected. Headcount at quarter-end was 492, versus 491 a year earlier.

Results:
- **Revenue:** $39.0M, down about 2% from $39.8M and $1.57M below consensus.
- **Instruments:** $12.8M, down 9%, on lower ASPs from strategic Revio pricing and fewer Vega units.
- **Consumables:** $20.1M, up 6%, but flat sequentially as customers used up old reagent inventory and validated SPRQ-Nx.
- **Service:** $6.1M, down from $6.7M, as a population sequencing program ended.
- **Pull-through:** about $202k per Revio.
- **Clinical shipments:** up 67%, still a mid-teens share of consumables.
- **Revio:** 20 units versus 15, beating Barclays' 15. 60% went to new customers and 45% came as part of multi-instrument orders. This included a significant order from a new, unnamed population-scale customer starting in Q3.
- **Vega:** 26 units versus 38, below Barclays' 32. 81% went to new customers, and ASPs returned to normal levels. U.S. public health labs bought units, including for wastewater testing.

By region:
- **EMEA:** $14.4M, up 52%, driven by clinical production and a multi-system Revio order for a national genomics initiative.
- **Americas:** $17.6M, down slightly.
- **APAC:** $7.0M, down 45%, after a large population program ended, academic funding weakened and customers burned down inventory.

Non-GAAP gross margin was 36% (Barclays read 35.6%, versus about 38.9% Street), down from 38% a year earlier. The causes were compute inflation, lower volumes and $1.1M of costs to bring Vega manufacturing in-house. Gibson said PacBio had bought enough memory through end-2026. Opex was $56.1M, down 3%. The net loss was $41.9M, or $0.14 per share, versus $0.12 expected by Barclays and $0.13 by consensus. Cash was $236.9M.

**SPRQ-Nx rollout.** More than a third of the installed base adopted the enabling software in June. Management expects more than half to adopt by end-Q3 and the vast majority by year-end. HiFi yield was "near identical across the first two uses with a slight decline on the third." On Vega, SPRQ-Nx raises output to 90 gigabases per run from 60 and lowers DNA input requirements. Two papers supported the clinical case:
- The *New England Journal of Medicine* (13 June 2026, Bitter et al.) found 96.4% concordance between long-read genome sequencing and standard care. Long reads improved diagnoses in 3.4% of cases, while standard care caught misses in only 0.2%.
- *Nature Genetics* (Sabbagh et al.) proposed "near-perfect genome sequencing."

A HiFi Solves subfertility study also appeared; subfertility affects about one in six couples. For Basecamp, hundreds of samples were being sequenced each week, with most to come in 2027.

**Guidance was cut again.** The new FY2026 figures:
- Revenue $155–165M.
- Gross margin 35–37%, including about $2.5M of Vega transition costs.
- Opex $215–220M.
- Year-end cash about $175–185M.
- Pull-through $200–225k.

The reasons given were slower SPRQ-Nx adoption, compute costs, strategic Revio pricing, no A&G recovery and a slower China rebound. **Cash-flow breakeven moved to 2028.** For 2027, management expects $15–20M of compensation savings and another $15–20M as HT platform development spending ends. Gibson said existing cash can fund the business through breakeven and the HT launch. Van Oene called the outlook "a really balanced view."

Barclays kept its Underweight rating and cut its target 17%, to $1.25, at 4x 2026E revenue of $159M (from $167M). The stock was at $1.30. Barclays argued the cut reflects an SPRQ-Nx "air pocket" bottoming in Q3–Q4 2026 rather than weak demand: "not as bad as it looks." It saw the hardest task as converting U.S. and smaller clinical labs to production. The U.S. market is "very different and more fragmented," and labs are held back by data-analysis limits, the need for automated sample preparation and a "legacy bias toward short-read." That makes the work "more hand-to-hand." Its view: the stock is "likely to lag peers given all of the moving pieces," with an "extended demand pause on capex" possible [Barclays, 2026-08-05].

### Morgan Stanley conference (15 September 2026)

Van Oene told Morgan Stanley's Michael Podoll that "this is not me making a wholesale change on the strategy. It really is... the execution." The priorities for the next 12–18 months:

1. **Land the restructuring.** Van Oene expects about **$40M of 2027 savings** from headcount and non-recurring expenses. "We've had to go through 3 restructurings in 3 years, and we've exited the short-read business."
2. **Complete the SPRQ-Nx transition.** The goal is to exit 2026 with consumable and margin growth. Pull-through is "closer to $200,000" and should stay there through Q3 in "a 2-quarter transition" before recovering by year-end.
3. **Launch the HT platform in 2027.** It was untouched by the cuts and is the largest R&D cost. Van Oene described it as end-to-end, including data software ("precision health intelligence") as well as the sequencer. Other R&D includes Kinnex updates and library-preparation automation. All Vega manufacturing is being in-sourced to Menlo Park.

The multi-use SMRT Cell was Van Oene's "most underappreciated" point. The chip is "the majority of the cost of our sequencing," and spreading it over two or three uses, and eventually four or five, changes both price and margin. "For the first time, I'm staring at a product line that will have the gross margin that will actually make us a sustainable company."

The three indicators Van Oene said to watch:
- Gross margin back above 40% through 2027.
- Clinical consumables growth of "50-plus percent" over the next couple of years.
- A successful HT launch that sets up 2028 breakeven. "Not many labs have ever run 10,000 genomes a year."

Regional strategy: Europe has moved HiFi to a frontline test for rare disease, meaning the first test ordered rather than a follow-up (reflex) test. Volumes there are in the "thousands of samples." The U.S. will start with carrier screening, such as Quest's ataxia panels, because Revio can handle "hundreds of thousands" of targeted samples but not yet that many whole genomes. PacBio will partner for interpretation and clinical reporting. It is seeing biopharma interest in HiFi for target discovery. Its software includes pipelines for **phasing** (assigning variants to each parent's copy), structural variants, copy number, **paralogs** (near-duplicate gene copies) and **methylation** (chemical tags on DNA), plus HiFi Solves' sovereign, federated data network.

On funding, Van Oene said "I haven't seen a budget flush," the usual year-end spending burst. Van Oene expects no NIH improvement and said U.S. revenue is flat because clinical growth only offsets academic declines. Basecamp-style AI deals are "not... in our base case model" for margins or breakeven [Morgan Stanley conf., 2026-09-15].

### The Barclays life-science-tools sector note (6 March 2026)

This note argued that the early-2026 "Claude crash" in Tools and contract research organization (CRO) stocks was largely overblown. The sell-off followed Anthropic's Claude life-science plug-in and its clinical-research plug-in on 3 February. **Tools** companies sell instruments, consumables and software to labs. **CROs** run clinical trials for drug makers.

Barclays' main argument is that AI will create more wet-lab work (physical experiments), not less. Biology "is incomplete at best and wrong at worst. This is why we do experiments." Faster AI-driven design frees scientists for lab time and drives more experiment iterations that feed back into models. Twist's AI-driven drug discovery boom is the evidence. AI should also raise pharma's return on investment, so pharma will not cut R&D.

For CROs, Barclays gave four reasons the fears are overblown:
- Regulation moves slowly: "the FDA only started allowing PDFs or digital submission of data in 2016/2017."
- Outsourcing will persist.

- Large CROs will build better AI tools than "a model vibe-coded by a pharma company."

The note says Barclays has the most conviction in 10x Genomics, Twist, Charles River and Danaher. 10x and Twist are "clear winners." ## Key tensions to keep in mind

The sources broadly agree on several points:
- Clinical demand (especially in Europe) and consumables are real bright spots.
- U.S. academic funding is not recovering.
- The SPRQ-Nx transition caused a temporary consumables dip.

They differ in tone and in their forecasts. Management calls SPRQ-Nx a margin breakthrough. Barclays sees an illiquid, cash-burning stock with a second-half-weighted catalyst path and, in its own models, a funding gap by 2027–2028. Management, by contrast, says current cash funds the company to breakeven.

Timing has repeatedly slipped:
- SPRQ-Nx moved from "early 2026" early access to a late-May launch.
- Breakeven moved from 2027 to 2028.
- Gross margin went from a 41–44% guide to 35–37%.

Van Oene's own comments are slightly inconsistent: a "2-quarter transition" versus a need to succeed "through 2027" to get margin above 40%. Basecamp and AI deals are upside that management deliberately excludes from its base case.
