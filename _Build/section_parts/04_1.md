2023 was the year Illumina launched its most important new sequencer in a decade, the NovaSeq X, and the year its financial story unraveled. In April the company beat its own forecasts, held its full-year outlook and promised rising margins. By August a new interim CEO was cutting guidance and calling the rollout "more challenging" than expected. By November a permanent CEO was cutting guidance again and warning that 2024 would look like 2023. In February 2024 management guided 2024 to flat revenue. Through all four calls, management kept framing the weakness mainly as a temporary platform transition plus a tough economy, while China was the one market where it openly admitted competition was hurting. The calls also tracked the long struggle over GRAIL, the cancer-screening company that European regulators ordered Illumina to divest.

Some conventions apply throughout. Unless stated otherwise, figures are **non-GAAP** (adjusted to exclude certain accounting items; Illumina's version still includes stock-based compensation). **"Core Illumina"** means Illumina excluding GRAIL. **Consolidated** figures include GRAIL. On every call, Investor Relations head Salli Schwartz reminded listeners that GRAIL must be "held and operated separately and independently from Illumina" under interim measures ordered by the European Commission (EC), which prohibited the acquisition under the EU Merger Regulation.

## Q1 2023 (call held April 25, 2023)

### Setting

The speakers were Francis deSouza (President and CEO), Joydeep Goswami (CFO and Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer) and Schwartz. The call fell on DNA Day, the 70th anniversary of the discovery of the double helix, and in the month of Illumina's 25th anniversary. deSouza noted that only about 4 million genomes have ever been sequenced, "representing only 0.07% of the population," so "we are still at the beginning of the genome era" [Q1 2023 call].

### Results against expectations

Consolidated revenue was about $1.09 billion. That was down 11% from Q1 2022, or 9% in **constant currency** (stripping out exchange-rate swings, net of hedging), but up 1% from Q4 2022. It beat the top of management's guidance range. It also beat analyst consensus by about $20.7 million, and non-GAAP EPS of $0.08 beat consensus by $0.05 [Seeking Alpha header; Q1 2023 call]. deSouza said both figures beat the guidance given at the start of the year but fell year over year "as expected." The beat came from shipping 67 NovaSeq X instruments, against an expectation of 40 to 50.

Management gave four reasons for the year-over-year decline:
- the expected fade of **COVID surveillance** (sequencing used to track virus variants);
- COVID-related disruption in China;
- some high-throughput customers moving to the NovaSeq X;
- customers coping with tight capital markets.

COVID surveillance revenue fell to about $11 million from $60 million a year earlier, a $49 million headwind.

Non-GAAP net income was $13 million, or $0.08 per diluted share, on about 158 million shares. That figure absorbed GRAIL's non-GAAP operating loss of $164 million. The non-GAAP tax rate was 27.3%, versus 17.8% in Q1 2022. Goswami explained that both quarters reflect **R&D capitalization** (a US tax rule that forces R&D costs to be deducted over several years rather than immediately). The dollar impact was the same in both quarters, but it weighed more on the rate because earnings were lower.

### Core Illumina by product line

Core Illumina revenue was $1.08 billion, down 12% year over year (10% in constant currency). That included about 400 basis points of COVID drag, where a **basis point** is a hundredth of a percentage point. The table shows the three product lines, each compared with Q1 2022.

| Line | Q1 2023 | Change and drivers |
|---|---|---|
| Sequencing consumables | $692M | −12% y/y, +1% sequentially; about 500 bp COVID headwind |
| Sequencing instruments | $154M | −27% y/y; 300 bp COVID headwind |
| Sequencing service and other | $119M | +7% y/y |

**Consumables** are the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every sequencing run. Beyond COVID, they fell because of lower **pull-through** (consumables spending per installed instrument) on the NovaSeq 6000, disruption in China, the move to the X, and tight capital markets.

**Instruments** fell mainly because NovaSeq 6000 shipments compared against a record Q1 2022, and because fewer mid-throughput machines shipped, especially NextSeq 550 units in China. NovaSeq X shipments partly offset this, though Illumina said it "remain[s] supply constrained in the first year of launch." Demand for the NextSeq 1000/2000 (mid-throughput benchtop sequencers) from new-to-Illumina customers stayed strong.

**Service and other** grew mainly on service contracts for a larger installed base.

Core **gross margin** (revenue minus production cost, as a share of revenue) was 65.2%, down 500 bp year over year. Goswami blamed two things. First, lower manufacturing volumes meant less fixed-cost leverage. Second, instrument margins were lower during the X launch, "which is typical with a new platform introduction." He expected gross margin to improve every quarter as X production scaled. Core operating expenses were $514 million, up $9 million, mainly from the full-year effect of 2022 hiring, but lower than planned thanks to cost containment.

Cash flow from operations was $10 million and capital expenditures were $52 million, so **free cash flow** (operating cash minus capital spending) was −$42 million. Cash and short-term investments were about $1.5 billion. Illumina repurchased no shares and used $500 million to repay 2023 term notes that matured in March.

### Sequencing activity

Goswami cited usage on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments, which he said is "directionally correlated with revenue over time." These figures were based on run counts:
- Total activity rose 6% from Q4 and 3% year over year.
- Research and applied activity rose 7% sequentially but fell 4% year over year.
- Clinical activity was "robust," up 7% sequentially and 15% year over year.

### Regions

Illumina introduced a new commercial structure that folds Asia-Pacific together with emerging markets in the Middle East, Africa, Turkey and the CIS. It now reports four regions: Americas, Europe, Greater China and AMEA (Asia Pacific, Middle East and Africa). The table shows Q1 results for each region.

| Region | Q1 2023 | Change | Drivers [Goswami] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Americas | $605M | +5% q/q; −6% y/y | Beat expectations on X shipments. Research was weaker (COVID fade, delayed recruitment on large projects). NextSeq 1k/2k shipments rose more than 20% |
| Europe | $261M | −9% (−4% cc) | Clinical growth led by oncology was more than offset by the COVID fade and fewer high-throughput instruments because of X supply limits |
| Greater China | $91M | −28% (−23% cc) | COVID disruption and customer liquidity and funding problems; lower consumables and mid-throughput |
| AMEA | $119M | −27% (−23% cc) | A large Japanese research project ended; COVID fade; fewer NovaSeq 6000s; Russia sanctions |

Goswami said Russia sanctions will persist through 2023. The outlook absorbs about $60 million of lost AMEA consumables revenue for the year.

### GRAIL

GRAIL revenue was $20 million, double the prior year and ahead of plan, on growing adoption of **Galleri** (its blood test that screens for many cancers at once). Revenue fell from Q4 because Q4 had included a milestone payment from **MRD** partnerships with drug companies (minimal residual disease testing, which detects traces of cancer left after treatment). Operating expenses were $173 million, up $34 million, as GRAIL funded clinical trials and built its sales organization.

GRAIL has received more than 85,000 Galleri orders since launch, about 20,000 of them in Q1. The 140,000-person NHS-Galleri trial finished enrollment in July 2022 in just over ten months. More than half of participants have returned for their second annual blood draw, and the trial is past its halfway point and on track for retention targets. John Hancock (a Manulife unit) will expand Galleri access to eligible life-insurance customers after a pilot. Providence will offer the test across 52 hospitals and 900 clinics in seven states.

### Guidance: 2023 held, Q2 set, a back-half ramp promised

Goswami held the full-year 2023 outlook:
- consolidated revenue growth of 7% to 10%, including Core Illumina growth of 6% to 9%, with about 200 bp of COVID headwind plus currency headwinds;
- GRAIL revenue of $90 million to $110 million;
- non-GAAP EPS of $1.25 to $1.50;
- consolidated operating margin of about 8% and Core operating margin of about 22%;
- Core sequencing revenue growth of about 8% at the midpoint.

All other guidance from February 7, 2023 was reaffirmed.

Three offsetting changes sat inside the unchanged range: lost Russia revenue, NovaSeq X shipments raised to more than 330 (from 300), and higher drug-discovery partnership revenue. At the midpoint, instrument growth rose to about 13%, consumables growth fell to about 5.5% (mainly because of Russia), and service revenue rose on pharma partnerships. Together these kept sequencing growth near 8%.

Goswami expected revenue to climb through the year, similar to 2017 when the NovaSeq 6000 launched. That year about 54% of revenue fell in the second half, about 26% in Q3 and the rest in Q4. The ramp assumed three things: rising quarterly X shipments, consumables growth as X customers start projects in the second half, and easing macro headwinds, including a China recovery.

For Q2, management guided consolidated revenue of $1.15 billion to $1.17 billion, about $70 million (670 bp) above Q1 at the midpoint. The drivers were more X shipments, more consumables and service, and higher GRAIL revenue, partly offset by a seasonal dip in microarrays. Midpoint EPS was guided at about $0.01, with consolidated operating margin about 1% and Core operating margin about 17%. Margins were held down by the March equity grants and merit raises, but were expected to improve in the second half.

### Cost cuts and 2024–25 margin targets

Earlier that day, Illumina committed to Core operating margins of 25% in 2024 and 27% in 2025, while still funding its key innovation roadmap. It announced more than $100 million of annualized cost cuts starting later in 2023, building on actions from November 2022. Goswami listed four sources of savings:
- reusing modular R&D building blocks, such as **XLEAP-SBS chemistry** (the faster, cheaper sequencing chemistry developed for the X) and the new flow cell, to make future platforms cheaper and faster to develop;
- moving work to lower-cost hubs;
- streamlining real estate, vendor spend and IT;
- focusing on highly differentiated products.

### Strategy and commercial highlights [deSouza]

deSouza's priorities for the rest of 2023 were to scale X production and distribution, drive **demand elasticity** (customers sequencing more as cost per sample falls), and improve margins. Q1 milestones included the first X shipments, the launch of Illumina Complete Long Read technology, and the launch of Illumina Connected Insights. Connected Insights is cloud software for interpreting results, aimed at helping more labs perform comprehensive tumor profiling.

**High-throughput.** Illumina exited Q1 with more than 200 X orders from more than 30 countries. About 40% came from clinical customers, more than expected, and about 15% from customers new to high-throughput. Early runs exceeded 7 terabases of output, with more than 95% of bases above Q30 (a standard accuracy threshold), "well ahead of published specifications." deSouza gave several examples of elasticity:
- a European institute planning "atlas-scale" multiomics, spatial and single-cell projects;
- clinical labs moving from targeted panels to exomes and whole genomes;
- an oncology lab adding **liquid biopsy** (cancer testing from blood), which needs 12 to 15 times more sequencing than solid-tumor testing;
- labs entering MRD testing.

Seventeen NovaSeq 6000s also shipped, mostly to expand existing fleets. More than 80% went to clinical customers and about 25% to oncology.

**Mid- and low-throughput.** NextSeq 1k/2k shipments rose 6% as customers expanded fleets or moved up from the MiSeq and NextSeq 550. More than 20% went to new-to-Illumina customers. Win rates were strong, but "we are seeing some sales cycles lengthen." About 30% of low-throughput shipments went to new customers.

**Clinical.** For the first time, clinical customers made up 50% of sequencing consumables revenue.
- **Oncology** consumables fell 3% year over year but rose 21% sequentially. deSouza warned that profitability pressure at some customers could slow test development.
- **TruSight Oncology 500 (TSO 500)**, Illumina's broad tumor-profiling kit, earned more than $25 million, up 26%, across more than 540 accounts.
- An expanded Myriad Genetics partnership broadens **HRD** testing (homologous recombination deficiency, a DNA-repair defect seen in ovarian, breast, prostate and pancreatic cancers). TSO 500 HRD (research use only) is now available in the US, with Florida Cancer Specialists as an early customer. The partners will also develop HRD as a **companion diagnostic** (a test that selects patients for a specific drug).
- In European reimbursement, seven Italian regions committed €10 million for lung-cancer **NGS** (next-generation sequencing). German insurers began paying for whole-genome sequencing in relapsed pediatric cancer. The Czech Republic added comprehensive genomic profiling to its fee schedule from January 1.
- **Reproductive health** consumables fell 4% (mainly because of currency) but rose 14% sequentially. Virginia and Michigan Medicaid began covering **NIPT** (noninvasive prenatal testing from the mother's blood) for all pregnancies, adding about 4.3 million covered lives and more than 70,000 pregnancies a year.
- **Genetic disease testing** had a record quarter, up 7% year over year and 6% sequentially. A large US insurer began covering whole-genome sequencing for rare disease in Managed Medicaid across 16 states (about 3.8 million lives). Illumina also partnered with Henry Ford Health on whole-genome sequencing in cardiovascular care for underserved populations.

**Research and applied** consumables fell 19%, mainly because of the COVID fade.

Coming launches included the Complete Long Read enrichment assay in Q3 2023 and XLEAP-SBS on the NextSeq 1k/2k in the first half of 2024. deSouza said Illumina was "on track to deliver on our financial commitments for 2023."

### Analyst Q&A

**Puneet Souda (SVB Securities)** asked whether 330 X units could rise, about distractions from the EC, the FTC and an activist campaign, and about biotech funding. deSouza said 330 is "more manufacturing bound than demand bound." Clinical demand was strong this time, unlike past launches where research led. Q2 was planned at 80 X units, stepping up toward 330. "The story of this year is the X," and "the vast, vast, vast majority" of management attention goes to products. Exposure to small biotechs is very small. Management did not directly address the activist campaign.

**Dan Brennan (TD Cowen)** asked about replacement ratios, whether customers can fill X capacity, and GRAIL timing. deSouza modeled demand "customer out." Of about 1,000 high-throughput customers, roughly 750 own a single NovaSeq 6000 and cannot buy less than one X, which implies about 750 units "right off the bat" before any elasticity. The other 250 will apply their own ratios. deSouza did not treat filling capacity as a constraint, because buyers want elasticity. On GRAIL, matters "come to a head" in late 2023 or early 2024. The EU jurisdiction appeal and the US FTC appeal were both pending, and the Fifth Circuit had just granted expedited review. Illumina expected the EC's divestiture order "shortly." Goswami added that Illumina earns money from clinical trials of new tests before those tests launch.

**Dan Arias (Stifel)** asked whether elasticity would arrive in the second half. Goswami said yes: installs happen in Q2–Q3, and projects ramp in earnest in Q4.

**Vijay Kumar (Evercore ISI)** asked about Russia, the sequential gross-margin dip and the margin ramp. Goswami put Russia at about $60 million for the year and $18 million in Q2. Gross margin dipped because the X carries low early margins (and more units shipped than planned) and because Q1 factory absorption is seasonally weak. Q2 margin would be held at 17% by the one-time raise cycle and by instrument-heavy mix after the Russia consumables loss. He expected to exit 2023 with gross margin in the high 60s and operating margin "close to the high 20% numbers."

**Tejas Savant (Morgan Stanley)** asked why X units beat but instrument revenue did not, and about NextSeq pricing against competitors near $2 per gigabase. Goswami said the offset was mainly fewer NextSeq 550s in China: "We're not seeing any differential with the expected prices nor a decrease in the prices that we track." deSouza said XLEAP will lower NextSeq prices to "maximize the market opportunity," while charging for unique features: onboard computing on FPGAs (programmable chips), storage optimization, and ambient shipping with no cold chain. On GRAIL, Illumina would "divest promptly" if it lost either appeal, and would keep GRAIL only if it won both and the business case still held.

**Julia Qin (J.P. Morgan)** asked about margin risk, manufacturing efficiency, R&D savings and China. Goswami held the 22% margin guidance (the transcript says "2022," evidently a slip for 2023). Initial X manufacturing efficiencies would arrive by end-2023, with more in years two and three. R&D savings would show "in '24 and beyond." On China: "we are not seeing the benefits of any stimulus in China, at least for our business."

**David Westenberg (Piper Sandler)** asked about NovaSeq 6000 decommissioning. Goswami said most customers, especially clinical ones, will keep their 6000s. Only 10 to 12 decommissions were expected among early X customers.

**Kyle Mikson (Canaccord)** asked whether Illumina is stuck in boom-bust product cycles. He cited strong growth after HiSeq X (2014–15) and NovaSeq 6000 (2017–18), followed by single-digit growth in 2016 and a setback in 2019. deSouza said that with half of consumables now clinical, growth comes increasingly from clinical adoption itself, not just upgrades. Goswami added that transition dips are larger for research customers than clinical ones.

## Q2 2023 (call held August 9, 2023)

### A changed company

The second call came after a leadership shake-up. Charles "Chuck" Dadswell, General Counsel, had been interim CEO for about two months after Francis deSouza left. Chairman John Thompson also left. Stephen MacMillan became Chairman, and Andrew Teno and Scott Ullem joined the Board. CTO Alex Aravanis left to become CEO of another company. Steve Barnard, "employee number four" and Illumina's first scientist hire, replaced him. Chief Medical Officer Phil Febbo also departed, and Illumina will review its medical organization before searching for a successor. Dadswell said the Board was "pleased with the outstanding candidates" for CEO and was considering internal and external candidates [Q2 2023 call].

### Results: a beat, then a cut

Consolidated revenue was about $1.18 billion. That was up 8% from Q1 and 1% year over year (3% in constant currency), above the top of guidance because of X shipments. It beat consensus by about $11 million. Non-GAAP EPS was $0.32 against guidance of about $0.01, beating consensus by $0.30. Net income was $50 million, including GRAIL's $164 million operating loss, and was helped by cost cuts, better gross margin and higher revenue. The tax rate was 39.3%, versus 25.8% a year earlier, again inflated by R&D capitalization against lower earnings. Diluted shares were about 158 million.

### The NovaSeq X rollout

Dadswell said the rollout "has been a more challenging process than we anticipated." He called the X "the most sophisticated platform we have ever launched," with Illumina's most comprehensive software, rolled out at "unprecedented magnitude and pace." Field issues typical of new launches had appeared. Illumina released a planned software update in June (first shipments had been in March) and sent technical teams worldwide. Onboarding would take longer than planned.

Demand still looked healthy. Orders exceeded 260 since launch. Q2 shipments of 109 beat the planned 80, bringing the installed base to 176. Twenty percent of buyers had ordered more than one X. Customers were seeking funding for larger **single-cell** and **spatial** experiments (analyzing cells one at a time, or mapping where molecules sit in tissue). Population genomics programs were planning **multiomic** work (combining several molecular data types). Large customers were moving from targeted panels and **exomes** (the protein-coding 1–2% of the genome) to whole genomes.

### Other strategic and commercial messages

Clinical reached about 53% of sequencing consumables.
- **Oncology coverage:** Anthem (the second-largest US commercial payer) and Blue Cross Blue Shield of Michigan began covering comprehensive genomic profiling for advanced cancer, adding more than 30 million lives. Switzerland began reimbursing large NGS panels.
- **TSO 500** was on track to exceed $100 million of 2023 revenue. Illumina completed its TSO Comprehensive submission for US **IVD** registration (in vitro diagnostic, an FDA-regulated test).
- **GRAIL:** Galleri passed its 100,000th commercial test, with more than 7,500 prescribers and more than 80 health systems. NHS-Galleri completed second-year visits, with 130,000 participants returning (91.3% retention), and began third-year invitations with appointments due that fall. At ASCO, the Oxford-sponsored SYMPLIFY study showed high specificity, positive predictive value and accuracy for detecting cancer and predicting its origin, and showed that such a test could support primary-care referral decisions. At AACR, GRAIL and AstraZeneca reported a 92% detection rate for MRD in relapsed or refractory patients across six blood cancers.
- **NIPT:** five state Medicaid programs (Louisiana, Michigan, North Carolina, Rhode Island and Tennessee) now cover all pregnancies. The Netherlands offers NIPT for all pregnancies, and Italy approved broader coverage.
- **Research:** the Alliance for Genomic Discovery (with Nashville Biosciences) now has five founding members: AbbVie, Amgen, AstraZeneca, Bayer and Merck. They will co-fund whole-genome sequencing of 250,000 samples. Phase one, announced in January, covers 35,000 samples, mostly from people of African ancestry.
- **Innovation:** PrimateAI-3D (an AI that predicts disease-causing mutations) was Science's June cover story, and DRAGEN 4.2 analysis software launched in July. Coming launches included the 25B flow cell for the X, the Complete Long Reads enrichment assay, XLEAP-SBS on NextSeq 1k/2k, and work in proteomics, multiomics and spatial.
- **Costs:** the $100 million-plus program was accelerated. A late-June 8-K disclosed headcount and real-estate cuts, alongside vendor and travel savings.

Dadswell closed: "While the year hasn't progressed the way we expected, we remain focused on execution, innovation, and supporting our customers."

## Q3 2023 (call held November 9, 2023)

### New CEO, "a disappointing result"

Jacob Thaysen had been CEO for a little over six weeks (about 40 days). Thaysen previously spent more than a decade at Agilent, running its Diagnostics and Genomics Group and then its largest unit, Life Science and Applied Markets. Thaysen said: "Illumina will remain the key player, even as others enter the market." Thaysen pointed to two and a half decades of infrastructure, offerings that "set the global standard," and innovation. Management called the quarter "a disappointing result" [Q3 2023 call].

Consolidated revenue was about $1.12 billion, flat year over year (+1% constant currency) and down 5% from Q2. It missed consensus by about $14 million. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.33 beat consensus by $0.19 and was roughly flat year over year. Net income was $52 million, including GRAIL's $155 million loss. EPS beat internal plans because of cost cuts and better-than-forecast gross margin. On a **GAAP** basis (standard accounting), Illumina lost $754 million, or $4.77 per share. The tax rate was 39.7%, versus 43.2% a year earlier, lower mainly because of increased amortization of capitalized R&D. Diluted shares were about 158 million.

## Q4 2023 (call held February 8, 2024)

### Results: modest beat

Thaysen had been CEO "a little over four months." The consolidated non-GAAP operating loss was $152 million, and diluted shares were about 159 million. The tax rate was 55.4%, versus 29.3% a year earlier. The full-year rate was 41.8%, versus 26% in 2022, reflecting R&D capitalization and GRAIL losses. Excluding GRAIL, the 2023 rate was in the mid-20s [Goswami, Q4 2023 call].

Core revenue was $1.1 billion, up 3%. It absorbed about 5 points of drag: about 3 from COVID and Russia, and about 2 from China. COVID revenue was about $4 million versus $20 million a year earlier. ### How the story evolved

In April, management described supply as the only limit on the X and promised Core margins of 25% in 2024. By August, the same launch was a cause of weakness: X buyers stopped buying 6000 reagents before ramping the new machine, and onboarding took longer than planned. By November, X shipment guidance fell from more than 390 back to 330–340, and the special committee took over GRAIL. Illumina actually shipped 352, but 2024 was guided flat with about 20% margins, five points below the April target. Throughout, management treated China as the one structural competitive problem and described the rest of the slowdown as timing and macro conditions. That claim rests mainly on management's own control-group data, which analysts such as Michael Ryskin openly questioned.
