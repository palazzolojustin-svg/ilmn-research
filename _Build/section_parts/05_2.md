## Q1 2026 (call held April 30, 2026)

### The headline: a beat and a raise, carried by instruments and clinical demand

Illumina opened 2026 with a quarter that beat both its own guidance and Wall Street's expectations. Management then raised its full-year revenue and earnings outlook after only one quarter. CEO Jacob Thaysen, who was also serving as interim Chief Commercial Officer, called it a "great start," driven by disciplined execution, clinical strength and growth in every region except China [Q1 2026 call]. Thaysen also thanked staff for managing a "higher cost environment," a phrase that foreshadowed the cost debate that ran through both 2026 calls.

The two forces behind the beat were instrument sales and clinical demand. Illumina placed more than 80 NovaSeq X sequencers, its current high-throughput machine, against a planning range of 50 to 60 per quarter. Clinical sequencing consumables grew about 20% outside China. Clinical consumables are the reagent kits that diagnostic labs must buy for every sequencing run. The weak spot was research and academic demand, which kept falling amid uncertainty over US government research funding.

According to the Seeking Alpha summary, revenue of $1.09 billion beat consensus (the average analyst forecast) by $17.61 million, and non-GAAP EPS of $1.15 beat consensus by $0.10 [Seeking Alpha header, Q1 2026 call]. Non-GAAP EPS is earnings per share excluding certain one-time or non-cash items. CFO Ankur Dhingra said revenue landed $20 million above the midpoint of Illumina's own guidance, mainly because instrument sales were better than expected, and that clinical consumables came in at the high end of expectations [Dhingra, Q1 2026 call]. Thaysen said revenue, margin and EPS all exceeded the guidance range.

### How Illumina reports growth

Vice President of Investor Relations Conor McNamara set out the reporting conventions, which apply to everything that follows [Q1 2026 call]:

- Growth rates are year over year and on a reported basis unless stated otherwise.
- "Organic" growth strips out currency movements and acquisitions, so it measures underlying business growth.
- "Rest of World (RoW) organic" growth also excludes Greater China. Illumina's China business has been impaired since the Chinese government placed the company on its "unreliable entity list," a list that restricts doing business in China [Q2 2026 call]. RoW organic growth is therefore management's preferred measure of the business it can control.

In January 2026 Illumina also changed its geographic reporting segments to match its commercial organization. Restated historical results are in a supplementary file on its website [McNamara, Q1 2026 call].

### Segment detail from the CFO

**Clinical versus research.** Clinical consumables grew 20% ex-China for the second straight quarter. Dhingra credited rising clinical test volumes and the adoption of "information-rich" tests in new clinical trials. He said products such as 5-base (an assay that reads an extra layer of DNA information, such as chemical methylation marks, in the same run) and TruPath widen access to richer data. Research and applied consumables, which serve academic, government and industrial labs, fell 12% ex-China because of funding uncertainty. Dhingra described macro commentary as "stable to improving," but said year-to-date trends matched what Illumina had assumed for 2026 [Q1 2026 call].

**Instruments and supply.** Illumina was supply-constrained in Q1 because NovaSeq X demand was so strong. Several multi-unit clinical orders came in, and high-throughput research placements also grew year over year [Dhingra, Q1 2026 call].

**Consumables drivers.** Most consumables growth came from high-throughput volume, as the NovaSeq X installed base (the number of machines in the field) expands and existing machines are used more heavily [Dhingra, Q1 2026 call].

**The NovaSeq X transition.** Illumina has been moving customers from the older NovaSeq 6000 to the NovaSeq X. The X delivers sequencing at a much lower price per unit of data, so each conversion lowers revenue per base before volume growth makes up the difference. The table below shows how far the transition had progressed by Q1 2026. The key point is that volume had moved much faster than revenue, and the remaining shift was concentrated in clinical.

| NovaSeq X transition measure (as of Q1 2026) | Share on X |
|---|---|
| All high-throughput volume | About 82% |
| High-throughput revenue | About 55% |
| Research and applied volume | 90% |
| Clinical volume | 76%, expected to exceed 80% to 85% by end of 2026 |

[Dhingra, Q1 2026 call]

**Sequencing activity.** Total gigabase output on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30% year over year. Gigabase output is the amount of DNA sequence data produced, measured in billions of DNA letters. Clinical output grew "well above" that rate [Dhingra, Q1 2026 call]. Output growing much faster than consumables revenue shows the X-transition price effect at work.

**Service and other.** Revenue from BioInsight partnerships and data deals can be lumpy from quarter to quarter [Dhingra].

**Microarrays.** Microarrays are an older chip-based technology that reads known DNA variants rather than sequencing. Revenue fell 20% RoW organic, largely because of specific large direct-to-consumer genetic testing customers [Dhingra].

### Cash, balance sheet and capital allocation

The quarter generated $289 million of operating cash flow. Capital expenditures (spending on equipment and facilities) were $38 million, leaving $251 million of free cash flow, the cash left after capital spending [Dhingra, Q1 2026 call].

Illumina repurchased 2 million shares for about $242 million in Q1, at an average price of $120.85. About $400 million remained on the existing authorization at quarter end. On the day of the call, the Board authorized an additional $1.5 billion of buybacks, and management said it would keep buying opportunistically [Dhingra].

The SomaLogic acquisition closed on January 30, 2026, for a net cash payment of $363 million plus potential royalties and milestones. SomaLogic is a proteomics business, meaning it measures proteins rather than DNA. After quarter end, Illumina paid a first $25 million milestone because SomaLogic had hit certain 2025 targets. Quarter-end cash, equivalents and short-term investments were about $1.16 billion. Gross leverage was about 1.5x, measured as gross debt divided by trailing 12-month EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a rough proxy for operating cash profit) [Dhingra].

### Strategic messages from the CEO

**Clinical is now the core of the business.** Thaysen said clinical customers account for more than 65% of sequencing consumables revenue. He named four drivers:

- broader use of sequencing-based diagnostics;
- more data-intensive tests, such as comprehensive genomic profiling (testing hundreds of cancer genes at once) and whole genome sequencing;
- new assays launching; and
- progress on reimbursement, meaning insurers agreeing to pay for tests.

Thaysen said clinical has a "long runway" [Q1 2026 call].

**Research is cautious but strategically important.** Research and academic demand remains cautious because of funding uncertainty. Thaysen nonetheless said Illumina keeps investing in proteomics and single-cell analysis (studying cells one at a time), with spatial products coming. The rationale is that research markets are where new technologies get adopted first, before they move into clinical use. Management expects a return to growth as funding uncertainty eases [Q1 2026 call].

**Innovation announced at AGBT.** AGBT is a major annual genomics technology conference. Thaysen highlighted the following products and programs presented there:

- **TruPath** is a whole genome sequencing approach that gives deeper insight without traditional library preparation (the multi-step process of turning a DNA sample into something a sequencer can read). It cuts hands-on time to about 10 minutes. Rare disease customers are using it to consolidate several tests into one workflow. Several customers are running clinical studies, and Thaysen expects demand to rise in coming quarters.
- **Spatial transcriptomics** maps where genes are active within a tissue sample. Engagement has been very high, and early-access users have generated data from difficult samples such as lymphatic tissue. The product was on track to launch later in 2026.
- **The NovaSeq X 18-month roadmap** includes new 14B and 35B flow cells, staggered flow cell runs, and "Q70" data quality. A flow cell is the consumable chip a sequencing run happens on, and its size is quoted in billions of reads. Thaysen said these features add flexibility, throughput and efficiency, and are still driving placements three years after the first X shipped. Illumina exited the quarter with a solid backlog of orders, which underpinned the raised instrument outlook.
- **BioInsight** is the data and AI business Illumina introduced in late 2025. Its flagship, the Billion Cell Atlas, was introduced earlier in 2026. It uses Perturb-seq, a method that switches individual genes off in cells and reads the effects by sequencing, to study how genes drive disease. Hundreds of millions of cells had already been generated, more companies wanted to join, and Thaysen said the data could feed AI models for drug discovery.

**Other updates.** SomaLogic was performing in line with expectations on both revenue and profit. Thaysen thanked three outgoing Board members and noted that he joined Illumina in 2023 [Q1 2026 call].

### Guidance: Q2 2026

Management guided Q2 to:

- RoW organic revenue growth of 4% to 6%;
- reported revenue of $1.12 billion to $1.14 billion;
- non-GAAP EPS of $1.20 to $1.25; and
- an operating margin of about 22%.

Dhingra gave three reasons for the lower Q2 margin:

1. a higher mix of instruments, which carry lower margins than consumables, along with related investments;
2. near-term inflation in freight and electronic component costs; and
3. a full quarter of SomaLogic costs.

Mitigating actions began in Q2 and are meant to fully offset the inflation during the year. Their effect should show in Q3 and Q4, so margins are expected to improve in the second half. Illumina is also investing to scale NovaSeq X supply, with progress expected through Q2 and into Q3. Dhingra expected Q2 NovaSeq X placements to be close to Q1 levels [Q1 2026 call].

### Long-range targets

Both executives said Illumina remained on track for the 2027 targets first set in 2024 [Thaysen, Dhingra, Q1 2026 call]:

- high single-digit revenue growth;
- continued margin expansion, with a 500 bps target by 2027; and
- double-digit to teens EPS growth "for years to come."

Thaysen described the growth path as flattish in 2025, "mid-single-ish" in 2026 and high single digit in 2027.

### Analyst Q&A

**Why not raise organic growth? (Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI).** Kumar asked why, given the beat, strong instruments and clinical, and a raised instrument guide, organic growth for the year was not raised. He also asked whether caution was built in and why clinical could not sustain 20%. Thaysen said clinical momentum should continue this year and beyond. Instrument placements were strong in Q4 and Q1, and the pipeline was strong. Because a new instrument starts generating consumables roughly six months after installation, Thaysen was optimistic about the second half. He added that it was only one quarter into the year and Illumina was "leaning in" by raising the top and bottom line early. Dhingra said the $20 million raise, about 0.5 point, put the guide toward the higher end of the range: "decimal points move," and there was nothing more to it.

**Clinical versus research placements, and X pricing (Puneet Souda, Leerink).** Thaysen said placements grew in both clinical and research:

- Clinical is more than 60% of placements. These are mostly incremental machines rather than replacements of 6000s, so they lift revenue immediately.
- Research placements included large projects and single-cell work, with no substantial mix change.
- Spatial should add upside from late 2026 into 2027.

Because most research customers had already converted to X, Thaysen said Illumina would no longer face the transition price headwind when that market recovers. Dhingra said pricing-transition assumptions were unchanged: the clinical transition continues, and its price effects are already in the guide.

**Backlog and the risk of a frozen market (Tycho Peterson, Jefferies).** Peterson asked Illumina to quantify the backlog and the units it could not ship. He also asked whether the 18-month roadmap might "freeze the market," meaning customers delay purchases to wait for new products. Thaysen said placements showed no freezing, the funnel was strong, and the roadmap (new flow cells, spatial, TruPath, 5-base) supports both clinical and research. Thaysen saw no substantial change in competition: "We know there's a lot of noise out there. We are looking forward to compete when they finally get to the market." Dhingra gave no backlog figure. He said remaining performance obligations (contracted future revenue disclosed in the quarterly 10-Q filing) were up more than 20% year over year across instruments and consumables. Q2 placements should be close to Q1, with a robust pipeline for the rest of the year, much of it tied to new clinical trials and tests.

**Margin path (Madeleine, for Doug Schenkel, Wolfe Research).** The analyst noted that Q2 operating margin guidance was below the Street's estimate and that supply investment would continue into Q3. Thaysen pointed to the multi-year margin improvement and restated the 2024 plan to rebuild growth to high single digits by 2027, with quarterly puts and takes and inflation to offset. Dhingra repeated the three Q2 factors (higher instrument mix, component and freight inflation, a full quarter of SomaLogic). Because mitigation starts in Q2 and becomes visible in Q3 and Q4, Dhingra said he felt good about a second-half margin ramp.

**Clinical conversion stage (Daniel Brennan, TD Cowen).** Thaysen said the customer base is mixed. Some clinical customers with regulated or FDA-approved tests on the NovaSeq 6000 will stay longer; Thaysen called them "laggards." Many others have already moved. By the end of 2026 the transitioning business should have largely moved, leaving a long tail. Thaysen saw no clinical deceleration.

**Research outlook and SomaLogic (Patrick Donnelly, Citi).** Donnelly asked what research customers were saying, given the 12% decline came against an easy comparison. Thaysen said Washington appears aligned on committing to NIH (National Institutes of Health, the main US funder of biomedical research) funding, and he expected funding to pick up during the year. However, released grants still have to turn into spending on tools and consumables, and customers are cautious until they know who receives grants. Little improvement is built into the guide, so any recovery would be upside. Dhingra added that more than 90% of research volume is already on X, so pricing comparisons should keep improving through the year. SomaLogic revenue is reported mostly within microarrays.

**Can clinical exceed the guide? (Subbu Nambi, Guggenheim).** Nambi noted the guide implied clinical growth of low double digits to teens and research down mid to low single digits, and asked whether clinical could go well above the high end. Thaysen said clinical had been strong for two quarters and through 2025, and the price headwind is receding as more consumables move to X. Mid-teens is strong growth, and anything higher would be upside. Dhingra said there were no signs of deceleration.

**Competition and the "$150 genome" (Kyle Mikson, Canaccord Genuity).** Mikson asked whether the modest raise accounted for a competitor launching that summer. The rival had disclosed a $150 whole genome and attractive pricing for "counting" applications, which measure how much of something is present rather than reading full sequences. The competitor is not named in the transcript, and the $150 figure came from the analyst, not from Illumina. Thaysen said he was pleased to raise after one quarter, repeated his confidence in Illumina's competitive position, and said the second-half competitive dynamic should not differ much from the first half. Those expectations are built into the guide.

**35B flow cell pricing (Daniel Arias, Stifel).** Arias asked what cost per gigabase or per genome Illumina would quote for the 35B flow cell against the $150 benchmark. Thaysen declined: "we are not ready at this point to go out and talk about pricing." Pricing will come closer to launch. Thaysen framed the opportunity instead. Moving rare disease testing from exome sequencing (reading only the roughly 2% of DNA that codes for proteins) to whole genome sequencing requires about 15 times more sequencing. Conversations with exome-heavy customers center on price elasticity: aggressive pricing brings aggressive volume growth, and customers' cost per gigabase keeps falling.

**Low- and mid-throughput (Catherine Ramsey (Schulte), Baird).** Thaysen said the MiSeq i100, a low-throughput benchtop sequencer launched around the end of 2024 or early 2025, had more than 1,000 placements in 2025, and momentum continued into 2026. Mid-throughput has been weak for longer, for three reasons:

- the MiSeq i100 takes some of its low end;
- some customers move up to high throughput; and
- mid-throughput buyers are the most sensitive to the macro environment, since many are not in steady production mode and want flexibility.

Thaysen expected mid-throughput to improve when research markets turn.

**Inflation and placement cadence (Casey Woodring, JPMorgan).** Thaysen spoke generally about operational discipline and a record of offsetting cost challenges. Dhingra did not quantify the memory chip and freight headwind, but said it was within a range Illumina can act on. Dhingra confirmed Q2 placements similar to Q1 and a fairly robust second-half pipeline, so full-year placements should come in reasonably above the initial range.

**Which clinical customers? (Jack Meehan, Nephron Research).** Meehan asked whether X demand came from multi-cancer screening, MRD (minimal residual disease, detecting traces of cancer after treatment), therapy selection or women's health. Thaysen answered "all of the above." Interest is broad and spans regions. It includes large US centralized labs reporting oncology momentum, plus rare disease and NIPT (noninvasive prenatal testing, which screens fetal DNA in the mother's blood).

**Discounting (Michael Ryskin, BofA).** Ryskin noted that placements of about 80 in both Q1 and Q2 were well above the 50 to 60 norm. Yet instrument revenue of $118 million was below the roughly $125 million to $130 million he expected, so he asked whether Illumina was discounting. Thaysen said customers buy the X to run consumables. Illumina showed pull-through above $1.3 million per X at the JPMorgan conference in January 2026; pull-through is the annual consumables revenue per installed instrument. Thaysen expected that figure to hold or rise. Customers ordering five, ten or more machines get a volume discount, which Thaysen called reasonable, but the priority is getting units placed. Dhingra added that some of the "roughly 83 units" were placed under reagent-rental or lease models, which recognize revenue differently.

The unit count varies across the call. Dhingra said "more than 80" in prepared remarks and "83 units" in this answer, and Thaysen said "80+," about 20 more than Q1 2025.

**Spatial competition (Mason Carrico, Stephens).** Carrico asked whether spatial is a rising tide or a market with distinct pockets, given recent competitor announcements. Thaysen called it a rising tide and praised 10x Genomics' launch: "I'm actually quite impressed of what [Serge] and the team is putting out there." Thaysen said the launch raises interest overall, that the technologies differ and serve somewhat different customer segments, and that there is room for several players. Illumina developed its spatial approach with customers, and Thaysen said, "I'm pretty sure we have a winner" for the launch later in 2026.

**Stalled X revenue share (Kyle Mikson, Canaccord, second question).** Mikson noted that X's share of consumables revenue held at 55%, the same as Q4, even though its share of volume keeps rising toward 80% to 90%. He asked what moderated and whether revenue share would stay in the high 50s. Thaysen said it is "a little bit dangerous" to read the figure precisely, because it is a directional trend that varies by quarter. The key point is that clinical customers continue to transition, with over 85% expected by end of 2026, at which point the transition will be behind Illumina.

### What Q1 left unanswered

- Management gave no backlog dollar figure, no inflation estimate, and no 35B or cost-per-genome pricing.
- The Q2 operating margin guide of about 22% was below the Street, per the Wolfe analyst.
- The tension between strong unit placements and lighter instrument revenue was explained only qualitatively, through volume discounts and rental or lease accounting.

## Q2 2026 (call held July 30, 2026)

### The headline: the fastest growth of Thaysen's tenure, and a bigger raise

Q2 was a stronger quarter than Q1 and produced a larger guidance raise. Revenue grew at the fastest rate since Thaysen joined Illumina in 2023. The drivers were clinical customers expanding sequencing-based applications and more than 95 NovaSeq X placements, up from more than 80 in Q1 and well above the 50 to 60 planning range. Both revenue and earnings beat management's guidance. Margins also beat, despite costs coming in higher than expected [Thaysen and Dhingra, Q2 2026 call].

Seeking Alpha reported revenue of $1.16 billion, up 9.44% year over year and $28.42 million above consensus. Non-GAAP EPS of $1.31 beat consensus by $0.08 [Seeking Alpha header, Q2 2026 call]. Both beat Illumina's own Q2 guidance of $1.12 billion to $1.14 billion in revenue and $1.20 to $1.25 in EPS, set in April.

The tone shifted from Q1. Management raised RoW organic growth guidance above the top of its original range and lifted clinical consumables to mid-teens. Thaysen began describing the clinical opportunity as a multi-year "wave." At the same time, clinical growth slowed from about 20% to 15%, memory and freight inflation proved worse than expected, and Q3 was guided to a visible slowdown. Those three points became the focus of analyst questions.

### Strategic messages from the CEO

**Durability of the clinical franchise.** Thaysen said deep relationships with leading US clinical customers and a large installed base "reinforce the durability of our position." Clinical markets remain about 65% of sequencing consumables revenue and the main growth driver. Clinical growth was broad across regions and applications, with particular strength in the US and Canada. NovaSeq X demand remained high "more than 3 years after launch." Strong placements over three quarters expand customer capacity and "will support consumable growth for many quarters to come." Thaysen noted that placements vary by quarter but that demand is elevated [Q2 2026 call].

**Research is better, but no recovery is declared.** Research and academic markets improved from Q1, and some signs of improvement appeared late in the quarter. Thaysen said customers remain cautious and "it's too early to call a recovery."

**Strategy framing.** Thaysen described Illumina's aim as delivering "the highest quality insights for the lowest end-to-end cost." He organized the strategy around NovaSeq X and around multiomics, meaning measuring several layers of biology (DNA, RNA, proteins) and combining them. Product updates included:

- **NovaSeq X** remains central. The roadmap set out earlier in 2026 gives customers confidence that the platform will support their workflows for years.
- **Whole-genome MRD research workflow (launched).** It is designed to shorten assay development timelines and lower costs. It runs on NovaSeq systems and is in early access with select customers.
- **StrataMap Spatial (launched this summer).** This is the sequencing-based spatial biology product promised in April as "on track to launch later in 2026." The launch fulfilled the Q1 commitment.
- **Proteomics.** After the SomaLogic acquisition, the newly branded SomaScan and SomaSeq offerings are generating strong interest and linking protein data with genomic data.
- **BioInsight.** Thaysen described it as Illumina's data and insights business for AI-enabled drug discovery at pharmaceutical companies. It combines sequencing, perturbation tools (experiments that switch genes on or off), compute and AI. The Billion Cell Atlas had delivered over 300 million cells, up from "hundreds of millions generated" in April. Revenue booking had started, and three new partners joined after quarter end, bringing the total to six. More BioInsight updates were promised later in 2026.

**Leadership changes.** Michael Sullivan and Julie Coletti joined the management team, bringing commercial and legal experience. David King and Dan Skovronsky joined the Board, bringing healthcare, diagnostics and R&D experience. In April, Thaysen had been interim Chief Commercial Officer and the company had thanked three departing Board members; the Q2 transcript does not state whether Sullivan took over the commercial role.

### Analyst Q&A

**Why not raise the 2027 outlook? (Puneet Souda, Leerink).** In a partly garbled question, Souda asked about second-half installs and noted there was "no clinical cliff" and 2026 guidance was already above 5%. He asked why investors should not expect more than high single-digit growth in 2027. Thaysen said he was convinced clinical momentum "will continue for years to come," as shown by elevated first-half placements, and that placed instruments eventually run consumables. About 18 months earlier the company had framed a logic of 50 to 60 placements per quarter, and recent quarters were clearly above that. Thaysen said quarterly moderation was possible but that placements should stay elevated in the second half. On the cliff, Thaysen said: "it's not a cliff. It's a wave, and we are surfing it." On 2027, Thaysen remained "very, very committed" to high single-digit growth and said Illumina would keep building customer value "and then we see where it takes us." Management did not upgrade the 2027 target.

**Clinical slowdown and NovaSeq 6000 overlap (Tycho Peterson, Jefferies).** Peterson asked why clinical consumables slowed from Q1. He also asked whether consumables were being "over-earned" while customers run the NovaSeq 6000 and X in parallel, implying a headwind when the 6000s are retired. Thaysen said volume keeps expanding on the X, many 6000s remain in use, and some customers bought 6000s this quarter to continue operations. Customers will eventually move to X. Thaysen called this a normal part of the business, noting that HiSeq systems (an even older platform) were still in the portfolio only a few quarters ago, and did not see it as a real headwind. On the slowdown from about 20% to 15%, Thaysen said it was "still very strong." The US was still above 20%, and there were "ins and outs" in the Middle East and Latin America, with the Middle East situation hurting growth in the short term. Mid-teens is above where the year started and supports the 2027 target.

**The Q3 step-down and AI revenue (Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI).** Kumar asked whether Q2 had one-offs, why Q3 RoW organic guidance of 4.5% steps down from 8%, and why 8% is not sustainable. He also asked what is assumed for AI-related revenue. Thaysen said elevated placements are "not a one-off," because clinical customers are building installed base ahead of volume in their pipelines. Comparisons get tougher because placements in early 2025 were weaker than in the second half, when momentum started building. Consumables should stay strong, keep stepping up, and become the main growth driver into next year.

On AI, Thaysen cited more than 300 million single cells delivered in the Cell Atlas and three more pharma partners (six in total). Customers value direct insight for drug discovery programs and, beyond that, a foundation for predictive AI models of biology. "It's still early days. We are making money already from day 1 on this, both on top line and bottom line." Thaysen saw significant opportunity over time but gave no specific AI revenue assumption.

**Seasonality, stocking and the extra week (Michael Ryskin, BofA).** Ryskin said Q3 looked like a surprising step down, with Q4 implicitly guided higher than expected. He asked about seasonality, stocking or backlog-driven purchases, and the effect of an extra week in Q4. Thaysen said Q2 was very strong and Q3 is always somewhat lower than Q4, so the phasing is normal and as expected, with clinical customers building installed base for growth they see. Dhingra said Q4 is normally the largest quarter and will remain so. The extra week in Q4 is "largely a consumables story" and adds perhaps 0.5 point of revenue from extra selling days. Dhingra did not quantify the margin or EPS effect and did not address stocking. Consumables growth has been steady all year, Dhingra said, and variability almost always comes from instrument placements, which have been elevated since the second half of 2025 and should stay elevated in the second half of 2026.

**Breadth of clinical strength (Dan Leonard, RBC Capital Markets).** Leonard asked how broad clinical strength is, given US growth above 20% and strong instrument buying by the largest customers, and whether the application mix had shifted. Thaysen said the opportunity of making NGS (next-generation sequencing, the modern high-volume method Illumina sells) standard of care in healthcare is still ahead. Growth is broad: the US leads, but every region except China is growing well. By application:

- Oncology leads.
- Within oncology, MRD is starting to drive momentum, but therapy selection (testing tumors to choose a treatment) is still larger in dollars.
- MCED (multi-cancer early detection, blood tests that screen healthy people for many cancers) will become a real contributor later, but "we don't really see that really in the number today."
- Rare disease and NIPT are still growing nicely.

This was more specific than Thaysen's "all of the above" answer to Meehan in April.

**Memory costs and GPUs (Subbu Nambi, Guggenheim, first question).** Nambi noted that memory chip prices were still rising and allocations were tight. Nambi asked how this would evolve through stronger Q4 placements and into 2027, as Illumina adds more GPU-based compute (graphics processors, which are in high demand for AI) to future products. Thaysen said the team has offset headwinds repeatedly over three years, citing a 200 bps margin improvement in 2025 in a relatively flat revenue environment. Memory and freight costs were a headwind "we didn't anticipate to the level we have seen," and the costs are real. Illumina is passing some of the increase to customers and pursuing operational efficiencies to offset the rest. Costs with good visibility have been identified and will be managed.

Dhingra said the higher memory cost was already in Q2 results, mostly in gross margin, so Q2 is a starting run rate. Extra inventory and supply had been secured for the next several quarters to protect against near-term memory price moves. On GPUs, Dhingra said: "our instruments do not use the GPU architecture." They use a different, cheaper and more cost-efficient architecture, which Dhingra called a unique strategic cost advantage.

This marked a shift from April. In Q1, management described component and freight inflation as within a manageable range and expected mitigation to "fully offset" it. In Q2, management conceded the costs exceeded its expectations and added price sharing with customers and component stockpiling to the response.

**Why placements are strong in year four (Subbu Nambi, second question).** Nambi asked whether the stronger-than-expected placements reflected customers who had been waiting for competitive information and then committed to Illumina, and how much reflected improving funding. Thaysen attributed it to Illumina's value proposition and customer confidence that Illumina helps them succeed. Continued innovation on X shows "a lot of leeway" in the platform, making it "a platform to invest in" in a healthy market. Thaysen did not directly address competition or funding.

**Placement mix and research ordering (Harrison, for Mason Carrico, Stephens).** Thaysen said most placements still go to clinical and should continue to, even if academia improves, though some went to academic research. Dhingra put the mix at about 70% clinical and 30% research, noting the research share is global rather than US-only. This is consistent with Thaysen's April statement that clinical was "more than 60%" of placements. Late in the quarter there was more US funding release activity and more requests and quotes. However, Dhingra said, "we're not assuming any meaningful improvement in that market or revenue for us for the rest of the year." The improvement was directional only.

**Tracking multiomics (Kyle Mikson, Canaccord Genuity).** Mikson asked how investors can tell whether the multiomics strategy (StrataMap, single cell, proteomics) is working, whether it will be reported separately, and how its revenue is accounted for. Thaysen said the 2024 strategy has two legs:

1. converting customers to the X platform, which is proven and visibly gaining momentum; and
2. driving multiomics.

"The future belongs to multiomics," Thaysen said. Genomics is the backbone, but multiple layers are needed: from genome to proteome, and from bulk to single-cell to spatial. These should be delivered as end-to-end workflows with software that links the different data types, not just as library preps or sequencing runs. In 2024 Illumina expected multiomics to add 1 to 2 points of growth in 2026 and 2027, and Thaysen said that is "playing out as we're expecting." StrataMap has drawn "significantly more interest" than planned, and TruPath has a lot of momentum and will be a very strong platform.

Dhingra said most multiomics products, such as single cell and TruPath, appear in consumables revenue. Billion Cell Atlas and other BioInsight revenue appears in services. Illumina will keep commenting on Billion Cell Atlas contributions as it did this quarter, but Dhingra made no commitment to a separate breakout.

**The Q3 margin step (Jack Meehan, Operon Research).** Meehan asked how much of the step from 22.4% in Q2 to 24% in Q3 comes from mix versus other factors, and about Q3 consumables versus instrument assumptions. Dhingra said the second-half margin step-up had been the plan since the start of the year, given tariffs and cost actions, and it was playing out. Excluding the EPS-neutral deferred compensation item, Q2 operating margin was about 23%, so the underlying step to Q3 is about 100 bps rather than 150. Part comes from a higher consumables mix and part from cost actions in gross margin during Q3, with more in Q4. Most actions should show up in gross margin. Dhingra gave no Q3 consumables-versus-instruments split.

**Low- and mid-throughput (Jen, for Casey Woodring, JPMorgan).** Thaysen said Illumina has the broadest instrument range. The X serves high-throughput "production-like" sequencing, which directly generates customers' revenue. Low- and mid-throughput machines are more project-based and driven by different factors. Mid-throughput remains particularly sensitive to the macro environment, as it has been for a long time. Thaysen still felt good about it, saw continued placements, and expected medium-term momentum from Illumina's investments there. The MiSeq i100, launched "a little over a year ago," has many placements and continuing momentum, and is affordable for most labs. Thaysen summarized: "high end and low throughput is very strong right now. Mid-throughput is more muted because of the macro environment." This repeated the April picture.

## How the story evolved across the 2026 calls

**Clinical demand: from "long runway" to "a wave."** In April, management called clinical growth a "long runway," backed by two straight quarters of 20% ex-China growth, but kept the clinical guide at double digits to mid-teens. In July, the quarterly rate cooled to 15% because of Europe, the Middle East and Latin America, while the US stayed above 20%. Management nonetheless raised the full-year clinical guide to mid-teens and recast the opportunity as a multi-year wave. The application picture also became more specific. Oncology therapy selection is the largest driver today, MRD is building, MCED is not yet visible in the numbers, and rare disease and NIPT continue to grow. Analysts' main worry shifted from whether clinical can hold 20% (Q1) to whether it is being inflated by customers running two platforms in parallel (Q2). Thaysen dismissed that concern.

**Instruments: from supply-constrained to capacity-building.** Placements rose from more than 80 (about 83) in Q1 to more than 95 in Q2, against a 50 to 60 planning range. In both quarters, roughly 60% to 70% of placements went to clinical customers. In Q1, analysts questioned discounting because instrument revenue lagged unit counts; Illumina cited volume discounts and reagent-rental or lease accounting. By Q2, instrument revenue grew 31%. Management also pushed back the expected consumables payoff from new placements: "roughly 6 months" in April became "at least 6 to 9 months" in July, putting most of the benefit in 2027.

**The X transition: nearly done, and less of a price drag.** Clinical volume on X rose from 76% to about 78%, with 80% to 85% targeted by end of 2026. X's share of high-throughput revenue moved from about 55% to about 59%. Management argued in both calls that the pricing headwind from the transition is fading, especially in research, where about 90% of volume has already converted.

**China.** China was excluded from the growth metrics management emphasized. In both calls, management described growth in every region except China. The Q2 call explained why: Illumina remains on China's unreliable entity list. Neither call gave any China revenue figure or discussed a possible recovery.

**Pricing.** Illumina declined to give 35B flow cell or cost-per-genome pricing in April. Its stated position is that lower cost per gigabase increases volume, for example by moving rare disease testing from exomes to genomes, which uses about 15 times more sequencing. In July, pricing came up in a new form: Illumina said it was passing part of the memory and freight inflation on to customers.

**Competition.** In April, an analyst raised an unnamed rival's $150 genome and summer launch. Thaysen said Illumina saw "a lot of noise" and expected little change in competitive dynamics in the second half. In July, competition barely came up. Asked whether customers had been waiting for competitive information before committing, Thaysen did not address the point directly. In spatial biology, Thaysen openly praised 10x Genomics' launch and called the market a "rising tide" with room for several players.

**Costs and margins.** In April, management guided Q2 operating margin to about 22%, below Street expectations, and described component and freight inflation as unquantified but manageable. In July, management acknowledged the inflation was worse than anticipated. It added price-sharing and component stockpiling to its response, which raised inventory and lowered operating cash flow. Margins nonetheless beat guidance (22.5% versus about 22%), the full-year 23.4% to 23.6% margin guide held, and the promised second-half step to about 24% in Q3 was confirmed.

**Research funding.** The research and applied decline narrowed from 12% in Q1 to 7% in Q2, with 9% sequential growth and late-quarter signs of more US grant releases. Management kept the full-year research guide at a mid- to high single-digit decline in both calls and declined to call a recovery.

**Multiomics and BioInsight.** Spatial moved from "on track for later 2026" to launched (StrataMap Spatial). SomaLogic moved from "in line" to tracking toward the high end of deal expectations. The Billion Cell Atlas went from hundreds of millions of cells generated to more than 300 million delivered, with revenue booking started and six pharma partners. Management restated its 2024 expectation that new products add 1 to 2 points of growth in 2026 and 2027, but did not commit to reporting them separately.

| 2026 guidance item | February (start of year) | April (Q1 call) | July (Q2 call) |

| Reported revenue | $20M below the April range | $4.52B to $4.62B | $4.60B to $4.64B |
| RoW organic growth | 2% to 4% | 2% to 4%, upper end | Greater than 5% |
| Clinical consumables | Double-digit to mid-teens | Unchanged | Mid-teens |
