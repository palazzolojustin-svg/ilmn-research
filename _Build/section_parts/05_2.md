## Q1 2026: Beat, Modest Raise, and a Supply-Constrained NovaSeq X (call held 2026-04-30)

### The headline: a beat on every line, driven by instruments and clinical demand

Illumina opened 2026 with a quarter that beat both its own guidance and Wall Street's expectations. Revenue was $1.09 billion, up 4.8% from Q1 2025 on a reported basis. That was about $17.6 million above consensus (the average analyst forecast) and $20 million above the midpoint of management's guidance [Seeking Alpha header; Dhingra, Q1 2026 call]. Non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS, profit per share excluding one-time and non-cash items such as acquisition amortization) were $1.15. That was up about 19% year over year, about $0.10 above consensus, and about $0.10 above the midpoint of the February guidance. CEO Jacob Thaysen, who was also serving as interim Chief Commercial Officer, said revenue, margin and EPS all exceeded the guidance range. He called it a "great start," driven by disciplined execution, clinical strength and growth in every region except China [Q1 2026 call].

Several reporting conventions run through all three 2026 calls. VP of Investor Relations Conor McNamara explained them. "Organic" growth strips out currency moves and acquisitions. "Rest of World (RoW) organic" growth also excludes Greater China. Growth rates are year over year and reported unless stated otherwise. Illumina also changed its geographic reporting segments in January 2026 to match its commercial organization, and posted restated history on its website [Q1 2026 call].

On a like-for-like basis, Q1 growth was thin. Organic growth was just 1.2%, because currency and acquired revenue each added just under 2 points to the reported figure. RoW organic growth was 3.5%, above the high end of guidance [Dhingra, Q1 2026 call].

### Clinical versus research: the defining split

Consumables (the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run) are Illumina's recurring revenue engine, and in 2026 they split sharply by customer type. Clinical customers, meaning labs running tests used in patient care, made up more than 65% of sequencing consumables revenue [Thaysen, Q1 2026 call]. Clinical consumables grew 20% excluding China for the second straight quarter [Dhingra].

Thaysen named several drivers:
- the spread of sequencing-based diagnostics;
- more data-intensive tests, such as comprehensive genomic profiling (testing many tumor genes at once) and whole-genome sequencing;
- new assays (tests) launching;
- progress on reimbursement.

Thaysen saw a "long runway" ahead. Dhingra added that products such as 5-base (an assay that reads extra information beyond the DNA letters) and TruPath broaden access to richer data.

Research and applied consumables, sold to academic, government and pharma labs, fell 12% excluding China because of uncertainty over research funding. Dhingra described macro commentary as "stable to improving," but said year-to-date trends matched the plan. Thaysen said Illumina keeps investing in proteomics (measuring proteins) and single-cell work, with spatial products coming. In Thaysen's framing, research markets are where new technologies first take hold before moving into clinical use, and a return to growth is expected once funding uncertainty eases [Q1 2026 call].

### NovaSeq X: demand outran supply

NovaSeq X is Illumina's current high-throughput sequencer. It was first shipped about three years before this call and replaces the older NovaSeq 6000. In Q1, Illumina placed more than 80 NovaSeq X units. The internal target is 50 to 60 per quarter, and Q1 2025 had about 20 fewer placements. Placements grew in both clinical and non-clinical markets [Thaysen; Dhingra, Q1 2026 call]. Dhingra later cited "83 units" in Q&A. The figure is given as both "more than 80" and 83, which is a small inconsistency.

Dhingra said Illumina was supply-constrained because demand was so strong. Several clinical customers placed multi-unit orders, and high-throughput research placements also grew. Illumina is investing to scale X supply, with progress expected through Q2 and into Q3. The company exited Q1 with a "solid backlog," which supported its higher instrument outlook.

Two measures show how far the move to the X had progressed by Q1:
- Overall, about 82% of high-throughput sequencing volume and 55% of the related revenue was on the X.
- In research and applied, 90% of volume was on the X. In clinical, 76% was, and management expected more than 80% to 85% by the end of 2026.

This "transition" matters because the X sells sequencing more cheaply per unit of DNA. Each customer that converts therefore temporarily lowers Illumina's revenue per unit of volume. Total gigabase output (the amount of DNA sequenced) on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30%, and clinical output grew well above that [Dhingra].

### Other business and innovation updates

**SomaLogic.** SomaLogic, a proteomics company, closed on January 30, 2026. Illumina paid net cash of $363 million plus potential royalties and milestones. After quarter end, Illumina paid a first $25 million milestone for 2025 targets. Thaysen said SomaLogic is performing in line with revenue and profit expectations [Dhingra; Thaysen, Q1 2026 call].

**Service revenue.** Revenue from BioInsight partnerships and data deals can be lumpy [Dhingra].

**Products shown at AGBT.** Thaysen highlighted several launches from AGBT, an annual genomics technology conference held early in 2026:
- **TruPath:** whole-genome sequencing that skips traditional library prep (the sample-preparation step), cutting hands-on time to about 10 minutes. Rare-disease customers use it to fold several tests into one. Several customers are in clinical studies, and Thaysen expects demand to rise.
- **Spatial transcriptomics:** a way to map where genes are active within a tissue. Engagement was very high, and early users got data from hard samples such as lymphatic tissue. Launch is on track for later in 2026.
- **NovaSeq X 18-month roadmap:** new 14B and 35B flow cells, staggered flow-cell runs, and Q70 data quality. A flow cell is the consumable chip a run happens on, sized by billions of reads. Thaysen said the roadmap is driving placements even three years after launch.
- **BioInsight:** Illumina's data and AI business, introduced in late 2025. Its first major project is the Billion Cell Atlas, introduced early in 2026. The Atlas uses Perturb-seq, an experiment that switches genes off and reads the effect by sequencing, to study how genes drive disease. Hundreds of millions of cells had already been generated, and more companies wanted to join. The data could feed AI drug-discovery models.

Thaysen also thanked three outgoing board members.

### Q1 analyst Q&A

**Why not raise organic growth? (Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI).** Thaysen said clinical momentum should continue this year and beyond. Placements were strong in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026, and an instrument starts generating consumables roughly 6 months after installation, which supports the second half. He said Illumina was "leaning in" by raising after only one quarter. Dhingra said the $20 million raise moves the guide toward the top of the range, and "decimal points move."

**Clinical versus research placements and transition pricing (Puneet Souda, Leerink).** Thaysen said clinical made up more than 60% of X placements. Those were mostly new, additional machines rather than 6000 replacements, so they add to revenue right away. Research showed large projects and single-cell work but no big mix shift, and spatial could add upside from late 2026 into 2027. Because most research customers have already converted, Illumina will not face the transition price headwind when that market recovers. Dhingra said the price assumptions were unchanged and already in the guide.

**Backlog size and whether the roadmap "freezes" the market (Tycho Peterson, Jefferies).** Thaysen said there was no sign of customers holding back and the sales funnel was strong. Thaysen saw no substantial change in competition: "We know there's a lot of noise out there. We are looking forward to compete when they finally get to the market." Dhingra gave no backlog figure. He noted that remaining performance obligations (contracted future revenue disclosed in the 10-Q) were up more than 20% year over year, across instruments and consumables, and that much demand comes from new trials and tests.

**Margin ramp (Madeleine for Doug Schenkel, Wolfe).** The analyst noted that the Q2 margin guide was below the Street. Thaysen recalled the 2024 plan: roughly flat growth in 2025, "mid-single-ish" in 2026, and high single digits in 2027. Dhingra repeated the Q2 pressures and said the second-half margin ramp should come from mitigation actions.

**Late-cycle clinical conversion (Daniel Brennan, TD Cowen).** Thaysen said some clinical customers with regulated or FDA-approved assays on the 6000 are "laggards" who will stay on the older machine longer. Most of the conversion should be finished by the end of 2026, leaving a long tail. Thaysen saw no clinical deceleration.

**Research demand and SomaLogic reporting (Patrick Donnelly, Citi).** Thaysen saw alignment in Washington on NIH funding and expected it to pick up during the year. However, released grants still have to turn into purchases, and customers are waiting to see who wins them. Little improvement is assumed in the guide, so any recovery is upside. Dhingra said more than 90% of research volume is already on the X, so price comparisons will keep improving. SomaLogic revenue is reported mostly within microarrays.

**Can clinical beat the high end? (Subbu Nambi, Guggenheim).** Thaysen said the transition price headwind is receding, mid-teens growth is strong, and anything higher would be upside. Dhingra said there were no signs of deceleration.

**Competition and the "$150 genome" (Kyle Mikson, Canaccord).** Mikson noted that a rival, not named in the transcript, had disclosed a $150 genome and attractive pricing for counting applications ahead of a summer launch. Thaysen said competitive dynamics in the second half should look much like the first half and are built into the guide.

**Pricing for the 35B flow cell (Dan Arias, Stifel).** Thaysen said moving rare-disease testing from exome (only the protein-coding genes) to whole genome takes about 15x more sequencing. With exome-heavy customers, the conversation is about elasticity: aggressive pricing brings aggressive volume growth. Thaysen said "we are not ready at this point to go out and talk about pricing" for the 35B flow cell.

**Low and mid throughput (Catherine Ramsey, Baird).** The MiSeq i100, a low-throughput benchtop sequencer launched around the turn of 2024–25, had more than 1,000 placements in 2025, and momentum continued [Thaysen]. Thaysen said mid-throughput has struggled longer. The i100 takes some of the low end, some customers move up to high throughput, and mid-range buyers are the most macro-sensitive and often not in production mode. Thaysen expects that segment to improve when research turns.

**Inflation size and placement cadence (Casey Woodring, JPMorgan).** Neither executive quantified the memory-chip and freight inflation. Dhingra said it is "within a range" Illumina can act on. Dhingra reiterated that Q2 placements would be similar to Q1 and the second-half pipeline was robust.

**Which clinical customers? (Jack Meehan, Nephron).** Thaysen answered "all of the above": large centralized U.S. oncology labs, rare disease, and NIPT (noninvasive prenatal testing, a blood test on the mother screening for fetal genetic conditions), across regions.

**Discounting (Michael Ryskin, BofA).** Ryskin noted that $118 million of instrument revenue fell short of the roughly $125–130 million implied by 80-plus placements. Thaysen said customers buy the X to run consumables. At the J.P. Morgan conference in January 2026, Illumina had shown pull-through (consumables revenue per instrument per year) above $1.3 million per X, and Thaysen expected that to hold or rise. Thaysen described volume discounts on orders of 5, 10 or more units as reasonable. Dhingra added that some of the roughly 83 units were placed under reagent rental or leases, where the instrument is provided in exchange for committed consumables purchases. Those arrangements recognize revenue differently.

**Spatial competition (Mason Carrico, Stephens).** Thaysen called spatial a "rising tide" with room for several players and praised 10x Genomics' launch: "I'm actually quite impressed of what [Serge] and the team is putting out there." Thaysen said Illumina built its approach with customers and "I'm pretty sure we have a winner."

**The X revenue share stalled at 55% (Mikson, second question).** Thaysen said reading the figure precisely is "a little bit dangerous," since it is directional and varies by quarter. The key point is that clinical conversion should pass 85% by the end of 2026.

## Q2 2026: Fastest Growth Under Thaysen, a Real Raise, and New Cost Headwinds (call held 2026-07-30)

### The headline: acceleration, led by instruments

Q2 was the strongest quarter of Thaysen's tenure. Revenue was $1.16 billion, up 9.5% reported and 6.5% organic, with currency and acquisitions adding about 3 points. That was about $28.4 million above consensus. EPS was $1.31, up about 10% year over year, about 13% excluding dilution from acquisitions, and $0.08 above consensus [Seeking Alpha header; Dhingra, Q2 2026 call].

RoW organic growth was 8.1%, which Dhingra also called "8% ... ex China." That was above the high end of the 4%–6% guidance and more than double Q1's 3.5%. Thaysen called it the fastest revenue growth since joining the company, driven by clinical customers expanding sequencing-based applications. Revenue, EPS and margin all beat guidance despite higher-than-expected costs.

On this call, McNamara made explicit why China is excluded from the key growth measure. Illumina sits on China's "unreliable entity list," a government sanctions list that restricts its business there [Q2 2026 call].

### Clinical slows to 15% but stays strong; research shows a flicker

Clinical consumables grew 15% excluding China in Q2, down from 20% in Q1. U.S./Canada still grew above 20%. Europe, the Middle East and Latin America grew more slowly "due to ongoing near-term dynamics in the region" [Dhingra, Q2 2026 call]. Clinical remained about 65% of sequencing consumables, with particular strength in U.S./Canada [Thaysen].

However, full-year guidance stayed at a mid- to high-single-digit decline. Thaysen said there were signs of improvement late in the quarter, but "it's too early to call a recovery."

### NovaSeq X: supply catches up, placements jump

Supply investments let Illumina place more than 95 NovaSeq X units. That was well above the "close to Q1" (about 80) expectation set in April and the 50–60 target. The new units included several multi-unit capacity expansions by large clinical customers, some for new clinical trials [Dhingra, Q2 2026 call]. Illumina also placed more than 10 older NovaSeq 6000s, because some customers plan to stay on that platform for years.

Thaysen noted that demand remained high "more than 3 years after launch." Thaysen argued that three strong placement quarters expand customer capacity and "will support consumable growth for many quarters to come."

Transition status moved further. About 83% of volume and 59% of revenue were now on the X, up from 82% and 55% in Q1. Clinical volume on the X was about 78%, up from 76%, and the 80%–85% end-of-2026 target held. Gigabase output again grew more than 30%, with clinical "well above that." High-throughput volume and rising pull-through drove most growth [Dhingra].

### Strategy and products

Thaysen framed the strategy as delivering "the highest quality insights for the lowest end-to-end cost." He described deep relationships with leading U.S. clinical customers and a large installed base that "reinforce the durability of our position." New items this quarter:
- **Whole-genome MRD research workflow.** MRD stands for minimal residual disease, meaning detection of trace cancer DNA after treatment. The workflow is meant to shorten assay development and cut costs, runs on NovaSeq, and is in early access.
- **StrataMap Spatial.** This is the sequencing-based spatial product promised in Q1, and it launched this summer.
- **SomaScan and SomaSeq.** These are the rebranded SomaLogic proteomics offerings, linking protein and genomic data. Dhingra said SomaLogic revenue was tracking toward the high end of deal expectations.
- **BioInsight and the Billion Cell Atlas.** Thaysen described BioInsight as combining sequencing, perturbation tools, compute and AI for pharma drug discovery. More than 300 million cells had been delivered, revenue booking had begun, and three new partners joined after quarter end, for six in total.
- **Leadership changes.** Michael Sullivan and Julie Coletti joined management, bringing commercial and legal experience. David King and Dan Skovronsky joined the board, bringing healthcare, diagnostics and R&D experience.

The 2024 plan expected 1–2 points of incremental growth from multi-omics in 2026–27, and that is "playing out as we're expecting." StrataMap interest is "significantly more" than planned, and TruPath has strong momentum. **Low and mid throughput (Jen for Casey Woodring, JPMorgan).** Thaysen said the X serves "production-like" sequencing that directly generates customer revenue, while low and mid throughput are project-based. The MiSeq i100 is affordable for most labs and keeps placing. Thaysen summarized: "high end and low throughput is very strong right now. Mid-throughput is more muted because of the macro environment."

## Q3 2026: Third Straight Beat-and-Raise, Transition Nearly Done, AI Story Grows (call held 2026-10-29)

### The headline: steady growth, and the margin step-up arrives

Q3 revenue was $1.17 billion, up 7.6% reported and about $13.2 million above consensus. Currency and acquisitions added just under 3 points [Seeking Alpha header; Dhingra, Q3 2026 call]. RoW organic growth was 4.7%, which Dhingra also rounded to 5%. That was in line with the ~4.5% guide and slightly above it.

EPS was $1.44, above the high end of guidance, up 7% from Q3 2025, and $0.08 above consensus. Year-to-date EPS was up 12%. Operating margin was 25%, 100 bps above guidance, on higher revenue and disciplined spending. Gross margin was 69.4%, up 20 bps year over year and up from 68.2% in the first two quarters.

Thaysen said: "We had a great third quarter with strong clinical sequencing demand and elevated NovaSeq X placements, driving revenue, margin and EPS above our guidance range," and "Our strategy is working."

One accounting wrinkle affects these numbers. Illumina recognized tariff refunds in Q3. The refunds raised operating income, margins and EPS and lowered revenue, and they were not in prior guidance. All figures discussed on the call exclude them [McNamara, Q3 2026 call].

### Clinical holds at 14%; research "stabilizing"

RoW clinical consumables grew 14%, similar to Q2. In Q&A, Thaysen described the figure as "14%, 15%." Research and applied fell 6%, also similar to Q2. That segment has now grown sequentially for two quarters, and Dhingra said "we are seeing signs research activity is stabilizing," the most positive language of the year. Full-year expectations were unchanged at mid-teens for clinical and a mid- to high-single-digit decline for research. Gigabase output again grew more than 30%.

### NovaSeq X: transition nearly complete

Illumina placed more than 80 X units in Q3. That brings the total to about 360 over four quarters, "more than in our first year of launch" [Thaysen, Q3 2026 call]. Several of Illumina's largest clinical customers placed multi-unit expansion orders. X placements rose year over year in both clinical and research.

Outright sales still account for more than 90% of placements. However, interest in leases is growing among research customers, and in Q&A Dhingra said it is also growing among mid-sized clinical labs.

Transition status stood at about 84% of high-throughput volume and 60% of revenue on the X. Research has been at 90% or more for five straight quarters, so it is "largely complete" with minimal remaining price headwind. Clinical volume on the X stayed at 78%, unchanged from Q2, and conversion has slowed. Some clinical labs keep existing on-market assays on the 6000 while adding X capacity for new tests. Dhingra now expects clinical conversion of 80%–85% "in coming quarters" rather than by year end. Year-over-year transition price headwinds should therefore be smaller in 2027 than in 2026. Because the shift is nearly done, Illumina will stop publishing its transition price/volume slide after Q4 2026.

### Strategy: data, AI and the "Illumina Insight" ecosystem

**Billion Cell Atlas.** Thaysen framed the Atlas as fixing AI's biology bottleneck, the lack of accurate, standardized, reproducible functional data: "There is no Internet of biology." The Atlas was "over halfway complete." It is building a proprietary, AI-ready perturbation data set, recording how cells respond when genes are changed, to help pharma link genetic signals to cell mechanisms and validate drug targets. GSK is the latest consortium member, and other companies want access to the finished Atlas.

**ASHG.** At ASHG (the American Society of Human Genetics meeting, held earlier in October 2026), Illumina showed customers combining genomics, transcriptomics (RNA activity), methylation (chemical marks on DNA) and proteomics. These were done across bulk, single-cell and spatial methods, with Illumina supplying the whole workflow at lower total cost.

**BioInsight and SpliceAI2.** Illumina launched the BioInsight platform, which unifies its analysis software, AI models and curated data. Among those models is SpliceAI2, which predicts how genetic changes alter RNA splicing (how gene messages are cut and joined). In rare-disease research data sets, SpliceAI2 found 17% more disease-relevant splice variants than other models. Thaysen said the next era of life sciences will be powered by software, AI and differentiated data, moving from predicting biology toward more proactive healthcare.

- Cash was about $1.34 billion and debt was $1.79 billion, down from $1.99 billion.

### Q3 analyst Q&A

**Stocking orders and the clinical jumping-off point (Peterson, Jefferies).** Thaysen did not mention stocking. Thaysen expects mid-teens clinical growth for the year and sees Q4 "coming along" well. Dhingra said Q4 2025 was an unusually tough comparison, with 11% consumables growth, yet Q4 2026 should still deliver "a pretty good 5%, 6%."

**The 2027 framework (Kumar, Evercore).** Kumar said the Q4 exit implies close to 6% RoW growth. That differs from management's ~4.5% guide, and the gap was not reconciled. Thaysen joked that 2027 took until the second question. Thaysen gave no details but called the market healthy and said clinical will drive growth "for the foreseeable future." Thaysen said multi-omics positions Illumina for a research recovery, and AI data demand is only beginning. Consumables have grown above 5% every quarter, "a good starting point," and Illumina is "on the latter end" of lapping the transition price impact. Thaysen did not answer whether 2026's placements create an instrument headwind in 2027.

**Pull-forward? (Doug Schenkel, UBS).** "This is a normal course of business," Thaysen said. Placements are driven by a healthy clinical market and more sequencing-intensive assays ("a lot more coming"). The AGBT innovations are convincing customers to bet on the X. Thaysen gave no capacity-versus-replacement split.

**Elasticity and 2027 (Souda, Leerink).** Thaysen said the 6000-to-X move cut price but created substantially more volume that has persisted, rather than a one-time gain under a fixed budget. Large-volume customers can negotiate price: "that's a conversation I like to have." Thaysen said Illumina is "still on the track to deliver on the '27 of high single digit," after mid-single-digit growth in 2025.

**New-product growth and margin runway (Nambi, Guggenheim).** Thaysen said the 2024 plan for new assays and data is "playing out well." The multi-omics portfolio launched over 18 months is "very significant," and with SomaLogic its momentum, from a low base, will show in 2026 and especially from 2027. Margins have improved about 400 bps despite this year's unexpected electronic-component, shipping and tariff costs, and "there is more to come... also in '27."

**Liquid-biopsy customers (Luke Sergott, Barclays).** Liquid biopsy means blood-based cancer tests. Sergott asked about these customers growing volumes and running concordance studies (validation that results match across instruments) as they move to the X. Thaysen described the "razor-razor blade" model, in which instruments drive recurring consumables: "nobody buys an instrument to put in the corner." Thaysen said whether clinical grows 14%, 15% or 16% matters less than the strength of the market. U.S./Canada clinical has grown very strongly for four quarters, and Thaysen does not expect that to stop. Dhingra cited new test launches, on-market volume growth and coming clinical-trial readouts as a "very rich pipeline."

**AI pricing and the "$50 genome" (Arias, Stifel).** Arias mentioned genomes discussed at around $50, a figure from the analyst that management did not confirm. Thaysen said AI value is "way beyond" whole-genome pricing. It lies in large multi-omic data sets, which "no other company in this industry" can build. "We love elasticity," Thaysen added, describing a data flywheel shared with customers. "I'm not super concerned about what others are doing right now." Thaysen did not address the 35B flow cell.

**Large-customer pricing in 2027–29 (Westenberg, Piper Sandler).** Westenberg noted that clinical is about 70% of consumables. He asked about expected discounts for large customers such as the makers of Signatera (an MRD test) and Shield (a blood colorectal-cancer screen), which are growing volume 25%–30%. Thaysen declined to discuss pricing, saying "when our customers are growing, we were also growing very healthy," and noted that 18 months earlier analysts doubted Illumina could grow at all. Dhingra said U.S./Canada consumables and U.S. clinical, a proxy for these customers, grew "well above" 14% in Q3.

**Upside beyond 2027 (Mikson, Canaccord).** Asked about approaching double-digit growth with high-20s margins, Thaysen said it is "absolutely not impossible" and "there's definitely upside to a high single-digit growth." But Illumina went from negative growth in 2024 to mid-single digits and wants to prove high single digits first. Customers increasingly want data sets and model performance, not just sequencing.

**When BioInsight shows up in results (Leonard, RBC).** Thaysen said the software is doing "extremely well." Data and multi-omics are on track for the planned 1%–2% of growth in 2027, and AI could make it "substantially bigger." Dhingra expects visible disclosure sometime in 2027. The Billion Cell Atlas and a second generation expanding to 5 billion cells are among the first drivers.

**Extra week and China (Woodring, JPMorgan).** Dhingra explained that Illumina adds a week roughly every four years to keep quarters on a 13-week cycle. The extra week was in guidance all year, falls over the holidays and early January, has minimal revenue impact, and adds a week of people cost. No unusual budget flush (year-end spending of leftover budgets) is assumed, so a strong flush would be upside. The Q4 consumables raise comes "entirely" from placements and demand.

On China, which returned to year-over-year growth in Q3, Thaysen said Illumina remains committed and is working with customers and regulators. Woodring raised new U.S. trade restrictions on some Chinese competitors. Thaysen said Illumina learned of them "as many of you did yesterday morning" and is still assessing the impact.

**AI as services or instruments? (Brennan, TD Cowen).** Thaysen said small lab-built data sets are noisy, and models trained on them often learn where the data came from rather than the biology. That creates a big service opportunity for standardized data. But customers will add their own data, so instruments sell too: "it will be a blend." Dhingra listed multi-omic combinations, such as whole genome plus proteomics, single-cell plus transcriptomics, and spatial plus transcriptomics, chosen by disease pathway. Neither gave X pricing for the quarter.

**China, again (Westenberg).** Westenberg cited China growth of 7% and double digits, a figure garbled in the transcript. Thaysen gave no number. Thaysen said Illumina focuses on what it controls, sees strong customer interest, believes the opportunity is big, and that Chinese customers want Illumina there.

**Leases in clinical? (Peterson).** Dhingra said leases and reagent rentals have long been offered and demand is rising in both research and clinical. The rise is concentrated among mid-sized clinical labs in the U.S., Europe and elsewhere, not the largest clinical customers, who are busy expanding capacity.

## How the Story Evolved Through 2026

Across the three calls, management's narrative moved from a cautious beat to growing confidence while keeping guidance disciplined. The table summarizes the key quarterly metrics so the trends can be read at a glance. RoW organic growth peaked in Q2, clinical cooled from 20% to the mid-teens, research declines narrowed each quarter, and margins rose steadily.

| Metric | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 | Q3 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1.09B | $1.16B | $1.17B |
| RoW organic growth | 3.5% | 8.1% | 4.7% |
| Clinical consumables (ex-China) | +20% | +15% | +14% |
| Research and applied consumables (ex-China/RoW) | −12% | −7% | −6% |
| NovaSeq X placements | >80 (83) | >95 | >80 |
| X share of volume / revenue | 82% / 55% | 83% / 59% | 84% / 60% |
| Clinical volume on X | 76% | 78% | 78% |

| FY EPS guide | $5.15–5.30 | $5.30–5.40 | $5.40–5.45 |

**Clinical demand.** The story shifted from "20% for two quarters" to a deliberately de-emphasized mid-teens rate ("whether 14, 15 or 16% matters less"). U.S./Canada stayed above 20% while Europe, the Middle East and Latin America softened. The application framing became steadier over the year: therapy selection now, MRD building, MCED later.

**Pricing.** Management repeatedly declined to give specifics on 35B pricing or large-customer discounts. It leaned on elasticity arguments, presenting lower prices as a driver of higher volume. Its main reassurance is that the transition headwind is nearly over: research is done, clinical is stuck at 78% because of regulated legacy assays, and 2027 headwinds should be smaller.

**Competition.** Management's language moved from looking forward to competing "when they finally get to the market" (Q1) to "not super concerned" (Q3). The $150 and $50 genome prices came from analysts, not management.

**China.** China went from being the only region not growing (Q1) to returning to growth (Q3), while remaining on the unreliable entity list. New U.S. trade restrictions on Chinese competitors were still being assessed.

**Multi-omics and AI.** The Billion Cell Atlas grew from hundreds of millions of cells to 300 million-plus delivered with six partners, then to more than halfway complete with GSK joining. AI moved from an aside to a core pillar. The 1–2 point 2027 growth contribution from new products and data was reaffirmed on every call, with management suggesting AI could make it "substantially bigger."

**2027.** Management reaffirmed high-single-digit growth each quarter and acknowledged possible upside, but declined to raise the target ahead of the Q4 call.
