## How to read this section

This part covers research on Illumina published from May 2026 onward. It draws on four sell-side brokers (Ameriprise, Freedom Broker, J.P. Morgan and Barclays) and two independent sources (Bloomberg Intelligence and a Seeking Alpha contributor). "Sell-side" means research written by brokers for investors; "independent" means research from firms that do not sell Illumina's securities.

The window opens with first-quarter 2026 results on 30 April 2026 and runs through a Seeking Alpha piece on 8 September 2026. Two results events frame the period: Q1 2026 (reported 30 April) and Q2 2026 (reported 30 July). Between them came Roche's entry into sequencing, which was the dominant debate. Roche held a Diagnostics Day on 12 May and launched its Axelios 1 sequencer in late June.

The stock is the backdrop to every call in this section. It closed at $120.37 on 30 April [JPM], then $126.74 at the 30 April close used by most notes, $160.03 on 9 June, $187.15 on 15 July, $205.09 on 30 July and $218.65 on 8 September. It was about $278 by early October. This rise drove most of the rating changes described below.

## Q1 2026 results (reported 30 April 2026)

### What the company reported

These are facts as summarized across all the Q1 notes.

**Headline results.**
- **Revenue:** $1.091B, up 4.8% year over year (y/y). Organic growth, which excludes currency and acquisitions, was +1.2%.
- **Revenue versus expectations:** consensus was about $1.07B, so the beat was $18M or 1.7%. Company guidance had been −1% to +1% organic, and J.P. Morgan had expected about 0%.
- **Growth outside China:** "ROW" (rest of world, meaning outside Greater China) organic growth was 3.5%, above guidance.
- **Adjusted EPS:** $1.15, against consensus and company guidance of about $1.05. Adjusted EPS means earnings per share excluding items such as amortization and one-offs. The result was up 18.6% from "Core Illumina" EPS of $0.97 in Q1 2025; "Core" means after the GRAIL divestiture.
- **Gross margin:** adjusted gross margin was 68.2%, up 80 basis points (bps) y/y. A basis point is 0.01 percentage point. Consensus was 67.1%, but Barclays' own estimate was 68.5%.
- **Operating margin:** adjusted operating margin was 21.9%, up 150 bps y/y. Consensus was 20.5%, and management said the result was about 150 bps above its guidance.
- **Operating expenses:** rose 3% to $506M because of SomaLogic team additions. SomaLogic is a protein-measurement business acquired on 30 January 2026 for $363M net cash. A first $25M milestone payment was made after the quarter.

**Products.**
- **Sequencing instruments:** $118M, up 8–9%, against a Street estimate of $102M.
- **Sequencing consumables:** $726M, up 2% organic and 5% organic outside China, just above the Street's $722M. Consumables are the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every sequencing run.
- **Q1 sequential drop:** consumables fell 4% from the prior quarter, which J.P. Morgan called the largest Q1 sequential decline since Q1 2020.
- **Clinical consumables:** grew 20% y/y outside China for a second straight quarter, and were more than 65% of sequencing consumables.
- **Research and applied consumables:** fell 12% outside China. This segment covers academic, government and industrial customers.
- **Microarrays:** fell 20% on a ROW organic basis because direct-to-consumer customers were soft. Microarrays are an older genotyping technology.
- **Service and other:** $151M in sequencing, against a Street estimate of $169M.

Totals differ between notes depending on definitions. Freedom Broker's company-wide figures were consumables of $797M (+3.8%) and service of $174M (+8.1%). Bloomberg Intelligence (BI) described service as down 20% to $174M. That conflicts with every other source and is probably an error.

**NovaSeq X placements.** NovaSeq X is Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer.
- Illumina placed more than 80 units, against a 50–60 quarterly framework and more than 60 in Q1 2025.
- More than 60% of the units went to clinical customers, and many added capacity rather than replaced the older NovaSeq 6000.
- There were multi-unit orders, and Illumina was supply-constrained.
- Some deals carried volume discounts or reagent-rental and lease terms. Under reagent rental, the customer gets the instrument in return for committed reagent purchases.
- The active installed base reached about 970 units.

**NovaSeq X transition.**
- NovaSeq X carried 55% of high-throughput consumables revenue, flat versus Q4.
- It carried 82% of high-throughput gigabases (Gb) shipped. A gigabase is a billion DNA letters, a unit of sequencing output.
- Research volume was about 90% migrated and clinical volume 76%. Management targeted 80–85% of clinical volume by year-end.
- Total sequencing activity rose more than 30%.

**Regions.**

| Region | Revenue | Reported growth | Organic growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| USCAN (US and Canada) | $590M | +6.3% | +5% |
| EMEALA (Europe, Middle East, Africa, Latin America) | $356M | +7.2% | +1% |
| APAC (Asia-Pacific) | $93M | +13.4% | +4% |
| Greater China | $52M | −27.8% | −29% |

**Cash and capital return.**
- Operating cash flow was $289M and free cash flow $251M. The Street had expected $502M of free cash flow [JPM].
- Illumina repurchased about 2.0M shares for $242M at an average of $120.85.
- On 28 April the board added a $1.5B buyback authorization, on top of about $400M remaining.
- Cash and short-term investments were about $1.16B, and gross leverage was about 1.5x debt to EBITDA.

**Products and pipeline.**
- TruPath cuts hands-on time for whole-genome sequencing to about 10 minutes.
- An 18-month NovaSeq X roadmap adds 14B and 35B flow cells, staggered runs and Q70 accuracy.
- A spatial product, measuring gene activity while preserving tissue location, was due later in 2026.

**Guidance.** Guidance is the company's own forecast. Full-year 2026 guidance was raised modestly:
- Revenue: $4.52–4.62B, up from $4.50–4.60B, a $20M rise at the midpoint. Consensus was about $4.53–4.54B.
- Adjusted EPS: $5.15–5.30, up from $5.05–5.20. Consensus was $5.11–5.12.
- Adjusted operating margin: 23.4–23.6%, up from 23.3–23.5%.
- Organic growth was held at 1–3% (2–4% outside China), and reported growth at 4–6%.
- Instruments outside China moved to flat to low-single-digit (LSD) growth, from an LSD decline to flat.
- Consumables were held at LSD to mid-single-digit (MSD) growth. That assumed clinical growth of double digits to mid-teens and a research decline of MSD to high-single-digit (HSD).

Barclays relayed further guidance details:
- SomaLogic adds 1.5–2 points to growth but costs 100 bps of margin and 18 cents of EPS.
- Currency adds about 1%.
- China revenue is expected at $210–220M, roughly a 1% headwind.
- Management now pointed to the high end of the 2–4% ex-China organic growth range.

J.P. Morgan relayed details from its follow-up with the company:
- Placements of about 80 in Q2 and more than 60 in each of Q3 and Q4.
- Pull-through of about $1.3M a year per NovaSeq X "or higher." Pull-through is the consumables revenue each installed instrument generates.
- Performance obligations, meaning contracted future deliveries, up more than 20%.
- Barclays put the backlog at about one quarter of revenue.

**Q2 guidance.** Revenue of $1.12–1.14B (+3–5% organic) was above consensus of $1.11B. But the adjusted operating margin of about 22% was below the 23.5% consensus, and EPS of $1.20–1.25 was below $1.25. The margin shortfall came from a heavier mix of instruments, freight and electronic-component inflation, NovaSeq X supply investment and a full quarter of SomaLogic.

**2027 targets.** Management reaffirmed:
- HSD revenue growth outside China.
- An operating margin moving toward 26%.
- Double-digit to teens EPS growth.

### Ameriprise (1 May): Buy

Macino [Ameriprise, 2026-05-01] kept the Buy rating. The argument was that 23.7x forward earnings "may not reflect the opportunity" in sequencing-based cancer testing. That multiple compares with a five-year average of 72.2x, which is distorted by the GRAIL years. Macino noted that Illumina "has not built much improvement into guidance," leaving upside if research funding recovers. They called the weakness "an opportunity to accumulate" for aggressive, multi-year investors.

The broader thesis rests on several points:
- Illumina is a vehicle for genomic medicine.
- NovaSeq X is a multi-year instrument tailwind.
- The company sizes the total sequencing market at $125B, of which only about $10B is served today.

Listed risks include:
- Problems with acquisitions or integration, and currency moves.
- Loss of market share and generic competition.
- Product or trial disappointments, regulatory delays and reimbursement.

Market data at the time:
- Market capitalization of $19.25B and a 52-week range of $73.86–155.53.
- Beta of 1.69. Beta measures volatility relative to the market; above 1 means more volatile.
- Price-to-book of 7.1x and price-to-sales of 4.6x.

Ameriprise showed FactSet consensus EPS of $5.18 for 2026 and $5.89 for 2027.

### Freedom Broker (1 May flash, 6 May update): Buy, $140 → $155

The flash note was titled "Solid Q1 Beat and Raise; Stock Reaction Appears Unjustified" [Freedom, 2026-05-01]. Zubkov argued that the roughly 1% after-hours dip was unwarranted. The beat was "high quality," and the "real surprise" was that instrument demand was moving from stabilization toward growth. Q1 revenue beat Freedom's own estimate by $16M, and EPS beat it by about $0.10.

Five days later Zubkov raised the target to $155, "mostly due to expected higher instrument growth" [Freedom, 2026-05-06]. The reasoning ran as follows:
- Instrument growth for the rest of 2026 should be 5%, up from Freedom's prior flat assumption.
- Consumables should accelerate in the second half (H2) of 2026 and into 2027 as installed instruments ramp. The NovaSeq X transition "may approach its peak" by year-end.
- Guidance "appears conservative," supporting a continued beat-and-raise cycle, in which a company beats forecasts and then raises guidance.
- Service growth, led by BioInsight (Illumina's data unit) and the Billion Cell Atlas, could help while research is weak, but those deals are lumpy.

Freedom's estimate changes:

| Measure | 2026E | 2027E | 2028E |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $4,601M (from $4,537M) | $4,824M (from $4,759M) | $5,036M (from $4,969M) |
| EPS | $5.31 (from $5.06) | $5.70 (from $5.61) | $6.37 (from $5.82) |

Freedom's model table mixed old and new figures without explanation.

The valuation blends three methods:
- 50% a five-year discounted cash flow (DCF). A DCF values a company on the present value of its future cash flows.
- 25% at 7x EV/sales (enterprise value divided by next-12-month sales).
- 25% at 30x P/E (price divided by earnings).

Freedom listed three risks: sequencing competition, weak academic funding, and China's "Unreliable Entity List" (UEL), which restricts Illumina's instrument sales there. Freedom disclosed that it may seek investment-banking fees from Illumina.

### J.P. Morgan (1 May): Neutral, $120 → $125

Woodring's team called the quarter "a solid start to the year" but stayed "on the sidelines until there is more visibility on how the landscape will evolve" [JPM, 2026-05-01]. Their concerns were pricing and competition, with Roche's Diagnostics Day on 12 May flagged as the next catalyst.

The valuation was a DCF using a 9.8% discount rate and 4.0% terminal growth. The discount rate was the weighted average cost of capital (WACC). Terminal growth is the long-run growth assumed after the forecast period.

J.P. Morgan's estimates:
- **EPS:** $5.21 for 2026, $5.93 for 2027 and $6.48 for 2028. Consensus was $5.12, $5.81 and $6.62.
- **Revenue:** $4,573M for 2026 and $4,799M for 2027.
- **Balance sheet:** net cash expected by the end of 2026.

Management said pricing headwinds should ease because research volume is more than 90% migrated to NovaSeq X.

### Barclays (30 April): Underweight, $110 → $122

The note was titled "If You Seed it, The Consumable Revs Will Come...Right?" Sergott framed the quarter as a two-sided debate [Barclays, 2026-04-30].

**The bull side.** Placements beat Barclays' estimate by more than 50%. As Sergott put it, "Apparently ILMN didn't get the memo" that every other tools company had seen weak academic capital spending. Management expected the strength to continue into Q2, which argues against a pull-forward. Because the placement beat produced only a small revenue beat, Barclays inferred that leasing was "seeding the market." Management's line was that "this isn't 2014 and sequencing is not a vanity research project anymore."

**The bear side.** Consumables came in only in line, and Barclays saw signs of lower price per gigabase. The evidence was that NovaSeq X's share of Gb rose 500 bps to 76% (Barclays' figure) while its share of consumables revenue stayed flat at 55%. Management called reading quarterly shifts "a frivolous exercise" because flow-cell mix creates noise. Barclays gave management "the benefit of the doubt" with "imperfect data," but said the issue "warrants watching." Its cynical reading was that Illumina is discounting ahead of Roche and in response to Ultima Genomics, another rival.

**The call.** Barclays said it was "hard to declare a clear victory," given only about a 50 bps top-line beat. It expected the shares to rise anyway. It called the margin ramp from Q2 to Q3 "not a layup." It stayed Underweight because it is "not sold on the ability to deliver HSD growth in '27."

**Valuation and estimates.**
- The target was 23.5x 2026 EPS of $5.17, up from 21x on $5.11.
- The 2027 EPS estimate moved to $5.65 from $5.64, below the $5.81 consensus.
- Scenario values were $150 upside and $60 downside.
- Illumina looks cheap on EV/EBITDA but expensive on P/E relative to peers.

### Bloomberg Intelligence (1 May): no rating

Adeline Zandi said Illumina appeared to be "in the early stages of trading price for volume" to defend share against Roche. They also saw "some conservatism" in a guidance raise of only $20M.

BI's broader equity outlook summarized the longer story. It described the NovaSeq X transition as "painful," with competition intensifying in mid-throughput sequencers. China sanctions have halved China's contribution, with no clear timeline for resolution.

## The Roche debate and J.P. Morgan's upgrade (May–July 2026)

### J.P. Morgan upgrades to Overweight, $185 (10 June)

J.P. Morgan said Illumina had been "one of the most highly debated stocks in our space" since Roche unveiled its sequencing-by-expansion (SBX) technology. The firm upgraded on the view that clinical customers are "stickier-than-appreciated" [JPM, 2026-06-10]. The thesis had five parts.

1. **Clinical labs are locked in.** The large US clinical labs certified under CLIA rules have all moved, or are moving, to NovaSeq X. Clinical work drives more than 40% of company revenue and more than 65% of sequencing consumables. Pricing headwinds from the transition are "largely gone exiting this year." Gb volume consistently grows at least 30%. Diagnostics executives voiced "general intrigue" about Roche but no urgency to switch. As reimbursement improves, the payoff from switching shrinks, especially for FDA-approved assays.
2. **A long volume runway.** Demand comes from three areas:
   - Therapy selection, meaning choosing drugs from a tumor's genetics.
   - Minimal residual disease (MRD) tests, which detect leftover cancer after treatment.
   - Multi-cancer early detection (MCED), blood tests that screen for several cancers at once.

   "Sequencing intensity" is also rising. Whole-genome sequencing uses about 15x the data of whole-exome sequencing. Single-cell and spatial experiments need 3–15x and 10–20x more data than a whole genome. J.P. Morgan expects clinical consumables to grow low-to-mid teens annually. Diagnostics test volumes are expected to grow from high teens to above 20%.
3. **Axelios looks manageable near term.** J.P. Morgan cited concerns raised by industry experts:
   - Reusable flow cells may carry contamination and carryover between samples.
   - Axelios needs more input DNA.
   - Its analysis software stack is fragmented.

   The firm wrote that "pricing alone does not seem to be enough," but conceded that Illumina's standing "is under more pressure now than in the past."
4. **Upside from research and multiomics.** US academic and government funding could recover in H2 2026 and 2027. Research is about 40% of revenue, including about 25% from US academic and government customers. Multiomics, meaning measuring several molecule types such as DNA and proteins, and BioInsight should add 100–200 bps of growth by 2027. The multiomics products are Fluent, SomaLogic and StrataMap.
5. **Valuation.** J.P. Morgan expects mid-to-high-teens EPS growth and a free-cash-flow yield of about 4–5%. Illumina trades at a 4.1x EV/EBITDA premium to the "Core Tools" group, compared with a historical premium of about 15x.

**Model.** J.P. Morgan forecasts HSD organic growth starting in 2027. That rests on clinical consumables outside China growing at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) in the low-to-mid teens through 2030. China is expected to decline by mid-teens. Gross margin stays in the high 60s because reagent rentals weigh on prices. Revenue is $4,583M in 2026, $4,919M in 2027 and $5,401M in 2028, with EPS of $5.23, $5.98 and $7.01. The DCF uses a 9.1% WACC and 4.5% terminal growth, and terminal value is 86% of the total.

**Customer survey.** J.P. Morgan surveyed 62 customers: 30 academic and 32 clinical, about 65% in North America. Key findings:
- **Spending:** customers planned to spend more in 2027 and 2028 than Illumina's long-term growth target implies.

  | Group | Instruments 2027 | Instruments 2028 | Consumables 2027 | Consumables 2028 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Clinical | +22.2% | +31.0% | +9.4% | +14.0% |
  | Academic | +18.6% | +19.0% | +9.0% | +12.8% |

- **Pricing:** planned volume grew faster than planned spend, by 2.4–5.2 points. That implies customers expect price cuts, but much smaller than the recent double-digit price declines.
- **Illumina's share:** academic labs expect Illumina's share of their sequencing to rise 1–3 points. Mid-sized clinical labs expect it to rise from 50% to 57%.
- **Awareness of Axelios:** 40% of respondents were unfamiliar with Axelios. On a 1–10 purchase-likelihood scale, academic labs averaged 6.2 and clinical labs 4.7. The most common answers were only 3 and 4.
- **Timing:** few would buy within 6–12 months (8%). The largest group, 35% and mostly academic, said 12–24 months.
- **Effect on Illumina:** 65% of potential buyers would reduce their Illumina use, but only about 11% would retire an instrument.
- **Reagent rental:** 37–39% of respondents said a reagent-rental offer from Illumina would make them less likely to buy Axelios.
- **Decision factors:** data quality led both reasons to consider Axelios and reasons not to.
- **Recent purchases:** only about 5% of respondents bought an Illumina instrument in 2025 or 2026 to date, which J.P. Morgan linked to tight academic budgets.
- **Lock-in:** one core-facility user said switching "will take Tremendous amount of service, labor, and time."

The survey has caveats. The sample is small, and only 37 respondents knew Axelios. Some slide counts and the NovaSeq 6000 share (16% versus 56%) are inconsistent. The survey deck lists Illumina as "Neutral" at $160.03 on the same day as the upgrade. J.P. Morgan's later Q2 note calls these the "May upgrade" and "May survey," which conflicts with the 10 June date.

### Barclays to $145 (23 June); Axelios launches (late June)

### Bloomberg Intelligence: "a more competitive era" (26 June)

Palmer called NovaSeq X "the entrenched standard." Customers' software, validation processes and staff are all built around it.

Palmer still found Roche's path "credible" for two reasons: its distinct chemistry and its reach into hospitals and cancer testing. If Axelios performs as advertised, it could:
- Pressure Illumina's pricing power.
- Speed up adoption of routine diagnostic sequencing.
- Give Roche more exposure to oncology.

Disruption "is unlikely to be immediate, but Roche could still chip away where workflow or economic advantages are clear."

## Q2 2026 results (reported 30 July 2026)

### What the company reported

**Regions (organic growth).**

| Region | Revenue | Organic growth |
|---|---|---|
| USCAN | $653M | +13% |

**Placements and transition.**

- More than 10 older NovaSeq 6000s were also sold, for assays staying on that platform.

**Capital return.** Illumina bought back 0.94M shares for $122M at an average of $129.07. About $1.8B of authorization remains.

**New products.** BioInsight's Billion Cell Atlas has delivered more than 300M cells, and the business now has six pharma partners. StrataMap spatial interest is above plan. Management still expects new products to add 1–2 points of growth in 2027.

- Clinical consumables: mid-teens growth. Instruments: LSD growth. Consumables overall: MSD growth.
- Research: still guided to an MSD-to-HSD decline.

Q3 guidance was revenue of $1.14–1.16B (+4.5% organic), a margin of about 24% and EPS of $1.33–1.38, against consensus of $1.36. An extra week in Q4 adds about 0.5 point of growth. Management said H2 instrument growth would not match H1, with placements dipping in Q3 and recovering in Q4.

### J.P. Morgan (31 July): Overweight, $230

J.P. Morgan rolled its valuation forward to a December 2027 target, using a 9.8% WACC [JPM, 2026-07-31]. It called HSD revenue growth and mid-to-high-teens EPS growth "highly achievable."

EPS estimates rose to $5.34 for 2026, $6.16 for 2027 and $7.41 for 2028. Bloomberg consensus was $5.25, $5.93 and $6.81. At $205.09 the stock traded at 38.4x 2026 EPS. It was up 56% year to date and 92% over 12 months.

### Barclays (31 July): Underweight, $145 → $160

The note was titled "Once a Prom King Always a Prom King." Sergott found "not much to pick at this Q," but stayed Underweight [Barclays, 2026-07-31].

**The bull math.** Bulls do simple arithmetic: 60% of the business grows above 20%, and if research stabilizes, total growth reaches double digits ("Even we can do this math without Excel").

**Barclays' objection.** That math ignores competition, which will require "continued price concessions, bundling, and favorable terms." Barclays sees Illumina as an MSD-growth business. It "cannot justify" a 35x P/E, which it called the highest in Tools.

**Competitors.** The note names "Element's Axelios" as a near-term threat in research counting applications. Axelios is in fact Roche's system, so the competitor meant was probably Element's AVITI. Barclays sees Roche as a long-term clinical threat. It said Illumina's 35B flow cell would need competitive pricing, which "inherent technological barriers" may prevent.

**Other points.** Barclays said multiomics "is beginning to bear fruit," but the claimed 1–2 points of growth cannot be verified. Management is "doing a great job," while the bears "continue to take our lumps."

**Valuation and estimates.**
- The target is 28x 2027 EPS of $5.73, up from $5.63. Consensus is $5.92.
- 2026 EPS is $5.34.
- Scenario values are $230 upside and $100 downside.

### Ameriprise (31 July): Buy

Macino said the case now "increasingly depends on" whether placements convert into consumables pull-through. New systems typically take six to nine months to reach normal usage. Even so, the "improving financial profile" remained an opportunity to accumulate.

At the time the stock traded at 36.3x forward earnings and 7.0x sales, with a market cap of $31.0B. FactSet consensus EPS was $5.30 for 2026 and $6.00 for 2027.

### Bloomberg Intelligence (31 July): "Boosts Visibility of 2027 Growth Goal"

Palmer and Fallon Helmon said the 2027 HSD target "appears increasingly visible." The 60-per-quarter placement goal is now "increasingly less meaningful."

They noted that clinical work's share of consumables rose 10 points in two years to 65%, which "adds ballast" to revenue. Their main negative was H2 cadence: Q4 needs "a more marked step-up" because Q3 guidance was "a touch softer than consensus."

BI also noted that buybacks totaled $740M in 2025.

### Freedom Broker (5 August): downgrade to Hold, $155 → $185

The note was titled "Accelerating Fundamentals Confirmed, but Much of the Upside Is Now Priced In." Zubkov wrote that today's placements are "laying the foundation for tomorrow's higher recurring consumables revenue." But they no longer considered guidance conservative, and expected future raises to be "more incremental" [Freedom, 2026-08-05].

**Estimates.** EPS rose to $5.38 for 2026 (from $5.31), $5.98 for 2027 (from $5.70) and $6.59 for 2028 (from $6.37). Revenue estimates are $4,637M, $4,900M and $5,151M.

**Valuation.** The multiples rose to 8x EV/sales and 35x P/E. Measured against the $198.16 prior close, the $185 target implies a return of −6.64%. Under Freedom's volatility-adjusted system, with annual volatility of 45.87%, that gives a score of −0.14, which maps to Hold.

## Seeking Alpha (8 September): Hold, DCF fair value $188

Stephen Ayers argued that Illumina is "priced for perfection it hasn't earned yet." The DCF value of $188 is 13.8% below the $218.22 price. It uses a 9.74% WACC and assumes about 11% revenue growth and free-cash-flow margins rising toward 27–30%.

**Track record.** Ayers rated the stock Buy in August 2024, and it has risen 64% since. They moved to Hold in March 2025 over China and Roche concerns.

**Main arguments.**
- **True growth is lower:** organic growth is about 4–6.5%.
- **The margin gain is mostly non-operating:** Q2 operating income rose $31M. Of that, $18M came from government incentives ($22M versus $4M) and $8M from the absence of restructuring charges. That leaves about $5M of real improvement on about $100M of extra revenue.
- **H2 looks steep:** Q4 margin must exceed 25% to hit full-year guidance. H2 free cash flow must reach about $527M to hit the roughly $940M implied for the year. Refunds from the IEEPA tariffs, which the Supreme Court struck down in February 2026, may help.
- **Buybacks nearly stopped:** purchases fell to 32K shares by June, with none from the April authorization. This is probably partly to manage cash ahead of a $500M note maturing on 9 September. Illumina issued $300M of 2029 notes in August.
- **Placements may be a pull-forward:** strong placements may have pulled demand forward ahead of Axelios. On the Q2 call, management insisted demand was genuine: "this is a very healthy market."

Ayers called the "1 to 2 points" of new-product growth a "major unsolved mystery."

## Background in the Bloomberg Intelligence outlooks

BI's outlooks restate the long-run context.

**Recent history.**
- Revenue jumped 40% in 2021.
- It rose 1% in 2022, then declined in 2023 and 2024.
- It grew 2% outside China in 2025.

**Market size.**
- The addressable market exceeds $125B.
- Illumina expected to capture 34% of the research market by 2027, as stated at its 2022 investor day.
- Liquid biopsy, blood tests for cancer DNA, is an $800M market.

**Customers.**
- Government funding is almost half of sales.
- Consumables are 60% of sequencing revenue.

**Deals and capital.**
- The $480M Solexa acquisition "may go down as one of the best health-care deals in history." Market cap peaked at $76B in Q3 2021.
- The GRAIL saga brought a $476M EU fine and $35–50M of spin-off charges. GRAIL was spun off on 24 June 2024, and Illumina kept a 14.5% stake plus royalties.
- Cash was $1.6B at the end of 2025, and 2025 free cash flow was $931M.

BI's earnings timeline traces the arc back to NovaSeq's launch in 2017. Earlier sections of the Core Notes cover that history. Recurring themes include:
- A 2023 margin collapse during the GRAIL period and the Icahn proxy fight.
- 352 NovaSeq X shipments in 2023.
- A cut to 2025 guidance from China's restrictions and a 25-cent tariff hit.
- A recovery through 2026.
