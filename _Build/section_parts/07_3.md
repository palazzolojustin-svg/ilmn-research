Its quality "isn't quite as good... but it's good enough," and too few instruments slow turnaround, "the only reason Ultima can't come up." Ultima now offers 10B flow cells, mix-and-match read lengths for 10x samples and new wafer pricing. Element offers more output and spatial features. Roche is "a little slow." Illumina "will lose... to Ultima for sure, and probably to Element," because it is "too spread out on customer service and instrument repairs, and their technology is released before it's ready." AXELIOS is "somewhere in between" major and minor, lacking flexibility and paired-end reads. The lab will buy before December 2026: Ultima most likely (leadership pushing; no Ultima in the Southeast), then Element ($350,000 with spatial), then Roche (likely too late).

**Products and support.** Most exciting: "Constellation methylation assays" (whole genome plus methylation); Vanderbilt is most excited by Illumina spatial. TruPath went from "not likely at all" to many orders; the lab upgraded an instrument on 8 Jul 2026. If cost stays down, most genomes could move to it because of DRAGEN (Illumina's analysis software): "No way can beat the DRAGEN." SomaLogic lacks workflow development; Olink is "growing too fast." Support "has improved," driven by competition and their feedback.

## Ioannis Ragoussis, Head of Genome Sciences, McGill Genome Centre (interview 10 Jul 2026)

**Who and vantage point.** A Guidepoint call moderated by Andrew Carmen, labelled "Academic KOL - Physician Scientist" (KOL: key opinion leader). Ragoussis has 35+ years in genomics, having built facilities at King's College London and Oxford's Wellcome Trust Centre, and has run all of McGill's genomics platforms for nearly 14 years. They are a customer and evaluator. Atera is on order.

**Illumina.** Illumina is "in full use," "constantly overbooked," "the go-to platform": "we are doing more sequencing than ever... with Illumina" [Expert: Ragoussis, McGill, Jul 2026]. Ramping programs include the Canadian Personal Health Initiative (~100,000 short-read genomes, all Illumina, which co-funds reagents, locking the platform) and the next Marathon of Hope phase (~20,000 tumours, mostly Illumina). They are applying for another NovaSeq X Plus. Illumina remains "the predominant force" for two to three years. Constellation was tested and "doesn't look... a game changer"; Illumina's spatial is "not suitable yet" for FFPE clinical samples.

**Ultima.** About half Illumina's cost; Solaris 2.0 chemistry improved quality and throughput; its 10B wafer is "much cheaper than Illumina's 10 billion." It suits Olink and single-cell, and whole genome work has begun. Barriers: bespoke pipelines per application, an immature ecosystem, scientific conservatism and funding inertia; it needs "at least another year" to approach Illumina's popularity. "Illumina is struggling to keep pace" on price but "may decide to dampen the prices dramatically"; a second platform is useful leverage. Their UG100-to-UG200 upgrade needed new liquid handling and separate high-performance computing, but is "not strictly necessary."

**Roche and 10x.** AXELIOS (not yet available; discussions to acquire one) is "the instrument that I can see as having the potential to replace the Illumina technology completely," with speed (data in hours) the main edge after they visited Roche's Genia unit. 10x single-cell use is up 10%-20%. Xenium is "the most robust" spatial platform, at capacity; Visium has "very, very little need." They were "very upset" Atera is a new instrument rather than a Xenium upgrade; it costs about double, offers ~25,000 versus ~5,000 targets, and suits only high-utilization labs; 10x is reconsidering pricing after customers "revolted." Roughly 20% of single-cell experiments could be replaced by one Atera run. (They referred to 10x software "StrataMap," apparently confusing it with Illumina's product.) Spatial will not dent sequencing given population genomics, exponentially growing liquid biopsy MRD and sequencing-based proteomics. Revio is at full capacity; Illumina Protein Prep and Olink are complementary. Macro: inflation contained, a weaker Canadian dollar, next year's budget the key risk.
