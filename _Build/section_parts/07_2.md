**Price.** Roche's $150 genome (announced at AGBT; the Broad dates it to March 2026) was read as un-disruptive. Cedars and CG said $50–75 was needed, and Yale already pays Illumina $150. The Broad compared it with Illumina's roughly $200 list price. Every cost-focused customer said prep, QC and labor make "cheap genome" claims misleading, with fully loaded genomes at $250–400.

**Turnaround.** Experts split on whether speed matters. Vanderbilt, the Broad and TCAG said it matters for NICU, stat and contract work. Cedars, Yale and UCSD said slow prep and clinical scheduling erase the gain.

**Who Roche targets.** Experts split again: CROs and counting work (biopharma, Regeneron), clinical diagnostics (TCAG, EpiCypher, CG) or research cores first (Cedars, former Illumina AD). Chip reuse of about 20 times and analysis readiness were repeatedly flagged as unknowns. DRAGEN was widely seen as an Illumina advantage that rivals lack.

**Funding weakness hit research demand unevenly.** Yale, UCSD and Arizona reported declines tied to slow NIH disbursement and multiyear awards, as did the Massachusetts core. The Broad, Dana-Farber and Delaware were flat or up, with Delaware warning that its 2025 record reflected pulled-forward spending.

**Elasticity is real but lagged.** The Broad doubled output on flat Illumina spend, the biopharma customer expects flat-to-lower spend on more data, and Cedars launched new tests only because the NovaSeq X was cheaper. UCSD and Duke warned that cheaper boxes cause a short-term consumable dip when budgets are tight.

**Pricing pressure is broad.** Experts reported Illumina discounting (Yale, Massachusetts, Vanderbilt, CG), a 3–5% February list increase plus tariff surcharges (Arizona) and strongly incentivized NovaSeq X migration.

**The NovaSeq X's economics favor scale.** Delaware skipped it, and Arizona and Massachusetts are losing work to CROs and commercial providers that run full flow cells. Cores describe a squeeze from CRO undercutting at UCSD, Arizona and Massachusetts.

**Element is quietly winning mid-throughput and multiomic slots.** Biopharma, Arizona and Delaware described adoption or upgrades, and EpiCypher nearly switched. The Broad and TCAG found no use for it, and Yale likes it but needs more throughput. Ultima appears as a cheap high-end or counting option with quality and pipeline caveats.

**Value is shifting beyond short-read sequencing.** 10x's Atera, launched at AACR 2026, was the period's other big event. UCSD and Duke see in situ whole-transcriptome spatial cannibalizing single cell and sequencing volume; Yale is waiting. Long reads threaten short-read methylation (EpiCypher), while TruPath drew opposite verdicts (Yale enthusiastic, Delaware dismissive). Illumina's 5-base solution drew consistent praise from UCSD and Delaware, while SomaLogic drew interest but no adoption commitments.

**How views shifted.** Compared with expectations voiced earlier, several experts recorded clear movements:
- UCSD's 2025 enthusiasm for a modular Roche box faded once the actual design emerged.
- Delaware's expected 2025 funding slump never came.
- The gene-therapy biotech and Arizona held their outlooks unchanged over 90 days, while Massachusetts turned more pessimistic.
- Roche's narrative moved from technology promise, based on AGBT data, to price disappointment and adoption hurdles as experts saw the $150 figure and the pre-launch status.
- Former insiders moved toward framing Illumina's issues as strategic (clinical versus research focus, software execution) rather than technological.
