## What Oxford Nanopore sells and why it matters for Illumina

Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) is a London-listed company in the FTSE 100. It makes DNA and RNA sequencers that work by pulling a single strand of genetic material through a tiny protein pore (a "nanopore") and reading the changes in electrical current as each base passes. Illumina's dominant technology works differently. It copies DNA in short fragments and reads them optically, an approach called sequencing-by-synthesis. Nanopore reads can be essentially any length, which analysts call "universal reads" [JPM, 2026-05-13]. Nanopores can also read RNA directly, and they can detect methylation, the chemical tags on DNA that affect gene activity, without extra lab steps. Results arrive in real time [DB, 2026-09-21].

ONT holds more than 2,500 active patents across more than 250 patent families. It is headquartered in the UK and manufactures there. It sells through direct sales teams and distributors in North America, Europe and Asia-Pacific [DB, 2026-09-21].

### Products

The product range runs from small to large.

- **MinION** is the small portable device. It returned to growth in 2025, largely because of Biopharma customers [Barclays, 2026-01-12]. The Mk1D MinION "has done okay" [Barclays, 2026-03-25].
- **GridION** is a mid-sized benchtop system. The GridION Dx is ONT's first in vitro diagnostic (IVD) device, meaning a regulated device for testing patient samples. It received CE and UKCA approval in January 2026 (the EU and UK regulatory marks). The "Q-line" GridION products are "locked down" versions built for regulated settings such as clinical labs and drug-manufacturing quality control (QC) [Barclays, 2026-03-25].
- **PromethION** is the high-throughput sequencer. It now accounts for more than half of group revenue [Barclays, 2026-09-28]. P2i is the PromethION model ONT is focusing on. The smaller P2 Solo was discontinued in early 2026.
- **ElysION** was a sample-preparation automation platform. ONT stopped it in 2025–26 because, in management's words, ONT "was not the best provider" of automation. This produced a £1.8m inventory write-down [JPM, 2026-03-02; Barclays, 2026-03-11].
- **Flow cells** are the consumable chips that hold the nanopores, and they are the recurring part of the business. Consumables were about 71% of revenue in H1 2026 [DB, 2026-09-21]. Management expects the steady-state mix to be 75–80% consumables, perhaps by 2028. It had earlier assumed a 60:40 split of consumables to devices [Barclays, 2026-09-08].

### Customers and regions

ONT reports four end markets:

- **Research:** academic and government labs.
- **Clinical:** anyone using the technology to diagnose patients. Most of this revenue comes from lab-developed tests (LDTs), which a single lab designs and runs itself, rather than ONT-branded diagnostic kits [Barclays, 2026-03-02].
- **BioPharma:** drug makers and contract manufacturers.
- **Applied Industrial:** for example plasmid sequencing, food safety and synthetic biology.

Clinical, BioPharma and Applied Industrial together are called "Applied Markets." In H1 2026 Research was 65% of revenue and Applied 35%, split into Clinical 15%, Industrial 12% and BioPharma 8%. Regionally, the Americas (AMR) were about 33%, EMEAI (Europe, Middle East, Africa and India) about 48% and APAC (Asia-Pacific) about 19% [DB, 2026-09-21].

In 2025 the NIH (the US National Institutes of Health, the main US research funder) represented about half of Americas revenue [Barclays, 2026-03-25]. Deutsche Bank instead puts ONT's exposure to NIH-funded customers at about 10–15% of group revenue [DB, 2026-07-20]. US federally funded customers were 13% of revenue in 2024 [JPM, 2026-01-20]. China was about 8% of revenue at the start of 2026 and 44% of APAC revenue [JPM, 2026-01-20; Barclays, 2026-03-11]. Management later described China as 10% of 2025 revenue, falling to about 7% [Barclays, 2026-07-13].

### Market size

In January 2026, outgoing CEO Gordon Sanghera put ONT's total market opportunity at $20–25bn, of which $13–14bn is unique to nanopore [JPM, 2026-01-20]. The new strategy narrowed this. Management now cites a serviceable market of about $14–16bn across its priority applications: about $4.1–5.5bn in BioPharma, about $6bn in selected Clinical uses and about $610–740m in targeted Research [DB, 2026-09-21].

### Competitive position versus Illumina, PacBio, Roche and others

All three brokers frame ONT as complementary to Illumina more than a substitute.

**Illumina.** Deutsche Bank's channel checks found ONT "complementary to short reads in many applications, not a broad substitute." They describe Illumina as the scale and workflow benchmark, with an installed base that generates durable consumables demand [DB, 2026-09-21]. ONT management criticises Illumina's long-read product, the TruPath assay, as "still short reads and stitching them together," and notes it needs a separate test for methylation [Barclays, 2026-03-11]. J.P. Morgan lists Illumina's very large installed base, pricing and accuracy as its first risk to ONT [JPM, 2025-12-08 onward].

**PacBio.** Deutsche Bank sees PacBio as the more direct long-read threat. Its SPRQ-Nx chemistry cut the listed cost per HiFi genome by about 30%. Expert feedback suggests PacBio may be preferred where high consensus accuracy and predictable output matter. ONT leads on read length, native RNA and DNA analysis, real-time results and flexibility [DB, 2026-09-21]. Earlier, Deutsche Bank had described PacBio as lacking direct methylation and high-throughput scalability, and as needing larger instruments [DB, 2026-01-22].

**Roche SBX / Axelios.** Roche's SBX (Sequencing by Expansion) technology shares only the nanopore sensing element with ONT. Sanghera argues it is fundamentally sequencing-by-synthesis and short-read [JPM, 2026-01-20]. Axelios reads average 400–600 bases and reach at most about 1,000. J.P. Morgan treats Roche as an "indirect additional competitor" that will take share mainly from short-read incumbents [JPM, 2026-05-13].

ONT CFO Nick Keher warned that Roche could destabilise short-read pricing [DB, 2026-01-22]. Management has said "ONT doesn't need to take a copy" and calls Roche "somewhat complementary" [Barclays, 2026-03-11; 2026-09-08]. Management also says Roche cannot sequence native RNA, while ONT "remains the only player able to" do so [Barclays, 2026-03-25].

**Element Biosciences** planned an IVDR (EU diagnostic regulation) product in 2026. Sanghera saw no threat from it because it is short-read [JPM, 2026-01-20].

**China.** There are 7–8 local competitors in China [Barclays, 2026-09-28], and Chinese rivals price at about a 30% discount [Barclays, 2026-09-08].

### Patents

ONT's patents range from about 6–7 years to nearly 20 years of remaining life, and each new pore brings a new patent. Some wet-chemistry manufacturing steps are deliberately left unpatented so they stay secret. Management says competitors are "not close" to replicating them [Barclays, 2026-09-08], though it concedes the gap with Chinese rivals will likely narrow [Barclays, 2026-09-28].

ONT won rulings in Europe and Australia. In Australia a competitor "agreed to infringement." After the European ruling, certain competitor products are excluded from parts of that market [Barclays, 2026-09-08; 2026-09-28]. J.P. Morgan still lists eventual competition from other electrical long-read sequencers, once patents expire, as a risk.

### Pricing per genome

This table compares the consumable cost to sequence one human genome, as relayed by J.P. Morgan after Roche's Diagnostics Day. ONT is the premium-priced option today.

| Platform | Cost per genome |
|---|---|
| ONT, 2 genomes per flow cell (early access) | ~$345 |
| ONT, 4 genomes per flow cell (roadmap) | <$200 |
| Illumina | $200 |
| Roche Axelios | $150 |
| Ultima Genomics (target) | $80–100 |

ONT intends to keep premium pricing. Its argument is that it delivers a "fully loaded genome" (long reads plus methylation), and that a short-read run plus a separate methylation assay would cost more in total [JPM, 2026-05-13].

## Chronology: results, guidance and management commentary

### Leadership change (August 2025 – March 2026)

In August 2025 ONT announced that co-founder and CEO Gordon Sanghera would step down [Barclays, 2025-12-08]. On 8 December 2025 it named Francis van Parys as his successor, effective 2 March 2026.

Van Parys has spent about 2.5 years as CEO of Radiometer, a Danaher subsidiary with more than $1bn of revenue. Radiometer makes acute-care, point-of-care diagnostics (tests run at or near the patient), covering blood gas and immunoassay testing. Before that he was President of Biotechnology Integrated Solutions at Cytiva for one year and VP Commercial for Asia-Pacific at Cytiva for 2.5 years. Earlier he spent 11 years in regional roles at GE Healthcare. In total he has more than 20 years leading multi-billion-dollar life science businesses.

Sanghera left the Board at close of business on 2 March 2026 and stays as an adviser until early 2027.

Analysts welcomed the appointment. J.P. Morgan was "encouraged" by the overlap between his point-of-care background and ONT's push into Biopharma and Clinical, and expected the shares to outperform by more than 5% that day [JPM, 2025-12-08]. Barclays called it "an incremental positive." It highlighted his experience in regulated markets, which ONT has been "progressively moving towards" through partnerships with bioMérieux and Cepheid. Cepheid, like Radiometer, is owned by Danaher [Barclays, 2025-12-08]. Deutsche Bank later said his background "bodes well" for growth in applied markets [DB, 2026-01-22].

### FY25 trading update (12 January 2026)

ONT said FY25 revenue would be £223–224m. That is 24% growth at constant currency (growth with exchange-rate effects removed), against FY24's £183.2m. It beat guidance of 20–23%, which ONT had steered to the top end of in November.

Brokers' pre-update estimates were close:

| Benchmark | FY25 revenue estimate |
|---|---|
| Barclays | £222.9m |
| Company-compiled consensus | £220.4m |
| Deutsche Bank's consensus figure | £221m |

Implied second-half revenue was £117.9m. That was about 3% above Bloomberg consensus [JPM], 2.7% above company consensus of £114.8m [Barclays], and 1% above J.P. Morgan's own estimate.

H2 growth of about 21% was roughly 7 points slower than H1. J.P. Morgan blamed a tough comparison: H2 2024 had grown 36%, versus 12% in H1 2024.

Full-year growth by segment was:

- Clinical: about 60%. J.P. Morgan estimated more than 70% in H2.
- BioPharma: about 30%, with 52% in H2.
- Applied Industrial: 27%, slowing to about 7% in H2 on tougher comparisons.
- Research: 15% "despite end-market funding pressures," with about 11% in H2.

PromethION grew more than 40%. Every region grew more than 20%. Americas growth accelerated to above 20% in H2 from about 17% in H1, driven by non-Research customers.

Cash was £302m, against estimates of £280.9m (consensus, per Barclays), £276m (consensus, per Deutsche Bank) and £287m (J.P. Morgan). Improving working capital and the new capex sales model helped (both explained below). Cash had been £403.8m at the end of FY24.

ONT gave no 2026 guidance. It reiterated its mid-term targets:

- Revenue growth above 30% a year (compound annual growth rate, CAGR) from FY24 to FY27.
- Gross margin above 62% by FY27. Gross margin is the share of revenue left after the direct cost of making products.
- Operating-cost growth of 3–8% a year.
- Adjusted EBITDA breakeven in 2027. Adjusted EBITDA is operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation, amortisation and one-off items.
- Positive cash flow in 2028.

Deutsche Bank said "some may need convincing" on the acceleration needed. It called the shares "unduly punished in H2-25" and named ONT a top UK small-and-mid-cap healthcare pick for 2026 [DB, 2026-01-12].

### J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference and dbAccess (January 2026)

At the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference, CFO Nick Keher called Adjusted EBITDA breakeven the "North Star." He said costs could be "modulated further if required." Underlying H1 2025 gross margin was already 61% [JPM, 2026-01-20].

**The capex model.** In 2025 ONT moved from leasing larger devices (customers paying over time) to a "capex" model where customers buy devices outright. At the start of 2025 most larger-device sales were leases; ONT exited the year at close to 100% outright sales. The shift added 3–4 points to H1 2025 revenue growth and £20m to cash flow.

**2026 headwinds Keher listed:**

- US federally funded customers.
- China export restrictions.
- Completed EMEAI contracts, to be offset by the ramp of the UK Biobank contract (50,000 genomes).

**2026 tailwinds:** Clinical and BioPharma. Americas H2 2025 revenue was up about 30%, and Applied markets now exceed half of Americas revenue. ONT has hired a former head of sales from a large sequencing peer, and Sanghera credited the sales team with share gains in the Americas.

**Product plans Sanghera described:**

- PromethION at 4 genomes per flow cell (2 already in early access).
- A GridION Q v2 for clinical and biomanufacturing QC.
- PromethION Q at the end of 2026.

Sanghera was most excited by infectious-disease clinical use.

Two days later, at the dbAccess UK conference, Keher acknowledged past problems with "launching things too early" [DB, 2026-01-22]. Deutsche Bank's takeaways from that meeting:

- Applied markets could reach about 50% of sales in time.
- BioPharma could reach 20–30% of revenue in time.
- Clinical IVD products are expected within three years (by 2028).
- Deutsche Bank and consensus expected breakeven in FY28, a year after the company's FY27 target.

### FY25 results and the guidance reset (2 March 2026)

FY25 revenue was £223.9m. Growth was 24.2% at constant currency and 22.2% reported, against consensus of £222.4m. H2 revenue was £118.3m, about 1% above consensus. In H2:

- Clinical grew 80%.
- BioPharma grew 52%.
- Applied Industrial grew 8%.

By region:

| Region | FY25 revenue | Growth | Main drivers |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMEAI | £100.4m | +26.1% | UK and Europe |
| AMR | £74.9m | +18.7% | US Clinical demand |
| APAC | £48.6m | +20.2% | Singapore population genomics programme, Japan, China (+15.2%) |

**Gross margin.** Reported gross margin missed: 58.6% for the year against consensus of 59.1% (Barclays also cites 59.6%), and 59.1% in H2. Adjusted for the ElysION write-down, H2 margin was 60.6% and full-year margin 59.4% (consensus 59%) [JPM; DB].

**Losses and costs.** The Adjusted EBITDA loss was £86.7m, better than the £90.2m consensus. The H2 loss was £38.4m, 9% better than consensus. H2 adjusted operating costs rose 1% to £137m, with R&D down 9% to £45m and SG&A (selling, general and administrative costs) up 6%. Year-end cash was £302.8m against £294.5m consensus.

**New guidance replaced the mid-term targets:**

- **FY26 revenue growth:** 21–25% at constant currency, against 27.5% consensus. J.P. Morgan applied a 2% currency headwind and got about £270m, 5% below consensus.
- **FY26 gross margin:** about 62%, against 60.9% consensus. Drivers are flow-cell recycling (reusing the consumable chip) and the pricing model.
- **FY26 operating costs:** growth of 0–5%.
- **FY26 Adjusted EBITDA loss:** £45–55m.
- **FY27:** growth "broadly similar" to FY26, replacing the old >30% CAGR. Barclays called this effectively removing the mid-term growth guide.
- **Unchanged:** EBITDA breakeven in 2027 and positive cash flow in 2028.

J.P. Morgan calculated that the guidance implied cutting 2027 consensus revenue by about 11% at the midpoint (£325.3m against £365.2m) and gross profit by about 8%.

Shares fell 12% on the day, against a 1% fall in the European sector index [Barclays, 2026-03-02]. They had already lost about 22% in the prior month. J.P. Morgan therefore called the cut "at least somewhat anticipated" and the breakeven reiteration "reassuring."

**Management commentary after the results** [Barclays, 2026-03-02]:

- The low end of guidance reflects a subdued US, the P2 Solo discontinuation and China. The top end needs more customers to convert to P2i.
- Recent Middle East developments are excluded.
- 2027 growth need only match 2026 to reach breakeven.
- A 62% margin in 2026 brings the old margin target forward a year. About 64% is achievable in 2027.
- PromethION flow-cell recycling gains were offset in 2025 by lower MinION recycling.
- The EBITDA loss improved 26% in FY25 and 32% in H2. Currency has been a £9m headwind since 2024.
- ONT restructured twice in 2025, in January and November.
- Reported inventory of £80m is net of provisions and can be cut further.
- Revenue is phased about 45:55 between H1 and H2.
- One mRNA contract is signed and management hopes to sign another in 2026. A large drug maker uses ONT for sterility testing.

### March conferences and the "clearing event" thesis

At the Barclays Global Healthcare Conference, Keher and Head of Investor Relations Charlotte Parry gave more detail [Barclays, 2026-03-11].

**Purchasing model.** The move to outright device sales added 350bps to gross margin in H1 2025 and 400bps for the full year (100 basis points = 1 percentage point). The revenue effect was similar in size. The 2026 margin benefit should be 100–200bps.

**Contracts:**

- In EMEAI, about $10m of finishing contracts nets against about $10m of UK Biobank revenue in 2026 (the full contract is in the low-teens of millions).
- In APAC, the end of Singapore's PRECISE contract is a $3m headwind in H1 2026. The full contract was $6m, split roughly evenly between H2 2024 and H1 2025.

**China.** Export restrictions created a low-single-digit-million-dollar backlog of flow cells. Management said the situation is "getting worse not better" and called China APAC's "wild card" for 2026.

**Other points:**

- The ViruSure biopharma deal is financially small.
- Drug makers and CDMOs (contract manufacturers) are not working together: pharma wants to bring more testing in-house, while CDMOs want to sell the capability to everyone.
- Methylation can predict transplant-organ failure and show whether cancer is returning.

On 25 March Barclays called the guidance cut a "clearing event" and transferred coverage to Jonathon Unwin [Barclays, 2026-03-25]. It explained the 2025 shortfall against the original >30% plan as follows:

- Pricing and purchasing-model changes added about 4 points, so underlying growth was about 20%.
- NIH cost 3 points. NIH-funded revenue grew 3% against the 20% planned.
- China export restrictions cost 1.5 points, linked to NVIDIA chip restrictions and customer restriction lists.
- That leaves a net 5.5-point gap. Management attributes it partly to a slower market and partly to internal execution: GridION Q for Biopharma and ElysION underperformed, and China could have been run better.

Management also said:

- Better PromethION flow-cell yield and recycling could lift PromethION gross margin by 10 points, implying about 65% for the group exiting 2027.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin could eventually reach the mid-20s.
- Because ONT has no US manufacturing, all US-dollar headwinds fall straight to profit.
- Inventory, built up under COVID-era supply agreements, should fall to £80–90m by 2027.
- Capitalised R&D is amortised over three years.
- The amPORE TB test, developed with bioMérieux and launched for research use only in November 2025, will not be a big revenue line. It does prove ONT can build a regulated test.

**BioPharma detail:**

- Contracts are each worth single-digit millions of pounds.
- They cover mRNA vaccine trials of about 1,000 patients.
- If a vaccine is approved with ONT written into its regulatory filing, revenue could be locked in for about 20 years.
- Transformational growth is now more likely in 2028 than 2027.
- BioPharma could still reach 20% of revenue by 2027, but Clinical has grown faster than expected.

### Roche Diagnostics Day and London Calling (May 2026)

**Roche Diagnostics Day (12 May).** Roche set a "summer 2026" launch for Axelios and called a successful first year 100 placements. J.P. Morgan compared this with earlier first-year launches: PacBio Revio placed 173 in nine months of 2023, Element's Aviti 112 and Illumina's NovaSeq X 352. It concluded that Axelios's effect on ONT's 2027 growth is "likely to be limited," even allowing for conservatism. Roche still targets "significant market share" and blockbuster sales over time, and cited positive feedback from "a few hundred customers." J.P. Morgan noted ONT's 99.8% accuracy using its Dorado v5 basecaller [JPM, 2026-05-13].

**London Calling (21 May).** At ONT's annual event, now under van Parys with CFO Keher and Chief Scientific Officer Lakmal Jayasinghe, the company announced:

- **PromethION Plus** from July 2026. It removes the wash-and-reload step and raises yield about 20%, from 149GB to 177GB per run.
- An upgraded **GridION Q-line** from June. It adds the R10.4.1 flow cell, rapid and rapid-PCR barcoding kits, an RNA flow cell and direct-RNA kit RNA004.
- **mRNA QC with Lonza.** Available now: sequence confirmation, N1mpU modified-base reads and poly(A) tail length. In development: 5′ cap and double-stranded RNA detection.
- **Viral contaminant (AVA) testing** in development, aimed at replacing several tests with one platform.
- The **HAC v6.0 basecaller** (the software that turns electrical signal into DNA letters). Accuracy rose to Q23.5 from Q22.6 (about 99.4%), with better insertion/deletion, variant and methylation calling. ONT says the slower "super accuracy" model is no longer needed. The software is on GitHub.
- **Peptide sequencing progress.** Proteins are cut into peptides attached to DNA handles, and the method detects modifications such as phosphorylation.
- **New executives:**
  - Davide Manissero becomes the first Chief Medical Officer on 3 August. He is currently CMO at Radiometer and was previously at QIAGEN, the ECDC and the WHO.
  - Andrew Watson becomes Chief Information Officer on 15 June. He joins from Mimecast, and previously worked at Healx and Dyson.
  - These follow Chief People Officer Tina St Leger.
  - Responsibility for sales and marketing moves from Keher to van Parys on 1 July.

ONT also argued that AI depends on data quality, which leaves it "uniquely positioned" to benefit [JPM, 2026-05-22].

### Post-results management meetings (September 2026)

**Barclays meeting, 8 September** [Barclays, 2026-09-08]:

- **Margins:** management said about 200bps of gross-margin expansion in 2027, not 400bps, is enough for breakeven. Clinical is "a volume game with competitive pricing," while BioPharma margins are far higher.
- **mRNA QC:** ONT's moat is reading RNA directly, which shows modifications, degradation and purity that copy-based methods lose. Each batch equals one patient. The list price is $2,000 per test.
- **Prioritisation:** management narrowed 47 applications to 18. Two are Research, eight BioPharma (five QC, three development) and eight Clinical.
- **Costs:** van Parys sees no need for big cost increases. R&D will focus on product enhancement rather than new platforms, and the generalist, academia-focused sales force needs only refinement.
- **Clinical partners:** few global diagnostics firms can license ONT's technology. Infectious-disease partners bioMérieux and Cepheid remain key because those tests need CE-marked IVD approval.

**Barclays investor trip, 28 September** [Barclays, 2026-09-28]:

- **Applications:** the 18 applications are 40–45% of revenue. The remaining 55–60% should grow at mid-single digits. Management calls the $700m target conservative.
- **mRNA pricing and demand:** realised prices are about $1,000 against a $2,000–2,500 list. ONT has declined 10-year fixed-price contracts. "Essentially all major players" in mRNA vaccines are evaluating or using ONT.
- **mRNA workflow:** ONT can replace Sanger sequencing, HPLC and mass spectrometry and cut QC timelines by 3–4 weeks. It is already written into BLA filings (regulatory applications for biologic drugs) for some critical quality attributes (CQAs). CQA1 is available now, CQA2 is due January 2027 and CQA3 in 2027. The initial addressable cohort is about 25,000 melanoma patients, with about 100,000 across the wider set, and 3–4 CQAs per patient.
- **China:** expected to keep declining, with no growth in the long-term plan.
- **Partners:** the Cepheid product is on track for 2027.
- **AI demand:** ONT has had enquiries about datasets approaching one million genomes, and UK Biobank data releases are due later in 2026.
- **R&D and M&A:** R&D stays flat in sterling. M&A means bolt-ons in bioinformatics or sample prep.
- **Next update:** a fuller strategy update is due in February 2027.

Deutsche Bank's 21 September review added:

- Consumables gross margin is about 75% (above 80% possible), and devices and services about 34% (heading to about 40%).
- Net cash should stay above about £100m through breakeven.
- BioPharma and Clinical could be about 65% of 2030 revenue.
- About 20 biopharma customers are evaluating ONT.
- ONT papers reached almost 1,900 in 2025, twice PacBio's and more than 30% of Illumina's (up from 2% in 2017).

Deutsche Bank warned that strong device growth came alongside only 2.7% consumables growth (period not stated, presumably H1 2026). It concluded: "The central risk is not whether ONT's technology is differentiated, but whether the company can translate that differentiation into scaled, recurring revenue" [DB, 2026-09-21].

### Valuation logic

All three brokers value ONT on EV/Sales (enterprise value divided by revenue), because it is not yet profitable. All three argue ONT grows fastest yet trades cheaper than Illumina and PacBio.

**J.P. Morgan:**

- Uses 5.5x EV/Sales. The 220p target includes 33p a share for royalties, which it expects to last more than 15 years and to make up 93% of the deal's value.
- Its growth forecasts kept falling: a 31% ONT sales CAGR for 2025–28 (December 2025), then 23% (March 2026), then 20% for 2025–30 (August 2026). It forecast 8% for Illumina and 12% for PacBio over the same period.
- In August ONT traded at 4.5x 2027 sales, against about 7x for Illumina and 5x for PacBio.

**Barclays:**

- Moved from a 20% premium to peers (5.7x), to 35% (5.5x), to no premium (5.2x in July, then 5.8x in August as peers re-rated).
- Its reasoning: "successive guidance cuts mean we risk adjust our conviction."
- Scenario values are 220p upside and 85p downside, based on 2030 sales of £1.1bn and £0.3bn against a base case of about £0.7bn.
- It argues that "simply achieving targets should be enough to … close the discount to peers."

**Deutsche Bank:**

- Blends a DCF (a valuation from projected cash flows, using a 9% discount rate and 3.5% long-term growth), which gives 205p, with 6.3x EV/Sales (weighted 60:40 to PacBio and Illumina), which gives 215p.
- In September ONT traded at 5.6x 2026 sales, against Illumina at 7.9x, PacBio at 5.3x and a broader tools group at 5.2x.

The main remaining disagreement is when ONT breaks even. Management targets 2027.

**Barclays and Deutsche Bank expect breakeven a year or more late:**

- Deutsche Bank forecasts a £9m loss in 2027, which it calls "broadly consistent with breakeven," and £20m profit in 2028. In July it had expected breakeven only in FY28/29.

### Risks the brokers cite

- **J.P. Morgan:** Illumina's scale and pricing; PacBio and Roche SBX (though SBX is "just short read and doesn't capture methylation"); patent expiry; and failure to improve margins.
- **Barclays:** poor execution, further guidance cuts, and Biopharma contracts not materialising.
- **Deutsche Bank:** converting device placements into consumables use; competition and pricing; China, the Middle East and US research funding; slow clinical evidence and reimbursement; manufacturing quality; and dependence on partners' sales for royalties.
