# Key takeaways: Sell-side and independent research on Illumina

This section follows how professional analysts viewed Illumina from November 2025 to September 2026. Their ratings are Buy, Hold or Sell calls, and their price targets are the share price they expect in about 12 months. The sources are five brokerage firms (J.P. Morgan, UBS, Barclays, Ameriprise and Freedom Broker) and two independent sources that do not sell Illumina's shares (Bloomberg Intelligence and a Seeking Alpha contributor). The main story is a stock that the Street underestimated. Over the period, Illumina's results kept beating forecasts, the share price roughly doubled, and most analysts raised their targets to keep up. The central debate never went away, though: can growth in clinical testing outrun price pressure from new competitors, above all Roche?

## The starting point: margins fixed, growth in doubt

By early 2026 every source agreed on one thing. Management's "self-help" had restored profitability. Self-help means cost cuts and other actions within the company's control, as opposed to waiting for demand to recover. The adjusted operating margin (operating profit as a share of revenue, excluding one-off items) reached 23.7% in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 400 basis points from a year earlier and above the Street's 22.5% [JPM, 2026-02-06]. A basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point. Bloomberg Intelligence (BI) said growing earnings on flat revenue "creates a higher floor for the stock" [BI, 2026-01-13].

Growth was the open question. Illumina guided to organic growth of only 1–3% for 2026. Organic growth strips out acquisitions and currency effects. UBS said this "compares unfavorably" with peers [UBS, 2026-02-05]. The bright spot was clinical consumables, the reagent kits that hospital and diagnostic labs must buy for every sequencing run. They grew 20% a year outside China in the fourth quarter of 2025. Research and academic customers stayed weak, squeezed by US funding cuts and by price cuts that came with the move to the new NovaSeq X sequencer. China, restricted by Beijing's "unreliable entity" list, had shrunk to about half its former size [BI, 2025-10-31].

In February 2026 the analysts split three ways:

- **Buy:** Ameriprise and Freedom Broker.
- **Neutral:** J.P. Morgan and UBS.
- **Underweight:** Barclays, meaning it expected the stock to trail its peers.

J.P. Morgan cut its target to $120, citing "competitive concerns and pressured pricing dynamics." Barclays held at $110 and warned that a guide relying on second-half growth was arriving just as competitors launched.

## The competitive shock that turned out milder than feared

Two rival launches dominated February. On 19 February, Element Biosciences unveiled its VITARI sequencer. UBS said its consumables cost roughly one-tenth of a NovaSeq X Plus run, though its output per run is about one-fifth [UBS, 2026-02-19]. Then on 26 February, Roche priced its Axelios 1 at $150 per genome. J.P. Morgan put NovaSeq X at about $200 per genome, so Roche's price was roughly 25% lower [JPM, 2026-02-26].

The sources read the Roche news differently, and that difference has run through the whole period:

- **UBS** called it "relatively benign." It argued that large Illumina customers already pay similar prices, and that Illumina's own roadmap implies about $150 per genome.
- **UBS's experts** pointed to real obstacles to switching: complicated sample preparation, immature software, and clinical doubts about reusing the sensor module 20 times.
- **UBS's estimate of exposure:** it flagged "counting" applications such as RNA sequencing as the most vulnerable. It put them at roughly 16% of Illumina's consumables sales [UBS, 2026-02-26].
- **Barclays** saw a "well capitalized competitor" that could force "another round of pricing headwinds in 2027 and beyond" [Barclays, 2026-02-05].

The stock fell about 10% in the two weeks before Roche's pricing, to $120.09 on 18 February, then recovered once the price matched expectations.

## What changed: two strong quarters and a re-rating

The turn came with results. The table below compares each quarter with what the Street expected and with the guidance in force beforehand. Both quarters beat on revenue and earnings per share (EPS), and both led to higher full-year guidance.

| | Q1 2026 (reported 30 Apr) | Q2 2026 (reported 30 Jul) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue vs consensus | $1.091B vs ~$1.07B | $1.159B vs $1.13B |
| Organic growth vs guidance | +1.2% vs −1% to +1% | +6.5% vs +3–5% |
| Adjusted EPS vs consensus | $1.15 vs ~$1.05 | $1.31 vs $1.23 |
| NovaSeq X placements | >80 vs 50–60 quarterly framework | >95 |
| FY2026 revenue guidance | Raised to $4.52–4.62B (from $4.50–4.60B) | Raised to $4.60–4.64B |
| FY2026 EPS guidance | Raised to $5.15–5.30 (from $5.05–5.20) | Raised to $5.30–5.40 |

The biggest surprise was instrument demand. Most placements went to clinical labs, and many added capacity rather than replacing older machines. Freedom said demand was moving "from stabilization toward growth" [Freedom, 2026-05-01].

J.P. Morgan made the key call. It upgraded to Overweight on 10 June with a $185 target, arguing that clinical customers are "stickier-than-appreciated." The firm based this on a survey of 62 customers. Many respondents had not heard of Axelios, few planned to buy one within a year, and only about 11% of potential buyers would retire an Illumina instrument [JPM, 2026-06-10]. J.P. Morgan raised its target to $220 in July and $230 after the second quarter.

The share price did most of the work in moving the rest of the Street. It went from $126.74 at the end of April to $205.09 on 30 July and about $278 by early October. The FactSet consensus target rose from $136.11 to $175.72 between 1 May and 31 July, yet still sat about 17% below the share price [Ameriprise]. The Bloomberg count of ratings shifted from 10 Buy, 11 Hold and 3 Sell in February to 13 Buy, 6 Hold and 3 Sell by late July.

## Where the sources agree

Nearly every source now accepts four points:

- **Clinical sequencing is a durable growth engine.** BI noted that clinical work's share of consumables rose 10 points in two years to about 65%, which "adds ballast" to revenue [BI, 2026-07-31].
- **The NovaSeq X transition is close to complete.** It carried about 92% of research volume and 78% of clinical volume by the second quarter. That should end the price erosion the transition caused.
- **Management is executing well.** Even Barclays conceded management is "doing a great job" [Barclays, 2026-07-31].
- **Roche's threat is gradual, not immediate.** BI said Roche "could still chip away where workflow or economic advantages are clear," but disruption "is unlikely to be immediate" [BI, 2026-06-26].

## Where they still disagree

The disagreement has shifted from the business to the price. By midsummer, few sources doubted the company's operating momentum. The question became how much of it the stock already reflected.

- **The bulls (J.P. Morgan, Ameriprise).** J.P. Morgan calls high-single-digit revenue growth and mid-to-high-teens EPS growth "highly achievable" [JPM, 2026-07-31]. Ameriprise kept its Buy but said the case now "increasingly depends on" new instruments converting into consumables sales. New systems typically take six to nine months to reach normal usage.
- **The valuation skeptics (Freedom, Seeking Alpha).** Freedom downgraded to Hold at $185 on 5 August, saying "much of the upside is now priced in" and that guidance was no longer conservative. Stephen Ayers of Seeking Alpha set a fair value of $188. They argued that most of the second-quarter margin gain came from government incentives and the absence of restructuring charges, not from operations [Seeking Alpha, 2026-09-08].
- **The bear (Barclays).** Barclays raised its target three times, from $110 to $160, but kept its Underweight. It sees Illumina as a mid-single-digit grower that will need "continued price concessions, bundling, and favorable terms." It "cannot justify" a price-to-earnings multiple of about 35x, which it called the highest in its life-science tools coverage.

## Warning signs worth tracking

The sources flag several weak spots, mostly as opinions rather than settled facts:

- **Clinical growth slowed.** Clinical consumables growth outside China eased to 15% in the second quarter from 20%.
- **Cash flow missed.** Free cash flow (cash left after capital spending) was $162 million in the second quarter, against a Street estimate of $321 million. Management blamed the timing of tax payments and inventory built up for scarce components.
- **Price per unit of data may be falling.** Barclays suspected Illumina was quietly discounting, because NovaSeq X's share of data output rose faster than its share of consumables revenue. BI described Illumina as "in the early stages of trading price for volume" [BI, 2026-05-01].
- **Placements may have been pulled forward.** Ayers asked whether strong placements reflected customers buying ahead of Axelios. Management insisted "this is a very healthy market" [Q2 2026 call].
- **New-product growth is unverified.** Barclays and Ayers both doubted management's claim that multiomics will add 1–2 points of growth in 2027. Multiomics means measuring DNA, RNA and proteins together, and here includes SomaLogic and the BioInsight data unit.

## Data quality caveats

A few source errors are worth knowing before reading the detail. BI's figure of a 20% fall in first-quarter service revenue conflicts with every other source. Barclays' July note calls Axelios an Element product, although it belongs to Roche. Firms disagree on the 2025 operating margin, with 23.1% from BI and UBS and 23.6% from Barclays. J.P. Morgan's survey materials carry conflicting dates. Finally, coverage ends in early September 2026, before the stock's further climb to about $278. Every target cited here except J.P. Morgan's now sits well below that price.
