## What "consensus" means and what this section draws on

**Consensus** is the average of the forecasts published by **sell-side analysts**, the bank and brokerage researchers who cover a stock and publish estimates for clients. Investors use consensus as the market's baseline expectation. When a company reports a quarter, it "beats" or "misses" depending on how its results compare with consensus, and the share price often reacts to that gap more than to the raw results.

This section rests on three Bloomberg Professional "Company Financials" printouts for Illumina (ticker ILMN US Equity). All three were generated on 2026-10-10 with the estimate source set to "Consensus" and no extra analytics applied:

- **Annual sheet** [BBG Annuals, 2026-10-10]. Reported actuals for fiscal 2023 to 2025 and consensus estimates for fiscal 2026 to 2032.
- **Quarterly sheet 1** [BBG Quarterlies, 2026-10-10]. Six reported quarters (2025Q1 to 2026Q2) and four estimated quarters (2026Q3 to 2027Q2).
- **Quarterly sheet 2** [BBG Quarterlies-2, 2026-10-10]. Ten estimated quarters, 2026Q3 to 2028Q4. Where the two quarterly sheets overlap (2026Q3 to 2027Q2), their figures are identical.

All figures are in millions of US dollars unless stated otherwise. Per-share figures are in dollars, and share counts are in millions. None of the sheets names an analyst or bank, gives ratings or price targets, explains why estimates changed, or says how many analysts contributed to each number.

Thin coverage matters for interpretation. Each line item is an average of whichever analysts chose to model it. As a result, lines often do not tie to each other, and far-out years (especially 2031–2032) or obscure lines may reflect only one or two forecasters. Growth rates, sums and comparisons labelled "our arithmetic" below are calculations from the sheets' own numbers, not statements by Bloomberg or by any analyst.

### Fiscal calendar

Illumina's fiscal year is a 52- or 53-week year that ends near, but not exactly on, December 31.

- **Reported years:** FY2023 ended 12/31/2023, FY2024 ended 12/29/2024, and FY2025 ended 12/28/2025.
- **Estimated years:** FY2026 ends 01/03/2027, FY2027 ends 01/02/2028, FY2028 ends 12/31/2028, FY2029 ends 12/30/2029, FY2030 ends 12/29/2030, FY2031 ends 12/28/2031, and FY2032 ends 01/02/2033.
- **Quarter ends:** 03/30/2025, 06/29/2025, 09/28/2025, 12/28/2025, 03/29/2026 and 06/28/2026 are reported. 10/04/2026 (2026Q3), 04/04/2027, 07/04/2027, 10/03/2027, 04/02/2028, 07/02/2028 and 10/01/2028 are estimated.

By date, 2026Q3 (06/28 to 10/04) spans 14 weeks, and 2026Q4 (10/04 to 01/03) spans 13. That makes FY2026 a 53-week year. The extra week may partly explain why the second-half 2026 estimates look strong. This is an inference from the dates only; the sheet gives no reason.

### Adjusted versus GAAP

**GAAP** results follow standard US accounting rules. **Adjusted** (non-GAAP) results exclude certain items, chiefly two:

- **Stock-based compensation (SBC):** the non-cash cost of paying employees in shares.
- **Amortization of acquired intangibles:** the gradual write-off of the purchase value of acquired technology and customer relationships.

Analysts and Illumina's own guidance focus on adjusted figures, so "consensus EPS" normally means adjusted **EPS** (earnings per share, profit divided by the number of shares).

### Revenue lines

Illumina's revenue lines appear throughout this section:

- **Consumables** are the reagent kits, flow cells and chips customers must buy for every sequencing or microarray run. They are recurring revenue and about three-quarters of sales.
- **Instruments** are the machines themselves, sold once. They are lumpy from quarter to quarter.
- **Service & Other** covers service contracts, genotyping services and similar revenue.
- **Sequencing** reads the DNA letters directly. **Microarray** is an older, cheaper chip-based technology that tests for known genetic variants.
- **GRAIL** was Illumina's cancer-detection blood-test business. It appears in the data only for 2023 and 2024.

## Annual consensus: the big picture

### Headline expectations

Consensus expects Illumina to return to growth after two years of shrinking sales, and to keep widening its margins.

- **Revenue.** Revenue fell 2.9% in 2024 (from $4,504M to $4,372M) and 0.7% in 2025 (to $4,343M). Consensus has it rising 6.5% to $4,624.8M in 2026, then growing every year to $7,370.5M in 2032 [BBG Annuals, 2026-10-10].
- **Operating margin.** Adjusted **operating margin** (operating profit as a share of revenue) jumped from 5.5% in 2023 to 13.3% in 2024 and 23.1% in 2025. Consensus expects 23.5% in 2026, rising to 30.7% in 2032.
- **EPS.** Adjusted EPS climbed from $0.86 in 2023 to $2.45 in 2024 and $4.84 in 2025. Consensus has $5.36 for 2026 and $12.30 for 2032.

Three points stand out.

**Growth is steady, then accelerates far out.** Consensus revenue growth sits in the 6–8% range through 2030. It then jumps to about 10% in 2031 and 2032, faster than any earlier forecast year. That late acceleration probably reflects a handful of long-range models rather than a broad view.

**Earnings grow faster than sales.** Adjusted EPS is expected to compound at roughly 11–19% a year, against 6–10% revenue growth. The gap comes from **operating leverage**: costs grow more slowly than revenue, so each extra dollar of sales adds proportionally more profit.

**The big margin step has already happened.** Adjusted operating margin went from 5.5% to 23.1% in two years (2023 to 2025). Consensus models only gradual gains from here, adding about 1–1.5 percentage points a year.

**Debt.** The reported quarters show two debt events. In 2025Q3, both of Illumina's two $500M notes were classified as current, so current debt was $998M and long-term debt $994M. ## How the latest actuals and estimates fit together

The quarterly and annual consensus are broadly consistent for 2026 but diverge for later years.

**2026 ties out.** First-half 2026 revenue was $2,250M, 7.1% above first-half 2025's $2,100M. Adding the consensus second half ($1,154M + $1,220M = $2,374M) gives $4,624M, matching the annual consensus of $4,624.8M. Adjusted EPS works the same way: $2.46 reported plus $2.90 expected equals the annual $5.36. Adjusted operating income also matches: $499M plus $589M is about $1,088M.

**Consensus expects slower growth in the second half.** Second-half 2026 is expected to grow about 5.8% over second-half 2025's $2,243M. That is slower than the first half's 7.1%, despite the extra week in 2026Q3 (our arithmetic).

**Later years diverge** (our arithmetic):

- Summing the 2027 quarters gives revenue of $4,899M and EPS of $6.08, slightly below the annual consensus of $4,918M and $6.11.
- The 2028 quarters sum to $5,296M and $7.34, above the annual $5,263M and $7.01.
- Buybacks don't reconcile. 2026 first-half buybacks ($370M) plus consensus second-half ($181M) give $551M, against the annual estimate of $420M. 2027 quarterly buybacks sum to $251M against $313M annually, and 2028 to $334M against $501M.
- 2026 FCF of $413M reported plus $619M expected is about $1,032M, against the annual $997M.

These gaps reflect different analysts contributing to the quarterly and annual series.

### Quarterly data-quality warnings

**Reported quarters:**

- 2026Q1 adjusted pre-tax income is printed as $131M, but operating income plus other income implies about $254M. It is likely a data error, since $131M equals 2025Q1 GAAP net income.
- 2026Q2 adjusted net income of $200.43M does not match pre-tax income of $260M less $52M of tax, which gives $208M.
- 2025Q1 cost of revenue ($358M) exceeds products plus services ($341M).
- 2025 depreciation figures are inconsistent across quarters.
- Return on equity and return on assets for 2025Q1 are negative despite profits.
- SBC and "other assets" are printed unscaled (for example, 7300 for about $73M).

**Estimate quarters:**

- Goodwill is printed as "128400" throughout, most likely $1,284M.
- Cash-plus-short-term-investments (for example, $846.7M in 2026Q3) is lower than cash alone, which is impossible.
- Cash flow statement end-of-period cash does not match balance sheet cash or the next quarter's opening cash.
