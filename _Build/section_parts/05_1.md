## Q1 2025: Guidance Cut on China, US Research Funding and Tariffs (call held 2025-05-08)

### The headline: a solid quarter, but a lower year

Illumina makes DNA sequencing instruments and sells the consumables (the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every sequencing run) that run on them. On its first-quarter 2025 call, management reported revenue and earnings per share (EPS, profit divided by the number of shares) at the upper end of its own guidance range. In the same call it cut its full-year 2025 outlook because of three outside pressures: China's restriction on Illumina's instrument exports, uncertainty over US research funding, and new US import tariffs. [Q1 2025 call, Thaysen]

CEO Jacob Thaysen called these "transitory challenges that will not define Illumina's long-term success." Thaysen reiterated the long-term goal set at the 2024 investor day: high single-digit revenue growth and 500 basis points (5 percentage points; one basis point is 0.01 percentage point) of operating margin expansion by 2027. That goal was now stated excluding Greater China. Thaysen described Illumina as "a resilient franchise with multiple drivers of near and long-term growth" and said the clinical move to the NovaSeq X "reaffirm[s] both the value we provide and the resilience of the clinical markets." [Q1 2025 call, Thaysen]

Interim Head of Investor Relations Brian Blanchett noted at the start that all figures refer to "core Illumina," meaning the company after it divested the cancer-screening business GRAIL. Growth rates in the prepared remarks are in constant currency (with exchange-rate effects removed) unless noted. [Q1 2025 call, Blanchett]

### Results against expectations

Total revenue was $1.04 billion. Management said this was down 1.4% as reported. Foreign exchange cost 1.2 points, so revenue was roughly flat in constant currency and at the top end of guidance. Revenue excluding China rose slightly in constant currency. Non-GAAP EPS was $0.97, toward the top of the guidance range. Non-GAAP means management's adjusted figures, which exclude items such as acquisition costs and stock compensation. CFO Ankur Dhingra called it a quarter of "excellent execution." [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]

Seeking Alpha's summary header, which is third-party data and not management's, said EPS beat consensus by $0.03 and revenue beat by $4.10 million. That header gave the revenue decline as 3.25% year over year, a figure that does not match management's 1.4% and whose basis is not explained.

Dhingra gave three reasons gross margin (revenue minus the direct cost of goods, as a share of revenue) fell short of plan. First, the mix shifted toward instruments, which earn lower margins than consumables. Second, Illumina was rolling out a software upgrade that improves the performance of its high-throughput instruments. As part of that rollout it pulled forward routine servicing, which cost more than assumed; most upgrades were due to be done by Q2. Third, manufacturing cost actions continued to make good progress. [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]

### Clinical versus research: the first signs of a split

In the second half of the quarter, uncertainty grew around funding from the National Institutes of Health (NIH, the main US federal funder of biomedical research). Research and academic customers then became "slightly more conservative" in buying consumables. Illumina estimated this cut consumables growth by about 1 point year over year. Actual sequencing activity on connected instruments stayed strong. [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]

Ordering was a bright spot. Q1 is seasonally the period when customers sign long-range purchase commitments, which build backlog. Illumina reports this backlog as "performance obligations" in its quarterly 10-Q filing. Dhingra said this year's ordering was stronger than in the previous couple of years. In Q&A Dhingra added that performance obligations were up double digits year over year. [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]

### The NovaSeq X transition

The NovaSeq X is Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer and is replacing the older NovaSeq 6000 (the "6K"). The X sequences far more cheaply per gigabase (a billion DNA letters, the standard unit of sequencing output). In the short term, revenue falls as a customer converts. Illumina expects the lower price to unlock enough extra volume to grow revenue later, a dynamic it calls "elasticity."

Illumina placed more than 60 NovaSeq X systems in Q1, after more than 90 in Q4 2024. Thaysen said this beat expectations. About 60% of Q1 placements went to clinical customers. Of high-throughput output, about 68% of gigabases and about 43% of high-throughput consumables revenue were on the X. More than 80% of research gigabases and over 50% of clinical volumes had moved to the X. [Q1 2025 call, Thaysen and Dhingra]

Dhingra reaffirmed the framework for the second half of 2025: about 50% of high-throughput revenue and about 75% of gigabases on the X. Thaysen said X consumables, and high-throughput consumables overall, rose sequentially, which shows the driver "demonstrate[s] elasticity." Dhingra explained the mechanics. As the older 6K consumables shrink as a share of the mix, the drag from the X's lower price fades, and more of each gigabase of growth turns into revenue growth. Total output on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30% year over year, with robust growth from both clinical and research. [Q1 2025 call, Thaysen and Dhingra]

### China, tariffs and the cost response

Thaysen described three pressures and Illumina's response to each. [Q1 2025 call, Thaysen]

- **China.** Illumina's ability to export instruments to China was restricted. It expected lower 2025 China revenue on minimal instrument placements. Until the situation is resolved it will guide Greater China separately from the rest of the world. It continues to talk with Chinese regulators about a "sustainable long-term presence."
- **US research funding.** Uncertainty was weighing on customers and delaying purchases. Illumina was offering flexible solutions to keep research projects going.
- **Tariffs.** The US had enacted a baseline 10% import tariff, with much higher rates for some countries. Illumina's plan was to offset part of the cost in 2025 through supply-chain changes, cost measures and pricing, and to mitigate more fully in 2026.

Dhingra estimated the gross 2025 tariff cost at about $85 million. The largest piece is goods shipped from Illumina's Singapore factory to the US. The rest is imported parts and subassemblies used in US manufacturing, and Illumina products sold into China. Because inventory takes time to flow through, Q2 would carry only part of a quarter's impact, and each later quarter about $30 million to $35 million. Guidance assumed no further tariffs, including counter-tariffs from the EU or others. Management expected to offset roughly half in 2025. [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra and Thaysen]

In March 2025 Illumina launched an additional global cost program worth $100 million to address China and the research environment. Thaysen said every action needed to capture the full 2025 savings was already in place. Dhingra said the program includes changes to stock-based compensation and is worth more than $225 million a year once fully annualized over four years. In Q&A Thaysen gave that annualized figure as "more than $200 million." Because of these actions, Dhingra said operating expenses would not show their usual rise after Q1 and would instead stay flat to slightly down for the rest of 2025. [Q1 2025 call]

On capital return, Illumina completed the purchase of about 1.73 million shares for $200 million in February, at an average of $115.74. It had $1.2 billion of buyback authorization left and planned to keep repurchasing, which Dhingra said would give a small EPS benefit within the year. [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]

### Full-year 2025 and Q2 guidance

Dhingra explained the sequence of revisions. In March, Illumina had held EPS guidance at about $4.50 while absorbing the loss of China revenue, tighter funding and the new cost actions. The May revision mainly added tariffs. The table below shows the new full-year outlook. [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]

| Item | Updated FY2025 guidance (May 2025) |
|---|---|
| Total revenue | $4.18–4.26 billion, a decline of 3% to 1% |
| Revenue excluding Greater China | Flat to +2% (about +1% at midpoint) |
| Greater China revenue | $165–185 million, cut $125 million at midpoint ($72M Q1, ~$60M Q2, ~$43M in H2 at midpoint) |
| Non-GAAP operating margin | About 21.5–22%, up about 45 basis points versus 2024 at midpoint, after a 125-basis-point tariff drag |
| Non-GAAP tax rate | About 22% (lowered) |
| Non-GAAP EPS | $4.20–4.30, versus about $4.50 in March; midpoint cut by $0.25, mainly because of tariffs |
| Consumables excluding China | Flat to +2%, led by clinical activity |
| Instruments excluding China | Roughly flat; steady X demand plus MiSeq i100 growth |

Dhingra itemized the rest-of-world revenue revision. Research weakness would reduce quarterly revenue by 2% to 4% over the next three quarters. Clinical customers, who had placed many instruments, would partly offset that with about 1% of extra quarterly growth. The net of research and clinical was about $60 million over the three quarters. Price increases would add revenue, mostly in the second half, because they apply only to new orders and so would do little in Q2. A more favorable exchange rate added about $25 million to reported revenue. Management called its China view "pragmatic" and said any positive resolution would be upside. [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]

Dhingra also split earnings by region. China would contribute about $0.35 of 2025 EPS, down from an estimated $0.76 in 2024. Rest-of-world EPS at the midpoint would be $3.90, about 15% growth on a comparable basis. [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]

For Q2 2025, Illumina guided to the following. [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]

- Revenue of $1.04–1.06 billion.
- China revenue of $55–65 million.
- Revenue outside China of $980 million to $1 billion, down 2% to 3%, mainly because of lower strategic partnership revenue and research weakness.
- Operating margin of about 21% and EPS of $1.00–1.04. Both include about $15 million of direct tariff cost.

The year was second-half weighted. That rested on consumables improving as the X transition matured and on cost and pricing actions building up. [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]

### Governance and innovation pipeline

Thaysen thanked former board chair Steve MacMillan and welcomed Scott Gottlieb as the new chair. Thaysen also welcomed Keith Meister to the board, citing Meister's investor experience in genomics and record of creating shareholder value. [Q1 2025 call, Thaysen]

Thaysen walked through a pipeline they described as "unmatched." [Q1 2025 call, Thaysen]

- **Spatial technology** (mapping where genes are active within a tissue sample). Announced in February, it offers a much larger capture area, higher resolution and greater sensitivity than existing tools, and can analyze millions of cells per experiment to find rare cell populations. It ties into Illumina Connected Multi-Omics, a new tertiary-analysis (data interpretation) product built on Partek visualization technology. Early-access customers praised that it needs no special equipment and simplifies analysis. Release was planned for 2026.
- **Perturb-Seq single-cell CRISPR.** CRISPR is a gene-editing tool, and this product lets researchers see how each genetic change affects individual cells at scale, for oncology, immunology and drug-target discovery. It captures both the CRISPR guide RNA and the cell's messenger RNA from the same cell, enabling genome-wide screens at "market leading costs." It builds on a technology acquired the prior year; the transcript does not legibly name it. Launch was expected later in 2025.
- **Proteomics** (measuring proteins), co-developed with Standard BioTools. It was in early access, with commercial launch targeted for the first half of 2025.
- **Constellation mapped reads and 5-base genome technologies.** Both were in early access, with full launches on track for 2026.

Thaysen concluded that "the core of Illumina is strong, and our growth projections ex-China [are] on track."

## Q2 2025: Beat, Raise and the SomaLogic Deal (call held 2025-07-31)

### The headline: profits well ahead, guidance up, instruments down

Three months later the tone improved. Revenue of about $1.06 billion was down about 3% year over year on both constant-currency and reported bases, at the high end of guidance. Management credited strength in high-throughput consumables. Non-GAAP operating margin was 23.8%, up 160 basis points. EPS was $1.19, up 9% year over year and "well above" the top of guidance. [Q2 2025 call, Dhingra]

Seeking Alpha's header said EPS beat consensus by $0.18 and revenue by $7.13 million. It gave a 4.77% year-over-year revenue decline, again different from management's figure. Thaysen said the results "reinforce our view that the underlying fundamentals remain robust." Illumina raised full-year guidance for revenue, margin and EPS, but cut its instrument outlook. It also announced it would buy SomaLogic. [Q2 2025 call]

### Results by line and region

The table below shows the Q2 breakdown, with management's comparisons.

| Line | Q2 2025 | Comparison [Q2 2025 call, Dhingra] |
|---|---|---|
| Greater China revenue | $63 million | $12 million below Q2 2024, slightly ahead of expectations |
| Revenue excluding China | n/a | Down about 2% (constant currency) |
| Sequencing consumables | $740 million | About flat year over year; up about 6% sequentially (reported) |
| Sequencing instruments | $96 million | Down about 18% year over year |
| Services and other | $136 million | Down about 5%, in line; core services and informatics grew high single digits excluding AGD timing |

Consumables did well. High-throughput consumables grew both sequentially and year over year, and NovaSeq X consumables revenue rose more than 10% sequentially. Clinical work was now roughly 60% of sequencing consumables. Clinical growth came from wider use of comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP, sequencing many cancer genes at once to choose a therapy) and from momentum in MRD. [Q2 2025 call, Thaysen and Dhingra]

Instruments were the weak spot. Dhingra tied the decline to tight budgets among high- and mid-throughput research customers. Pipelines held, but decision times lengthened. About 60% of X placements went to clinical customers. The MiSeq i100 Plus was doing well, and the base MiSeq i100 model would launch in August 2025. China instrument sales fell about 40% because of the export restrictions. [Q2 2025 call, Dhingra]

The services decline again reflected the prior year's partnership revenue from the AGD consortium (Alliance for Genomic Discovery, a large sequencing and data program). [Q2 2025 call, Dhingra]

### NovaSeq X progress and sequencing activity

Illumina placed more than 50 X systems in Q2. More than 80% of research volume was now on the X. Clinical was about 55% converted; clinical customers were validating and balancing spending between existing tests and new ones. Overall, about 69% of high-throughput gigabases and about 44% of high-throughput consumables revenue were on the X, up from 68% and 43% in Q1. Dhingra expected to reach roughly 50% of revenue and 75% of gigabases toward the end of 2025, driven by clinical. [Q2 2025 call, Thaysen and Dhingra]

Output on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments again grew more than 30%, led by clinical, with research more muted. Dhingra cautioned that output is a directional gauge of demand, not a predictor of near-term revenue. [Q2 2025 call, Dhingra]

### Margins, cash and buybacks

Gross margin was 69.4%. That was up 200 basis points from Q1 and stable year over year, helped by a higher consumables mix and operational initiatives, even though tariffs cost about 110 basis points net. [Q2 2025 call, Dhingra]

- Operating expenses were $484 million, down about 6% ($32 million). There was no seasonal step-up after Q1.
- Operating profit grew about 4% even though revenue fell.
- Other expense was $10 million, the tax rate was 22.2%, and diluted shares were about 157 million, 2 million fewer than in Q1.
- Operating cash flow was $234 million, capex $30 million and free cash flow $204 million.
- Illumina bought about 4.5 million shares for $380 million at about $85 each, leaving about $800 million of authorization, and planned to keep buying.
- Cash and short-term investments were about $1.16 billion, and gross leverage was about 1.7x.

### Clinical strength, research caution

Thaysen said clinical "has proven more resilient and in some areas, is exceeding expectations." Growth came from three areas. [Q2 2025 call, Thaysen]

- **Oncology:** wider CGP adoption and rising interest in sequencing-heavy MRD tests.
- **Genetic disease:** growing national genome programs, and broader use of whole-genome and whole-exome sequencing for rare disease. Whole-exome sequencing reads only the protein-coding portion of the genome.
- **Reproductive health:** rising sample volumes for NIPT (non-invasive prenatal testing from the mother's blood), especially in the US as labs finished validation and ramped up.

Over the long run, Thaysen expected next-generation sequencing (NGS, modern high-volume DNA sequencing) to become standard of care in therapy selection, early detection and monitoring, and then spread to other diseases. [Q2 2025 call, Thaysen]

US research, by contrast, stayed constrained. Some labs were delaying projects or hiring because they feared future grants would not come. Q2 trends matched late Q1. In China, exports remained restricted while Illumina continued to seek a "long-term sustainable presence." Dhingra said Chinese customers stayed engaged and consumables "held up well." [Q2 2025 call, Thaysen and Dhingra]

### Strategy: three pillars and the SomaLogic acquisition

Thaysen reaffirmed the long-term targets, now phrased as high-single-digit revenue growth and non-GAAP operating margin rising to 26% by 2027. The targets rest on three pillars:

1. Core sequencing, including the X transition.
2. A scalable entry into multiomics (measuring several kinds of molecules, such as DNA, RNA and proteins, rather than DNA alone).
3. Expanding services, data and software.

Thaysen said the company's vision was moving from a cost-per-gigabase model to "delivering the highest-quality biological insight at the lowest end-to-end cost." [Q2 2025 call, Thaysen]

In June 2025 Illumina had agreed to buy SomaLogic from Standard BioTools. The deal expands Illumina into affinity-based proteomics, which measures proteins using reagents that bind to them. Thaysen described this as "a small but fast-growing segment of the broader proteomics market." [Q2 2025 call, Thaysen]

- SomaLogic's SomaScan assay measures more than 9,500 human proteins from small samples. Its proprietary SOMAmer binding reagents offer "unmatched sensitivity, scalability and reproducibility" and broader coverage at lower time and cost than other methods.
- Illumina had held an exclusive commercial relationship with SomaLogic since 2021. Thaysen said that partnership deepened Illumina's conviction, and that Illumina's product development and global reach could scale the technology faster and more efficiently.
- The deal strengthens the case for the NovaSeq X and could extend to single-cell and spatial work. Illumina's portfolio now spans DNA, RNA, methylation (chemical tags on DNA that switch genes on or off) and proteins.
- Terms: $350 million in cash at closing plus up to $75 million in near-term performance milestones and royalties. Closing was expected in the first half of 2026, pending regulatory approval. [Q2 2025 call, Dhingra]

Thaysen also highlighted the MiSeq i100 Plus, a benchtop (lab-bench-sized) sequencer launched in late 2024. More than 500 had been placed, and customers were reordering within months. [Q2 2025 call, Thaysen]

- Customers called it a "game changer" for its fast turnaround, ease of use, and reagents that can be shipped and stored at room temperature.
- It supports 18 proven workflows, 9 of them fully integrated from library prep (preparing DNA for the sequencer) through analysis.
- It appeals to labs new to NGS and to emerging markets, reduces reliance on central labs and shortens time to diagnosis. Thaysen said it "sets a new standard for the future of Illumina innovation."

### Raised 2025 guidance and Q3 outlook

Despite saying they remained "cautious" on the second half, Thaysen and Dhingra raised the full-year numbers. The table compares the new guidance with May's. [Q2 2025 call, Dhingra]

| Item (FY2025) | New guidance (July) | Change from May |
|---|---|---|
| Greater China revenue | About $200 million | +$25 million at midpoint |
| Rest-of-world growth (constant currency) | 0% to +2% | Reiterated |
| Total growth (constant currency) | –2.5% to –0.5% | Raised |
| Reported revenue | $4.23–4.31 billion | Midpoint up about $50 million |
| Rest-of-world consumables | +1% to +3% (including pricing) | Up from flat to +2% |
| Rest-of-world instruments | –6% to –4% | Lowered from roughly flat |
| Non-GAAP operating margin | 22–22.5% | Up about 50 basis points |
| Tax rate | About 20% | Lowered |
| Diluted shares | About 157 million | Lowered on buybacks |
| Non-GAAP EPS | $4.45–4.55 | +$0.25 at midpoint |

Dhingra explained the moving parts.

- Of the $0.25 EPS increase, about $0.10 came from tax, about $0.10 from China, and the rest from currency and margin.
- China consumables purchases had been resilient and were expected to hold "at least in part through Q3."
- The instrument cut was "largely due to conservatism from our research customers that we saw towards the end of Q2." Weakness was in the mid- and high-throughput category, though X demand was still expected to rise slightly and i100 placements to grow.
- The lower tax rate came from legislation passed in early July 2025 that made US research spending tax-deductible. Because Illumina does much of its R&D in the US, this helps 2025 and beyond. It did not affect Q2, because it was enacted after quarter end.
- The margin raise reflected cost discipline, stronger China revenue and favorable currency.

For Q3, guidance called for growth outside China of 1% to 2%, China revenue of $35–45 million and total constant-currency revenue down 1.5% to 2.5%. The other Q3 targets were operating margin of about 22%, a tax rate of about 16%, about 155 million shares and EPS of $1.15–1.19. Revenue would rise in Q4. About half of that increase would come from normal year-end instrument seasonality and the rest from data services such as AGD and new products such as proteomics. [Q2 2025 call, Dhingra]

### Analyst Q&A

**Vijay Kumar (Evercore ISI)** asked for a bridge of the guidance change and about the size of the pipeline.

- Thaysen said early-access feedback on Constellation, 5-base and spatial was "very, very strong." Engaging customers early lets them plan publications and grant applications, which should add "growth starting from '26 and onwards."
- Dhingra said the guidance raise came from currency and China. Outside China, the instrument outlook was cut, mainly in research, while the consumables outlook rose.
- AGD business had been about $80–90 million in 2024, weighted to the first half, so H2 2025 comparisons are easier. Illumina expected some AGD and possibly new services in H2.

**Subbu Nambi (Guggenheim)** asked about competition and whether Illumina could match the fast turnaround of Roche's coming platform.

- Thaysen said Illumina spends more time on customers than on competitors and has the industry's largest R&D budget.

**Subbu Nambi (Guggenheim)** asked about NovaSeq 6000 pull-through (consumables use per installed machine) and regional outlook.

- Thaysen said many customers build new assays on the X and keep the 6000 for validated older ones, which supports 6000 usage, though there will be fewer 6000 users over time.
- Europe has done "tremendously well" and should keep growing; APAC and EMEA should "rebound somewhat."
- Dhingra said the research transition is substantially complete and clinical is in its latter stage, mostly done by volume by end-2026.

### Looking back on 2025

Over four calls the story inverted. In May, management was defending a guidance cut and asking investors to look past China and NIH. By February it was reporting 20% clinical growth outside China and its strongest quarter in years. Year-end EPS of $4.84 came in well above the $4.20–4.30 May guide and the $4.50 original guide.

- **Clinical** moved from "resilient" to the engine of growth. Clinical consumables outside China went from mid-single-digit growth in Q1 to double digits in Q3 and 20% in Q4.
- **Research** never recovered. It moved from an expected roughly 15% academic decline to "stabilizing, but still soft," and was guided to decline again in 2026.
- **China** shrank but repeatedly beat the cautious guide. The full-year figure was raised from $165–185 million to about $200 million and then about $220 million, with one OEM step forward but no resolution.
- **Pricing** shifted from price increases to offset tariffs, to "very stable," to openness to selective price cuts where elasticity justifies them.
- **Competition** was framed throughout as a "technology overview," a niche (NICU turnaround) or a single-dimension pitch. Management consistently said it saw no customer delays.
- **Multiomics** grew from a pipeline list into an acquisition (SomaLogic), launches (Protein Prep, 5-base) and a new data business (BioInsight). Management now expects multiomics and data to add 1–2 points of growth from 2027.
