This section follows Illumina's four earnings calls on fiscal 2025 results:

- Q1 on May 8, 2025
- Q2 on July 31, 2025
- Q3 on October 30, 2025
- Q4 on February 5, 2026

The main speakers were CEO Jacob Thaysen and CFO Ankur Dhingra. Brian Blanchett, interim head of investor relations (IR) and VP Finance and Treasurer, hosted the first two calls. Conor McNamara, VP of IR, hosted the last two.

Some conventions apply throughout. All figures are for "core Illumina," meaning the company without GRAIL, the cancer-screening business it divested in 2024. Most profit figures are non-GAAP (adjusted to exclude items such as acquisition costs and stock compensation). Growth rates are in constant currency (with exchange-rate effects removed) unless noted. "China" means Greater China, including Taiwan and Hong Kong. Comparisons with analyst consensus come from Seeking Alpha transcript headers, not from management.

The year's arc in brief:

- **May 2025:** Illumina cut guidance because of three outside shocks: Chinese export restrictions, US research-funding uncertainty and tariffs.
- **July 2025:** Guidance was raised, and Illumina bought a proteomics company.
- **October 2025:** Illumina returned to growth outside China and passed its NovaSeq X transition targets early.
- **February 2026:** Illumina reported 7% growth outside China in Q4, led by 20% clinical consumables growth. It guided 2026 conservatively.

Throughout the year, clinical demand strengthened, research stayed weak, China shrank but held up better than feared, and management kept downplaying competition.

## Q1 2025 (May 8, 2025): guidance cut on China, research funding and tariffs

### Results against guidance and consensus

Illumina makes DNA sequencing instruments. It also sells consumables (the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run), which are the larger and more profitable part of revenue.

**Revenue.** Q1 2025 revenue was $1.04 billion, down 1.4% as reported. That decline included a 1.2-point currency headwind, so revenue was roughly flat in constant currency and at the top of guidance. Outside China, revenue grew slightly. Seeking Alpha's header put the decline at 3.25% and said revenue beat consensus by $4.1 million. Its basis is unexplained and differs from management's figure.

**EPS.** Earnings per share (EPS) were $0.97, toward the top of guidance and $0.03 above consensus. Dhingra called the quarter "excellent execution" [Q1 2025 call, 2025-05-08].

The table below breaks Q1 into its product lines and profit items. It shows consumables edging up while instruments and services slipped. Gross margin rose but fell short of plan.

| Sequencing instruments | $109M | ~Flat; more NovaSeq X units than expected, plus the MiSeq i100 launch |
| Services and other | $142M | Down ~5%, as expected, because 2024 included partnership revenue from the AGD consortium; excluding it, core services and informatics grew mid-single digits |

Dhingra gave three reasons gross margin missed plan [Q1 2025 call]:

- A heavier mix of lower-margin instruments.
- A software upgrade rollout for high-throughput instruments. As part of the upgrade, Illumina pulled forward routine servicing, which cost more than assumed. Most upgrades would be finished by Q2.
- Manufacturing cost work, by contrast, was progressing well.

### Demand: clinical solid, research turning cautious

**Research.** In the second half of the quarter, uncertainty over National Institutes of Health (NIH) funding made research and academic customers "slightly more conservative" in buying consumables. Dhingra estimated this cut consumables growth by about 1 point. Sequencing activity on connected instruments stayed strong [Q1 2025 call].

**Long-term contracts.** Q1 is the season for long-range purchase commitments. These are reported as "performance obligations," which function as backlog. Commitments were stronger than in the past couple of years, with performance obligations up double digits year over year. Dhingra called that encouraging.

**NovaSeq X transition.** The NovaSeq X is Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer. Customers are moving to it from the older NovaSeq 6000 ("6K"). Q1 transition metrics [Q1 2025 call]:

- Placements: more than 60 X systems in Q1, after more than 90 in Q4 2024. Thaysen said this beat expectations.
- Customer mix: about 60% of Q1 placements went to clinical customers (labs running patient tests).
- Overall share: about 68% of high-throughput gigabases (billions of DNA letters, the unit of sequencing output) shipped on the X, but only about 43% of high-throughput consumables revenue. The gap exists because the X is cheaper per gigabase.
- By customer type: research was over 80% converted by volume, and clinical was over 50%.
- Framework, reaffirmed: by the second half of 2025, about 50% of high-throughput revenue and about 75% of gigabases would be on the X.

Thaysen said X and total high-throughput consumables rose from Q4 2024, which "demonstrate[s] elasticity." Elasticity here means lower cost per unit leading customers to sequence more. Dhingra explained the mechanism: as the older 6K kits shrink in the mix, the price drag from the cheaper X fades, so more of the gigabase growth becomes revenue growth. Total output on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30%, with both clinical and research strong.

### Cost cuts and buybacks

**Cost program.** In March 2025, Illumina launched an extra $100 million cost-reduction program in response to China and research weakness. By the call, the actions needed for full 2025 savings were in place.

The program includes stock-compensation changes. Dhingra put its fully annualized run-rate value at over $225 million over four years. Thaysen, in Q&A, said "more than $200 million." Because of these cuts, operating expenses would be flat to slightly down for the rest of 2025, rather than rising seasonally after Q1 as usual.

**Buybacks.** Illumina bought back about 1.73 million shares for $200 million in February, at an average of $115.74. That left $1.2 billion on the authorization, and further buybacks were planned [Dhingra, Q1 2025 call].

### Full-year 2025 guidance cut

Dhingra explained the history. A March revision had already absorbed the loss of most China revenue, tighter funding and the new cost cuts, while holding EPS at about $4.50. The May cut added tariffs on top.

The table shows the new full-year ranges. The key change is the much lower China revenue, now guided separately until the export situation is resolved.

**China's share of earnings.** Dhingra said Greater China would contribute about $0.35 of 2025 EPS, versus about $0.76 in 2024. Rest-of-world EPS at the guidance midpoint would be $3.90, up about 15% on a comparable basis.

**Rest-of-world revenue bridge.** For the next three quarters, Dhingra laid out these moving parts:

- Research weakness: cuts 2–4% per quarter.
- Clinical strength: adds about 1% per quarter.
- Net of those two: about $60 million.
- Pricing actions: add revenue mainly in H2, since price changes apply only to new orders.
- Currency: about $25 million better than previously assumed.

**China outlook.** Management called its China stance "pragmatic." Any resolution would be upside.

**Tariffs.** Dhingra estimated the gross 2025 tariff cost at about $85 million. The largest part comes from goods shipped from Illumina's Singapore plant into the US. The rest comes from imported parts used in US manufacturing and from products sold into China. Because of inventory timing, Q2 would see a partial hit, then $30–35 million per quarter. Guidance assumed no further tariffs or counter-tariffs. Illumina expected to offset about half in 2025, through supply-chain changes, pricing and expense measures, and more fully in 2026.

### Q2 2025 guidance

Q2 guidance [Dhingra, Q1 2025 call]:

- **Revenue:** $1.04–1.06 billion in total.
  - China: $55–65 million.
  - Outside China: $980 million to $1 billion, down 2–3% because of partnership-revenue comparisons and research weakness.
- **Operating margin:** about 21%.
- **EPS:** $1.00–1.04.

Both margin and EPS include about $15 million of direct tariff cost. The outlook implied a stronger second half, relying on the X transition, cost cuts and pricing.

### Strategy, governance and pipeline

**Governance.** Thaysen thanked former chair Steve MacMillan and welcomed new chair Scott Gottlieb. Keith Meister joined the board; Thaysen cited Meister's experience as a genomics investor.

**Framing.** Thaysen called the three pressures "transitory challenges that will not define Illumina's long-term success." The responses:

- **China:** keep talking with regulators about a sustainable presence.
- **US research:** offer flexible terms to keep customer projects going.
- **Tariffs:** the US now applied a 10% baseline tariff, with higher rates for some countries. Illumina would respond through supply chain, cost and pricing.

**Long-term targets.** Thaysen reiterated high-single-digit revenue growth and 500 bp of operating-margin expansion by 2027, now stated excluding China. Illumina, Thaysen said, is "a resilient franchise with multiple drivers of near and long-term growth."

**Pipeline.** Thaysen called the innovation pipeline "unmatched" and listed:

- **Spatial technology** (mapping where genes are active within tissue). Announced in February, it offers a larger capture area, higher resolution and more sensitivity than rivals, and analyzes millions of cells per experiment. It links to Illumina Connected Multi-Omics, new tertiary-analysis (data interpretation) software built on Partek visualization. It needs no special equipment. Early access had begun, with launch planned for 2026.
- **Perturb-Seq** (a single-cell CRISPR gene-editing screen). It captures the CRISPR guide RNA and messenger RNA from the same cell, enabling genome-wide screens at "market leading costs." It builds on technology acquired in 2024, whose name is inaudible in the transcript. Launch was due later in 2025.
- **A proteomics (protein-measurement) product** co-developed with Standard BioTools. It was in early access, with launch targeted for H1 2025.
- **Constellation mapped reads and the 5-base genome.** Both were in early access, with launches planned for 2026.

## Q2 2025 (July 31, 2025): beat, guidance raised, SomaLogic deal

### Results

**Profit.** EPS was $1.19, up 9% year over year, "well above" the top of guidance and $0.18 above consensus. Operating margin was 23.8%, up 160 bp, and operating profit grew about 4% on lower revenue [Q2 2025 call, 2025-07-31].

### Demand and the X transition

**Clinical.** Clinical had become about 60% of consumables. Thaysen said clinical "has proven more resilient and in some areas, is exceeding expectations." The drivers by application:

- **Oncology:** wider use of comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP, which sequences many cancer genes to choose therapy) and growing interest in sequencing-heavy MRD tests.
- **Genetic disease:** national genome programs and more whole-genome and whole-exome sequencing for rare disease. Whole-exome sequencing reads only the protein-coding genes.
- **Reproductive health:** rising US volumes of NIPT (non-invasive prenatal testing from the mother's blood) as labs finish validation.

**Research.** US research stayed constrained. Labs were delaying projects and hiring because of grant worries, similar to late Q1.

**X transition metrics** [Dhingra, Q2 2025 call]:

- Placements: more than 50 X units, about 60% to clinical customers.
- Overall share: about 69% of high-throughput gigabases and 44% of high-throughput revenue were on the X.
- By customer type: research was over 80% converted, and clinical was about 55%.
- Momentum: X consumables revenue grew more than 10% from Q1, and high-throughput consumables grew both sequentially and year over year.
- Output: grew more than 30%, led by clinical. Dhingra called output a directional gauge of demand, not a revenue predictor.
- Outlook: the year-end goals (50% of revenue, 75% of volume) would be driven by clinical.

**Instruments.** Instrument sales fell because high- and mid-throughput research buyers held back. Sales pipelines held up, but decisions were taking longer.

**Other products.** MiSeq i100 Plus had more than 500 placements, and the base MiSeq i100 was due in August. Excluding AGD timing, services grew high single digits.

### MiSeq i100 and the multiomics strategy

**MiSeq i100 Plus.** Thaysen said customers called the benchtop (lab-bench-sized) MiSeq i100 Plus a "game changer," citing:

- Faster turnaround and ease of use.
- Room-temperature reagents.
- 18 proven workflows, 9 of them integrated end to end.

It appeals to first-time NGS (next-generation sequencing) labs and emerging markets, and Thaysen said it "sets a new standard for the future of Illumina innovation."

**Long-term targets.** Thaysen reaffirmed high-single-digit growth and an operating margin of 26% by 2027. Three pillars support those targets:

1. Core sequencing, including the X transition.
2. Multiomics (measuring DNA, RNA, proteins and other molecules together).
3. Services, data and software.

The pitch was shifting from cost per gigabase to "the highest-quality biological insight at the lowest end-to-end cost."

**SomaLogic deal.** In June 2025, Illumina agreed to buy SomaLogic from Standard BioTools. The terms were $350 million in cash at closing plus up to $75 million in near-term milestones and royalties. Closing was expected in H1 2026, pending regulators.

- SomaLogic works in affinity proteomics, which measures proteins using binding reagents. Thaysen called it "a small but fast-growing segment."
- Its SomaScan assay measures over 9,500 human proteins from small samples. It uses proprietary SOMAmer binders that offer "unmatched sensitivity, scalability and reproducibility."
- The deal extends an exclusive partnership dating from 2021.
- Rationale: Illumina's scale should speed adoption, the assay strengthens the NovaSeq X, and it could extend to single-cell and spatial work.
- With SomaLogic, the portfolio spans DNA, RNA, methylation (chemical tags on DNA that switch genes on or off) and proteins.

**Tone.** Management was "cautious" on H2 because of NIH uncertainty but raised guidance anyway.

### How the story changed through 2025

**China** went from a separately guided collapse in May to a resilient business. Full-year guidance moved from $165–185 million to about $200 million, then about $220 million. Approval to serve OEM partners came in October, and talks continued while Illumina remained on the UEL.

**Clinical** went from mid-single-digit consumables growth in Q1 to double digits ex-China in Q3 and 20% in Q4. That validated management's elasticity argument.

**Research** fell sharply. Management assumed about a 15% academic decline for the rest of 2025, and research consumables reached flat only by Q4. 2026 guidance still assumes a decline.

**Pricing** went from tariff-driven increases in Q1, defended against a "free sequencing" competitor, to "very stable" pricing by midyear. By late 2025 the transition-related price headwind was fading.

**Competition** was dismissed in Q1 as "a technology overview." By Q3 and Q4, management claimed to lead "across all these dimensions," saw "little competition," and was open to lower prices where demand is price-sensitive.

**Multiomics** grew from early-access products into a growth pillar, with SomaLogic, Protein Prep, 5-base and BioInsight. Management promised at least 1–2 points of growth from 2027.
