## Where this part picks up

This part covers Illumina's financial results from the preliminary fiscal 2025 numbers, released in January 2026, through the third quarter of 2026. It also covers how 2026 guidance moved during the year. Two terms recur throughout:

- **Non-GAAP** figures are the company's adjusted numbers. They strip out items such as amortization of acquired intangibles, acquisition costs, restructuring, and gains or losses on investments.
- **Organic growth** removes the effects of currency and acquisitions.

Illumina uses three further conventions:

- **ROW organic growth** ("rest of world") also excludes **Greater China**, which in Illumina's usage includes Taiwan and Hong Kong.
- **Constant currency (CC)** growth removes only exchange-rate effects.
- **Recurring revenue** is consumables plus service and other revenue. Consumables are the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run. Recurring revenue was about 90% of the total in FY25 [JPM, 2026-01-13].

Illumina says non-GAAP operating margin and EPS are the metrics its board uses to judge and pay management. Its performance stock units are tied to operating margin, EPS (resumed in 2026) and relative total shareholder return [JPM, 2026-01-13; Q1 2026 10-Q].

## Preliminary FY25 results and the FY27 targets (January 2026)

At the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference, CEO Jacob Thaysen pre-announced preliminary, unaudited FY25 results [JPM, 2026-01-13].

- **Q4'25 revenue** was about $1,155M, up 5% reported and 4% in constant currency from $1,104M.
- **FY25 revenue** was about $4,339M against $4,332M in FY24, flat on both bases. The FY24 comparison is for Core Illumina, excluding GRAIL.
- **Excluding Greater China**, Q4 grew 7% in CC ($1,100M reported) and FY25 grew 2% ($4,096M). This compares with a 1% decline in FY24 ($4,019M against $4,054M in FY23).
- **Greater China revenue** was therefore about $55M in Q4 and $243M for the year.

Earnings were also preliminary:

- **Non-GAAP EPS** was $1.27–$1.30 for Q4 and $4.76–$4.79 for FY25, against $4.16 in FY24.
- **GAAP EPS** (the standard accounting measure) was much higher, at $2.14–$2.17 for Q4 and $5.42–$5.45 for FY25. The main reason was a $2.13 per share net gain on strategic investments, which non-GAAP excludes. Other FY25 adjustments included acquisition costs ($0.53), transformational initiatives ($0.39), an intangible impairment ($0.15) and a tax item ($0.26). The tax item was a valuation allowance on U.S. foreign tax credits after U.S. tax legislation signed July 4, 2025.
- **FY24 GAAP EPS** was $5.61, inflated by a $2.87 legal benefit from reversing an accrued European Commission fine.
- **Non-GAAP operating margin** was about 23% in FY25 against 21% in FY24. On a GAAP basis it was 19% against 34%, with the FY24 figure again lifted by the fine reversal.

Other figures from the deck:

- **Free cash flow (FCF)**, meaning operating cash flow minus capital spending, was about $1.0B over the twelve months to September 2025. That was $1.1B of operating cash less $0.1B of capital expenditure. FCF conversion (FCF divided by non-GAAP net income) was above 140%, and return on invested capital was above 20%.
- **Gross leverage** (debt divided by a measure of earnings) was about 1.7x, or 0.6x net of cash. Management said it aims to keep an investment-grade credit rating.
- **FY25 buybacks** were about $740M.
- **NovaSeq X**, Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer, had about 95 placements in Q4, about 260 shipments in FY25, and an active installed base of about 890.
- **Pull-through**, the annual consumables revenue each instrument generates, averaged about $1.35M for a NovaSeq X against about $670K for the older NovaSeq 6000.

Management restated three **FY27 targets**:

1. High-single-digit (HSD) ex-China CC revenue growth, up from –1% in FY24 and +2% in FY25.
2. More than 500 basis points of non-GAAP operating margin expansion, from 21% in FY24 to 26% in FY27 ex-China. A basis point (bp) is one-hundredth of a percentage point. About 200 bps had been delivered in FY25.
3. Double-digit-to-teens EPS growth, shown at about 15% for FY27 from the $4.78 FY25 midpoint.

### Q1 2026 (reported April 30)

Revenue rose 5% to $1,091M [Q1 2026 10-Q].

- **Consumables** rose 4% to $797M, which management credited to high-throughput demand as the NovaSeq X installed base grows.
- **Instruments** (the sequencing machines) rose 7% to $120M.
- **Service and other** rose 8% to $174M, helped by SomaLogic from January 30.

GAAP operating margin rose to 19.2% from 15.8%. Gross margin improved to 66.1% from 65.6% on lower field service and warranty costs, partly offset by lower-margin SomaLogic revenue and tariffs. Total operating expense fell 1% to $512M:

- **R&D** fell to $240M, mainly on $11M less restructuring.
- **SG&A** (selling, general and administrative) rose to $272M, driven by the ERP upgrade (a replacement of the company's core financial software), legal costs and SomaLogic.
- **Restructuring charges** were $1M against $31M a year earlier.

GAAP net income was only 2% higher at $134M. The reason was a $71M swing in other income: a $45M strategic investment loss, mainly on the retained GRAIL stake, against a $31M gain a year earlier. The GAAP tax rate fell to 14.8% from 27.9% on prior-year return adjustments and the Singapore earnings mix. Diluted shares fell to 154M from 159M.

### Q2 2026 (reported July 30)

Revenue rose 9% to $1,159M [Q2 2026 10-Q]. The main drivers were:

- **Instruments** grew strongly, with sequencing instruments up 30% organically to $125M on NovaSeq X and MiSeq i100 shipments.
- **Service and other** rose 20% to $177M.
- **Sequencing consumables** reached $775M, up 4% organically.

Non-GAAP margins fell year over year. Gross margin dropped 120 bps and operating margin 130 bps [Q2 2026 deck]. On a GAAP basis, gross margin rose to 66.4%, but only because Q2'25 carried a $23M intangible impairment. Excluding that, management said gross margin declined. It cited four causes: a mix shift toward instruments, lower-margin SomaLogic revenue, tariffs (lower than in Q1) and higher memory-chip and Middle East freight costs.

Non-GAAP SG&A rose to 24.0% of revenue from 22.7%, on compensation and intellectual-property litigation costs. Higher government incentives only partly offset this. GAAP net income fell 12% to $207M ($1.35 per share) because the investment gain shrank: $0.15 per share this year against $0.65 a year earlier.

For the first half, GAAP operating margin was 20.2% against 18.0%, and non-GAAP was 22.2% against 22.1%.

### Q3 2026 (reported October 29) and the tariff refund

In February 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) did not authorize the tariffs imposed from April 2025. In March, the Court of International Trade ordered U.S. Customs to process refunds. Illumina's largest tariff exposure is imports from its Singapore plant [Q1 and Q3 2026 10-Qs].

Illumina booked the refund in Q3 [Q3 2026 10-Q]:

- **Cost recovery:** a $79M reduction to cost of revenue.
- **Customer credits:** a $37M reduction to revenue, with a matching liability, for refunds it will pass to customers. Of this, $28M was in sequencing consumables, $4M in instruments, $3M in service and $2M in microarray. By region, $28M was in the U.S. and Canada and $9M in EMEALA.
- **Net effect:** a $42M benefit to gross profit.
- **Cash:** $32M had been received by quarter-end, with $47M still receivable.

These items lifted GAAP gross margin to 73.5% and GAAP operating margin to 27.0%. Excluding them, non-GAAP gross margin was 69.4% (up 20 bps) and operating margin was 25.0% [Q3 2026 deck]. Management described gross margin excluding the benefit as "relatively flat." A simple recalculation that adds back only the $42M gives about 69.8% against 67.6%, but the non-GAAP basis supports management's description.

Other Q3 results:

- **GAAP revenue** rose 4% to $1,129M. On a GAAP basis, instruments fell 5% to $105M and service rose 17% to $183M.
- **Ex-refund sequencing figures:** consumables were $792M (up 6% organically), instruments $106M (down 1%) and service $158M (up 8%).
- **GAAP net income** rose 47% to $220M, helped by a 16.4% tax rate. The Q3'25 rate was 31.8%, which included the $42M tax valuation allowance.
- **Venture funds** lost $31M.

For the nine months, revenue was $3,380M (up 6%), GAAP operating margin 22.5% against 19.0%, and non-GAAP operating margin 23.1% against 22.9%. Non-GAAP EPS was $3.90 against $3.49.

## SomaLogic and the GRAIL stake

Illumina closed the SomaLogic acquisition from Standard BioTools on January 30, 2026, for $460M [Q1 2026 10-Q]. SomaLogic measures proteins, and the purchase price comprised:

- $382M of cash.
- $81M of contingent consideration, meaning future payments tied to performance.
- Less $3M for settling a preexisting relationship.

The purchase price was allocated to:

- **Goodwill** of $171M (the premium over the fair value of assets), expected to be tax-deductible.
- **Intangibles** of $186M: developed technology $158M over 9 years, trade name $16M over 7 years and customer relationships $12M over 8 years.
- **Inventory** of $57M, of which $22M is classed as long-term under a new accounting policy.

Separately, Illumina bought a $50M patent portfolio, amortized over 14 years. Intangible amortization is expected to be $78M in 2027 and $76M in 2028 [Q3 2026 10-Q]. Transaction costs were reported as $7M in Q1 but as about $6M year-to-date in the Q3 filing.

The earnout was settled in two steps:

- **2025 milestone:** Illumina paid $25M in Q2 (the Q1 filing dates the payment to April).
- **Buyout:** on July 24, 2026, Illumina agreed to pay $30.04M for the remaining 2026 milestone of up to $50M, a 2% royalty, a 5% Single-SOMAmer royalty and a license. No earnout obligations remain.

By our arithmetic from the filing's pro forma tables, SomaLogic brought in about $24M of revenue in Q3'25.

Illumina sold its remaining **GRAIL shares** in 2026, for $206M of proceeds year-to-date. The stake fell from $215M at year-end to $66M after Q1, $88M after Q2 and $2M after Q3.

Illumina also owes **contingent value rights (CVRs)**, payments to former GRAIL holders. They equal 2.5% of GRAIL's annual revenue up to $1B and 9% above that, through August 2033. Payments were $1.2M year-to-date. Revenue subject to the CVRs was $129M for Q4'25 through Q2'26, against $106M a year earlier. The CVR liability moved from $54M at year-end to $35M after Q1, $40M after Q2 and $36M after Q3. The revenue risk premium used to value it fell from 3% to 2%. Illumina also has a $1M indemnity accrued for a GRAIL award worth up to $78M.

Smaller items from the quarterly filings:

- **Government incentives:** credits of $41M year-to-date against $11M a year earlier.
- **Share-based compensation:** $207M year-to-date against $212M.
- **Remaining performance obligations** (contracted revenue not yet recognized) shrank from $1.1B in Q1 to $1.0B in Q2 and $933M in Q3. The share due within 12 months fell from 85% to 83% to 76%.
- **Contract liabilities** fell from $346M to $319M.
- **Restructuring:** the Q3 filing reports no restructuring charges in 2026, which conflicts with the $1M shown for Q1. It reported $41M for the first nine months of 2025.
- **ERP upgrade:** expected to go live in H1 2027.

Litigation over the GRAIL acquisition continues [Q3 2026 10-Q]:

- **State class action:** certified on May 15, 2026, with trial set for March 20, 2028.
- **Federal class action:** the Third Amended Complaint was dismissed with leave to amend on September 30, 2026, and a Fourth Amended Complaint followed on October 21.

Management lists continuing risks as China, tariffs, NIH funding, freight and memory-chip costs [Q3 2026 10-Q].
