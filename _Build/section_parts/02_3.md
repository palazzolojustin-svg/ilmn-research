## How to read this part

This part covers Illumina's financial results and guidance from the preliminary full-year 2025 figures, released in January 2026, through the second quarter of 2026, which ended June 28, 2026. It draws on five company documents: the CEO's slides at the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference [JPM, 2026-01-13], the first- and second-quarter 2026 earnings slide decks [Q1 2026 slides, 2026-04-30; Q2 2026 slides, 2026-07-30], and the matching quarterly reports filed with the SEC [Q1 2026 10-Q, 2026-05-01; Q2 2026 10-Q, 2026-07-31]. All of these are the company's own materials. They contain no analyst questions, ratings, price targets or consensus estimates, so every view below is management's unless marked as an inference.

Three reporting conventions recur throughout:

- **"Organic" growth** strips out currency movements and acquisitions.
- **"ROW" (rest of world)** means everything outside Greater China. Greater China includes Taiwan and Hong Kong.
- **Non-GAAP figures** are company-adjusted numbers. They exclude items such as amortization of acquired intangibles, acquisition costs, restructuring, and gains or losses on investments. **GAAP** figures follow standard U.S. accounting rules.

Illumina says non-GAAP operating margin and EPS are the key metrics its board uses to judge and pay management. EPS (earnings per share) is profit divided by the number of shares.

## Full-year 2025: preliminary results

### Headline numbers

At JPM, CEO Jacob Thaysen pre-announced approximate, unaudited FY25 results ahead of the full report scheduled for February 5, 2026 [JPM, 2026-01-13]:

- **Full-year revenue** was about $4.34B ($4,339M). That was flat against FY24's $4,332M, both as reported and in constant currency (growth with exchange-rate effects removed).
- **Q4'25 revenue** was about $1.155B, up 5% reported and about 4% in constant currency from $1,104M a year earlier.
- **Non-GAAP EPS** was $4.76–$4.79 for the year (midpoint $4.78), up from $4.16 in FY24. For Q4 it was $1.27–$1.30.
- **Non-GAAP operating margin** for FY25 was about 23%, against 21% in FY24. Operating margin is operating profit as a share of revenue. The deck used the latest guidance given at Q3'25 earnings for this figure.

Management summarized FY25 as having "returned Illumina to growth." It cited NovaSeq X milestones and new launches (Protein Prep, 5-Base, Perturb-Seq, Illumina Connected Multiomics). It also cited the launch of BioInsight, its data-and-AI business, which included a "billion-cell atlas" pharma partnership.

At JPM the company described its FY25 profile as follows:

- Revenue of about $4.3B.
- About 90% recurring revenue, meaning consumables plus service and other revenue.
- About 8.6K employees and customers in about 160 countries.

### GAAP versus non-GAAP

GAAP EPS was well above non-GAAP EPS in FY25. The main reason is that GAAP includes $2.13 per share of net gains on strategic investments, chiefly the retained GRAIL stake, while non-GAAP removes them.

The bridge from preliminary GAAP diluted EPS of $5.42–$5.45 (Q4: $2.14–$2.17) to non-GAAP EPS was made up of these items:

| Adjustment | FY25 | Q4'25 |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition-related costs | +$0.53 | +$0.24 |
| Transformational initiatives (mainly restructuring severance and costs of upgrading the ERP system, the company's core business software) | +$0.39 | +$0.06 |
| Intangible impairment | +$0.15 | – |
| Strategic investment gains | –$2.13 | –$1.24 |
| Other | +$0.14 | +$0.12 |
| Tax | +$0.26 | –$0.05 |

Two of these items need more explanation:

- **Tax.** The FY25 tax adjustment includes a one-time valuation allowance on deferred tax assets tied to U.S. foreign tax credits. It followed U.S. tax legislation signed on July 4, 2025.
- **Other.** This includes a donation to the Illumina foundation, costs of board membership changes, and legal accruals.

For comparison, FY24 GAAP EPS was $5.61 and non-GAAP EPS was $4.16. The FY24 adjustments were:

- acquisition-related costs: –$1.00
- transformational initiatives: +$0.38
- impairment: +$0.02
- legal contingency and settlement: –$2.87
- other expense: +$1.86
- tax: +$0.16

On margins, FY25 GAAP operating margin was 19%. Adding back acquisition costs (2 points), transformational costs (1 point) and impairment (1 point) gives 23% non-GAAP. FY24 GAAP margin was 34%, flattered by the reversal of an accrued European Commission fine, which accounts for the 10-point legal adjustment, and non-GAAP was 21%.

### Installed base and pull-through

The installed base is the number of Illumina sequencers in active use. It is the engine of recurring consumables revenue (the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run). Pull-through is the average annual consumables revenue per instrument.

At JPM, Illumina reported about 21,000 active instruments. These break down as more than 12,000 low-throughput, more than 6,000 mid-throughput and about 2,000 high-throughput (HT) machines, excluding the older HiSeq line. Theoretical HT capacity roughly doubled from 2022 to 2025.

The table below shows FY25 figures by instrument family. The extraction pairs some columns imperfectly, so treat the mapping as approximate. The key takeaway is that a NovaSeq X generates roughly twice the consumables of the NovaSeq 6000 it replaces.

|---|---|---|---|
| NovaSeq X (first shipped Q1'23) | ~260 | ~890 | ~$1.35M |
| NovaSeq 6000 | ~30 | ~1,360 (after ~330 decommissions) | ~$670K |
| NextSeq 1k/2k | ~380 | ~2,990 | ~$90K |
| NextSeq 500/550 | ~100 | ~3,460 | ~$100K |
| MiSeq i100 / MiSeq | ~1,200 | ~8,860 | ~$30K |
| MiniSeq / iSeq 100 | ~90 | ~3,840 | ~$20K |

Illumina placed about 95 NovaSeq X units in Q4'25.

By consumables revenue, the business is about 60% Research & Applied and 40% Clinical, with a further ~3% "Other," mostly infectious disease. Within clinical, oncology is about 51%, genetic disease about 25% and reproductive health about 21%.

### Cash, leverage and buybacks

On a last-twelve-months basis as of September 28, 2025:

- Operating cash flow was $1.1B and capital spending was $0.1B, giving free cash flow (cash left after capital spending) of about $1.0B.
- FCF conversion (free cash flow divided by non-GAAP net income) was above 140%.
- Return on invested capital was above 20%.
- Gross leverage (debt divided by EBITDA, a measure of operating earnings) was about 1.7x, or about 0.6x net of cash. The stated goal is to keep an investment-grade credit rating.

Illumina repurchased about $740M of shares in FY25 [JPM, 2026-01-13].

### FY27 targets and strategy framing

Management restated three FY27 targets, which it first set out in 2024 [JPM, 2026-01-13]:

1. **High-single-digit ex-China constant-currency revenue growth.** The track record so far is –1% in FY24 and +2% in FY25.
2. **At least 500 basis points of non-GAAP operating margin expansion,** reaching 26% in FY27 on an ex-China basis. A basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point. Margin was 21% in FY24 and about 23% in FY25, so about 300 bps of expansion remains.
3. **Double-digit to teens EPS growth.** FY27 is shown at about 15%.

The deck framed growth around three pillars:

- **Core sequencing and the NovaSeq X transition.** Upcoming items include NovaSeq X v1.4 for clinical workflows in H1'26, Constellation in H1'26, and DRAGEN v4.4 and 5-Base, both already on the market.
- **Multiomics, meaning measuring several biological layers such as DNA, RNA and proteins.** Products include Single Cell (H1'25), Connected Multiomics (H2'25), Protein Prep (Q4'25) and Ex-Situ Spatial (H1'26).
- **Data, AI and services,** through BioInsight.

On capital deployment, management listed:

- investment in growth;
- bolt-on acquisitions judged on market expansion, EPS accretion and return on capital, with SomaLogic then expected to close in 1H'26;
- anti-dilutive buybacks (repurchases that offset share issuance to employees) plus opportunistic repurchases.

Management also claimed the following about clinical testing, footnoting these claims as "directional" internal estimates:

- Clinical applications are less than 20% penetrated.
- A genome uses about 15x the data of an exome (more than 120 Gb versus about 8 Gb). Gb, or gigabases, measures the volume of sequencing data.
- Future clinical data growth will exceed a 30% compound annual rate.

## SomaLogic: what it cost and how it is accounted for

Illumina closed the purchase of SomaLogic, a proteomics (protein measurement) company, and related assets from Standard BioTools on January 30, 2026. The total price was $460M: $382M in cash plus $81M of contingent consideration, less $3M for settling a prior relationship.

The deal included two kinds of contingent payments:

- **Milestones** of up to $75M. A $25M milestone for 2025 was met and paid in April 2026. Up to $50M was tied to 2026 revenue.
- **A 2% royalty** on SOMAmer-based library-prep kit sales for 10 years, through January 2036.

The remaining contingent liability was $37M at June 28. On July 27, 2026, Illumina paid $30M to buy out the 2026 milestone and the royalty early.

The purchase price was allocated as follows:

- **Goodwill of $171M,** tax-deductible. Goodwill is the premium paid above the value of identifiable assets.
- **Intangibles of $186M:** developed technology $158M (9-year life), trade name $16M (7-year) and customer relationships $12M (8-year).
- **Inventory of $57M,** of which $22M is classified as long-term because its manufacturing takes more than a year. This was a new accounting policy for Illumina; non-current inventory was $26M by June.
- **Other items:** $19M of cash, $13M of receivables, $19M of property and $14M of lease assets, less liabilities.

Deal costs of $7M were expensed in Q1.

On a pro forma basis, as if SomaLogic had been owned since the start of 2025:

- Q1'26 revenue would have been $1,099M.
- Q2'26 revenue would have been $1,159M with net income of $192M, against $1,076M and $241M in Q2'25.
- First-half revenue would have been $2,258M with net income of $333M, against $2,136M and $318M.

Together with a separate $50M patent-portfolio purchase (14-year life), net intangibles rose to $430M in Q1 and stood at $410M in Q2, up from $210M at year-end. Gross intangibles rose from $724M to $961M. Expected amortization is $78M in 2027, $76M in 2028, $46M in 2029, $40M in 2030 and $131M thereafter. The remainder-of-2026 figure was $59M in the Q1 filing and $39M in the Q2 filing.

## GRAIL residual exposure

Illumina still holds GRAIL stock and owes contingent value rights (CVRs) to former GRAIL holders. The CVRs pay 2.5% of GRAIL's covered revenue up to $1B a year, and 9% above that, through August 2033.

Payments are small so far, though GRAIL's covered revenue is growing:

- In Q1'26 Illumina paid $410K, based on $44M of covered revenue in Q4'25, up from $360K and $38M a year earlier.
- Over two quarters, payments were $794K on $84M, against $660K on $70M. Management credits sales of GRAIL's Galleri test.

The CVR liability was $35M in March and $40M in June, against $54M at year-end. It is valued with a Monte Carlo simulation (a statistical model of many possible outcomes) using a 3% revenue risk premium. The two filings describe the market-cap averaging window differently, 25-day versus 60-day.

## Other financial details

### Debt

Illumina's term debt is $2.0B of principal in four $500M notes:

- 4.650% due September 9, 2026 (now current debt)
- 5.750% due December 13, 2027
- 4.750% due December 12, 2030 (issued November 2025)
- 2.550% due March 23, 2031

The carrying value was $1,991M in Q2, with a fair value of $1,959M. A $750M revolving credit line, which matures January 4, 2028, is undrawn. It requires debt below 3.5x adjusted EBITDA (4.0x after a qualifying acquisition), and Illumina is in compliance. First-half interest expense was $47M against $50M; interest income was $19M against $21M.

### Order backlog and deferred revenue

Remaining performance obligations (contracted revenue not yet delivered) were about $1.1B in Q1, with 85% due within 12 months. In Q2 they were $1.0B, with 83% due within 12 months. Development and licensing contracts added about $38M in Q1 and $43M in Q2.

Contract liabilities (deferred revenue) were $344M in Q1 and $333M in Q2, against $346M at year-end. Revenue recognized from prior deferrals was $98M in Q1 and $71M in Q2.

Grant receivables from government incentives were $24M in Q1 and $45M in Q2.

## Headwinds management flagged

**Tariffs.** Tariffs in place since April 2025 hit mainly imports from Illumina's Singapore plant. In February 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that IEEPA (an emergency-powers law) does not authorize tariffs. In March 2026 the Court of International Trade ordered customs refunds to be processed in phases. Illumina is pursuing refunds, but recoveries so far are immaterial, and "timing and amount... remain uncertain."

**Cost pressures.** Freight costs and memory-chip costs are rising in 2026.

**Demand risks.** These include NIH research funding cuts, ongoing China restrictions and armed conflict.

**ERP rollout.** The ERP upgrade, begun in Q3 2025, is due in H1 2027. Management warned it could affect internal controls.

**GRAIL litigation.**

- A Delaware derivative suit (brought on the company's behalf against its directors) had a motion-to-dismiss hearing on February 13, 2026; the motion is still pending.
- A federal securities class action awaits a ruling on the motion to dismiss, with no hearing set.
- In the California state class action, class certification was granted May 15, 2026, and Illumina's motion for judgment on the pleadings was denied June 26, 2026. Illumina sought appellate review on July 28, 2026, and a hearing on amending its answer is set for October 30, 2026.
- The board again deferred shareholder litigation demands on February 4, 2026.
