## What Oxford Nanopore sells and how it positions itself against Illumina

Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) is a UK-listed company, founded in Oxford in 2005, that makes DNA and RNA sequencers based on nanopores. Nanopores are tiny protein holes. A single strand of DNA or RNA is threaded through one, and the machine reads it from changes in an electrical current. Its revenue comes from two places. Devices are the instruments: the small portable MinION, the benchtop GridION, and the PromethION range, which includes the P2 Solo, the P2i and the large P24. Consumables are the flow cells (the chips that hold the pores) and the reagent kits that customers must buy for every run. About 70% of revenue comes from flow cells and kits made in ONT's own factory in Didcot, UK, which management says has capacity for the next 3 to 5 years of growth [Q4 2025 call].

Management's central claim is that ONT reads **native DNA and RNA**, meaning the original molecule itself, with no copying or chemical conversion. Read lengths run from a few hundred bases to millions of bases. Because the molecule is read directly, ONT also detects methylation, the chemical tags on DNA that affect gene activity (sometimes called the "fifth base"), and RNA modifications in the same run. Its other claimed advantages are speed (sample preparation takes hours, not days), no need to batch samples, and small, cheap instruments.

Co-founder and outgoing CEO Gordon Sanghera put it this way: "We are not just a longread company. It's that native DNA that makes us highly differentiated." Sanghera also claimed that "there is no other company in this space" that sequences direct DNA and RNA [Q4 2025 call]. Illumina, by contrast, makes "short-read" sequencers. These read DNA in short copied fragments at very high volume and low cost per base. That positioning shapes how ONT talks about Illumina, which is covered under Competition below.

The company says it has customers in more than 125 countries and over 1,300 employees, and that 20,000 publications have used its technology in the 10 years since its first products [Q4 2025 call]. It launched DNA products about 10 years ago and direct RNA about 5 years ago. Revenue has grown at a 28% compound annual growth rate (CAGR, the average yearly growth rate over a period) since its IPO.

ONT sells into four **end markets** (customer categories):
- **Research:** academic and government labs, about two-thirds of revenue.
- **Clinical:** labs that diagnose patients.
- **Biopharma:** drug developers and manufacturers.
- **Applied industrial:** synthetic biology and core service providers.

Clinical, biopharma and applied industrial together are called the **applied markets**.

## March 2026: FY2025 results and the CEO handover [Q4 2025 call, 2026-03-02]

### Leadership change

Gordon Sanghera stepped down as CEO on 2 March 2026, after more than two decades leading the company. Francis Van Parys started as CEO the same morning. Van Parys spent that day meeting staff in Oxford and did not join the call.

Chair Duncan Tatton-Brown said Van Parys's experience lies in scaling innovation-driven life-science businesses, "particularly within regulated and commercial environments." Sanghera will remain an adviser through early 2027.

Tatton-Brown praised the R&D talent and the commercial progress of recent years. He also said the executive team "now has a few gaps," mainly at senior level, which Van Parys can fill over the next few years. CFO Nick Keher expected further executive additions to support the higher-priority end markets.

Sanghera used the call to reflect on the company's history:
- **Origins.** Sanghera began studying Hagan Bayley's single-molecule "stochastic sensing" work in spring 2004. The company was spun out with GBP 0.5 million from IP Group. Its founding vision was "the analysis of anything by anyone, anywhere," which Sanghera called "a work in progress."
- **Proudest moments.** NASA taking the technology into space (it is slated for the Artemis mission), and Project NEEMO, which found over 7,500 new species in the Mariana Trench.
- **Future scope.** The platform could extend to proteins and to small molecules, metabolites and volatile organic compounds.
- **On the doubters.** Sanghera recalled being told "it will never work... you can't raise money in London... you will never be profitable." Sanghera acknowledged that some think "the return on the investment is low, but this company will be around in 100 years' time."
- **On genomes.** Sanghera called T2T (telomere-to-telomere, meaning fully complete) genomes "the new gold standard," which Sanghera said Craig Venter is building.
- **Signing off.** "I'm not retiring," Sanghera said, quoting David Bowie: "I don't know what I'll be doing next, but I can guarantee it will be interesting."

Gross margin is the share of revenue left after the direct cost of making products. It was **58.6%** in FY2025, up from 57.5% in FY2024 and in line with the 59% guidance. The bridge below shows how Keher explained the change, in basis points (bps, where 100 bps equals 1 percentage point).

In 2025, gains from recycling PromethION flow cells (reusing chips that customers return) were largely offset by lower recycling of MinION flow cells.

### FY2025 costs, EBITDA and cash

Adjusted operating expenses (OpEx) rose only 1%. Two restructurings held them down:
- In January 2025, to move capital toward higher-growth areas.
- In November 2025, a "strategic realignment" around high-priority end markets.

The **adjusted EBITDA** loss narrowed by GBP 31.2 million, or 26%. EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation; "adjusted" strips out certain items. GBP 18 million of the improvement came in the second half, a 32% improvement for that half. Adjusted EBITDA has improved in every half since the start of 2024. Currency movements have cost about GBP 9 million of EBITDA cumulatively since January 2024. Keher said breakeven would come from above-market growth plus cost control, including non-headcount savings in IT, logistics and working practices.

Year-end cash was **GBP 302.8 million with no debt**, after a net outflow of GBP 101 million. Keher said cash burn fell by GBP 50 million. The main items were:
- **Operating loss before restructuring:** GBP 79.1 million.
- **Restructuring costs:** GBP 13.8 million (GBP 5.2 million in H1 and GBP 8.6 million in H2).
- **Working capital:** an inflow of GBP 13.4 million, with inventory down GBP 18 million.
- **Assets placed at customers:** GBP 10.1 million, down GBP 10.5 million thanks to CapEx-first pricing. A second-half rise of GBP 4.6 million was mainly devices for the UK Biobank contract.
- **CapEx and capitalised development:** GBP 45.5 million (GBP 41.5 million capitalised development and GBP 4 million CapEx). CapEx was unusually low because large projects such as the Spectrum Building were finished, and is unlikely to stay that low in 2026.
- **R&D tax credits:** GBP 18.9 million from two credits, also unlikely to repeat.
- **Other inflow:** GBP 15.3 million from the bond portfolio.

Inventory was GBP 80 million net of provisions, so the gross figure is higher. Keher called it a Board priority and said it could fall by "tens of millions" gross over time, mainly in MinION flow cells. "We've got cash stored up there. We should unlock it." Better yields reduce how much ONT needs to make. Higher recycling, by contrast, slows inventory reduction.

On the MinION recycling dip, Keher said there was "nothing untoward." MinION customers are smaller, so getting flow cells back from them is harder, and the recycling rate is something ONT controls.

### Strategic review and portfolio cuts

ONT's 2025 strategic review identified roughly **$13–14 billion of higher-priority segments**. These span research, clinical, biopharma manufacturing QC and other markets where its data richness, speed and accessibility give it an edge. Segments were ranked by ease of access, differentiation and scalability, not size alone, across 47 end-market segments in total. The resulting product decisions were:
- **P2 Solo:** sales end from June 2026, with effort redirected to the P2i.
- **ElysION** (an automated sample-prep and sequencing system): internal development is paused and direct sales have stopped.
- **Focus:** integrated systems (P2i, P24) and **Q-Line** products, the version of the instruments locked down for biopharma QC.
- **GridION Q-Line version 2:** due in the second half of 2026.
- **PromethION Q-Line:** re-timed to late 2027.

ONT also began ERP and CRM software overhauls (enterprise resource planning and customer relationship management). Keher called the result "a structurally more focused business with improving operational leverage."

### Other management commentary in the March Q&A

**Gross-margin levers (Keher).** CapEx-first pricing plus PromethION recycling are the main levers. If PromethION recycling reached MinION-like levels, PromethION gross margins could rise about 10 percentage points.

**Devices (Keher, answering Kyle Mikson of Canaccord).** The pricing change "caught customers off guard" and slowed device placements in 2025. Keher expected device revenue to grow faster than consumables in 2026 ("no doubt we'll get that wrong").

**Focus areas (Sanghera, answering Veronika Dubajova of Citi).** "Success can be painful with infinite possibilities... and finite resource." Sanghera named the priorities as:
- direct RNA and direct DNA with modifications;
- **adaptive sampling**, where the sequencer reads the first seconds of each DNA strand and ejects it if it is off-target. Sanghera said this makes "2 days, $300 to $500 of target enrichment disappear";
- "reuse by design," meaning a PromethION chip redesigned for reuse, with "radical transformative revenue and margin gains."

Sanghera declared that "2026 will be the year of the methylome." UK Biobank and the Office for Life Sciences are sequencing 50,000 samples on ONT, which Sanghera said will produce the first comprehensive 5-base genome. Sanghera also described a shift from "a heavy R company with some D" toward more development, driven by regulated applied markets that offer "recurring sticky revenues."

**Biopharma QC (answering Samuel England of Berenberg).** Biopharma QC is the testing that checks a drug batch meets its specification. Keher was "extremely frustrated" at not being able to name customers. ONT has signed one mRNA-vaccine production contract and hoped for another in 2026. A large European biopharma uses ONT centrally for sterility testing across several sites. Sanghera added that in the first three weeks of a fee-for-service contract with Eurofins, the technology caught a customer contamination that "saved several millions."

**PromethION throughput (answering Weston).** Higher-throughput "genomes per flow cell" capability is in customer beta testing but will suit only certain segments at first. Sanghera said competitors are in "a race to the bottom for whole genomes." ONT will use this throughput only where it competes with incumbents, because "everybody does not need that overcapacity."

**Clinical definition (answering Miles Dixon of Peel Hunt).** "Clinical" means customers using ONT to diagnose patients, judged by how they are paid, with some "grayness" with translational research. Sanghera noted that the bulk is **LDTs** (laboratory-developed tests that a lab builds and validates itself, signed off by a physician), not ONT-supplied diagnostic kits.

**R&D and proteomics (answering Dixon).** On R&D, Keher said: "We're not stopping innovation." Proteomics spend is in the single-digit millions of pounds. Sanghera said peptide mapping could earn revenue sooner than full protein sequencing, which is 3–5 years away. GLP-1 drugs are peptides that ONT can read directly, which Sanghera said "nobody else can do."

**Strategy and hires (answering Andrew Whitney of Investec).** ONT chose "biology over chemistry," so small-molecule sensing is not being pursued. Sanghera declined to advise Van Parys on hiring: "As manager this season and not here next season, I'm not about to start telling Francis who he should recruit."

**Clinical competition (Keher).** New competitors are entering clinical, but ONT wins on richness of data (methylation, long reads and structural variants in one run) and on turnaround time.

## March 2026: CFO at the Barclays Global Healthcare Conference [Barclays, 2026-03-11]

In a fireside chat with Barclays analyst Jon Unwin, Keher expanded on each end market.

### Applied industrial

ONT sees about **$1–1.5 billion** in synthetic biology where it has "a right to win." It is replacing Sanger sequencing (the older, low-throughput method) there, helped by the ability to "debatch," meaning run a few samples on demand rather than waiting to fill a large batch. Keher named the plasmid-sequencing company Plasmidsaurus as a customer doing "very well."

### Clinical

Keher said clinical growth came from the technology being "really of age" in rare disease, infectious disease and oncology.
- **Rare disease.** ONT claims a higher diagnostic yield than short-read technology because it detects structural variants (large DNA rearrangements). US reimbursement is emerging, and customers are starting to treat ONT as a frontline test rather than a "reflex test" (a follow-up test ordered after another one).
- **Infectious disease.** ONT offers metagenomic sequencing (reading everything in a sample when you do not know what is there) in 3–4 hours. Cepheid (a Danaher company) and bioMérieux will take it into **IVD** (in vitro diagnostics, regulator-approved test kits). These partners see ONT as "intel inside" for high-value segments where PCR tests "don't quite do it."
- **Regulatory.** ONT developed its own AmPORE TB test with **CE-IVD** status (the European regulatory mark). Keher admitted that, with hindsight, "maybe we might have thought about doing that in a different way." The GridION Dx also has that status. Most partners only need a locked-down "Q-line" version of the platform to build their own tests.
- **Oncology.** St. Jude children's hospital told ONT that adaptive sampling can replace about $1,000 of pathology tests for AML (acute myeloid leukemia) with a $150 test giving "the exact same answers." Keher called adaptive sampling "a big driver for this company in the oncology space."
- **Methylation.** Keher said "we have the gold standard for methylation." Keher's argument was that conventional platforms take a deep but narrow view, while ONT captures 100% of methylation, the "entire haystack." Keher cited a New York customer using methylation to predict organ-transplant failure, with similar logic applying to cancer recurrence. Keher said the 50,000-sample UK Biobank study should spark "a whole new wave of interest" from pharma and diagnostics.

### Market sizing (Keher's estimates)

- Total spending on next-generation sequencing (NGS) is $9–10 billion, of which clinical is one-third to one-half.
- ONT's serviceable addressable market is $20–25 billion, because it also counts conventional tests such as PCR.
- Clinical is about $10 billion and biopharma $3–4 billion.
- The "should-win" set of target markets is $13–14 billion.

Keher said displacing tests that already work is "maybe a fool's errand." ONT sits "on top" of existing lab devices, follows a "land and expand" approach, and admits it lacks some competitors' ecosystems, "but that is coming."

### Biopharma QC

- **Plasmids are the "door opener."** Keher said "the gold standard will become ONT," replacing Sanger.
- **mRNA vaccines.** ONT has signed a contract with "one of the leading players" in QC. Here ONT replaces "7 to 8 orthogonal types of tests" such as HPLC, mass spectrometry and gels, by reading the molecule directly. Keher said "we're being evaluated by all of the players," though "these things take time."
- **Other uses.** AAV (adeno-associated virus, a gene-therapy delivery vector) and sterility testing.
- **Volume.** For personalised cancer vaccines, testing is per patient. For sterility work, one facility may monitor several sites.

### Why the >30% target was missed

Unwin noted that actual growth was running 5–10 points below the 2024 target. Keher explained the history:
- When Keher joined in 2024, the company was aiming for breakeven in 2026, which was not achievable.
- ONT reset to EBITDA breakeven in 2027 and cash breakeven in 2028. To support these goals, it guided to over 30% CAGR for 2024–27, gross margin above 62%, and cost growth of 3–8% a year.
- Growth came in at 23% in 2024 and 24% in 2025.
- The market proved tougher than expected: NIH cuts came after the guidance was set, and China export controls hit. "We didn't expect it to be this size of a headwind."
- On execution, "we could have done better as well."

ONT cut costs twice in 2025, showing "we do what we say." The 2027 breakeven was "written in stone, and we've kept it in stone." Keher still claimed ONT is "growing a lot faster than all of the competition, undoubted."

### Van Parys's diagnosis and strategy

Van Parys came from GE Healthcare, Cytiva and Radiometer, and cited the Biacore platform's move into biomanufacturing QC as a model. Van Parys described themself as "an operator at heart... competitive, pragmatic and focused on execution."

Van Parys's diagnosis was that:
- adoption is below potential;
- customer experience has been inconsistent;
- roadmaps were "driven more by what is scientifically possible than by customer problems";
- the company lacked portfolio focus and leadership depth.

None of this, Van Parys said, requires reinventing the company.

**Four pillars.** The new strategy rests on customer-centric growth, focused innovation, disciplined execution and a high-performance culture. Pillars 2 and 3 will be detailed at full-year results. ONT will own library prep, sequencing and base calling (turning the electrical signal into DNA letters) and rely on partners for sample extraction, automation and analysis. The serviceable market is put at **$14–16 billion** (quoted as £ at RBC). The priority areas are:
- **Research:** genome centres and biobanks where long reads and methylation "change the answer." Four methylation and pharmacogenomic panels are shipping, and tumour profiling is due from H2.
- **Biopharma:** the fastest-growing priority. One mRNA QC run replaces separate identity, integrity and purity tests.
- **Clinical:** rare disease, tumour profiling and acute infection, often partner-led. One leukaemia study consolidated 8 conventional assays into one adaptive-sampling run costing about $350–450, versus about $1,650–2,050.

**2030 targets.** These are more than **$700 million organic revenue**, an adjusted EBITDA margin above 15%, and positive, growing free cash flow from 2028. Organic growth is targeted "above mid-teens" from FY2026, rising over time, with a longer-term ambition of $1 billion-plus. Clinical and biopharma (with applied markets) are expected to rise from about 35–45% of revenue today to about 65% by 2030. Royalties, the upfront fee and future business development are excluded as upside. Partnerships must earn more than 15% return on invested capital, and M&A may come "a little bit later than '28."

With RBC's Charles Weston, the CEO and CFO added several points.
- **Hires.** ONT announced a Chief Development and Product Officer and a Chief Marketing and Communications Officer, and hoped for a full team by Q4 2026.
- **Target applications.** ONT will focus on 15–20 target applications. About 40–45% of current revenue already falls in them.
- **Biopharma QC pipeline.** About 20 pharma companies are evaluating ONT, over half of them global players. Evaluations have been slow because customers assess once a year, their own drug assets slip, and ONT's path "has not gone in a straight line."
- **GridION Q v2.** Released the week before the chat, it adds audit features needed for **BLA** filings (biologics license applications) and does not require revalidation. mRNA and plasmid test packs are on sale; sterility and AAV are still to come.
- **Pricing.** The mRNA test pack lists at about **$2,000 per patient**. Keher said $1,000 after discounts is "not a bad place to start" for modelling. Keher added that companies spend "quite a few million dollars" each on in-house plasmid Sanger sequencing.
- **Biopharma market.** ONT sizes it at £4.1–5.5 billion.
- **Rare disease.** ONT has data showing a "30% uplift in diagnostic yield versus short-read technologies." Keher cited Natera's use via MyOme as validation.
- **Partnership categories.** There are three: IVD with Cepheid and bioMérieux (returns "not... before 3 to 5 years"), LDT enablement with service labs, and a compatibility programme. ONT will partner with automation vendors rather than build its own.
- **Research markets.** Europe and Asia ex-China are growing in the high single digits. The Americas are "flat to down," because NIH money "just isn't flowing." China is down "for everybody."
- **Pricing premium.** A customer would pay a premium over short-read, "but they won't pay where it is today."
- **The $700 million target.** It reflects "10 haircuts" and assumes "quite healthy competition." Van Parys wants to reach "$1 billion sooner rather than later."

## September 2026: Morgan Stanley conference [MS conf, 2026-09-15]

Speaking to Kallum Titchmarsh, Van Parys said H1 "has gone reasonably well" and the company is on track for full-year guidance: "so far, so good." Keher said the second-half weighting was expected.

**China.** Keher expects no H2 recovery, with the decline holding at about 16% but "stabilization." ONT is restructuring its China sales organisation and distributors and is "not giving up on China."

**Industrial.** Industrial growth was "slightly weaker than we'd like." Plasmid price deflation and multiplexing (running several samples per flow cell) are squeezing revenue, and growth may stay low for "the next couple of years." Food safety work is being deprioritised.

**Clinical.** Clinical grew 35%, with "good win rates" in competitive situations. Van Parys said diagnostic yield can move "from 30% to maybe 60% in some cases."

**Research.** Research grew in the mid-single digits. NIH is "not getting worse," and research should fall to about 40% of revenue by 2030. List prices are:
- MinION: $5,000 with 5 flow cells;
- GridION: $58,000;
- P2i: about $80,000.

**2030 plan.** The plan assumes no further utilisation gains and falling PromethION flow-cell prices.

**Margins.**
- **Consumables:** gross margin above 80% is "absolutely possible."
- **PromethION recycling:** only a "low percentage" of flow cells is recycled so far. Doing more could add about 10 points of PromethION margin.
- **Devices:** device margins should be broadly flat in 2027, as chip costs offset cost-down work.
- **Clinical:** margin is held down by price competition with "the other long-read player" (PacBio).

**Path to 2027 breakeven.** Keher listed mid-teens growth, a few hundred bps of margin expansion, and flat costs.

**AI and UK Biobank.** UK Biobank results on 50,000 ONT samples are due at the end of 2026, with 450,000 more samples a possible next step for AI work.

**What investors miss.** Van Parys said the long-read versus short-read and accuracy debates are "all behind us now." Keher said ONT is a "true sensing platform... playing in a different park" that investors do not yet appreciate.

## Competition, including with Illumina

ONT management repeatedly frames Illumina's long-read and methylation launches as validation of its own approach. All of the following are management claims.

**Sanghera on Illumina [Q4 2025 call].** "Illumina has finally agreed long reads are important and they've launched a synthetic long-read play. This is the fourth one, I think. So good luck with that." On Illumina's methylation profiler: "Bring it on, native, direct, all in one read and one price will outperform the market."

**Keher on Illumina's TruPath [Barclays, 2026-03-11].** Unwin cited a Broad Institute expert on a Barclays panel who called TruPath "a direct shot at PacBio and ONT." Keher said TruPath is the renamed Constellation technology ("we've seen it coming for quite a while") and "yet another attempt" at long reads. Keher's criticisms:
- It "is essentially a technology that's stitching together. So it's imputed. It's not direct."
- Its price, required volume, host instrument and timeline are all unproven.
- ONT gives "methylation for free," with no second test.

Keher added that it is "endearing that everybody is recognizing that long reads are important."

**PacBio.** Keher called PacBio "good technology." ONT claims advantages in native reads, any read length, RNA modifications and form factor (instrument size). In clinical, the two compete on price [MS conf].

**Roche.** At Barclays, Keher called Roche's nanopore-type approach a "very different type of technology." Van Parys later called Roche's Axelios short-read focused [Q2 2026 call].

On competition generally, Keher said: "There's more competition in the market. I wish there was less" [Barclays].

No ratings or price targets were stated in any of the five sources. The only analyst figures were these:
- **JPMorgan (Zain Ebrahim)** cited consensus of a GBP 14 million adjusted EBITDA loss, apparently for 2027, against Keher's GBP 45–55 million 2026 figure. The two were not reconciled [Q4 2025 call].
