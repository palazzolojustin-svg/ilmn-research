Across 2024, Illumina's message shifted in a consistent direction. Revenue guidance went down step by step, while margin and earnings guidance went up. In the first quarter, management simply held full-year guidance and stayed cautious. By the fourth quarter, it was guiding to a return to growth in 2025, an operating margin of about 23%, and earnings per share up about 10%.

Three threads run through all four calls:

- **The NovaSeq X transition.** Customers moved from the older NovaSeq 6000 to the newer NovaSeq X, which costs them much less per unit of data. Volume rose sharply, but revenue grew far more slowly. Management responded by disclosing more and more transition metrics.
- **Weak instrument sales.** Management repeatedly blamed tight customer capital budgets, while analysts kept probing competition.
- **Cost discipline.** Management increasingly used cost savings to support earnings even as sales disappointed.

China ran underneath all of this. It was weak for most of the year, stabilized late in 2024, and then became a fresh risk when a Chinese government action was announced just before the Q4 call.

## Q1 2024 (call held May 2, 2024)

### Setting and leadership

This was the first call for new CFO Ankur Dhingra, who had joined about two and a half weeks earlier. Former CFO Joydeep Goswami had also been Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer. Goswami stayed on as an advisor through June 30 and answered several questions on the call.

CEO Jacob Thaysen thanked Goswami for more than four years of service. Thaysen also announced that Jakob Wedel, who had joined Illumina in November, was now Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer.

Salli Schwartz, VP of Investor Relations, reminded listeners of a legal constraint. Under transitional measures ordered by the European Commission, which had prohibited Illumina's acquisition of GRAIL under EU merger rules, GRAIL "must be held and operated separately and independently from Illumina." GRAIL is a cancer-detection test company that Illumina was being forced to divest. For that reason, management discussed "Core Illumina," meaning Illumina excluding GRAIL.

### Results against expectations

**Revenue.** Core revenue was about $1.06 billion, down 2% from Q1 2023. The decline was the same on a reported basis and in constant currency (stripping out exchange-rate effects). Management said this was ahead of its own expectations. Dhingra attributed the beat to three things:

1. Strong high-throughput consumables. Consumables are the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every sequencing run.
2. The timing of revenue from certain strategic partnerships.
3. Some customers taking delivery of a few NovaSeq X instruments in Q1 rather than Q2.

**Earnings.** Consolidated non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS) was $0.09. Non-GAAP means adjusted figures that exclude certain items; Illumina's version still includes stock-based compensation. Management said EPS beat its internal expectations, "driven primarily by higher core revenue and lower core operating expenses."

The Seeking Alpha transcript header, which reflects the publisher's comparison with analyst consensus (the average analyst forecast), told a different story. It scored EPS as a $0.02 miss. It also showed consolidated revenue of $1.08 billion, down 1.01%, beating consensus by $25.23 million.

**Segments.**

- **Consumables: $698 million, up 1% year over year.** Growth came mainly from high throughput. Compared with Q4 2023, NovaSeq X consumables grew by double digits after the 25B flow cell launched. The 25B is the largest-capacity consumable for the X.
- **Instruments: $110 million, down 29%.** Two causes:
  - Mid-throughput shipments fell, as expected, because customers' capital and cash-flow constraints held back purchases.
  - NovaSeq X placements were lower than in Q1 2023, when Illumina shipped launch units to fill a strong pre-order book.
- **Services and other: $151 million, up 27%.** Growth came from strategic partnerships and from service contracts on a growing installed base. In Q&A, Dhingra said a little less than half of the growth came from specific pharma customer services whose timing fell in the quarter. Roughly half came from core instrument services.

**Sequencing activity.** Total output on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments, measured in gigabases (billions of DNA letters read), grew more than 35% year over year. It grew at a high-single-digit rate versus Q4. Management called this "directionally correlated with revenue over time."

Research and applied customers benefited from the X ramp and from 25B adoption at large core labs (shared sequencing facilities). Clinical activity still ran mainly on the NovaSeq 6000.

**Margins and costs.**

- **Gross margin: 67.1%, up 190 basis points (1.9 percentage points).** The gain came from a richer consumables mix and from cost savings, including freight and productivity. Lower-margin partnership revenue and higher warranty and field-service costs partly offset it.
- **Operating expenses: $491 million, down $23 million.** The decline mainly reflected headcount cuts. Expenses also came in below plan because project spending slipped into Q2 and one-time reversals lowered stock-based compensation.
- **Operating margin: 20.6%, versus 17.4% a year earlier.**

**GRAIL.** GRAIL was still consolidated in Illumina's results this quarter.

- Revenue was $27 million, up 35%, driven by Galleri, GRAIL's multi-cancer blood test.
- Operating expenses were $197 million, up $24 million on commercial and R&D hiring.
- The non-GAAP operating loss was $185 million.

**Consolidated figures.**

- Revenue was $1.08 billion, down 1%.
- Non-GAAP net income was $14 million, or $0.09 per diluted share, after absorbing GRAIL's loss.
- The tax rate was 46.4%, versus 27.3% in Q1 2023. Both years were distorted by consolidating GRAIL's loss, which grew $21 million. Excluding GRAIL, the rate was in the mid-20s in both years.
- Diluted shares were about 159 million.
- Operating cash flow was $77 million and capital spending $36 million, giving free cash flow (cash from operations minus capital spending) of $41 million.
- There were no buybacks. Cash and short-term investments were about $1.12 billion.

**Regions.** Thaysen gave these year-over-year changes:

- Americas: down 4%.
- EMEA (Europe, Middle East and Africa): down 3%.
- Greater China: down 14%.

Thaysen also said "Europe revenue was up 7%" and warned that Europe would decline in Q2 against heavy X shipments in Q2 2023. The transcript does not reconcile EMEA down 3% with Europe up 7%, so treat these figures with caution.

### Guidance

Guidance covered Core Illumina only and assumed no 2024 impact from a GRAIL divestment. EPS guidance would come only after the divestment closed.

- **Full year 2024 (reiterated, not raised):** revenue about flat versus 2023, and operating margin about 20%.
- **Q2 2024 (new):** revenue of $1.072 to $1.084 billion, which is 6.5 to 7.5 points lower than Q2 2023, and operating margin of about 18%.

**Why Q2 would be lower.** Dhingra said the year-over-year decline was "predominantly because of the lower NovaSeq X instrument shipments, given the significant backlog we worked through early last year." At the midpoint, Q2 would still be $22 million higher than Q1.

The Q2 margin dip reflected merit raises and the annual stock-grant cycle, which accounted for about half of the sequential expense increase. The other half came from project spending pushed out of Q1 and milestones landing in Q2. Dhingra expected operating margin to "improve sequentially every quarter."

**Full-year margin.** Gross margin gains and lower headcount would be partly offset by normalizing performance pay and by inflation. Goswami noted headcount was down about 12% year over year.

**Backdrop.** Total performance obligations (contracted backlog) rose more than 20% from Q4 in the usual seasonal rebuild. However, management saw "no significant improvement" in the economy or in China and was watching a stronger dollar. Dhingra summed it up: "While we are seeing early strength in consumables to start the year, it's being offset by capital constraints that are continuing to weigh on instrument purchases."

### Strategic messages

Thaysen called the quarter "a decent start to the year" but said management remained "cautious due to the persistently challenging global macro environment where customers are still constrained in their purchasing decisions." He organized his remarks around three priorities.

**Priority 1: driving the top line.**

- **NovaSeq X.** Illumina shipped 55 X instruments, taking the installed base above 400.
- **Consumables.** The 25B kit (launched Q4 2023), the 1.5B kit (Q1 2024) and an upgraded 10B flow cell completed the X product range.
- **Mid-throughput.** XLEAP-SBS, Illumina's newer sequencing chemistry, reached mid-throughput customers through a new P4 flow cell on existing NextSeq 1000/2000 instruments. Feedback was "exceptional," with read counts and quality above specification. Customers also praised the accuracy of on-board DRAGEN, Illumina's genomic analysis software.
- **Partnerships.**
  - Pillar Biosciences, a partner since 2017, won FDA approval for a pan-cancer IVD on MiSeq Dx. An IVD (in vitro diagnostic) is an FDA-regulated diagnostic test.
  - Bristol Myers Squibb joined Johnson & Johnson Innovative Medicine in collaborating on Illumina's whole-genome MRD assay. MRD (minimal residual disease) testing detects traces of cancer remaining after treatment.
  - Thaysen said to "expect to see more of these types of activities."

**Priority 2: operational excellence.**

- In March, Illumina merged marketing and commercial into one "customer first" global organization.
- It is improving the supply chain.
- It adjusted pricing across the portfolio "to better cover our global operational cost."
- It exited some Bay Area and San Diego facilities.
- In China, new regional head Jenny Zheng is pursuing an "in China for China" approach, with more local manufacturing and local partnerships.

**Priority 3: resolving GRAIL.**

- In April, the European Commission approved Illumina's divestment plan. The plan allows either a sale or a capital-markets route such as a listing, and Illumina had progressed on both.
- A capital-markets route requires capitalizing GRAIL with about $1 billion, which is about two and a half years of funding under GRAIL's plan and includes GRAIL's existing cash.
- Terms would be final by the end of Q2.

**Customers and multiomics.** Customers told Thaysen they were scaling projects on the X, doing whole-genome MRD work, and sequencing more deeply. They were also adding multiomics (combining DNA with other molecular layers such as RNA and proteins) and using methylation, which is chemical tagging of DNA, as a disease marker. Thaysen called multiomics "a significant opportunity." He described the Partek software acquisition as "a building block" toward "sample to answer" offerings that cover the whole workflow, and said "Illumina is at the heart of the ecosystem."

**Strategy update.** A full update would come at a virtual event in the fall. It would emphasize sample-to-answer offerings, keep Illumina's open platform, and lean on its "decade-long technology roadmap" in "an increasingly competitive space," with the goal of "making customers the heroes in their labs."

**X placements.** Illumina placed 58 X Plus systems, for a base of 527, about 40% of it at clinical customers. The X Series passed $1 billion in cumulative revenue.

**Capital.**

- Illumina issued $500 million of bonds at a 4.65% coupon and, with cash on hand, repaid the costlier $750 million term loan.
- It closed the Fluent acquisition.
- It bought back 770,000 shares for $98 million, an average of $127.71 per share, under a newly authorized program.

### Guidance

Dhingra was "disappointed in our lower revenue guidance." The economy "does not support any uptick in purchasing behavior through the end of the year," even though utilization and pull-through stayed strong.

The table below shows the updated 2024 guidance and how it changed from August.

| 2024 item | New | Versus Q2 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Down about 3% (Q4 about $1.07B) | Lowered to the low end of the prior range |
| Instruments | Down mid-30s % | Unchanged |
| Consumables | Upper end of low single-digit growth | Unchanged |
| Operating margin | 21–21.5% | Raised |
| Tax rate | ~24% | Lowered |
| EPS | $4.05–4.15 | Raised |

**Q4 assumptions.**

- MiSeq i100, a new low-throughput benchtop sequencer, would add minimal Q4 revenue, and legacy low-throughput sales would dip as customers wait for it.
- Second-half X shipments would still exceed first-half shipments.
- 2025 guidance would come around the January JPMorgan conference, along with the usual placement update.

### Strategic messages

Thaysen said customer funding "remain[s] healthy" but the near-term economy "has been persistently constrained." He described his first year as refocusing on the core, launching a strategy, resetting leadership and building a customer-first structure, "a good start to our multi-year transformation journey." He felt "confident" about high-single-digit growth by 2027.

On customers, he admitted core-lab directors had shown him "we needed to do better" and said they now see Illumina "present itself in a new way."

**Products.**

- **MiSeq i100.** Features include room-temperature reagents (no freezer needed), faster runs and 18 proven workflows. Customers called it "a game changer." Early-access units would ship in late Q4.
- **Constellation.** This mapped-read technology adds genomic information beyond standard SBS (sequencing by synthesis, Illumina's core chemistry). It runs library prep (preparing DNA for sequencing) directly on the flow cell, cutting hands-on time from hours to minutes.
- **5-base genome.** This delivers variant and methylation data from a single prep. Constellation and the 5-base genome would launch over the next 12 to 18 months, with data due at ASHG (a major genetics conference) later that week.
- **NovaSeq X.** The single-flow-cell X and the 100- and 200-cycle 25B kits remained on track for year-end.

### Analyst Q&A

The dominant theme was a feared "clinical cliff": the worry that clinical customers moving their tests to the cheaper X would cut consumables revenue sharply in 2025.

**Doug Schenkel (Wolfe): what drops in Q4, and is there a clinical cliff?**

- Thaysen said "I've heard this clinical cliff a few times, and I don't think that's the way we look at the business right now." Customers move test by test over a year to 18 months, and clinical volume was already above 40% on the X.
- The Q4 trim reflected missing year-end instrument buying.
- Dhingra said the Q4 consumables dip is "simple seasonality" from holidays.

**Puneet Souda (Leerink): mid-throughput and 2025.**

- Thaysen said competition "keeps us on the toes."
- Under China head Jenny, China revenue was flat sequentially, "a change in pattern," but "too early to call it up."
- Mid-throughput weakness is mostly economic: small biotechs are outsourcing sequencing instead of buying instruments.
- He reaffirmed positive growth in 2025.

**Vijay Kumar (Evercore): price and the margin base.**

- Dhingra said the per-gigabase price decline was "closer towards 25 rather than 20," at the high end of the 20–25% Analyst Day model, because the transition accelerated.
- The 500-basis-point target runs from a 21% base and relies on gross margin in the "high 60s to 70% range."

**Dan Brennan (TD Cowen): research versus clinical, mid-throughput and 2025 placements.**

- Dhingra said both research and clinical "grew nicely." He first said consumables grew 6%, then corrected it to 7%.
- No mid-throughput or 2025 X-placement guidance was given.

**Tycho Peterson (Jefferies): clinical pricing and contracts.**

- Thaysen said validation costs sometimes keep tests on the 6000. Migrating tests are often larger or deeper, "so the elasticity is still working out." On the cliff: "That's not how we see it."
- Multi-year oncology contracts, which Illumina calls the open offer letter, are not tied to a specific instrument and carry over to the X.
- Dhingra said consumables still grew high single digits because more than half of volume is already on the X.

**David Westenberg (Piper Sandler): can new tests be revenue-positive, and what about Q4 seasonality?**

- Thaysen said oncology customers are adding markers, including methylation, which require deeper sequencing.
- Dhingra said Q4 instruments are guided up sequentially. X consumables' shorter shelf life encourages year-end inventory management.

**Conor McNamara (RBC): where is the $20–25 million cut?**

- Dhingra said instrument deal closings were "more variable of late." He suggested splitting the cut roughly equally across instruments, consumables and services. Services is affected because milestones on companion diagnostic (CDx) deals were delayed into 2025 following FDA approval of the TSO Comprehensive assay. A CDx is a test that identifies patients for a specific drug.
- A "$10.70 billion-ish" figure in the transcript appears to be mis-transcribed.

**Dan Arias (Stifel): when will the macro headwind abate?**

- Thaysen said "can't wait for the day" macro talk ends. He pointed to "green shoots" in consumables and the i100, but noted the next day's US election "is going to change everything again."
- Dhingra said consumables are the leading indicator; instruments still have to play out.

**Patrick Donnelly (Citi): cost-savings pacing.**

- Dhingra was comfortable with the $100 million of 2024 savings. The $200 million program would come from global capability centers, shipping changes, manufacturing consolidation and R&D review, with somewhat more sales and marketing reinvestment. He saw no unusual headwinds.
- Thaysen said this "is what good companies do."

**Subha Nambi (Guggenheim): free cash flow and buybacks.**

- Dhingra said nothing unusual drove free cash flow. Conversion of net income into cash was about 150% and should stay well above 100%. Buybacks would at least offset dilution, then be opportunistic. M&A would be revenue-accretive tuck-ins, and no bonds come due soon.

**Sung Ji Nam (Scotiabank): Europe versus the Americas.**

- Thaysen said the US faces more mid-throughput competition and tougher X comparisons, which should ease going forward.

**Ben Mee for Mason Carrico (Stephens): Fluent's role.**

- Thaysen said Fluent's instrument-free approach can "expand the market opportunity for all of us," and Illumina remains committed to its single-cell partners.

**Dan Leonard (UBS): low throughput and XLEAP.**

- Thaysen said the i100's impact would come over 12 to 18 months, starting in 2025. More than 60% of NextSeq users had adopted XLEAP.
- Dhingra said low-throughput instruments are about 1% of revenue or less.

## Q4 2024 (call held February 6, 2025)

### Results against expectations

**Revenue and earnings.** Revenue was $1.1 billion, up about 1% year over year in constant currency. This was the first year-over-year growth in this series, and it beat company expectations on higher X shipments.

Dhingra reported EPS of $0.95, above company expectations on higher revenue and a lower tax rate. Seeking Alpha's header instead showed EPS of $0.86, a $0.06 miss, and revenue down 1.60%, beating by $554.68K. The revenue gap is probably reported versus constant currency; the EPS gap is unexplained.

**Segments.**

- **Consumables: $698 million, up about 2%.** The increase was mainly high throughput, with mid-throughput growing against an easy comparison.
- **Instruments: $155 million, down 3%.** This was much better than earlier quarters, because MiSeq i100 sales partly offset weak mid-throughput.
- **Services and other: $151 million, down 1%,** because of partnership timing in the prior year. Service contracts, CDx revenue and informatics grew.

**Margins and earnings.**

- Gross margin was 67.4%, up 270 basis points, despite a seasonally heavy instrument mix.
- Operating expenses were $526 million.
- Operating margin was 19.7%, up 120 basis points.
- The tax rate was 23.7% for the quarter and 23.6% for the full year.
- Net income was $152 million on about 160 million shares (transcribed as "$160 million").

**Cash and buybacks.**

- Operating cash flow was $364 million and capital spending $42 million, giving free cash flow of $322 million.
- Illumina bought back 134,000 shares for $17 million, about $129 each. Cash ended at about $1.22 billion.

**Regions.** In constant currency:

- Americas, more than half of revenue: up 3%.
- Europe: up 3%.
- The region transcribed as "MER": down 10%.
- Greater China: up 1%.

**Transition and placements.**

- Illumina placed 91 X systems, for a base of 630. 2024 pull-through averaged $1.3 million per system.
- More than 65% of high-throughput gigabases and about 40% of high-throughput consumables revenue were on the X.
- Total gigabases grew more than 30%.
- Savings exceeded $100 million in 2024.

### China

Thaysen opened with China. Days earlier, China's Ministry of Commerce had taken an action that analysts described as adding Illumina to an "unreliable entity list." Thaysen said Illumina was "in dialogue with the relevant parties and promptly seeking additional information to reach a resolution." He said Illumina complies with local laws, that China is about 7% of revenue, and that the market holds "significant opportunity." An analyst put China at about $300 million in 2024. Management declined to discuss timelines or whether the action could be reversed.

### 2025 guidance

Guidance repeated the mid-January outlook, except that the tax rate was lowered to about 22.5%. It excludes any China impact and assumes current economic and political conditions continue. Management was also watching Washington developments on research funding.

The table below summarizes the outlook.

| Item | 2025 guidance |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Low single-digit growth, weighted to the second half |
| Consumables | Low single-digit growth, led by clinical |
| Instruments | Low single-digit decline |
| Operating margin | ~23%, up 170 bp from 2024 |
| EPS | $4.50–4.65, about +10% at the midpoint |
| X transition | ~75% of high-throughput gigabases on X by the second half (Thaysen also said "by midyear") |
| China | Assumed roughly flat |
| Q1 2025 | Revenue flat to down 1%, operating margin ~20.5%, EPS $0.93–0.98 |

Total sequencing revenue was expected to grow in line with total company revenue. In Q1 2025, consumables were expected to rise from Q4 and grow year over year, while instruments would likely decline year over year because of fewer instrument sales and partnership timing.

**Long-term targets.** Thaysen reaffirmed the 2027 targets and named a 26% operating margin. He listed three growth drivers:

1. The core sequencing business as the X transition progresses.
2. Scaling multiomics in 2025 and 2026.
3. Expanding data and services.

### Strategic messages

Thaysen called 2024 "transformative," citing the new strategy, the X transition, a new leadership team, and margin gains in a tough market that "showcase… the new Illumina operating model."

**Partnerships.**

- **NVIDIA.** The partnership pairs Illumina software with NVIDIA's AI tools.
- **Truveta Genome Project.** Working with Truveta, Regeneron and US health systems, the project will sequence 10 million exomes (the protein-coding portion of the genome) "exclusively using Illumina technology."

**Products.**

- **Single-flow-cell NovaSeq X.** Began shipping in December.
- **New 25B kits.** The 100- and 200-cycle kits support Illumina's single-cell solution, formerly Fluent's PIPseq.
- **MiSeq i100.** More than 70 units were placed with early-access customers by year-end.
- **Proteomics.** A workflow built with Standard BioTools includes a 50,000-sample UK Biobank pilot.
- **Constellation.** Early access was extended, and customers valued its detection of structural variants (large DNA rearrangements).
- More news was promised at the AGBT conference later in February.
