# **Change at the Helm for Focused Commercial Push** 

We wish outgoing CEO Christian Henry the best, as his tenure wasn’t always smooth sailing, but he always made it fun. New CEO Mark Van Oene helms the ship and narrows the focus to drive SPRQ-Nx conversion, transition clinical customers from validation/pilot programs to production scale, and... 

_... to complete the HT platform capable of handling the expected volumes from Basecamp._ 

With mgmt changing, we truly enjoyed our time with outgoing CEO Christian Henry and believe he did a great job instilling a new vision and bringing the technology into the forefront of genomics. At the same time we are excited to watch incoming CEO Mark Van Oene continue the strategy of penetrating the clinical opportunity in a focused way. More specifically, he laid out very specific KPIs for success over the next year, including 1) driving clinical adoption and to convert users from pilot programs/validation stage into more production volumes, 2) converting users to SPRQ-Nx quickly, and 3) to get the HT platform out for next year so it can handle the Basecamp volumes at an approachable cost. 

To us, the harder goal is to convert the clinical customers into production stage volumes, particularly in the US and in smaller labs. Mgmt has done a good job in EU and in natl. health systems for the adoption of HiFi for rare disease testing. In the US, the market is very different and more fragmented, and the reasons why clinical labs have not moved into production scale vary by each customer. These reasons range from an inability to handle the data analyses, to wanting more automated upfront workflows, to getting used to the technology vs. their legacy bias on short-reads. So this is going to be more hand-to-hand for the commercial organization. To address this challenge, they have already started making changes to the commercial team, getting more targeted and strategic with marketing, and bringing in the right people for the right clinical customers to press the conversion. 

Change is good and we understand the mgmt transition came with the reorg, guide cut, and overall honing in on the strategy. On guidance, it seems more to do with the air pocket caused by SPRQ-Nx than poor demand for the technology. We should see most of the existing Revio customers that can convert be ready to transition by the year end, and as such, the air pocket associated with the transition is likely to drag out to a trough in 3Q/4Q, which led to the guide cut. Overall, a disappointing headline, but when we dig deeper it is not as bad as it looks. It is just a quarter where we see a lot of changes and time will tell on the success of the new strategy.

## Key Notes From The Call 

**CEO transition effective immediately** 

- CEO responsibilities transitioned immediately from Christian Henry to COO Mark Van Oene, who previously led R&D, operations, commercial execution, and the rollout of Revio, Vega, and SPRQ-Nx. 

- °<sup>Christian Henry will remain on Board and in advisory capacity to Mark Van Oene.</sup> 

- Mark highlighted EMEA's clinical commercial success as the operating model he intends to scale globally, with priorities centered on clinical sequencing adoption, SPRQ-Nx utilization, and enabling larger-scale genomics programs. 

##### **Revio placements at 20 in 2Q comes in ahead of our expects driven by clinical customers** 

- PACB shipped 20 Revio systems in 2Q26 (vs. our estimate of 15), with 60% of placements going to new customers and 45% sold as part of multi-instrument orders. 

- °<sup>Several multi-system Revio orders were tied to customers scaling production sequencing</sup> programs, including clinical accounts and population-genomics initiatives unlocked by SPRQ-Nx economics.

- Mgmt highlighted a significant new population-scale customer (incremental to Basecamp) - evidence of traction in large scale sequencing markets. 

- Revio pull-through at $202k in the quarter was pressured by SPRQ-Nx transition, leading to the annualized pull-through guide-down to $200k-$225k. 

##### **Vega still pressured by A&G weakness, but ASPs recovered and PACB is seeing new opportunities emerge** 

- Vega shipments at 26 systems vs. 38 in the prior-year quarter as A&G funding constraints continued to pressure capital spending. 

- Despite lower unit volume, 81% of Vega placements were to new customers and mgmt noted that Vega ASPs returned to normalized levels after promotional pricing used in 1Q26. 

- Public health laboratories represented an area of strength, with wastewater testing and other smaller/targeted applications beginning to contribute to adoption. 

**Reorganization targets Clinical execution without impacting key R&D programs** 

- The restructuring will reduce the workforce by ~40 employees and further aligns resources toward high priority growth initiatives. 

- Marketing was integrated more closely with the commercial organization to improve clinical customer engagement and replicate successful EMEA go-to-market practices globally. 

- Management explicitly stated that no key R&D platform programs were impacted by the reorganization 

- °<sup>$15M - $20M annual compensation expense savings are expected to benefit 2027.</sup> 

**Clinical momentum continues to offset persistent weakness on the academic side** 

- Clinical consumables shipments grew +67% year over year and represented a mid-teens percentage of total consumables shipments in 2Q. 

- Multiple publications in the New England Journal of Medicine and Nature Genetics were highlighted as strengthening evidence supporting HiFi long-read sequencing as a first-line clinical diagnostic approach. 

- A&G funding constraints remained the primary HW across the Americas and parts of APAC. No meaningful recovery is assumed. 

**EMEA remains the growth engine and serves as the blueprint for expansion in other regions** 

- EMEA revenue increased +52% y/y, driven by hospitals and clinical customers transitioning from pilot projects into routine production sequencing. 

- °<sup>PACB pointed to the region's clinical commercial success as the model for future execution</sup> in the Americas and APAC. 

- °<sup>Clinical account engagement strategies developed in EMEA are being integrated into the</sup> broader commercial organization. 

**SPRQ-Nx adoption progressing as expected but validation cycles delay pull-through, driving the guide-down**
