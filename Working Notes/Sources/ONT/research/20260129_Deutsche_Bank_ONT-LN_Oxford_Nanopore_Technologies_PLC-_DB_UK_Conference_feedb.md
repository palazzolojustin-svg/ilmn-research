#### Summary 

This report summarizes insights gained from our recent meeting with Nick Keher, Chief Financial Officer of Oxford Nanopore, at the dbAccess UK&I Conference, covering the company's strategic direction, growth opportunities, and competitive positioning. The overarching message is one of increasing momentum outside of core Research, driven by uptake in Clinical and Biopharma applications, and a clearer path towards profitability.

**DB view:** We see ONT as a UK champion with disruptive technology and large addressable markets to penetrate. Controlled costs and a shift to a capital pricing model are further sharpening the path to profitability and cash flow break-even. A healthy cash balance (c.£300m) means they are well-funded to achieve this. Despite superior growth, the shares trade on 4.5x EV/sales for FY26, representing a 20-30% discount to sequencing peers (ILMN 5.5x, PACB 6.3x), and in our view, continue to offer good value. We reiterate our Buy rating, with our TP rolled to 225p (blended DCF and EV/sales). **ONT remains one of our top picks in UK SMID Healthcare for 2026.** 

#### **Key takeaways:** 

#### Diversification & high-growth segments 

Revenue is increasingly diversified, stemming from a higher quality, less concentrated customer base. While Research (66% of revenue) remains foundational to the company, we are starting to see a momentum shift towards the higher-growth applied markets. Nanopore sequencing is increasingly well validated, while higher growth applied markets could be c.50% of group sales in time. 

- n **Research:** Grew 15% in FY25, primarily driven by government and academic funders. Teen growth remains realistic, with potential for improved performance in the Americas. This segment provides a stable base in our view. 

- n **Clinical:** This segment grew 60% in FY25, particularly strong in infectious disease diagnostics where their rapid turnaround times and long-read capabilities are proving highly disruptive. - n **Biopharma:** Poised for acceleration, as adoption in QA/QC for mRNA, biologics, and sterility testing gains traction. This segment could contribute 20-30% of group revenue in time as evaluation phases convert and contracts materialize. 

- n **Industrial Applied:** Grew 27% in FY25, benefitting from synthetic biology and service labs. 

Competitive differentiators - true long-read & speed 

Crucially, ONT's small form factor, rapid turnaround, direct methylation detection and ability to read "very long" DNA strands provide an advantage over competitors. They differentiate clearly from Illumina's short-read limitations and while PacBio may have good long-read capabilities, it is seen as lacking direct methylation, scalability for high throughput, and requires larger, more complex instrumentation. While Roche is actively developing and using a nanopore-based sequencing approach, ONT highlights its limited read length and inability to do direct methylation, rendering it more aligned with Illumina's short-read approach. Nick Keher, CFO, noted that while Roche's device could destabilize the short-read market on price, ONT does not view it as directly competing with its core long-read advantages. 

Path to profitability/cash flow break-even becoming clearer 

The path to profitability is indeed sharpening, albeit a couple years away. They remain confident in achieving break-even by FY27 (Cons/DBe: FY28), underpinned by their 30% CAGR target, improving gross margins (increased throughput & flow cell recycling), and tight cost control. ONT maintains a healthy cash balance of c. £300m and, in our view, is well-funded to reach break-even. 

Fresh leadership to build on the legacy of founders 

As previously announced, Gordon Sanghera will step down from the Board on 2 March 2026 and will remain in an advisory capacity through to early 2027. Nick Keher, CFO, acknowledged past challenges with "launching things too early," indicating a more mature and deliberate approach going forward. This is significant, in our view, especially considering the incoming new leadership. Francis Van Parys was recently appointed as his successor (Danaher, GE Healthcare, Cytiva and currently Radiometer) and, in our view, appears to be a good fit given global experience in large companies across Biopharma and clinical diagnostics, as well as regulatory experience. This bodes well for the company's objective to accelerate growth into the applied markets.

Macroeconomic fluctuations often account for most of the risks associated with exposures to instruments that promise to pay fixed or variable interest rates. For an investor who is long fixed-rate instruments (thus receiving these cash flows), increases in interest rates naturally lift the discount factors applied to the expected cash flows and thus cause a loss. The longer the maturity of a certain cash flow and the higher the move in the discount factor, the higher will be the loss. Upside surprises in inflation, fiscal funding needs, and FX depreciation rates are among the most common adverse macroeconomic shocks to receivers. But counterparty exposure, issuer creditworthiness, client segmentation, regulation (including changes in assets holding limits for different types of investors), changes in tax policies, currency convertibility (which may constrain currency conversion, repatriation of profits and/or liquidation of positions), and settlement issues related to local clearing houses are also important risk factors. The sensitivity of fixed-income instruments to macroeconomic shocks may be mitigated by indexing the contracted cash flows to inflation, to FX depreciation, or to specified interest rates - these are common in emerging markets.  The index fixings may - by construction - lag or mis-measure the actual move in the underlying variables they are intended to track. The choice of the proper fixing (or metric) is particularly important in swaps markets, where floating coupon rates (i.e., coupons indexed to a typically short-dated interest rate reference index) are exchanged for fixed coupons. Funding in a currency that differs from the currency in which coupons are denominated carries FX risk.
