# **Guidance cut a clearing event?** 

Growth guide reduced to 21-25% for 26 and 27, vs >30% CAGR 24-27E previously. We see this as a clearing event for the stock, with execution against new, achievable growth targets likely to drive a rerating in the shares. We present feedback from CFO meetings and updated margin and cashflow analysis. 

Following H2'25 results and our recent conversations with management (Conference Takeaways, 11 Mar), we update numbers and transfer coverage to Jon Unwin. ONT has reduced its growth outlook for the next two years, but continues to target achieving adj EBITDA breakeven by 2027E. We believe lowering mid-term growth guidance removes an overhang from the stock (consensus was already below guidance), and  that execution against new, achievable growth targets should drive a rerating in the shares. We forecast a 27% constant currency revenue CAGR 2025-2030E, and see the biggest opportunity for outperformance in Biopharma, which management has indicated could account for 20% of Group sales by 2027. However, our risk adjusted estimates see Biopharma at just 13% of sales by 2028. In this note we provide updated revenue, cost and cashflow analysis which make us incrementally more positive on gross margin expansion in 2026 along with ONT's ability to contain operating costs and to satisfy its cash requirements without requiring a capital raise. We continue to view the shares as undervalued relative to peers, given ONT's significantly higher top-line growth potential. 

**Changes to numbers.** We lower our growth outlook, reducing revenue by 4% in 2026E and 8% in 2027E. We increase our gross margin expectations for 2026 and 2027, and lower our adj operating expense growth expectations. Ultimately, we still believe adj EBITDA breakeven will most likely occur in 2028E (rather than 2027E as guided) but expect growth to be the dominant narrative for the stock. We expect positive OCF in 2028E and positive Equity FCF by 2029E. 

**Valuation and price target.** We forecast ONT will grow at a 24% reported revenue CAGR 2025-2028E. The average EV/Sales (2026E) for the covered peers is 4.0x , compared to ONT at 3.3x. We believe ONT deserves a 35% premium to the peer group at a 5.5x EV/Sales multiple to reflect its premium growth. Arguably the premium should be higher - PacBio (UW, covered by Luke Sergott), ONT's closest competitor, is trading on 5.5x Sales despite offering just 9.2% 25-27E revenue CAGR, compared to ONT's 24%, but we risk adjust to reflect historical guidance adjustments. Our updated cashflow analysis now implies a cash positive position in 2026, and as such, despite number cuts and a trimming of our multiple, our price target increases to 172 GBp (from 170 GBp). We reiterate our Overweight rating.

## Top-line guidance cut a clearing event? 

2025 constant currency growth was 24%, and management has indicated it expects 2026E and 2027E growth to be in 21-25% range. This would make ONT the fastest growing stock in our coverage. Previously, however, management had expected to be able to grow even faster at >30% CAGR 2024-2027E. We explore the possible reasons for the step-down in growth expectations below. We see the new targets as clearing an overhang from the equity story, providing a way for a valuation re-rating should management execute against the new targets, which are achievable, in our view, given the increasing relevance of non-Research end-markets in ONT's revenue mix. 

#### **Why was 2025 growth below initial expectations?** 

Purchasing model changes and price increases, which were decided on post-issuance of the original midterm growth outlook, made up c.4% of 2025 growth. This suggests “underlying” growth of c.20.0% (Figure 1) - one-third below initial expectations of >30%. Reconciling this to the 30% mid-term guide  is a 3% headwind from NIH funding and a 1.5% headwind from China export restrictions, both of which were unknowns at-/ have worsened since the the company provided the original mid-term guidance. That leaves a net 5.5% underperformance vs expectations which according to management has been driven in part by a slower market, but also internal execution issues including product launches not performing as well as hoped, such as the GridION Q for the Biopharma space, and the-now-discontinued Elysion. In addition, management has commented that they could have executed better in China (Conference Takeaways, 11 Mar).

## Feedback from our conversations with management 

We have spent time with management regularly over the past few months, most recently at our Global Healthcare Conference (Conference Takeaways, 11 Mar), and summarise key points from their comments below. 

- **Purchasing model change:** H1'25 benefit to gross margin was +350bps, full-year 2025 benefit was +400bps, with top-line impact similar. Benefit should all be felt by end of 2026. Primary benefit is from selling devices and consumables at a higher gross margin, offset partially by a mix shift to more devices (which are lower margin than consumables). For 2026 GM benefit could be 100-200bps. 

- **Contracts:** 

- °<sup>**EMEAI:**$10m of contracts rolling off to be offset by UK Biobank of ~$10m (2026 impact, full</sup> contract is low-teens millions). The two contracts net off, but there is no growth. May be some timing differences as UK Biobank ramps through the year, but others a H1 headwind. 

- °<sup>**APAC:**PRECISE contract rolling off is a $3m headwind in H1'26 (contract was $6m split</sup> roughly 50:50 H2'24 and H1'25). APAC going to look a little weaker in H1 as a result. 

- **Gross Margin improvement:** Improving yield of PromethION flow cells, plus recycling, can help to improve PromethION gross margin by 10pp. That would translate to a 65% gross margin, which could be possible exiting 2027E. 

- **Adj EBITA breakeven:** Further modulation of the cost base is possible. Adj EBITDA breakeven is defined as £0m or above. 

- **Adj EBITDA margin:** No reason why over time, adj EBITDA margin can't increase to mid-20s. 

- **NIH:** Represents 50% of the Americas business, and grew 3% in 2025 vs expectations of 20% when setting the mid-term guidance. As such, it has been a 3% headwind to Group growth in 2025. For 2026 NIH environment looks stable, rather than growing or declining. 

- **US outlook:** H2'25 was more constructive than H1'25 and they expect it to be even more constructive in 2026. 

- **China:** Export restrictions were a c. 1.5% headwind to growth in 2025, driven by restrictions on NVIDIA chips, plus restriction lists of who ONT can sell to. NVIDIA chips being able to be sold in China would not lead to instant full relief of this headwind. China could be the wild card for APAC growth in 2026; it is 44% of revenue in APAC, and it grew teens percentage in 2025. Management thinks it can execute better in China. Export restrictions from flow cell sales backlog is in LSD$m; this is getting worse not better. 

- **Product launches:** Elysion and GridION Q launches underperformed expectations. Mk1D minION has done okay. GridION Q 2.0 expected to be launched in Q1'26. Q-line products are for regulated environments and need to be "locked down" to comply with regulation; ensures standardisation and repeatability. 

- **Capex model and consumables pricing:** Combined these two effects have contributed roughly 4% of revenue growth in 2025. Consumable list-price increases are not the realised increase in ASP as ONT offers volume discounts. Under previous opex-first sales model, revenue for the device was recognised over a one-year period, in line with the consumables
