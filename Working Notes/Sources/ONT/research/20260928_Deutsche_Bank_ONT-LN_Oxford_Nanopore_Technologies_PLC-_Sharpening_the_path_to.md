### Oxford Nanopore at a glance 

Oxford Nanopore Technologies specialises in the development and commercialisation of nanopore-based sequencing technologies used for information-rich, rapid, accessible and scalable analysis of DNA and RNA. The platform is also being developed for the analysis of proteins and metabolites. Its novel features provide richer data and faster results through a more affordable and accessible platform than legacy technologies. The platform is protected by an intellectual-property portfolio comprising more than 2,500 active patents across more than 250 patent families. 

Its technology is used by researchers and industry to analyse DNA and RNA, supporting advances in diagnostics, personalised medicine and food production. Oxford Nanopore has a global presence across North America, Europe and AsiaPacific. While its headquarters and primary manufacturing are in the UK, the company operates through direct sales teams and distributors worldwide. 

- n **Research (65% of revenue):** The majority of ONT’s users are research scientists seeking to understand fundamental biology or develop methods that apply genomic data across broader markets. 

- n **Applied Markets (35% of revenue):** Molecular information can support real-world decision-making across healthcare, BioPharma, Industrial and other settings. ONT aims to enable routine applications that deliver richer biological insights in real time and often at the point of care. Applied Markets comprise Clinical at 15% of revenue, Industrial at 12% and BioPharma at 8%.

### Investment Case 

ONT offers differentiated sequencing technology, an expanding opportunity beyond Research and a credible route towards profitability. The new strategy is intended to close the gap between the capabilities of the platform and the commercial value captured, concentrating investment on applications where richer biological information, speed and flexible deployment materially improve the customer outcome. 

#### Differentiated technology with a selective right to win 

ONT combines native DNA and RNA sequencing, long and ultra-long reads, methylation, real-time analysis and flexible device formats. We see the strongest competitive position in applications where these capabilities materially change the biological or clinical answer, rather than in commoditised high-throughput sequencing. The more selective strategy should help turn technology leadership into complete, adoptable workflows. 

#### BioPharma and Clinical provide the growth acceleration 

Research remains ONT’s largest market and should continue growing, but BioPharma and Clinical are expected to deliver most of the acceleration towards the >$700m 2030 target. BioPharma offers the nearer-term opportunity as validated QC workflows can become recurring and transferable across molecules. Clinical is the larger longer-term opportunity, although adoption requires evidence, reimbursement, regulation and laboratory integration. 

#### A clearer path to profitable growth and cash flow breakeven 

Adjusted EBITDA losses have narrowed materially, and management continues to target breakeven in FY27 and positive FCF in FY28. The principal levers are underlying revenue growth, further gross-margin improvement and operating leverage. Management indicated that consumables gross margins are already c.75%, with potential above 80%, while Devices & Services gross margins are c.34%, with a longer-term path towards c.40%.The licensing agreement provides further high-margin upside but is not required to deliver breakeven. Management expects net cash to remain above c.£100m through breakeven, reducing financing risk and allowing continued investment in innovation, workflow development and manufacturing capacity. The balance sheet also provides flexibility to pursue partnerships or selective M&A where these can accelerate adoption without undermining financial discipline. 

#### Superior growth at a peer discount 

Consensus expects ONT to deliver a c.17% sales CAGR over FY25-28, materially ahead of sequencing and broader tools peers. ONT nevertheless trades at a discount to direct sequencing comparators, reflecting its pre-profitability status and execution risk. Delivery against the new strategy, stronger consumables pullthrough and progress towards breakeven should support re-rating potential. 

#### Key investment debate: Commercial conversion 

The central risk is not whether ONT’s technology is differentiated, but whether the company can translate that differentiation into scaled, recurring revenue. Progress should be assessed through consumables utilisation, conversion of BioPharma evaluations, clinical workflow validation and recurring customer adoption, rather than device placements or technical milestones alone.

### Expansion into higher growth applied markets 

- n **Research:** Research remains ONT’s largest end market and a core strength, representing c.65% of H1-26 revenue. Growth is currently midsingle digit, reflecting US Research budget pressure and programme comparatives, but we expect it to improve towards low double digits over the medium term as headwinds moderate and priority applications gain traction. Research should grow in absolute terms but fall to c.40% of group revenue by 2030 as BioPharma and Clinical scale faster. The latest NIH disbursement data remain consistent with stabilisation in the US Research funding environment, although evidence of a broader recovery remains limited. 

- n **BioPharma:** We see BioPharma as the principal growth engine through 2030. ONT is targeting R&D and manufacturing QC workflows where its technology can consolidate multiple assays into simpler, faster tests with richer information. Around 20 customers are evaluating the technology across mRNA, direct RNA, cell and gene therapy and manufacturing QC. Validation can be lengthy, but adoption should become recurring and transferable across molecules once embedded in manufacturing processes or regulatory filings. 

- n **Clinical:** Clinical is the largest longer-term opportunity, focused on rare disease, rapid whole-genome sequencing, tumour profiling and acute infectious disease. Long reads, phasing and native methylation provide clear differentiation, but adoption is constrained by evidence, regulation, reimbursement and laboratory integration. We expect strong growth through 2030, with the larger inflection beyond 2030 as workflows progress from specialist laboratories towards broader IVD deployment. 

- n **Applied Industrial:** Industrial is no longer a material driver of group acceleration. Management expects steady mid single-digit growth across plasmid sequencing, food safety, synthetic biology and biosecurity; we assume mid-single digit. Industrial should consequently remain a relatively small component of Applied Markets and group revenue through 2030.

#### US Research funding: Stabilisation intact, but recovery remains tentative 

The latest NIH disbursement data remain consistent with stabilisation rather than a broad-based recovery in the US Academic & Government market. Monthly disbursements improved from c.$3.6bn in July to c.$3.9bn in August, leaving Q3to-date disbursements at $7.5bn. This follows $11.0bn in Q1 and $11.4bn in Q2, when disbursements increased 5% YoY and 3% QoQ and provided the first clearer evidence that the deterioration in US Research funding may be bottoming. Deutsche Bank’s July channel checks also highlighted improving order activity across consumables and selected capital equipment, although a broader capex recovery was considered more likely in 4Q26 or 2027. 

With September still to come, Q3 is tracking broadly in line with recent sequential levels but below a demanding prior-year comparison. September could still benefit from the fiscal year-end funding cycle: separate NIH awards data show that 89% of the estimated FY26 extramural grants budget had been obligated by 31 August, with $3.7bn remaining to be deployed. While not directly comparable with the Treasury disbursement series, this supports the potential for a stronger final month. 

For ONT, the updated data do not yet point to a meaningful near-term acceleration in Research demand, but they continue to reduce the risk of further deterioration in the funding environment. Improving NIH trends are therefore more important as the removal of an external headwind than as an immediate growth catalyst. A broadly stable Q3 outcome would be modestly supportive heading into FY27, although sustained improvement in laboratory purchasing and capital-equipment demand is still required before calling a broader recovery.

### Nanopore sequencing is increasingly well validated 

Publication trends remain supportive of ONT’s scientific relevance and expanding research ecosystem. ONT’s publication output has increased substantially relative to PacBio and Illumina, supporting the breadth of applications being developed on the platform. However, publications are a lagging and imperfect proxy for commercial adoption. Studies often follow instrument purchase and data generation by several years, while publication growth does not necessarily translate into recurring consumables demand, validated Clinical workflows or BioPharma revenue. We therefore view the data as evidence of scientific validation and platform engagement, rather than a direct indicator of near-term sales. 

- n **A lagging measure of adoption:** Customers typically purchase an instrument, generate data over months or years and subsequently publish their findings. Publications can support future demand by expanding awareness of the technology and enabling follow-on research, but do not provide a contemporaneous measure of utilisation or revenue. 

- n **ONT continues to gain scientific mindshare:** ONT publications increased from fewer than 100 papers in 2014 to almost 1,900 in 2025. Growth accelerated from 2020 and remained robust despite a more challenging Research environment. PacBio publication growth has been materially slower, with ONT now generating roughly twice as many annual publications. 

- n **Gaining relative to Illumina:** Illumina remains substantially larger in absolute terms, but its publication output has broadly plateaued since 2020-21. ONT increased from c.2% of Illumina’s annual output in 2017 to more than 30% in 2026, highlighting growing relevance within sequencing ecosystem. 

- n **Commercial proof requires different measures:** As ONT expands into applied markets, publications may become a less complete measure of adoption because commercial work is less likely to be published. Consumables pull-through, customer retention, workflow validation and recurring revenue will therefore become more important indicators of commercial progress. 

**The growing scientific evidence base supports ONT’s technological relevance, but commercial success will ultimately depend on how effectively this differentiation translates into validated workflows, utilisation and competitive advantage.**

### Competitive landscape 

The latest Illumina and PacBio calls validate the Clinical and BioPharma opportunities targeted by ONT, but also highlight intensifying competition. Illumina brings scale, mature workflows and a broad ecosystem, while PacBio is improving HiFi economics and Clinical evidence. Our channel checks and preliminary expert feedback support the view that ONT remains genuinely differentiated where long and ultra-long reads, native DNA/RNA analysis, real-time results and flexible deployment materially improve the customer outcome. However, the technology remains commercially underleveraged, with accuracy, output consistency, workflow standardisation and computational requirements cited as barriers to broader routine adoption. 

Management has acknowledged the correct competitive weakness: adoption remains below potential and product development has historically been driven too heavily by scientific possibility rather than customer needs. The new focus on fewer high-value applications, complete workflows and partnerships is therefore appropriate, but ONT must convert its technical differentiation into recurring commercial adoption before competing platforms narrow the gap. 

- n **Clinical validates the strategy, but raises the execution bar:** Illumina’s growth is increasingly Clinical-led, while most PacBio Revio placements went to Clinical accounts. This supports ONT’s focus, but shows that customers require complete, validated and standardised workflows rather than sequencing performance alone. ONT’s differentiation must therefore be supported by clinical evidence, regulatory readiness, laboratory integration and dependable output. 

- n **Illumina remains the scale and workflow benchmark:** Illumina’s installed base, customer relationships and mature ecosystem generate durable consumables demand. Channel feedback suggests ONT is better viewed as complementary to short reads in many applications, rather than a broad substitute. Recent P2i placements must therefore translate into sustained utilisation and consumables pull-through. 

- n **PacBio is the more direct long-read threat:** SPRQ-Nx lowers PacBio’s listed HiFi cost per genome by c.30%, strengthening its position in wholegenome sequencing, rare disease and large cohorts. Preliminary feedback suggests PacBio may remain preferable where high consensus accuracy and predictable output are critical, while ONT offers advantages in read length, native analysis, real-time results and platform flexibility. PacBio’s uneven consumables pull-through nevertheless provides no clear evidence of rapid displacement of ONT. 

- n **Utilisation matters more than placements:** PacBio customers are validating new workflows and working through inventory, while ONT delivered strong device growth but only 2.7% consumables growth. For both companies, recurring consumables demand following validation and installation is the key measure of adoption. 

- n **BioPharma is the key nearer-term growth opportunity:** ONT is targeting R&D and manufacturing QC workflows where native long reads could consolidate multiple assays into simpler workflows. Around 20 BioPharma customers are evaluating the technology, with conversion into validated, audit-ready and recurring use central to delivering the 2030 targets.

### Risks 

- n **Commercial execution and adoption:** ONT’s medium-term targets require faster conversion of customer evaluations and device placements into recurring consumables revenue, particularly across BioPharma and Clinical. Slower workflow validation, weaker utilisation or delayed contract conversion could reduce growth and operating leverage. 

- n **Competition and pricing:** ONT competes with larger, more established sequencing and life-science tools companies. Improvements in competing platforms, including lower sequencing costs, higher throughput and broader workflow ecosystems, could constrain market share, pricing and consumables pull-through. 

- n **Research and geographic exposure:** Research remains ONT’s largest end market, leaving demand exposed to academic and government budgets. Export controls and commercial disruption in China, geopolitical uncertainty in the Middle East and weaker US Research spending could also create revenue volatility. 

- n **Clinical and BioPharma execution:** Expanding into regulated and routine applications requires clinical evidence, validated workflows, laboratory integration, reimbursement and regulatory capabilities. Longer-thanexpected adoption cycles would challenge the acceleration in BioPharma and Clinical required to deliver the 2030 revenue target. ONT has made progress in clinical collaborations, but wider adoption remains dependent on workflow maturity and commercial execution. 

- n **Profitability and cash generation:** The investment case assumes continued gross-margin progression, operating-cost discipline, FY27 adjusted EBITDA breakeven and positive FCF thereafter. Slower revenue growth, adverse mix, higher compute or manufacturing costs, or additional investment requirements could delay profitability and increase cash consumption. 

- n **Technology, manufacturing and product quality:** ONT must continue improving sequencing accuracy, output, reliability and cost while scaling manufacturing capacity and maintaining product quality. Delays in product development, lower manufacturing yields or supply-chain disruption could affect adoption and margins. 

- n **IP, partnerships and royalties:** ONT relies on a substantial intellectualproperty portfolio and increasingly on partnerships to accelerate market access. Patent disputes, weaker protection, partner execution or lowerthan-expected counterparty product sales could reduce anticipated licensing and royalty income. The new royalty stream is excluded from guidance because the counterparty’s future revenue cannot yet be reliably forecast.

Upside surprises in inflation, fiscal funding needs, and FX depreciation rates are among the most common adverse macroeconomic shocks to receivers.
