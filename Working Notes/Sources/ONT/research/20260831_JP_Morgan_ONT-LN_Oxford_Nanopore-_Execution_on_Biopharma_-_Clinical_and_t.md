# **Oxford Nanopore** 

### **Overweight** 

###### **ONT.L, ONT LN** 

#### Execution on Biopharma & Clinical and transition to profitability should continue to support re-rating 

Price (26 Aug 26):182p 

**▲Price Target (Dec-27):220p** Prior (Jun-27):190p 

Following Oxford’s 1H 2026 results last week (see our take <u>here) as well as new CEO</u> Francis van Parys’ strategic update, 2030 outlook issuance, we update our forecasts with MSD-HSD Revenue cuts from 2027-30, though with a significant 38% Adj. EBITDA upgrade in 2026 followed by more modest changes beyond, given we remain confident in the achievability of Adj. EBITDA breakeven in 2027.  For 2030, we see ONT’s organic ex royalty target as achievable, with Biopharma and Clinical growth drivers both increasingly coming into view, including the mRNA personalised cancer vaccine opportunity following MRNA’s Phase III adjuvant melanoma trial success last week as well as ONT’s expanded Cepheid relationship on infectious diseases. We move to a Dec-27 PT of 220p (from June-27 190p) based on 5.5x 2027 EV/Sales largely driven by the inclusion of 33p/share, primarily from the royalty stream on the diagnostics licensing agreement, with execution on ONT’s strong 20% 2025-30 Revenue CAGR together with transition toward profitability expected to continue to support a re-rating.

## Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks 

**Oxford Nanopore** _(Overweight; Price Target: 220p)_ 

##### **Investment Thesis** 

In 2026, we expect ONT performance to be driven by operational execution on expansion into Biopharma and Clinical/Applied use of Nanopore sequencing, which should improve confidence in ONT’s ability to deliver a 20% Revenue CAGR from 2025-30. We forecast a 2025-30 Revenue CAGR for ONT of 20%, driven by expansion into Biopharma and Clinical (32% CAGR) as well as expanding use in Research (8% CAGR). In 2026, we forecast 25% LC Revenue growth for ONT and a -£29m Adj. EBITDA loss, which should increase confidence in ONT’s ability to achieve their Adj. EBITDA breakeven target by 2027. On our forecasts, ONT is currently trading on 4.5x 2027 EV/Sales, a discount to peers Illumina (c. 7x 2027 EV/Sales) and PacBio (c. 5x 2027 EV/Sales), despite ONT offering a superior growth profile relative to both, given we forecast a 2025-30 Sales CAGR for ONT of 20%, ahead of Illumina’s 8% 2025-30 Sales CAGR and PACB’s 12% 2025-30 Sales CAGR. We set a Dec-27 PT of 220p, 5.5x 2027 EV/Sales, which we see as undemanding for a strong mid-term growth story. 

_(Note Illumina is covered by JPM US Life Sciences and Tools Analyst Casey Woodring; Moderna is covered by JPM US Biotech analyst Jessica Fye)._ 

##### **Valuation** 

We set a Dec-27 PT of 220p, 5.5x 2027 EV/Sales, which we see as undemanding for a strong mid-term growth story.  On our forecasts, ONT is currently trading on 4.5x 2027 EV/Sales, a discount to peers Illumina (c. 7x 2027 EV/Sales) and PacBio (c. 5x 2027 EV/Sales), despite ONT offering a superior growth profile relative to both, given we forecast a 2025-30 Sales CAGR for ONT of 20%, ahead of Illumina’s 8% 2025-30 Sales CAGR and PACB’s 12% 2025-30 Sales CAGR _._ 

##### **Risks to Rating and Price Target** 

Key downside risks to our Overweight rating include: 

- Competition from well-established short-read Optical sequencing companies, in particular from Illumina, with their very large installed base, and competitive pricing and accuracy. 

- Future competition from other medium-longer read sequencing companies, such as PacBio, with their competing Optical sequencing approach, or Roche with their SBX Nanopore product, albeit at present this is just short read and doesn’t capture methylation. 

- Eventual competition from other long-read Electrical sequencing companies, as and when ONT patents expire. 

- Failure to execute on margin improvement / reaching profitability, due to either pricing pressure and hence slower Gross Margin improvement, or due to higher OpEx, as the company continues to invest in the business.
