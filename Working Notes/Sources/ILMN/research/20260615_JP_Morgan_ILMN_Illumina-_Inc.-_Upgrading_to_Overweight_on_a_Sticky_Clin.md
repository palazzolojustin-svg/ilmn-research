Illumina (ILMN) has been one of the most highly debated stocks in our space over the last year and a half since Roche (ROG.SW, covered by J.P. Morgan analyst Richard Vosser) introduced its Sequencing by Expansion (SBX) technology underpinning its new Axelios 1 sequencer (see our AGBT <u>note</u> for more). We have been on the sidelines this year with a Neutral rating, looking for more visibility into the competitive landscape in addition to Academic & Government (A&G) budget constraints. That said, over the last few quarters we have become more comfortable with ILMN’s customer stickiness, particularly among large, high-growth clinical customers. Our survey (see slide deck <u>here) of 62 respondents (30 research and 32</u> clinical) leans toward a favorable outlook for ILMN, showing that both research and clinical customer groups expect instrument and consumables spending in 2027 and 2028 to be above ILMN’s current organic growth trends and long-term target of +HSD organic growth. These customers also indicated that ILMN’s overall NGS share is expected to increase over the next two years. Relative to our broader coverage universe, we view ILMN as a diversified derivative play on the structural tailwinds seen in our Specialty Diagnostics (Spec. Dx) names across therapy selection and MRD. Beyond test volume growth, we have consistently seen and heard from experts (see our <u>AMP</u> and <u>ASCO</u> takeaways) that sequencing intensity is growing as diagnostics companies shift to WGS and transcriptome sequencing, which should serve as an added tailwind for ILMN over time. All in, we model ILMN reaching +HSD organic growth in 2027 with a +mid/high-teens adj. EPS growth profile through 2030E (vs. Core Tools average of +MSD organic growth and +LDD adj. EPS growth in 2028E). Lastly, on valuation, ILMN is trading at a ~3x premium to Core Tools on 2027E EV/EBITDA vs. a historical average premium of ~15x (2015-2024) on 1-year forward EV/EBITDA, and has a leading FCF yield (~4-5% 2026-2028E) on par with high-quality Core Tools names (DHR, MTD, TMO, WAT). With that in mind, we are upgrading our rating on ILMN to Overweight and increasing our DCF-derived December 2026 price target from $125 to $185.

##### **<mark>Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation</mark>** 

#### **Investment Thesis** 

ILMN’s clinical franchise is anchored by major CLIA labs that have largely transitioned to the NovaSeq X and face meaningful switching friction, supporting a stickier-than-appreciated user base and a multi-year test volume runway that underpins our +HSD organic growth forecast through 2030E. Structural tailwinds across therapy selection, MRD, and MCED are further amplified by rising sequencing intensity as labs adopt WGS, RNA, and multiomic workflows. Competitive risk from Roche’s Axelios 1 appears manageable near-term given unresolved concerns around reusable flow-cell contamination, and higher DNA input requirements, while incremental upside comes from a recovering U.S. A&G end market into 2H26/2027 and ILMN’s expanding multiomics portfolio (Fluent, SomaLogic, StrataMap). With +mid/high-teens adj. EPS growth and a leading ~4-5% FCF yield, ILMN offers scarcity value at a 4.1x EV/EBITDA premium to Core Tools (well below its historical ~15x premium) making the relative valuation attractive even after the recent run-up. As such, we are upgrading ILMN to Overweight with a December 2026 PT of $185. 

#### **Valuation** 

Our Dec. 2026 price target of $185 is derived using a DCF methodology with a CAPM-derived WACC of 9.1% and terminal growth of 4.5%.

- **We see ILMN’s clinical customer base as sticky, with structural test volume tailwinds and increasing sequencing intensity.** At the end of the day, >40% of ILMN’s revenues are exposed to clinical diagnostics consumables (clinical customers comprise 65%+ of ILMN’s sequencing consumables) which is the fastest growing end market (besides maybe semis) in our coverage (+high-teens/>+20% annual volume growth across our Spec. Dx universe from 2026-2028E). All of the major CLIA labs are ILMN users and have transitioned (or are in the process of transitioning) testing to the NovaSeq X (76% of clinical HT Gb was shipped on NovaSeq X as of 1Q26) with the associated pricing headwinds largely gone exiting this year (recall, ILMN has consistently seen underlying consumables volume growth of ≥+30% using sequencing output in Gb as a proxy). Based on what we’ve heard from some of our Spec. Dx management teams, there is general intrigue, but they aren’t urgently planning to switch and are either actively transitioning to the NovaSeq X or have just transitioned. Given that pricing and reimbursement continue to be tailwinds across the space, we see the gross margin (GM) payoff from moving to Axelios 1 diminishing (especially in regulated workflows for FDA-approved assays given the added risk). Looking ahead, ILMN will benefit from structural tailwinds in the Spec. Dx space including TAM expansion in therapy selection (see our ASCO note), clinical adoption of MRD (with guideline support in the outer years providing a significant catalyst), as well as the commercialization of MCED/singlecancer screening assays. Paired with robust test volume growth, we see sequencing intensity per test likely continuing to expand over time as labs switch to/add larger panels (including WGS, which is an ongoing trend - see our AMP note) and add on RNA sequencing; WGS requires ~15x as much data (in Gb) per sample compared to WES per ILMN. All in, we see these market factors driving sustained +low/mid-teens clinical sequencing consumables growth in the outer years. 

- **There are also unanswered questions around Axelios’ workflow (specifically, the reusable flow cells) and pricing alone does not seem to be enough to fully convert customers.** Operationally, the reusable cartridge/flow-cell concept is a potential point of hesitation for clinical customers as industry participants (per AlphaSense Expert Insights) have flagged elevated contamination and cross-sample carryover risk and raised the question of whether performance can remain consistent across reuse cycles, which is an especially high bar for regulated, reproducible clinical workflows. Additional workflow concerns, including reportedly higher DNA input needs (limiting compatibility with low-yield specimens) and a fragmented analysis stack that depends on multiple third-party tools, may complicate integration and add “hidden” costs.

One customer noted, _“it is difficult to suddenly switch as we are using almost every product of Illumina and we do not need to add any more vendors,”_ while another said, _“we are currently receiving good services and do not have any reasons to evaluate any other vendor.”_

- **Key takeaways from our customer survey.** We conducted a survey (see slide deck <u>here)</u> of 62 ILMN customers, including 30 research and 32 clinical customers, primarily in North America (65%) with the remainder in Europe. Overall, the survey suggests a positive spending outlook, with respondents expecting instrument and consumables spending to increase notably in 2027 and 2028. For research customers, instrument spending is expected to grow +18.6% y/y in 2027 vs. 2026 and +19.0% y/y in 2028 vs. 2027, while consumables spending is expected to grow +9.0% y/y and +12.8% y/y, respectively. Meanwhile, clinical customers are expected to see even stronger growth, with instrument spend growing +22.2% y/y in 2027 vs. 2026 and +31.0% y/y in 2028 vs. 2027, while consumables are expected to grow +9.4% y/y and +14.0% y/y, respectively. Notably, consumable volume growth exceeded customer spending expectations for both research (spread between volume and spend is +2.4% in 2027 and +4.5% in 2028) and clinical (spread between volume and spend is +5.2% in 2027 and +5.1% in 2028), indicating favorable pricing for the customers, albeit below the -DD% pricing pressure we’ve seen for ILMN. In terms of expected ILMN share of NGS activity, research customers across all size cohorts generally expect a modest increase in ILMN share ranging from ~+1-3% increase, while among clinical customers, the increase is more concentrated with mid-sized customers expecting a ~+7% increase in share. When asked about Axelios 1, of the customers who were familiar with the instrument, research customers noted that they were more likely to purchase the instrument (with an average likelihood of purchasing at 6.2/10) vs. clinical customers (4.7/10). Among those considering a purchase, 8% expect to purchase within 6-12 months (skewed to clinical), 35% within 12-24 months (skewed to research), 32% in 24+ months (skewed to clinical), and 24% are not sure (skewed to clinical).

## Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks 

**Illumina, Inc.** _(Overweight; Price Target: $185.00)_ 

#### **Investment Thesis** 

ILMN’s clinical franchise is anchored by major CLIA labs that have largely transitioned to the NovaSeq X and face meaningful switching friction, supporting a stickier-thanappreciated user base and a multi-year test volume runway that underpins our +HSD organic growth forecast through 2030E. Structural tailwinds across therapy selection, MRD, and MCED are further amplified by rising sequencing intensity as labs adopt WGS, RNA, and multiomic workflows. Competitive risk from Roche’s Axelios 1 appears manageable nearterm given unresolved concerns around reusable flow-cell contamination, and higher DNA input requirements, while incremental upside comes from a recovering U.S. A&G end market into 2H26/2027 and ILMN’s expanding multiomics portfolio (Fluent, SomaLogic, StrataMap). With +mid/high-teens adj. EPS growth and a leading ~4-5% FCF yield, ILMN offers scarcity value at a 4.1x EV/EBITDA premium to Core Tools (well below its historical ~15x premium) making the relative valuation attractive even after the recent run-up. As such, we are upgrading ILMN to Overweight with a December 2026 PT of $185. 

#### **Valuation** 

Our Dec. 2026 price target of $185 is derived using a DCF methodology with a CAPMderived WACC of 9.1% and terminal growth of 4.5%. 

#### **Risks to Rating and Price Target** 

Downside risks include: (1) Worse clinical and research customer attrition to Axelios; (2) slower clinical sequencing consumables growth due to delayed guidelines/approvals in key diagnostics end markets such as MRD and MCED; (3) pricing pressure across sequencing instruments and consumables due to competition; and (4) minimal improvements in the research funding environment.
