## **Illumina Overview** 

Illumina, Inc. (ILMN) is up 64% since my rating upgrade to Buy from August 2024. The genomic sequencing company appeared to be turning the corner following the GRAIL spin-off. 

The following March, I downgraded the stock to Hold due to concerns over China headwinds and emerging competition from Roche Holding AG (RHHBY). That's where I've been since I was on ILMN. 

My last look was in June 2026. Illumina posted ~5% Y/Y revenue growth in Q1 and guided FY26 to between $4.52 billion and $4.62 billion. 

However, my DCF analysis revealed that ILMN was valued at an 8% CAGR and mid20s FCF margins. This seemed to price in the operating inflection that was occurring (e.g., opex discipline, share buybacks). That's why I held to "Hold," but upgraded to a more optimistic "market perform," from "market underperform." 

ILMN has since increased another 20.6%, versus S&P 500 gains of just 3.7%.

## **Q2 Earnings** 

Of course, when modeling, my focus is on revenue growth and FCF margins. So, let me get right to those figures (I'm sure you're aware of the topline numbers already). 

Revenue jumped 9.5% Y/Y (8.1% excluding impacts of currency, acquisitions, and China) to $1.16 billion in Q2. However, organic growth was really 6.5%. Free cash flow was $162 million—this actually dropped from Q2 2025 ($204 million). $162 million comes out to an FCF margin of ~14%. 

Management increased its FY26 revenue guidance to between $4.60 billion and $4.64 billion. This implies between 6% and 7% growth over FY25. 

Now, there are a lot of caveats associated with some figures.

## **Outlook** 

Q1 and Q2 operating margins were 21.9% and 22.5%. Q3 is guided to 24%. To hit the full year of 23.4% to 23.6%, Q4 has to be >25%. 

A similar dynamic is playing out with free cash flow ($251M in Q1, $162M in Q2)— which was flat Y/Y in H1 2026 despite over $100 million more in revenue. The full-year math _implies_ about $940 million, which means about $527 million in the back half (author estimate).

Now, my last DCF model implied that Illumina was valued at 8% CAGR and a mid-20s percent FCF margin, terminating at 2.5% growth and 27% FCF margin. Now that its stock is up 20%, I wanted to remodel. 

I'd argue now that the market is pricing in a major growth inflection and also sees Illumina returning to pre-GRAIL margins. 

Year 1 revenue of $4.62 billion is the FY26 guide midpoint. 

Management's 2027 target is for "high-single-digit" growth. I modeled a base revenue growth of 11%, which staggers growth as 7.7% in Yrs 1-3, 16.5% in Yrs 4-6 (MCED scaling, multiomics inflection, and research normalization), and 8.8% in Yrs 7-9. Growth terminates at 3%. 

And although the estimated FY26 FCF margin is ~20%, I modeled for 22%, 26%, and 30% for the three phases. Net SBC (which was 5.7% of TTM revenue) comes out to 17%, 22%, and 27%. 

ILMN's 1.09 beta informs the 9.74% WACC (discount rate).

It takes time for consumable revenue to follow instrument placements. Management has cited six to nine months to reach normalized pull-through, making the benefit show up next year. Then, of course, Illumina's game plan is to layer on multiomics (i.e., SomaScan, SomaSeq, TruPath, BioInsight) once customers are moved to the X.

## **Competition** 

Roche launched AXELIOS 1 in late June using sequencing by expansion with a US price of ~$750,000. Roche is first focusing on research and academic labs. Illumina's X lists at roughly $1 million. Interestingly, X placements suddenly exceeded 95 in Q2 (they were "over 80" in Q1) against a "50 to 60" framework. 

During the call, an analyst brought this up: 

_(...) you had a couple of stronger-than-expected placement quarters. Like come on, we are in the fourth year of instrument launch, and this is - these are the instrument numbers, something definitely to pause and think about. Was this at all a function of the market waiting for more competitive information? And related, how much of this is a function of an improving funding environment?_ 

Management's answer was that it was genuine demand. 

_I think it speaks to, again, the opportunity. I mean the value proposition we have with our customers. And so they feel that, with the conversation we have with them, they continue to see that Illumina is here to help them be successful. The innovations we continue to move on to our X platform also prove that there's a lot of leeway in that platform. So it is a platform to invest in. So I think it speaks to how we continue to drive innovation and, of course, also that this is a very healthy market. So I think that's a combination that is winning right now._ 

Mind you, this call occurred just four weeks after AXELIOS launched ~25% below X's list price.

So, alternatively—and I'm speculating here—it could have also been a pull-forward ahead of a competitive alternative. Illumina had every incentive to help customers buy _now_ (pricing, bundled terms, etc.). It's also possible that Illumina held shares by conceding on price. 

It'll be interesting to see if the 80+/quarter placement rate holds in H2. 

## **Conclusion** 

I think Illumina's operations appear much better at a glance. When you dig deeper, there isn't a whole ton to be excited about—especially within the context of its current valuation. 

Per my DCF model, ILMN is priced for low-double-digit CAGR and FCF margins approaching 30%. These are pretty high expectations given current results (mid-singledigit organic growth and ~20% FCF margins). It's definitely quite optimistic. And should any setbacks occur, the bottom line is figured to be low. 

That said, I don't view the valuation as _atypically_ unreasonable, especially given that genomic sequencing is slated for massive growth (double-digit CAGR in many projections). So, I'm inclined to keep ILMN at a "Hold" until there is more clarity on midprojection growth prospects (~2028+) and terminal value (how competition impacts the business).
