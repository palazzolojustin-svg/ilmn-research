## Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks 

**Adaptive Biotechnologies** _(Overweight; Price Target: $25.00)_ 

#### **Investment Thesis** 

As a leader in the expanding immune medicine market ($49B TAM across research, clinical diagnostics, and drug discovery), Adaptive Biotechnologies (ADPT) is uniquely positioned with its proprietary platform that decodes the genetic language of the body’s adaptive immune system. This platform underpins the company’s immunosequencing assay and its flagship clonoSEQ minimal residual disease (MRD) test for blood cancers. Recent quarters have seen a notable acceleration in clinical clonoSEQ MRD volumes driven by deeper penetration into the community setting, ongoing expansion of clinical guidelines, and increasing EMR integrations. Looking ahead to 2026, we see multiple catalysts including broader adoption of blood-based testing and expansion into under-penetrated indications. ADPT is also gaining traction with pharma partners seeking to leverage its differentiated immune medicine (IM) data assets. The recently announced Pfizer (PFE) data licensing agreement in December exemplifies the growing strategic value of ADPT’s platform and provides a clear path to upside for the IM business over time. 

#### **Valuation** 

Our December 2026 DCF-derived price target of $25 assumes a CAPM-derived WACC discount rate of 8.5% and +5% terminal growth. 

#### **Risks to Rating and Price Target** 

Downside risks to our rating and price target include: (i) Pipeline development hurdles could discourage, delay, or prevent progress in ADPT’s TCR-antigen training data initiatives, potentially resulting in delayed or unrealized milestone payments and revenue streams; (ii) Uncertainty around the timing and amount of reimbursement for ADPT’s clinical tests could introduce volatility to the business model; (iii) Intensifying competition in the NGS heme MRD market may pressure market share and pricing; and (iv) As ADPT approaches a profitability inflection point, the company may miss its targets if opex is higher than anticipated to support continued growth in the clinical MRD business.

## Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks 

**BillionToOne** _(Overweight; Price Target: $145.00)_ 

#### **Investment Thesis** 

BillionToOne (BLLN) is a molecular diagnostics company focused on women’s health and oncology testing. The company has pioneered a novel, proprietary single-molecule nextgeneration sequencing (smNGS) platform powered by patented quantitative counting templates (QCTs), enabling it to overcome the background noise that often limits the accuracy of traditional sequencing methods. This allows for more precise detection and measurement of genetic targets in patients’ blood. BLLN’s technical edge is translating into strong commercial momentum in its Prenatal testing portfolio and is driving rapid adoption of its Oncology testing portfolio. Additionally, BLLN has demonstrated a proven ability to reduce cost of goods sold through workflow improvements and to scale efficiently with disciplined capital deployment, resulting in gross margins and operating margins above its peers. We forecast above-market growth over the coming years as the company expands its salesforce, which remains relatively small compared to peers, supporting healthy margin expansion while also generating peer-leading free cash flow. Overall, we believe BLLN’s premium to the diagnostics peer group is justified, and as such, we rate BLLN as Overweight. 

#### **Valuation** 

Our December 2026 DCF-derived price target of $145 assumes a CAPM-derived WACC discount rate of 8.7% and +5.0% terminal growth. 

#### **Risks to Rating and Price Target** 

Downside risks to our rating and price target include: (i) MolDX denying coverage for Northstar Response based on data from the NORTH study or other clinical trials, which would negatively impact our Oncology revenue and operating profit forecasts; (ii) new competitive launches in Prenatal NIPT for recessive conditions, which could slow share gains and present downside to our revenue and profitability projections; (iii) delays in R&D for Northstar MRD, which could push out revenue for this product compared to our assumption of 2027; (iv) a slower-than-expected salesforce buildout, which could limit BLLN’s market expansion and pose downside risk to our top line estimates; and (v) higherthan-anticipated operating expenses required to bring MRD to market and expand the salesforce, which could hinder the profitability ramp we currently forecast.

## Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks 

**Guardant Health** _(Overweight; Price Target: $185.00)_ 

#### **Investment Thesis** 

A pioneer in the highly attractive liquid biopsy market, GH develops and commercializes proprietary blood-based tests for the detection and management of cancer, with use cases in Therapy Selection ($10B TAM), Recurrence Monitoring and MRD ($20B TAM) and Cancer Screening ($100B TAM). We continue to see a favorable setup for the stock given the early stage of liquid biopsy adoption, robust clinical validation, encouraging recent payor traction in Therapy Selection (both in clinical and biopharma end-markets). With MRD in early-innings of roll-out, and long-term upside coming from the company’s Shield platform in cancer screening, we remain Overweight. 

#### **Valuation** 

Our December 2026 DCF-derived price target of $185 assumes a CAPM-derived WACC discount rate of 8.4% and +5.0% terminal growth. 

#### **Risks to Rating and Price Target** 

Downside risks to our rating and price target include: (i) The timing of Medicare and commercial reimbursement adds an element of volatility to the business model due to the novelty of the liquid biopsy testing paradigm and inherent uncertainty around payor decision timelines, and any reimbursement delays could hamstring GH’s clinical test volume growth in the near term. (ii) Rapidly emerging competition could adversely affect the business model, particularly as the liquid biopsy market is a relatively new space (with a lot of opportunity), and several companies are investing significant resources into developing products to enter the market. (iii) Pipeline development hurdles can discourage, delay, or prevent progress and adversely impact GH’s entry into the recurrence monitoring and screening markets. (iv) Regulatory risk is inherent to the business model.

## Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks 

**Alamar Biosciences** _(Overweight; Price Target: $32.00)_ 

#### **Investment Thesis** 

Alamar Biosciences (ALMR) is a proteomics platform company focused on enabling ultrasensitive protein measurement for research today and clinical diagnostics over time. ALMR’s technical edge is translating into robust early commercial traction in the RUO setting, supported by growing external validation (100+ customer publications/preprints) and biopharma demand. Looking ahead, ALMR has a credible pathway to expand into the clinical end market via ARGO DX, leveraging beachheads in neurology and immunology and a strategy centered on trial embedding, LDT partnerships, and IVD partners. We forecast industry-leading growth as ALMR scales its installed base and drives high consumables pull-through in a razor/razor-blade model, supporting meaningful operating leverage and an inflection to EBIT positivity by 2030. Overall, we believe ALMR’s premium valuation versus peers is justified by its differentiated platform and superior growth profile, and we reiterate our Overweight rating with a DCF-derived December 2026 price target of $32. 

#### **Valuation** 

Our December 2026 DCF-derived price target of $32 assumes a CAPM-derived WACC discount rate of 9.2% and +5.0% terminal growth, with the change driven primarily by the WACC discount rate. 

#### **Risks to Rating and Price Target** 

Downside risks to our rating and price target include: (1) slower translational execution and/or delays or setbacks in FDA 510(k) clearance for ARGO DX, which could push out the shift from RUO to clinical; (2) greater competition in proteomics (including reimbursed LDTs and FDA-authorized blood biomarker tests), which could limit placements and consumables pull-through; (3) a concentrated assay menu and reliance on Alzheimer’s near term, where slower menu expansion and/or weak trial read-throughs could curb adoption and utilization; (4) A&G funding pressure, which could soften demand; and (5) negative outcomes in the Olink IP litigation (injunction, royalties/damages, or redesign), which could disrupt NULISA sales and raise costs.

## Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks 

**Illumina, Inc.** _(Overweight; Price Target: $220.00)_ 

#### **Investment Thesis** 

ILMN’s clinical franchise is anchored by major CLIA labs that have largely transitioned to the NovaSeq X and face meaningful switching friction, supporting a stickier-thanappreciated user base and a multi-year test volume runway that underpins our +HSD organic growth forecast through 2030E. Structural tailwinds across therapy selection, MRD, and MCED are further amplified by rising sequencing intensity as labs adopt WGS, RNA, and multiomic workflows. Competitive risk from Roche’s Axelios 1 appears manageable nearterm given unresolved concerns around reusable flow-cell contamination, and higher DNA input requirements, while incremental upside comes from a recovering U.S. A&G end market into 2H26/2027 and ILMN’s expanding multiomics portfolio (Fluent, SomaLogic, StrataMap). With +mid/high-teens adj. EPS growth and a leading ~4-5% FCF yield, ILMN offers scarcity value at a 4.1x EV/EBITDA premium to Core Tools (well below its historical ~15x premium) making the relative valuation attractive even after the recent run-up. As such, we reiterate our Overweight rating with a December 2026 PT of $220. 

#### **Valuation** 

Our Dec. 2026 price target of $220 is derived using a DCF methodology with a CAPMderived WACC of 8.9% and terminal growth of 4.5%. 

#### **Risks to Rating and Price Target** 

Downside risks include: (1) Worse clinical and research customer attrition to Axelios; (2) slower clinical sequencing consumables growth due to delayed guidelines/approvals in key diagnostics end markets such as MRD and MCED; (3) pricing pressure across sequencing instruments and consumables due to competition; and (4) minimal improvements in the research funding environment.
