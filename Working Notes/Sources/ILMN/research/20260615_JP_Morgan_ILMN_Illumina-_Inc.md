# PM summary: Illumina (ILMN) Customer Survey 

**Key takeaways from our ILMN Customer Survey** 

###### ◼ **<u>Survey Background:</u>** 

- We surveyed 62 ILMN customers, including 30 academic customers and 32 clinical customers. The purpose of the survey was to understand how current ILMN customers view the competitive environment, with particular attention to the impact that the introduction of the Axelios 1 instrument may have on the sequencing space. We therefore focused on respondents from North America, ~65%, while the remaining respondents were from Europe. 

- For academic customers, research areas focused on single cell biology and human genomics, and the primary role was scientist or researcher. For clinical customers, the types of clinical testing centered on oncology, including somatic testing and hematologic malignancies, and the primary roles were lab manager or supervisor and lab director. In terms of lab size, research labs were more normally distributed, with an average annual budget of about ~$3.2M, while clinical labs have an average monthly clinical sample volume of about ~2,140. 

###### **<u>ILMN usage trends and outlook expectations:</u>** 

- Academic labs spend, on average, ~16% of their annual operating budgets on sequencing instruments and ~13% on sequencing consumables; meanwhile, clinical customers spend ~2x more than academic customers on both sequencing instruments and consumables from a dollar basis. 

- For **instrument spending** , academic customers expect average spend in 2027 to increase ~+18.6% vs. 2026 and to increase ~+19.0% in 2028 vs. 2027. Clinical customers expect instrument spend in 2027 to increase ~+22.2% vs. 2026 and to increase ~+31% in 2028 vs. 2027. For **consumables spending** , academic customers expect average spend in 2027 to increase ~+9.0% vs. 2026 and to increase ~+12.8% in 2028 vs. 2027. Clinical customers expect consumable spend in 2027 to increase ~+9.4% vs. 2026 and to increase ~+14% in 2028 vs. 2027. 

- Across all respondents, the most commonly used instruments spanned low to high throughput platforms, with 77% owning MiSeq, 69% NextSeq 1000/2000, 58% NovaSeq X/X Plus, and 16% NovaSeq 6000. Outside of ILMN, other platforms included Oxford Nanopore PromethION, and PacBio Revio. 

- Looking at current vs. expected share of ILMN NGS activity by customer size, academic customers across all three size groups expect ILMN activity to increase by ~1-3%, while for clinical customers, only mid sized customers expect NGS activity to increase by ~+7%. 

###### **<u>Axelios 1 Customer Sentiment</u>** 

- Among academic and clinical customers who were familiar with the Axelios 1, academic customers were more likely to purchase the instrument, with an average purchase intent score of 6.2/10, vs. 4.7/10 for clinical customers (10 being most likely to purchase). 

- Among respondents considering an Axelios 1 purchase, the largest share, 35%, expects to purchase within 12-24 months, skewing toward academic customers. A close second, ~32%, expects to purchase in 24+ months, skewing toward clinical customers. And if these customers were to purchase an Axelios 1, the majority (65%) of respondents expect to reduce their ILMN footprint. 

- For academic customers evaluating purchasing the Axelios 1, the top decision factors are data quality, turnaround time, throughput/capacity, and cost per sample. For clinical customers evaluating the Axelios 1, the top factors are data quality, cost per sample, turnaround time, vendor service/support, and throughput or capacity. For academic customers not evaluating/considering purchasing the Axelios 1, the top barriers are data quality, cost per sample, assay/menu availability, and workflow ease/ automation. For clinical customers not evaluating the Axelios 1, the top barrier is data quality, cost per sample, throughput/capacity, and turnaround time.
