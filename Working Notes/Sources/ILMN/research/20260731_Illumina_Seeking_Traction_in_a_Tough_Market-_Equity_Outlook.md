### Long-Term Drivers 

#### Illumina's Recovery Timeline Brighter Amid Dynamic Backdrop 

Illumina's financial performance is tethered to demand for its sequencing consumables and instruments. Guidance for 2026 expects instrument sales to be down low single-digits to flat, with consumables up low to mid-single digits, excluding China. Guidance for 2026 calls for more than 5% core revenue growth, as China-related geopolitical friction and research-funding uncertainty continue to cloud the outlook. The company had significant revenue growth during the pandemic peak (2021) but has seen flat-to-modest declines since, despite the launch of a new flagship system. Management's 2027 targets include a 26% operating margin, high-single-digit core growth, and EPS growth in the double digits to teens. 

The spinoff of Grail in June 2024 let Illumina return full attention to core operations and removed a massive drain on profitability. (08/10/26)

### Outlook Under Pressure 

#### Illumina Must Rebuild Confidence in Trajectory, Position 

Illumina aims to create long-term value via a multiyear systems-upgrade cycle, its partnership model and momentum in population and clinical genomics. After underwhelming 2023-24 results tied to the NovaSeq X launch ramp-up, 2025 saw added pressure from China's unreliable-entity designation, with 2026 now shaping up as a measured step forward on 5% ex-China growth. (08/10/26) 

#### 2026 Outlook Firmer, Headwinds Still There 

Illumina will need to rebuild confidence in its ability to deliver on longer-term goals after a choppy stretch that followed an outsized 40% gain in 2021: core sales rose just 1% in 2022, then declined in 2023 and 2024 amid a slower-than-expected NovaSeq X adoption curve. What began as a hoped for 2025 turnaround ultimately proved muted, ending the year with just 2% organic revenue growth exChina as tariff headwinds and weak research demand offset clinical strength. Management expects mitigation actions -- pricing, supply-chain shifts, and cost cuts to increasingly take hold through 2026, alongside operating margin guidance of 23.4-23.6%, which includes a 100 bp headwind from the SomaLogic acquisition. The outlook still assumes a soft research market, with potential upside if NIH funding loosens. (08/10/26)

### Long-Term Sequencing Dominance 

#### Illumina's Broad Genomic Lineup Serves a Global Customer Base 

Illumina's reach in genomic tools was unparalleled for more than a decade. It serves a global market that spans research and clinical customers with an ever-broadening lineup of systems aimed at providing a solution for every setting. Competition is increasing from new entrants like Element Biosciences and Ultima Genomics, with Roche's return to the market now a reality. (08/10/26) 

#### Customer Base Expands With New Applications 

Illumina's customer profile is diverse both geographically and across applications. Genetic sequencing is increasingly common in commercial markets such as oncology, agriculture and reproductive health, bringing new customers. The majority of sales are still domestic, but the market abroad is growing as more countries fund genomic research. Government funding accounts for almost half of Illumina's sales. U.S. government action can temporarily cap growth, as budget sequestration and a federal shutdown did in 2012. Covid-19 added another wrinkle, as the timeline for academic and research labs was thought to dictate the pace of a recovery but 2021 saw record demand across instruments. 

Growth is shifting toward clinical end-markets, with consumables now 60% of sequencing revenue, driven by NovaSeq X volume and oncology demand. (08/10/26)

#### Holy Grail of Early Cancer Screening 

Illumina's 2016 formation of Grail aimed to commercialize a sequencing-based liquid-biopsy screen for cancer that had the potential to unlock a massive market opportunity. Illumina closed the Grail acquisition in August 2021 without first gaining EU approval. After an arduous dispute, it was mandated that Illumina divest Grail. Liquid biopsies are non-invasive blood tests that search for circulating tumor cells or DNA. The ability to catch early-stage cancers before patients become symptomatic represents a holy grail for clinicians. Without Grail in the equation, Illumina must now devise a new strategy to participate in screening growth. 

Companies Impacted: Liquid-biopsy tests are being pursued by a multitude of public and private genomic-product companies including Guardant Health, Tempus AI and Exact Sciences, among others. (08/10/26)

#### Helix Consumer Platform Pruned 

The creation of Helix, a consumer genomics platform, marked an evolution in Illumina's long-term strategy as sequencing became more accessible. The company worked with two venture investors funding the startup with $100 million. Helix was a natural extension as genomic sequencing becomes more accessible to consumers. The research market is maturing and each iteration of new instruments provides additional marginal performance gains than prior generations. 

After years of weighing on profitability, Illumina opted to deconsolidate Helix from its operations, effective in 2019. We viewed this as a prudent step to tighten the portfolio and increase flexibility as other sequencing drivers arise and gain more momentum. (08/10/26)

### Cash Choices Subdued 

#### Illumina's Capital Plans Far Less Tractable After Grail Deal 

Illumina's acquisition of Grail put a pause on share repurchases, which resumed at a modest pace in 2024. The divestiture order imposed a $476 million fine for closing the deal without approval, in addition to $35-$50 million of incremental charges, but will allow Illumina to keep exposure to the screening market by retaining a 14.5% stake and reestablishing its royalty agreement. (08/10/26) 

#### Financial Flexibility Returning After Grail 

Illumina was drawing increased questions about the use of its rising cash balance until the failed PacBio acquisition in 2020. That cash was ultimately earmarked for Grail when the company closed the deal and spent its cash hoard. The balance stood at $1.6 billion at year-end 2025 and they closed out the year with $931 million in free cash flow. It will likely require some time to regain its pre-Grail flexibility. The spin-off of Grail wrapped up in 2Q24, with Illumina retaining a 14.5% minority interest. Historically, capital has been deployed through share buybacks that largely offset the dilution of employee stock options. 

Illumina spent $115 million on repurchases in 2024 and $740 million in 2025, after not buying back any stock in 2022 or 2023. The most recent buyback program which has $1.8 billion remaining as of 2Q. (08/10/26)
