#### **SMART SUMMARY** 

## **Summary** 

The conversation covered the evolving landscape of sequencing technologies in Europe, focusing on the adoption of long-read versus short-read platforms, barriers to switching, and the competitive dynamics affecting market share among major sequencing providers. 

EXPERT BACKGROUND 

- Molecular biologist with a focus on sequencing and high-throughput assays, including experience at GenDX 

   - (NGS-based transplantation, supporting Illumina, PacBio, ONT applications), Bionano Genomics, and 

   - currently overseeing sales across 27 Central European countries for a leading long-read sequencing company. 

EXPERT SENTIMENT 

- The expert believes the shift from short-read to long-read sequencing is accelerating, with long-read 

   - technologies increasingly addressing previous bottlenecks and expanding into new applications. 

- They view Illumina's market position as vulnerable due to inflexibility, lack of customer-centricity, and the emergence of strong competitors offering better value or specialized solutions. 

- The expert predicts Illumina's market share in Europe will decline significantly over the next three to five years, though the overall sequencing market will continue to grow. 

#### TAKEAWAYS 

- Currently, 10-20% of new sequencing projects in Europe are built around long-read technologies, with this share growing as bottlenecks are addressed. 

- Clinical applications such as pharmacogenomics and rare disease benefit significantly from long-read 

   - sequencing, while pathology remains dominated by short-read due to sample storage limitations. 

- Illumina's entrenched position is supported by established workflows, bioinformatics, and pricing, but 

   - customer dissatisfaction and competitive alternatives are driving a gradual shift away from their platforms. 

- Government-funded initiatives in countries like Germany are enabling public labs to experiment with long-read technologies, while commercial labs remain more price-sensitive and loyal to Illumina due to cost and workflow integration. 

- The expert expects Illumina's European market share to drop from 75-85% to 55-60% in the next three to five 

years, as competitors like PacBio, ONT, Roche, Element, and Ultima gain traction.
