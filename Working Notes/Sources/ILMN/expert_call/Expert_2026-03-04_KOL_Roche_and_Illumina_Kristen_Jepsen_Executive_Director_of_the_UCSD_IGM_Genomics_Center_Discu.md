### **SUMMARY** 

### **Overview** 

The discussion focused on the latest developments in genomics technologies, particularly the Roche AXELIOS sequencer, and its implications for academic core facilities. The expert, a director at a genomics center, shared insights on the operational and market dynamics of sequencing technologies, comparing them with existing solutions like Illumina and Ultima. The conversation also touched on broader market trends, including funding challenges and the competitive landscape in genomics. 

### **Key Insights** 

- The expert expressed skepticism about the Roche AXELIOS sequencer's modularity and cost-effectiveness for core facilities, noting that its library preparation requirements and pricing do not align well with the diverse needs of academic labs. There is a perception that while the AXELIOS offers longer reads, its accuracy and pricing do not significantly outshine competitors like Illumina. 

The expert highlighted the importance of support and service, expressing concerns about Roche's ability to match the established support infrastructure of Illumina.

### **Applications & Use Cases** 

The AXELIOS sequencer is positioned for high-throughput genome centers rather than diverse academic core facilities, due to its specific library preparation and sequencing capabilities. Its longer reads are beneficial for applications like isoform analysis in single-cell RNA sequencing, but the expert noted that its cost and operational requirements limit its broader applicability. 

### **Quantitative Insights** 

|Metric|Value/Insight|
|---|---|
|AXELIOS Cost per Run|$2,400 for 16 whole genomes|
|Cost per Duplex Genome|$150 per sample|
|AXELIOS Sensor Reuse|Up to 20 times|
|Initial Investment|Approximately $48,000|
|Illumina X Plus Pricing|Competitive due to market pressure|



### **Market Strategy** 

The expert noted that Roche's strategy seems to target high-throughput centers, but its current pricing and operational model may not attract existing Illumina users. Illumina, on the other hand, is leveraging its established market presence and support infrastructure to maintain its competitive edge, offering discounts and new technologies like TruePath to retain customers. 

### **Stage & Timing** 

The Roche AXELIOS is in its early stages of market introduction, with plans to place around 20 units initially. The expert suggested that the technology is not yet ready for widespread adoption in academic core facilities, which are currently facing funding challenges and are cautious about investing in new, unproven technologies. 

Overall, the discussion highlighted the complexities and competitive pressures in the genomics technology market, with established players like Illumina continuing to innovate and adapt to new entrants like Roche.
