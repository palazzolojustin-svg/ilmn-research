#### **In our opinion, the notion that AI will disrupt wet lab work or replace it entirely is deeply flawed, if we were to put it lightly.** 

We have talked about this dynamic ad nauseum (here), but our conviction has only grown since the first Life Science Claude plugin was introduced. The assertion that AI will phase out the wet lab seems to oversimplify and overstate our understanding of biology.

To be sure, there are aspects to drug discovery that will become more efficient. For example, we have had Alphafold predicting protein structures for a few years now, experiment planning has been simplified going from days to hours, and other usage cases of AI as an efficiency tool that helps to increase the ROI on drug discovery. We get the pushback, as predicting how a molecule will behave in a complex biological environment seems ripe for AI disruption. However, our understanding of biology is incomplete at best and wrong at worst. This is why we do experiments. We need to fill in the gaps of our knowledge and the beautiful thing about science and discovery is that as soon as you fill a gap, 5 more open. Thus, there will be areas where AI can replace a lot of experimentation, but in those areas where we have a limited understanding of the science, we will still need to do the experiments to test our hypotheses. 

- In the very LT, we can see the case for some wet lab use to be AI’d, but that is after an acceleration in very specific data collection NT-MT. So, instead of less wet lab work, we see the likelihood that MORE wet lab work is necessary in the race to train up these AI models. We are already seeing this dynamic with TWST and the boom in its Drug Discovery business led by AI data collection. 

- °<sup>We are also seeing it on the data analysis side for large-scale proteomics/multiomics</sup> projects. The amount of data created by these projects is nothing short of impressive, and AI models/tools will only help to create deeper insights that open doors to new venues of research in a shorter amount of time. Claude’s plug-in for TXG single cell workflow is a perfect example of this, in our view. 

- Within pharma, we see a similar argument as within general research. AI is currently accelerating certain aspects of drug disco, including experiment design and iterating on the molecule early to increase the likelihood that the molecule reaches the FDA approval finish line (failing in the late stage is expensive). We see a couple opportunities for more wet lab work here, a) with less time spent on experiment design, scientists have more time to do wet lab work and b) with how scientists are now iterating more frequently in early development, they are doing more wet lab experimentation, feeding these results back into their AI models and repeating that step until the molecule has been optimized. Tl;dr, more time on scientists hands → more time feeding AI models and iterating on the molecule to increase the probability for FDA approval → more wet lab work. 

- Finally, we agree that AI has the potential to accelerate pipeline work and increase the probability that a molecule makes it through the pipeline. What is underappreciated is that this will likely increase ROI for Pharma (molecules more vetted in early phase because of AI, less spending on failed clinical trials) and increase the number of drugs coming through the pipeline (AI accelerated processes). We do not see why pharma arbitrarily lowers its ROI targets because more molecules are coming through. Pharma will spend on R&D if the ROI is there (which AI thus far has promised to elevate), which to us dispels the debate that Pharma will spend less on R&D and therefore wet lab work. 

#### **AI likely has more impact on CROs than core Tools, but we still think fears over AI’s disruption to CROs are overblown.** 

1. **Clinical research is one of the most regulated industries in the world, for good reason as they deal with life and death.** The Claude plug-in was cool, but there is no way that the FDA and other global regulating bodies are going to accept a complete shift in trial paradigms in a few-year time frame. The FDA only started allowing PDFs or digital submission of data in 2016/2017. 

2. **Clinical development outsourcing is here to stay.** We hear about parts of functions being brought in-house, and this may be true in some instances, but the broader trend of

##### **Valuation Methodology and Risks** 

##### **U.S. Life Science & Diagnostic Tools** 

##### **Avantor (AVTR / AVTR)** 

**Valuation Methodology:** Our $8.50 PT applies a ~10x multiple to our FY’26 EPS of $0.83. 

**Risks which May Impede the Achievement of the Barclays Research Valuation and Price Target:** Upside risks include (1) lower investments needed to execute on the turnaround than expected, (2) faster-than-anticipated strategic review process that improves fundamentals ahead of expectations, and (3) the business does not continue to lose share as seen recently. 

##### **Certara (CERT / CERT)** 

**Valuation Methodology:** Our $8 PT applies a ~10.5x multiple to our FY'26 EBITDA estimate of $131M. 

**Risks which May Impede the Achievement of the Barclays Research Valuation and Price Target:** Downside risks include (1) a reversal in acceptance/adoption of biosimulation data from the FDA and other regulatory bodies, 2) competitive threats from biosimulation software and techenabled service providers, and (3) industry risks around a potential slowdown in the biopharma R&D environment. Upside risks include (1) meaningful acceleration in bookings that drives upside to topline faster than anticipated, and (2) greater than expected pricing power which leads to better growth >LSD and margin expansion. 

##### **ICON (ICLR / ICLR)** 

**Valuation Methodology:** Our $120 PT applies a ~9x multiple to our FY’26 EBITDA estimate of $1.57B. 

**Risks which May Impede the Achievement of the Barclays Research Valuation and Price Target:** Downside risks include (1) synergy and integration risk associated with the PRAH acquisition, (2) elevated cancelations coming through from several COVID-19 vaccines/therapeutics gaining approval, and (3) CRO industry risks around the biopharma funding environment. Upside risks include (1) meaningful acceleration in bookings/ revenue growth, and (2) margins do not compress as much as feared due to mix and pricing pressures. 

##### **IQVIA (IQV / IQV)** 

**Valuation Methodology:** Our $210 PT applies a ~12.5x multiple to our FY'26 EBITDA estimate of $3.98B. 

**Risks which May Impede the Achievement of the Barclays Research Valuation and Price Target:** Downside risks include (1) failure to execute on its strategy to drive share gains in the biopharma services industry with the company's compelling suite of solutions; (2) a series of elevated cancellations over several quarters; (3) a failure to win new business; and (4) CRO industry risks around the biopharma funding environment, or the threat of AI materializes worse than expected.
