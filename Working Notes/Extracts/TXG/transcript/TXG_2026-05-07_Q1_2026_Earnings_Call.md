Analysts from Citi, William Blair, Wolfe Research, Stephens, Barclays, TD Cowen, Canaccord, Bank of America, Guggenheim, Deutsche Bank, Leerink, Jefferies, Stifel. · earnings call*

**Summary.** 10x Genomics reported Q1 2026 revenue of $150.8 million (up 9% year over year excluding prior-year settlement revenue), with consumables up 13% and instruments down 24%. The call centered on Atera, a new spatial biology platform that management called the most significant product introduction in company history, with initial shipments in the second half of 2026 (about 40 units across Q3 and Q4, mostly Q4). Full-year 2026 revenue guidance of $600-625 million was maintained, with Q2 and Q3 expected to step down because customers are delaying purchases of current spatial instruments to wait for Atera.

## Overview

This is the transcript of 10x Genomics' (TXG) first-quarter 2026 earnings call, held May 7, 2026. 10x Genomics makes instruments and reagent kits for two main fields. Single cell analysis (measuring gene activity in individual cells, sold under the Chromium brand) and spatial biology (measuring gene activity while preserving where in a tissue each molecule sits, sold under the Xenium and Visium brands). The headline event was the April 2026 launch of Atera, a new spatial platform.

The Seeking Alpha header reports an earnings summary: EPS of $0.07, which beat by $0.13, and revenue of $150.84 million (down 2.61% year over year), which beat by $4.28 million. The year-over-year decline reflects a one-time settlement revenue item in Q1 2025. Excluding it, management reports 9% growth.

The key points are:
- Q1 revenue of $150.8 million came in ahead of the consensus implied by the beat reported by Seeking Alpha.
- Consumables (the reagent kits customers must buy for every experiment) grew 13%, while instruments fell 24%.
- Full-year 2026 guidance of $600-625 million was maintained.
- Atera is expected to ship starting in the second half of 2026, mostly in Q4, with about 40 units sold across Q3 and Q4.
- Management expects Q2 revenue to fall sequentially from Q1 (a low single-digit decline) and Q3 to be similar to Q2, because customers are waiting for Atera.

## Q1 2026 results

### Headline revenue

- **Total revenue:** $150.8 million in Q1 2026. Excluding non-recurring settlement revenue in Q1 2025 (from patent litigation settlements), revenue was up 9% year over year. CEO Serge Saxonov rounded this to $151 million [Q1 2026 call].
- CFO Adam Taich said the quarter reflected continued momentum in key business drivers, plus "some benefit from orders received late in the fourth quarter that shipped in early January" [Q1 2026 call].
- Seeking Alpha's summary shows revenue was down 2.61% year over year on a reported basis, because Q1 2025 included the settlement revenue.

### Consumables and instruments

The table below shows year-over-year changes by revenue category in Q1 2026, as given by the CFO. Consumables are recurring and tied to usage; instruments are one-time capital purchases, so they track customer budgets.

| Category | Q1 2026 change vs. Q1 2025 | Driver cited |

| Total consumables | +13% | Growth in both Single Cell and Spatial |

| Spatial consumables | +31% | Xenium consumables |

Single cell consumables revenue rose less (6%) than reaction volumes (double-digit) because of pricing and mix. Saxonov said in Q&A that Flex Apex pricing and mix dynamics were "similar to what we saw in Q4" [Q1 2026 call]. A reaction is one unit of consumable use, so volume growing faster than revenue implies lower revenue per reaction.

Saxonov also said Single Cell again saw double-digit consumable reaction volume growth "driven by Flex Apex," and that new products "with more accessible pricing have been unlocking new ways of Single Cell demand." In Spatial, double-digit consumables revenue growth was "driven by Xenium momentum," and he said spatial biology "remains in the very early stages of adoption and the vast majority of the opportunity is still untapped" [Q1 2026 call].

### Geography

Excluding license and royalty revenue in Q1 2025, Americas revenue was up 9%, EMEA up 16% and APAC up 5% year over year [Q1 2026 call, Taich].

### Margins, expenses and cash

- **Gross margin:** 70% in Q1 2026 versus 68% in Q1 2025. The increase was primarily from lower warranty costs and lower inventory write-downs, partly offset by lower license and royalty revenue [Taich]. An analyst (Wolfe) noted the margin came in a little better than they and the Street expected.
- **Operating expenses:** Total operating expenses fell 15% year over year, mainly from lower outside legal expenses and lower personnel costs. Q1 2025 included a one-time gain on a patent litigation settlement; excluding it, operating expenses fell 20% [Taich].
- **Cash:** $540 million in cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities at quarter end, up $113 million year over year and up $16 million from the prior quarter [Taich].
- **One-year reflection:** Taich recalled that on the Q1 2025 call the company was in a highly uncertain macro environment after "drastic changes to government research funding" and withdrew full-year guidance. Since then the company has "consistently executed, improved our operating profile and have grown our cash balance by over $100 million. The business is in a meaningfully stronger position today."

## Guidance

- **Full-year 2026 revenue:** Maintained at $600-625 million. Excluding upfront revenue from 2025 patent litigation settlements, this represents 0% to 4% growth over full-year 2025 [Taich].
- **Consistency with February:** At the midpoint, the outlook is consistent with what was given in February, including double-digit growth in both Single Cell consumable reactions and Spatial consumables revenue.
- **Atera built in:** The initial outlook was built "with the Atera launch in mind," including the expectation that some customers may delay purchases of current Spatial products while waiting for Atera.
- **Gross margin:** Still expected in the mid-60s percent for the full year (Taich, in Q&A), despite Q1 at 70%.
- **Operating expenses:** Taich said the company expects 2026 operating expenses to be roughly flat year over year, while retaining flexibility to keep investing.

### Quarterly cadence

Taich laid out the shape of the year:
- Q1 was a higher proportion of full-year revenue than usual, partly due to the late-Q4 2025 orders shipped in January.
- Q2 revenue is expected to step down sequentially from Q1, reflecting lower Spatial sales as customers wait for Atera to ship. In Q&A he specified this as a low single-digit percentage decline quarter over quarter.
- Q3 is expected to be broadly similar to Q2.
- In Q4, Atera shipments are expected to begin contributing meaningfully, though initial Atera production capacity will be limited in 2026 as production ramps. The balance of the guide is reached in Q4.

## Prepared remarks: Atera and strategy (Saxonov)

### Why Atera was built

Saxonov argued that biology is highly complex and that tools have historically lacked the scale and resolution to address it. He also said the biology toolset has been fragmented: one set of technologies for tissues, another for cells and another for molecules. Spatial biology, he said, is the convergence of these, "measuring molecules, cells, and tissues together, preserving a full context at massive scale and incredibly high resolution." Until now, researchers faced trade-offs among throughput, specificity, sensitivity, scale and performance. Atera was built to remove those trade-offs.

He called Atera "the most significant product introduction in our history" and said it "is poised to become the large-scale discovery engine, whether for basic research, AI-driven science, or translational applications." He added: "We believe that now spatial will increasingly become the default approach for analyzing biological samples."

### What Atera does

- **Whole transcriptome (all expressed genes) at single-cell-like sensitivity.** For the first time, spatial whole-transcriptome analysis with single-cell sensitivity at scale, with sensitivity comparable to Flex Apex, which he called "the gold standard for single cell analysis."
- **Specificity and data quality.** Enabled by refined probe chemistry; he described this as non-negotiable so researchers can trust the data.
- **Throughput.** A single instrument can process up to 800 whole-transcriptome 1 cm² samples per year, or more than 3,000 using the targeted Atera Select panels. This enables large cohort studies not previously feasible.
- **Workflow.** Atera builds on the proven Xenium workflow, runs on standard off-the-shelf glass slides (making it scalable across labs, enabling distributed sample collection and access to archived samples from biobanks), and uses a universal sample preparation approach that works across tissue types, including different sample types on the same slide.
- **Software and compute.** Image processing, the most computationally intensive step, runs on the instrument using high-performance onboard GPUs and optimized algorithms, so data is processed in real time at the source. Customers can analyze on their own on-premise infrastructure or use the 10x Cloud platform for analysis and collaboration.
- **Roadmap.** Atera is built as a long-duration, upgradeable platform. Planned capabilities include workflow automation for true "sample-in, data-out," protein multiomics, in situ sequencing for spatial mutation and variant detection, and continued development of the 10x Cloud for multi-sample analysis and collaboration.

### Customer response and timing

- Saxonov said the early response "has been extraordinary" and exceeded already high expectations. One customer called it the largest technology leap they had seen in their career; another called its capabilities "the holy grail."
- The company is taking pre-orders, with initial shipments expected in the second half of 2026.
- "Even in a challenging capital equipment environment, customer conversations have immediately centered on how to incorporate Atera into their research programs, not whether to buy it." Often the discussion assumes it is a "must-have platform" and quickly moves to multi-year programs and large cohort studies.

### Expanded addressable market

Saxonov said Atera expands the addressable market across three high-growth segments:
1. **Discovery research:** initiatives like the Human Cell Atlas can map entire organs and tissues at unprecedented scale and speed.
2. **Translational research:** clinical cohorts can be characterized to understand patient response patterns. He cited collaborators already validating this, as shown by Carl June at AACR.
3. **Large AI models:** described as "an entirely new market," where Atera's "billion-cell-per-year capability" enables virtual models at a new scale.

### AI as a structural tailwind

Saxonov repeated that AI is "a significant and structural tailwind" because progress depends on generating "vastly more of the right kinds of data," and the company's platforms were built for that purpose. Flex Apex and Atera are seeing intense interest from customers building AI models of biology. Partnerships announced over the past year:

- Chan Zuckerberg Initiative: the Billion Cells Project.
- Arc Institute: the Virtual Cell Challenge.
- Xaira Therapeutics: building the largest perturbation dataset ever reported.
- Bioptimus (announced last month, April 2026): an AI-focused biotech building STELA, a global initiative to profile up to 100,000 patient tissue specimens across three continents, to build the data backbone for a "world model of biology." It starts on Xenium and plans to expand to Atera over time.
- Chan Zuckerberg Biohub (announced the week before the call): a new $500 million Virtual Biology Initiative to build AI models that simulate cells and tissues in silico. Saxonov said there is "increasing evidence that scaling laws apply in biology just like they do in other domains," that "many orders of magnitude more data" are needed, and compared the effort to the Human Genome Project in catalyzing a new era. He said, "As AI models improve, we expect an exponential increase in the demand for the kind of data our technologies produce."

### Translational and clinical

- Saxonov said interest is growing in translational applications across academia and biopharma, as customers seek human-relevant drug targets and biomarkers of response for therapies that only work in subsets of patients.
- Flex Apex has been "a game changer" for large-scale translational studies thanks to strong performance on FFPE samples (formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue, the standard preserved form of clinical specimens), streamlined workflows and a cost profile that enables large cohorts. Xenium has proven robust on clinical samples; Atera is expected to extend that with larger studies and more information per sample.
- A particularly large opportunity lies in later-stage translational settings, where biomarker strategies are essential for understanding patient response and toxicity. Over time these trends reinforce the potential for single cell and spatial in diagnostics.
- The next critical step is robust clinical evidence on large patient cohorts, pursued on two parallel paths: (1) supporting customers' studies and partnering to enable clinical deployment later; (2) the company's own internal efforts for specific high-value applications. Progress is being made in the two previously announced areas: tissue-based spatial profiling for oncology and blood-based single cell monitoring in autoimmune disease.

### Closing comments

Saxonov said the team "executed with relentless discipline" through the past year, launching multiple critical products including Flex Apex and Atera, and thanked the team.

## Question-and-answer session

### Patrick Donnelly, Citi

**Question:** Which end customers show the highest interest in Atera, and how should the launch timing affect the portfolio, given the expectation that some customers will delay Spatial purchases in Q2 and Q3?

**Answer (Saxonov):** Pre-order demand has been "really encouraging, really strong," and interest is "coming from everywhere": he could not point to any customer area not expressing interest. The company is focusing on customers who can scale quickly, serve demand for others (such as service providers) and evangelize the platform. On timing, order patterns were anticipated and "it's all going really very much in accordance with our expectations." Customers adjust thinking about future projects in light of Atera, putting some pressure on spatial business, but this is incorporated in the guide. Meanwhile customers continue running experiments on current products because they have established workflows, ongoing studies and grant budgets, and like the data. He added that customers deciding now on Atera "probably won't be getting their instruments until well into 2027, right, most of them," so existing Spatial products continue at a robust pace.

### Matthew Larew, William Blair

**Question:** Last quarter AI was a relatively low percentage of revenue; given the new initiatives, what could the AI-related addressable market (TAM) ultimately look like and how might it grow?

**Answer (Saxonov):** He did not give a number. He said AI is a structural tailwind: increasingly large, powerful models need large amounts of the right kind of data, and measuring molecules, cells and tissues at scale and high quality is "precisely what we have built" and "the central premise of the mission of the company." AI is already "really pervasive" across customer segments and applications; he does not think there is a large project where AI is not a big driver, if not the biggest. Even smaller experiments are often run with an eye to feeding AI models and scaling up later. So the AI wave "is really lifting all of it," as a structural accelerant across the product line and application set, "in many ways... by design."

### Madeline Mollman (for Doug Schenkel), Wolfe Research

**Question:** Will inflation in memory or petroleum-based plastics affect full-year gross margin, and will Atera ramping dilute margins?

**Answer (Taich):** The company is monitoring costs closely, including those two and other inflationary input costs, and the team is "managing that quite well." Margins are still expected in the mid-60s for the year. Atera shipments in the second half will be heavily weighted to Q4, and as typical in the portfolio, instrument margins are below those of the standard portfolio, so he anticipates "a little bit of margin pressure" as Atera moves in, particularly in Q4, but is highly confident of ending at mid-60s for the full year.

### Mason Carrico, Stephens

**Question:** How material is the Q1-to-Q2 Spatial instrument decline, how many Atera instruments will be placed in Q4, and how many placements can production support in 2027?

**Answer (Taich):** A step down is expected from Q1 into Q2 and Q3. Xenium will continue to sell, but at a lower rate than last year, particularly over the next two quarters, and this is built into guidance. On Atera, production is ramping, and between Q3 and Q4 the company anticipates selling "approximately 40 units," mostly weighted to Q4, all factored into guidance. He did not give a 2027 unit figure in his answer.

### Luke Sergott, Barclays

**Question:** What is the pricing strategy and positioning of Atera versus Xenium and the rest of the spatial portfolio? Is the plan to cannibalize other platforms onto one box, or to segment the market?

**Answer (Saxonov):** He said calling Atera the biggest launch in the company's history was intentional, and he is keenly aware of the company's history with the Single Cell improvement. Short-term impacts are planned for and incorporated in the guide. Looking to next year, Atera is "poised to really kind of really break open a lot of markets, a lot of opportunities, a lot of customer samples and a lot of new customers" by removing constraints:
- Whole transcriptome removes the customization constraint that limited applications and tissue types.
- Throughput lets users run a full large cohort rather than a few samples at a time.
- Off-the-shelf slides give access to archived samples and biobanks, multiplying available samples.

He said pricing thinking is consistent with enabling this very large expansion of opportunity (basic discovery, translational research, AI-driven applications). He did not state Atera prices. He said existing products, Xenium and Visium, are "really powerful," and for the foreseeable future "we expect them to be really, really robustly used"; over time more applications, customers and samples will migrate toward Atera, which would be "an amazingly great thing for us."

### Kyle Boucher (for Dan), TD Cowen

**Question:** Where is the jumping-off point for operating expenses given the large year-over-year and quarter-over-quarter decline?

**Answer (Taich):** Costs have been managed in a "super disciplined way." Excluding last year's one-time settlement gain, operating expenses are down 20% year over year. He anticipates giving flexibility to keep investing, with operating expenses roughly flat year over year for the full year.

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord

**Question:** (1) Why were Spatial instruments weaker than expected in Q1, and how should that line look going forward, especially with a possible market freeze? (2) Is Q2 guided to a mid-single-digit or low-single-digit decline quarter over quarter?

**Answer (Taich):** On Q2: a low single-digit decline quarter over quarter, with Q3 fairly similar and the balance achieved in Q4. On Q1 Spatial instruments: there was "fairly significant anticipation" of the April announcement. The company ran a campaign at AGBT (the genomics industry conference), word spread, and some early-access customers were working with the company under NDA. Customers who would have added a new Xenium or expanded a Xenium fleet decided in Q1, and will decide, to wait for Atera. He said the macro backdrop for capital expenditure is not meaningfully different from before, "but I'd be remiss if I didn't say it's still fairly constrained," and the predominant reason for the Q1 Spatial instrument result was anticipation of the launch. Enthusiasm for Atera is "extremely high" and "even in a constrained capital environment, customers are finding CapEx. We've got orders." The company timed the launch to fit budgets, including customers with fall year-ends and grant submissions, and so customers can earmark funds for Atera in 2027 budgets.

### Unknown analyst ("Anvika," for Michael Ryskin), Bank of America

**Question:** What are the primary bottlenecks to scaling Atera output beyond limited 2026 production, and how fast can it ramp to meet demand?

**Answer (Saxonov):** Atera is "a really sophisticated instrument," and the company wants to be "very smart and measured around how we build it and how we test it," being "very, very careful with these kinds of launches." He said the company feels good about what it can do in 2026 and will keep ramping production as it exits the year into next year. He did not name specific bottlenecks.

### Thomas VonDerVellen (for Subu Nambi), Guggenheim

**Question:** What pricing headwind did Flex Apex create in the quarter, and what is expected in Q2?

**Answer (Saxonov):** The general trend in mix and pricing around Flex Apex has been similar to Q4. It is still very early in the launch: partial Q4 plus Q1, a bit over one quarter. He is very happy with progress, with "really great feedback from customers and really large scaling up of volumes." He gave no Q2 pricing figure.

### Sam Martin (for Justin Bowers), Deutsche Bank

**Question:** What is Visium demand year to date (Visium HD launched just over two years ago), and does Visium still have a place in the portfolio given Xenium and now Atera?

**Answer (Saxonov):** He reiterated the market trend toward imaging-based readout, especially Xenium, which Atera should reinforce. But Visium "has its place": many customers love it, it runs robustly and consistently across very large sample numbers, and the company will continue to support it and ensure customer success.

### Puneet Souda, Leerink Partners

**Question:** Does Atera accelerate the decline in Visium usage, can anything mitigate it before launch, and do customers want the launch pulled forward?

**Answer (Saxonov):** There will be some effect: there has been a trend of converting to Xenium and it will continue with Atera. Atera "is going to put pressure on both Spatial platforms," Visium too, but this is in the guide. He emphasized built-in stickiness: customers have ongoing projects, workflows and familiar data, which is why continued robust use is seen in the field. On demand, people are "very, very interested," and the team is working "really, really hard" to deliver the instrument as fast as possible while remaining deliberate and rigorous. He did not directly say whether the launch would be pulled forward.

### Priya Patel (for Tycho Peterson), Jefferies

**Question:** What is biopharma partners' feedback on Atera's readiness for large Phase 2 and Phase 3 clinical trials, given the throughput of about 3,000 core biopsies per year?

**Answer (Saxonov):** The sample counts refer to 1 cm² areas, and more samples can be put on a slide, for example with tissue microarrays, so further scaling is possible depending on biopsy format. Biopharma conversations have been "really encouraging," with both Apex and Atera resonating because of the translational applications, sample types and scaling they open. He expects them to be "a big driver, especially as we kind of think about next year and beyond." He did not describe specific Phase 2/3 trial readiness feedback.

### Paul Stewardson (for Dan Arias), Stifel

**Question:** Could some Flex volumes migrate to Atera given its single-cell-level sensitivity, and what is Chromium reaction volume growth excluding Flex Apex?

**Answer (Saxonov):** The question is reasonable but "not something that we're expecting to be an issue in the near term nor is it anything that we're hearing from customers." Configuration, capabilities, pricing and workloads are "sufficiently different." In the long run, massive single-cell-type experiments will be possible with Atera that weren't before, but he sees that as the future, and the company is "certainly not seeing any effect on the Single Cell business at this stage." He did not give a reaction growth figure excluding Flex Apex.

### Closing thoughts (Saxonov)

On the setup for 2027 and beyond:
- **Product lens:** 2027 will be the first full year of Atera, "really the year of Atera." Flex Apex will by then have widespread adoption and single cell pricing "will have stabilized," so volume growth will translate into revenue growth.
- **Market lens:** an exponential increase in AI-driven research and the scaling of translational cohorts are "set to kick in" next year.
- **Macro lens:** "at the very least, we will have good compares," and if conditions improve, "we should see an acceleration to all the other trends."
