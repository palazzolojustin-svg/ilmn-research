# 10x Genomics (TXG) at the BofA Global Healthcare Conference 2026: Atera spatial launch, Q2–Q4 guide shape, Chromium pricing/volume

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/TXG/transcript/TXG_2026-05-13_BofA_Global_Healthcare_Conference.md` · Date: 2026-05-13 · Seeking Alpha transcript of a BofA Securities fireside chat; analyst Michael Ryskin (BofA Life Science Tools and Diagnostics); speakers Serge Saxonov (Co-Founder, CEO) and Adam Taich (CFO) · conference*

**Summary.** 10x Genomics management discussed a strong Q1 2026 and the launch of Atera, a new high-throughput, whole-transcriptome spatial platform they call the biggest launch in company history. They said Atera shipments (about 40 units) will be limited by supply and weighted to Q4 2026, that Xenium sales will step down in Q2 and Q3, and that the full-year guide issued in February is unchanged in shape. They also said Chromium single-cell volume is growing double digits and pricing is nearing equilibrium, and they flagged clinical diagnostics as a long-term opportunity.

## Context and format

This is a fireside chat (a moderated one-on-one conversation rather than scripted remarks) at the Bank of America Global Healthcare Conference, recorded on May 13, 2026. Michael Ryskin of BofA Securities questioned Serge Saxonov (CEO and co-founder of 10x Genomics, "TXG") and Adam Taich (CFO). The transcript header shows the company's Q1 2026 results, reported 2026-05-07: revenue of $150.84M, down 2.61% year over year, which beat the consensus estimate by $4.28M, and EPS of $0.07, which beat by $0.13. These figures come from the Seeking Alpha page header, not from the speakers. Background terms: single cell means analyzing individual cells (10x's Chromium product line); spatial means measuring gene activity while keeping track of where in a tissue it occurs (10x's Xenium, Visium and the new Atera products).

## Q1 2026 recap (Saxonov)

Saxonov said he was "really happy with the quarter" and that it was "quite a strong performance along multiple dimensions." [BofA, 2026-05-13]

- **Single-cell reaction growth.** Reaction volume (the number of single-cell reactions customers run, a measure of usage) grew by double digits again. Saxonov said "the pricing declines were more than made up with the volume growth," so single-cell revenue growth held up even as prices fell.
- **Spatial consumables.** Revenue from spatial consumables (the reagent kits customers must buy for each run) grew by double digits again, "several quarters running."
- **Operating profile.** The company improved its operating profile after a long focus on cost-structure control, and it increased its cash position and balance sheet.
- **Atera.** The biggest news was the launch of Atera, a new spatial platform, which Saxonov said was launched about three weeks before the conference (he had also said so on the earnings call the week before). He called it "the biggest launch in our company's history" and said the company sees it "reshaping the industry and really opening up Spatial in a huge way." He said customer reception had been "really phenomenal."

## Atera: origins and design

**Development history.** Saxonov said the program goes back far, in some ways "almost the very beginning of the company," drawing on technology pieces from different platforms. The formal program began right around when the Xenium platform launched. The company approaches problems "from first principles" and had long held strong conviction that spatial biology is a fundamental direction. Saxonov said the technology wasn't there about 5 years ago, but the company knew what it needed to reach, and it invented and developed across multiple fields to get there. Years of customer feedback about what constrained users, and what kept some on the sidelines, shaped the design. He said Visium and Xenium, despite their success, face "fundamental throttles" to the market, and Atera was built to address "every single one of them." He added, "No one really quite expected what we were going to announce."

**Advantages over Xenium (Saxonov):**
- **Throughput.** Existing systems, including Xenium, are "still constrained on throughput." Atera gives a "massive increase" in throughput versus Xenium, whether measured by samples run or tissue area analyzed.
- **Plex level and whole transcriptome.** Plex means how many genes are measured at once. Xenium relies on targeted panels (pre-selected gene lists), which limits unbiased discovery (looking broadly without presupposing which genes matter). Choosing the exact panel can be "quite a project." With whole-transcriptome capability (measuring essentially all genes), "you don't have to choose."
- **Sensitivity.** Previously, pushing plex up meant trading off throughput and sensitivity. Atera delivers high throughput, whole transcriptome and "really high sensitivity, something that no one had thought possible before." Saxonov said "we actually increased sensitivity in a massive way." This matters for people on the sidelines who compared spatial data to established methods such as FISH (a technique that labels specific genes in tissue) and did not see the genes they expected. With Atera, "you see these cells with just massive numbers of transcripts lighting up and it resonates with the biology they expect."
- **Standard glass slides.** Probably the most notable additional feature is the ability to work with off-the-shelf standard glass slides. Saxonov said it "feels like a minor detail," but it is usually enabling for large-scale translational studies and for dipping into existing biobank cohorts (stored tissue collections).
- **Single platform.** All of these benefits arrive together in one platform and technology.

## Atera launch ramp, supply and capacity

**Shipments and confidence.** Ryskin noted that management had talked about some Atera shipments in Q3, heavily weighted to Q4, about 40 units in the second half. He asked whether that reflected orders in hand. Saxonov said the platform has only been known to customers "for a very, very short time," even for early-access users, yet interest was "tremendous" and order flow "incredibly encouraging, right out of the gate, not really anything like we've seen before." He said supply constraint will determine the 40 number for the rest of the year and into 2027: the company is working to scale production but wants to be "rigorous and deliberate," and "supply is going to be the constraint."

**Capacity investment (Taich).** He said the investment is "not a significant investment relative to sort of where we are in manufacturing capacity," because the company will leverage capabilities and experience built around Xenium. There is "some dual capability" in Pleasanton (headquarters and a manufacturing site) and in the Singapore manufacturing facility, with the exact supply location still being worked out. The company is procuring ahead, especially long-lead-time items, and is starting to procure for the first half of 2027. He said management feels "really confident" in the roughly 40 units for this year and is starting production planning for next year.

**Margins (Taich).**
- Atera as a whole platform will be "margin accretive" (raise 10x's overall gross margin) as it scales.
- In Q4 2026, a very heavy instrument quarter, Atera instrument gross margins should be fairly similar to those of Xenium instruments, which is "lower than the corporate average for sure."
- Atera consumables margins, a design goal begun a couple of years earlier, will be in the same range as Chromium's.
- As the installed base grows and utilization is high (the first 40 go to customers who will plug in and run them), the margin mix "should actually get better than where our corporate averages have been."

**Order disclosure (Saxonov).** He would "hesitate to provide specific numbers around orders because we've not done it before. It's not our policy," but said it is reasonable to expect "some directional kind of indication of the velocity of demand."

**Who is buying (Saxonov).**
- There is "quite intense interest" from people new to spatial, or at least new to 10x spatial, who can now "step off from the sidelines" and run large cohort studies that no technology previously handled at high throughput.
- 10x's priority is to place instruments with customers who can run them at scale immediately, which biases early placements toward substantial existing Xenium users and centralized players such as service providers and core labs (shared facilities serving other researchers' demand).
- The company also wants customers who will become "evangelizers" and drive demand and validation. Demand is strong enough that 10x has "the luxury of prioritizing." It will expand to more new customers later, depending on how fast they can scale up.
- Interest is "across the board"; he could not point to any single segment.

**Utilization.** Atera was built from the start as a "somewhat more centralized system" than 10x's earlier systems. Saxonov believes this is the right approach for the current market and said the era of roughly 10 years when "every single lab" bought its own instrument is not coming back. Early signals are consistent with this. Atera has a "substantially higher ceiling" in throughput per instrument than Xenium, and the company expects "materially higher utilization rate pretty quickly out of the gate." He did not give a utilization number.

## Xenium, Visium and the transition; guidance shape

**Long-run versus short-run (Saxonov).** Asked what share of spatial revenue Atera might have versus Xenium in 3 to 5 years, he said in the long run "things will just consolidate onto Atera," with benefits to customers and to 10x (gross margin profile, speed of turnaround, scaling). In the short run, "you're always surprised by how sticky things are," because of ongoing projects, comfort with existing systems, workflows, data, grants and collaborations. He said 10x expected, and is seeing, continued momentum for both Visium and Xenium. This applies especially to consumables, and somewhat to Xenium instruments that have been in the funnel a long time (long sales-cycle items materializing now). Customers will not pull back and wait a year or more for Atera, so there will be "a much reduced number, but … continued number for Xenium."

**Q1 spatial instrument softness.** Ryskin said Q1 spatial instruments were a little lighter than BofA expected and had been tied to a market pause. Saxonov said 10x announced that something was coming in the middle of Q1, instrument purchases are "always back-end loaded," much of that business got delayed, and most people expected a new Xenium version ("in the air"). He said this gave a strong basis for projections.

**Guide consistency.** Saxonov said the projections are "fairly consistent with what we had thought coming into this year," that Atera planning has gone on a long time, and that the initial guide "very much incorporated all this thinking." Results so far "very much in concordance." He described a step down expected in Q2 and Q3 (a similar amount in each), then "a substantial kickup" from Atera placements, all within the usual quarterization (how revenue is spread across quarters). "We feel very comfortable where we are with the guide, especially after like what we've seen in Q1."

**Quarterly shape (Taich).** Confirming Ryskin's summary that Atera offsets any Xenium slowdown for a couple of quarters, with consumables possibly taking off in 2027 or 2028, Taich said "That's exactly right, Mike." Details:
- After a strong Q1, Q2 should show a low-single-digit sequential reduction (quarter-over-quarter) in dollars.
- Q3 should look fairly like Q2.
- At the guidance midpoint, Q3 to Q4 implies a mid-teens step-up, which he estimated at "probably 17%, 18%" ("just trying to do midpoint math"). He said this is "not dramatically different than" prior-year seasonal patterns (the sentence is cut off in the transcript, and the comparison appears to refer to the usual Q3-to-Q4 step-up; treat as unverified).
- "Nothing is easy," but "we feel really confident about the guide."
- Management knew for a long time that Atera would launch at AACR (a cancer research conference) and deliberately chose to reinstate an annual guide (10x had not been giving full-year guidance). The guide's parameters included double-digit single-cell reaction growth and double-digit spatial consumables revenue growth. At the February guidance, management noted the capital-equipment (CapEx) market had not "tremendously recovered," and all of this fits the calculus given in February. No numeric change to guidance was announced at this event.

## Chromium: price, volume and revenue

**The debate (Ryskin, Saxonov).** Ryskin said the price/volume/mix transition has been debated for over a year. Saxonov recalled that about two years earlier there was a debate over whether single cell had any market left and a thesis that usage would decline. 10x's strong belief was that there is "way more volume than people were appreciating." The first goal was to show consistent volume growth, which has now happened for "quite a number of quarters running," with double-digit single-cell volume growth "despite the fact that the end markets have been just dropping out from under us, especially going back to the first half of last year with the academic kind of disruption." The second goal was to show that pricing declines could be absorbed, and the last couple of quarters show the company getting "to that ballpark." The next goal is for pricing to equilibrate so that volume growth translates into revenue growth. Saxonov said "we're getting closer to that point as we think especially towards 2027," which puts 10x "in a really good position."

**Sources of volume (Saxonov).**
- Big projects and AI projects (including large-data efforts such as the Million Cell Atlas initiative raised by Ryskin) are "unquestionably" incremental. They "would not have happened at like previous price points and with previous sort of generations of products," and are enabled by the lower price point.
- There is also a trend toward larger studies, especially with Apex, where single cell shifts from "an esoteric technique where you pick and choose your samples and run a few at a time" to one where "the entire cohort runs it." "Flex and especially Apex has been sort of a game changer," and customers say it changes how they think. (Flex and Apex are Chromium product offerings; the transcript does not define them.)

**AI programs maturity.** Very large projects are running and protein-protein-interaction (PPI) models are being published, but it is "very much early days." Every model's conclusion is that the next step is 10x as much data. Saxonov said "everything that we're seeing is in the sort of early stage of that exponential," and AI's lesson over 10 years is "insatiable hunger for data."

**Does Apex suffice, and where Atera fits.** Customers always ask for more. Saxonov said Apex is still early and enables the next order of magnitude. Beyond that, at "2 logs of scaling" (about 100x), Atera becomes compelling because it can handle huge cell numbers. He said this is not current single-cell demand, but some future massive projects could land on Atera.

## Sequencing costs and competitors (Ultima, Roche)

Ryskin asked whether the cost of sequencing, which sits alongside 10x costs in mega projects, is a bottleneck. Saxonov said that with both Ultima and Roche, "the price per read has dropped like hugely," which is "definitely a huge enabler." Such projects would have been constrained by sequencing cost a few years ago but are now "absolutely possible," and this is "a massive change in the ecosystem, which is something we absolutely love." Ryskin asked whether $80 to $100 per genome is therefore not a problem; Saxonov said no, and that with Roche and Ultima the cost per read is even lower than the whole-genome price implies, because single-cell work can use simplex reads (reads that do not use the full genome-style format), so it becomes "even cheaper than the whole genome price would indicate." These are Saxonov's statements; no Illumina commentary was made.

## Roadmap and clinical diagnostics

**Pipeline (Saxonov).** He cautioned that Atera is a huge launch, and a road map on top of Atera is already laid out for "the next year and beyond." Items:
- Full end-to-end automation, for which the instrument is "really well suited."
- Proteins and multiomics (measuring proteins alongside RNA). "I don't think anyone has quite got proteins right yet," and he sees a huge opportunity.
- In-tissue sequencing, "base-by-base" sequencing, opening new applications.
- Software, computation and AI, which relieve a long-standing bottleneck: platforms generate large-scale data, and the bottleneck has been analyzing it for insight. He said 10x now has the means to deliver insights efficiently to all customers, which is "absolutely game changing" internally.

**Clinical diagnostics.** Saxonov returned to a theme begun earlier in 2026: the potential of clinical diagnostics using these technologies has "come into much sharper focus," because of technology progress, what Atera now offers at scale, and the ability to "generate clinical evidence at scale" on both Chromium and spatial. Scientific literature increasingly shows biomarkers affect disease progression and therapy success, and the world is moving toward more therapies and a greater need for diagnostics that guide therapy selection. He said 10x is "leaning in very intensely," but "it's not necessarily a story quite yet in terms of revenue for the near term"; longer term "there's potential for really massive businesses to grow out of that."
