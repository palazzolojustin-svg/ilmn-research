## 1. Event overview and agenda

**Format.** Bruno Eschli (Head of Investor Relations) opened the "Annual IR Diagnostics Day 2026" in London. There were ten speakers, in two 60-minute presentation sessions, each followed by a 20-minute Q&A moderated by Birgit Masjost (Investor Relations Officer, who leads diagnostics IR). Session 1 covered Group strategy, Diagnostics strategy, the portfolio and Roche Information Solutions (digital). Session 2 covered the four customer areas: Molecular Labs (including AXELIOS and Foundation Medicine), Pathology Lab, Near Patient Care and Core Lab assays.

**Special focus.** Eschli said this year's focus was "the upcoming global launch of our truly transformative next-generation DNA sequencing technology, which is called AXELIOS" and the end-to-end oncology ecosystem built around it. That ecosystem was recently strengthened by:
- a partnership with Freenome for early cancer detection;
- the acquisition of SAGA Diagnostics, known for ultrasensitive minimal residual disease (MRD) testing. MRD testing looks for tiny traces of cancer left after treatment;
- the acquisition of PathAI, a Boston company developing AI-based digital pathology. This was announced "just last Friday" (about 8 May 2026) and is described as a "definitive merger agreement" that is still subject to regulatory approvals.

Eschli said these together "will not only help us to revolutionize cancer detection and care but also they have the promise to contribute in the future to drug development efforts."

**Format change for the CEO.** Thomas Schinecker said that, as mentioned on the Q1 call, he will attend the Pharma Day and the Diagnostics Day in separate years. He attended this one for two reasons: the sequencing launch ("something that I know for many years, I've always been asked, when are you going to launch… I always said we are going to launch, and we will have a very competitive solution") and the PathAI agreement.

## 2. Group strategy and outlook (Thomas Schinecker, Group CEO)

### Progress against the 10-year ambitions
- **Pharma:**
  - Goal: 20 transformative medicines by the end of the decade.
  - 66% of the portfolio has best-in-disease potential.
  - Average peak-year sales per pipeline project are up 67%, and total pipeline value is up 63% (versus 2022), which Schinecker called the highest pipeline value in the company's history, though the number of projects is lower.
  - Up to 19 medicines could launch by the end of the decade, and there are 19 Phase III readouts between 2027 and 2030.
  - Lead identification and optimization output is about double the ~50 per year of three years ago, with AI playing a role.
  - Average probability of technical success (PTS) in the pipeline is about 70%. Schinecker said people's PTS projections above 50% have historically meant the trial succeeded.
- **Diagnostics:**
  - Goal: double R&D output and reach more patients.
  - Roche Diagnostics holds more than 50% of FDA diagnostics approvals over the last 6 years, while not spending more than 50% of the industry's budget.
  - Three instrument launches with blockbuster potential in a short period: continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), mass spectrometry and now next-generation sequencing. Schinecker said he does not think any other diagnostics company has done this.
  - Menu additions highlighted: Lp(a), pTau217 for Alzheimer's (press release that morning), NfL for multiple sclerosis progression, and the interferon-gamma release assay (IGRA) for TB.

### Healthcare trends driving strategy
- Oncology, neurology and cardiovascular/renal/metabolic (CVRM) account for more than 50% of the global disease burden. Adding immunology and ophthalmology takes it to about 60%.
- Healthcare costs grow faster than GDP, so solutions must improve outcomes and cut costs. Hemlibra was cited as an example (fewer hospitalizations).
- Decentralization of care (hospital to outpatient, primary care, home). Diagnostics example: Lumira (low-cost GP testing). Pharma example: home drug-delivery systems.
- Early detection, monitoring and intervention (Freenome for early cancer detection; SAGA for MRD).
- More than 50% of the world lacks access to basic care.
- Transformative technologies, especially AI.
- Strategy is framed as prevention, stopping and curing disease: "not only lifespan, but health span." Examples: giredestrant in the adjuvant setting (early-stage, after surgery) and CAR-T cells for resetting the immune system in autoimmune disease.

### AI and data
- Three levels. "Everyday AI": 100,000 employees trained, 75% now use AI daily. "Reshape": AI applied across service, marketing/sales and R&D (for example, generating study protocols). "Big ideas": "lab in the loop" drug discovery.
- AI tools are embedded in 100% of large-molecule drug discovery programs, and target entry is more than doubled.
- Roche's AI work began in 2016 with an NVIDIA collaboration, partly because the sequencer needed chips able to handle its data. Schinecker said the sequencer handles "three high-definition movies per second" of data, with speed to rise further, and that no previous computer chip could do this. The NVIDIA collaboration on drug discovery was expanded in 2023. Roche says it now has the highest compute power in the pharma industry.
- Roche is a founding member of the SAIL Foundation (a shared-license agreement with companies such as Anthropic, Microsoft and IBM).
- Schinecker's PathAI rationale: cancer cases and diagnostic complexity are rising while the number of pathologists is falling.

### Sustainability, people and culture
- 100% of sites on sustainable energy. Water use down 10% last year (26% since 2020). Greenhouse gas emissions down 34% last year and 78% since 2004. The goal is "real zero," not net zero.
- Number one in the global patient reputation survey. Diagnostics delivered 31 billion tests, more than 3 to 4 per world inhabitant. Roche has the first new antibiotic in 50 years, and is working on AIDS and cervical cancer screening in Africa.
- Culture: "high-performing organization" for 3 years. Bonuses were changed so low performers are capped and spot awards go to high performers. Turnover is very low among high performers and 35% to 37% among low performers ("exactly what we want"). An employee survey the previous Monday scored 82 (+3 versus 3 years ago), 12 points above the global benchmark (versus 8 a year earlier), while industry satisfaction fell 3 points.
- Productivity: about CHF 1 billion of R&D costs cut and reallocated into projects. Flatiron lost about EUR 250 million a year three years ago and is now profitable (first quarter of 2026) while growing high-teens. Schinecker said Roche works on productivity in a "calm way" rather than announcing "8,000 or 9,000 people reductions" like other companies.

### Group growth outlook
- Group delivery: sales +5%, +8%, +5% and core EPS +8% (per his slide), and an average of about 8% growth over the last 2 years and 3 quarters, despite the MabThera, Herceptin and Avastin biosimilar cliff and the COVID sales roll-off.
- Diagnostics: "growth in the mids- to high single digits over the long term and core [operating profit] growth ahead of sales."
- Pharma (a change from September 2025): then, the on-market portfolio was said to deliver growth to 2028 and then offset generic erosion. After positive readouts in the last 6 months (4 consecutive positive Phase IIIs for Gazyva, giredestrant adjuvant data, and fenebrutinib data), Roche says "we will no longer have a situation… where we are not going to grow," with growth continuing beyond the decade. Adjuvant is more than 70% of the market for SERDs (a class of breast cancer drugs).
- Schinecker said consensus models imply a biosimilar gap of about CHF 6.7 billion over 5 years, and that he sees "more potential for the upside than for the downside," with many things not in models.

**Four goals:** expand leadership in the clinical lab (Roche is number 1 in in vitro diagnostics); become a leader in decentralized testing; differentiate through comprehensive solutions in the highest-burden disease areas; help patients navigate the disease continuum (oncology and neurology ecosystems).

## 8. AXELIOS: technology, performance, pricing and strategy (Josh Lauer, Molecular Labs)

### PCR strategy and menu
Roche leads in higher-volume automated central-lab PCR (infectious disease, blood screening). Over 16 assays are planned over 3 years. 2026 highlights: the hepatitis E multiplex blood screening assay; the BV/CV assay (STIs); in the next couple of months, a hepatitis D assay, the first fully automated hepatitis D RNA test (hepatitis D affects over 12 million people; uses: testing chronic hepatitis B patients and monitoring new hepatitis D therapies). Later: completing syndromic and STI menus, GI and respiratory panels, and self- or home-sampling.

### Architecture (SBX, sequencing by expansion)
Lauer described three components:
1. **Synthesis instrument:** turns a DNA molecule into an "Xpandomer," which makes single-molecule sequencing at scale possible by improving the signal-to-noise ratio.
2. **Sequencing instrument:** runs Xpandomers through an 8-million-well reusable CMOS array with very high data rates.
3. **Analysis module:** a high-performance compute stack that processes reads in real time, so processing is almost complete when the run finishes (no waiting for each flow cycle to complete a read).

### Workflow and speed
- Customers can load 1 to 4 pools, tailoring throughput; "Mid-throughput users can actually access the same economics as high throughput users."
- Library prep is similar to traditional methods and has been automated on Roche, Beckman and Hamilton platforms.
- Fully loaded with 4 queued runs: 64 genomes in under 2 days (Lauer said traditional technologies take significantly more than 2 days of on-board sequencing time alone).
- World-record NICU (neonatal intensive care) run using an amplification-free workflow: a full VCF file from a sample in under 4 hours (Broad and Boston Children's showed same-day results).
- The sensor module can be reused up to 20 times. Carryover is lower than in traditional systems (Lauer's explanation: Xpandomers are more delicate than DNA and are easily cleaned out).

### Performance statistics (shown at AGBT, as expected at the summer launch)
- **Duplex mode:** roughly a couple of terabases, enough to always generate 16 genomes or more at Q38 or higher (Q38 is a quality score; higher means fewer errors), with longer inserts than the traditional 150 base pairs.
- **Simplex mode:** over 40 billion short reads in 4 hours (over 10 billion reads an hour), "The sequencing world has never seen output like this"; longer reads (500 to 1,000 base pairs) still deliver over 10 billion reads in 4 hours.
- Lauer said a new technology matching or exceeding accuracy, cost and performance of traditional platforms out of the gate is "a rare thing." Customers repeatedly emphasize "speed, speed, speed."

### Pricing
- **List price: $150 per genome**, with "no volume-based discounting. Everyone can achieve this pricing." Lauer said that is competitive with high-throughput platforms and "way more than competitive" for lower-throughput users, who normally pay much more per genome.
- **Simplex: $0.06 per million reads**, "multiples lower than the nearest competing technology."
- **Liquid biopsy panel example:** a commonly used 1.1-megabase panel at normal depth for clinical-grade accuracy costs about **$25 a sample**, "multiples lower than what you can achieve with other technologies."

### Target enrichment clarification
After AGBT, analysts asked how target enrichment works. Previously only Simplex data with UMIs (unique molecular identifiers, used to stack consensus reads) was shown. Lauer said SBX errors are largely random, so stacking reads can push beyond Q38; Roche has shown Q50 and does not yet know the ceiling. It is also possible with Duplex, but through a different workflow (a traditional workflow with PCR-mediated steps at the end to extend at a hairpin and make a Duplex construct), good for applications where 2 reads suffice.

### Applications and ecosystem
- This is Roche's "first-gen RUO (research use only) platform." Demonstrated: germline and oncology with high accuracy, fresh frozen and FFPE tissue, and MRD (by Foundation Medicine).
- Long tail: single cell, Perturb-seq CRISPR screens, spatial, proteomics (collaborations with Olink, Pixelgen and Alamar), novel isoform detection.
- Open platform: data with Watchmaker and other vendors; a partnership with 10x; Roche will engage the NGS ecosystem. The long-term ambition is a clinical platform (end-to-end workflows, automation, library prep), and use for multi-omics beyond DNA.
- Roche "intend[s] to invest in the next 20 years of improvements on SBX": scale up and down, extend read lengths, optimize chemistry accuracy, extend sensor reuse.
- Foundation Medicine's MRD data (presented at AACR): AXELIOS detected more MRD-positive cases than a competing technology, and did better in earlier-stage disease. Roche is also working with Freenome on its multi-cancer early detection platform.

## 9. Foundation Medicine (Dan Malarek, CEO)

- Founded in 2010 out of the Broad Institute. First to launch a comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP) assay; over 1.5 million CGPs delivered; more than 40% of US oncologists ordered in the last 12 months (via portal and EMR integrations); over 100 approved companion diagnostics ("more than the entire rest of the industry combined"); works with the top 80 biopharma companies and supports over 300 active clinical trials.
- Adoption gap: one large community oncology practice (treating about 20% of US cancer patients) gets CGP for only about 50% of metastatic patients; Malarek says it should be above 90%.
- Market view: oncology testing across biopharma, clinical therapy selection and monitoring should "essentially double over the next 5 years," driven by new targeted modalities (bispecifics, ADCs, molecular glues and others), rising CGP adoption and molecular monitoring (about 50 million people live with cancer, nearly doubling in 10 years).
- Quality example: MTAP loss (present in about 10% of cancers; a biomarker for MAT2A and PRMT5 inhibitor trials). A Sarah Cannon Research Institute study of 3 labs found FMI detected MTAP loss at published incidence rates while the other labs detected 40% to 60% less. FMI is the enrollment assay for 15 MTAP trials. FoundationOne CDx is the only FDA-approved tissue NGS test validated to detect a copy number loss (PTEN).
- Products: **F1 Monitor** (liquid-only, metastatic setting; detects progression at roughly half the lead time of CT/PET imaging; also reports resistance mutations). **Pathlight MRD** (from SAGA, once the deal closes): detected recurrence over 13 months before CT/PET in early-stage breast; Medicare covered for Stage II and III breast across all subtypes, over 6 years and 12 time points; plan to use AXELIOS for whole-genome tissue baseline and Digital LightCycler for multi-timepoint testing. **Flexomics** platform extending into neurodegenerative, metabolic and autoimmune diseases.
