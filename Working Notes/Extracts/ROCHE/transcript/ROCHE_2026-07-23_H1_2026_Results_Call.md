# Roche H1 2026 (Q2) Results Call – Group, Pharma and Diagnostics results, AXELIOS 1 sequencer launch, pipeline Q&A

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ROCHE/transcript/ROCHE_2026-07-23_H1_2026_Results_Call.md` · Date: 2026-07-23 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Roche Holding AG half-year 2026 results webinar; speakers Thomas Schinecker (Group CEO), Alan Hippe (CFO), Teresa Graham (CEO Roche Pharmaceuticals), Matthew Sause (CEO Diagnostics), Bruno Eschli (Head of Investor Relations); analysts from Citi, Redburn, BofA, UBS, Bernstein, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Barclays, Goldman Sachs, Berenberg, Deutsche Bank, BNP Paribas, HSBC · earnings call*

**Summary.** Roche reported H1 2026 sales growth of 6% at constant currency, core operating profit growth of 10% and core EPS growth of 9%, and confirmed its full-year outlook while lowering its expected loss-of-exclusivity (LOE) hit to about CHF 600 million. Diagnostics grew 3% (6% excluding China pricing reform) and launched the AXELIOS 1 sequencing system (sequencing by expansion, SBX) on June 29, 2026, a direct new entrant in the next-generation sequencing market where Illumina competes. Most of the call covered the Pharma pipeline (giredestrant, divarasib, obesity assets, Gazyva, Vabysmo, Ocrevus) and the PathAI and Nurix deals.

# Roche H1 2026 Results Call (23 July 2026): Detailed Notes

The call was a half-year results webinar. The source is a Seeking Alpha transcript, headed as the Q2 2026 call and dated 24 July in the header, but the event itself was 23 July 2026. The Q&A text appears twice in the transcript (a duplicated passage). The notes below record each item once.

## 1. Headline results and guidance

### Group headline numbers [H1 2026 call, Schinecker and Hippe]

Group sales grew 6% at constant exchange rates (CER, meaning with currency movements stripped out). Pharma grew 6% and Diagnostics grew 3%. In Swiss francs, group sales fell 2%, because currency was a headwind of 8 percentage points. In US dollars, Hippe said sales would have grown 8%.

Core operating profit (profit from operations before one-off and non-cash accounting items) grew 10% at CER, ahead of sales growth. The core operating margin rose 1.7 percentage points at CER. Core EPS (earnings per share on the same "core" basis) grew 9% (9.3% before rounding).

Schinecker said the weak flu season cut group sales by about 1%, or more than CHF 200 million. Without it, group growth would have been 7%.

Diagnostics grew 6% excluding the impact of China's healthcare pricing reform.

### Financial statement detail [Hippe, H1 2026 call]

**Core operating profit and net income**
- In Swiss francs, core operating profit was down 1%.
- The financial result and taxes did not hold back the step from operating profit to core net income.
- Core EPS growth (9%) was lower than operating profit growth because of strong Chugai results. Roche owns 60% of Chugai, so 40% of its profit is removed from core EPS. Hippe put the effect at minus 1.6 percentage points.
- IFRS net income (the formal accounting measure, including non-core items) rose 6% at CER and fell 6% in Swiss francs. Impairments were the main difference from core; they are explained in Note 9 (Intangible Assets) on page 65 of the interim financial statements. Restructuring charges were lower than in H1 2025.

**Cash flow and debt**
- Operating free cash flow rose 21% at CER and 2% in Swiss francs.
- Free cash flow rose 58% at CER and 26% in Swiss francs. Hippe said this partly reflects timing: some tax payments did not fall in the first half and will fall in the second half.
- In the operating cash flow bridge, operating profit net of cash adjustments increased significantly. The change in net working capital reflected payables and was expected to wash out in Pharma. Investment in PP&E was flat. Investment in intangibles was lower than last year, when the Zealand deal cost CHF 1.2 billion in cash.
- Net debt rose CHF 5.9 billion from end-2025 to end-June 2026. Gross debt was stable at CHF 31.6 billion at year-end versus CHF 31.8 billion at half-year, so the increase came from a lower cash position. The drivers were taxes, treasury items of minus CHF 2.1 billion, and the dividend payment. Hippe called this the normal first-half pattern, which is worked against in the second half.
- Equity fell because the dividend is deducted from equity; Hippe expects equity to rise again as second-half profits come in.

**Sales bridge**
- Pharma excluding LOE (loss of exclusivity, when patents expire and generics or biosimilars arrive) plus the LOE effect of minus CHF 244 million gave Pharma growth of 6% at CER.
- The LOE hit came in "a little bit better than we all expected", so Roche now expects to lose roughly CHF 600 million to LOE in 2026, lower than the figure given at the start of the year.
- Diagnostics: plus 6% excluding China, and minus 15% in China.

**P&L lines**
- Other revenue rose CHF 258 million. It included a royalty-claim settlement of roughly CHF 70 million, an expected milestone income from Chugai for Foundayo, and a Pharma cross-licensing settlement of CHF 126 million.
- Cost of sales rose 5% in both divisions while volumes grew 8% (Pharma volume +9%, Diagnostics +5%). In Pharma, cost of sales was driven by higher profit-share expenses on higher Xolair sales, and royalty expenses on higher Ocrevus and Evrysdi sales. In Diagnostics, it was driven by more installed instruments, the manufacturing ramp-up of new launches, and US tariffs of roughly CHF 43 million.
- R&D rose 1%, reflecting cost discipline alongside investment in oncology, CVRM (cardiovascular, renal and metabolism) and neurology.
- SG&A rose 3%. Pharma marketing and distribution rose, while admin was flat or slightly lower.
- Other operating income and expense reflected lower income from product disposals than last year (a decrease of CHF 104 million in the core EPS bridge).
- Other revenue is normally about 3% of sales. Hippe said that with Foundayo (a product marketed by a partner; Roche earns royalties and a Chugai milestone) coming in for the next couple of years, the ratio should rise to about 5% by the end of the decade.
- Core margin rose 1.7 points at CER, with Pharma up 2 points and Diagnostics up 0.6 points. Both divisions benefited from further centralizing SG&A (especially legal) under corporate: a benefit of CHF 105 million to Pharma and CHF 24 million to Diagnostics in H1 at CER. Hippe stood by the CHF 300 million full-year shift figure but said it "could be a little bit lower", with a ramp-up in the second half.
- Core net financial result improved by CHF 42 million, helped by equity securities gains at the Roche Venture Fund and a US-dollar benefit of CHF 43 million on interest expense. Net interest income was lower because of lower cash. "Other" included higher currency losses.
- Core tax rate was 17.5%, a slight rise on H1 2025. Hippe said timing effects benefited the first half and the rate will rise in the second half, with the full year around 20%, "perhaps a little bit lower".

**Currency**
- Hippe said that with rates held at end-June levels, the year-to-September currency hit would be minus 5 percentage points. For the full year he gave minus 4 points for sales, minus 6 points for core operating profit and minus 6 points for core EPS, the same as the Q1 prediction. He said the situation is stabilizing but volatility is still huge.

### Guidance [Schinecker and Hippe]

The 2026 outlook was confirmed: mid-single-digit sales growth (currently 6%), high-single-digit core EPS growth (currently 9%), and a further dividend increase in Swiss francs. The one change is the LOE impact of roughly CHF 600 million, lower than at the start of the year. "Everything else is confirmed as we had it at the beginning of the year."

### Other group highlights [Schinecker]

- Five US FDA priority reviews so far in 2026: giredestrant (adjuvant early breast cancer), Enspryng (thyroid eye disease, TED), Tecentriq ATOMIC (dMMR/MSI-H colon cancer), Gazyva in INS and Gazyva in MN (membranous nephropathy). Teresa Graham later listed the same five.
- Two positive Phase III results in the quarter. The Phase III success rate this year is 80%, "significantly above the average of the industry".
- No "red boxes" (setbacks) on the pipeline slide in Q2.
- Business development highlights: PathAI and Nurix Therapeutics. Roche has 16 blockbusters and is globally number one in Diagnostics.
- Second-half news flow: Phase III readouts for Itovebi and Lunsumio, and potentially a new molecular entity (NME), sefaxersen, which would be a fourth NME launch alongside three already planned. More Phase II readouts are due, and a large amount of news flow is expected in 2027–2029.
- Ahead of Q&A, Schinecker said Roche has increased the value of its pipeline by 93% versus end-2022, an all-time high, with 19 medicines that could launch by the end of the decade.

## 2. Pharma division

### Overall [Graham, H1 2026 call]

Pharma sales were CHF 23.6 billion, up 6% at CER, 8% in US dollars and down 1% in Swiss francs. All regions grew; International and Japan grew double digits. The EU returned to growth after Q1 was hit by pricing and one-off effects on specific products. Volumes were up 9%. Core operating profit rose 10% at CER with a core margin of 53%, helped by R&D cost management. The top brands (Xolair, Hemlibra, Ocrevus, Phesgo, Vabysmo, Evrysdi) generated roughly CHF 1.4 billion of new sales at CER in H1.

### Oncology (CHF 7.4 billion, +1%)

**HER2 franchise**
- Phesgo's global conversion rate (share of HER2 patients moved from intravenous Herceptin/Perjeta to the subcutaneous combination) is 54%, 1 point below Q1 because two more countries were added to the metric. Roche expects it to correct and aims for at least 60% at peak.
- Kadcyla is in line with expectations, with competitive pressure in the US and EU.
- Graham reiterated that the HER2 franchise should peak at about CHF 9 billion (at 2024 constant exchange rates) in 2026, then decline steadily through the end of the decade to a tail of about CHF 4 billion (mainly Phesgo), about CHF 1 billion for Kadcyla, and a bit of Herceptin plus Perjeta. No US Perjeta biosimilar is expected before 2028 and no EU one before 2027. She said there is no cliff in the franchise.

**Breast cancer pipeline**
- Itovebi's launch is going with good momentum.
- Giredestrant: FDA PDUFA (target decision) date for lidERA (adjuvant ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer) is 30 November. For evERA the PDUFA date is 18 December. Schinecker said launch is expected toward the end of the year.

**Lung cancer**
- Alecensa faces increasing competition in the EU and US, so flat growth is expected for the year.
- Tecentriq: new indications drive growth, especially IMforte in small cell lung cancer. IMvigor011 (muscle-invasive bladder) was approved in the US and ATOMIC (dMMR colon cancer) was filed in the US and EU. Full-year outlook of low-single-digit growth was confirmed.
- Divarasib: Phase III Krascendo 1 (second-line-plus KRAS G12C non-small cell lung cancer) was positive in a head-to-head comparison with approved G12C inhibitors, showing superiority on PFS (progression-free survival) and OS (overall survival), with OS significance already reached at interim. Safety was consistent and manageable. Data will go to health authorities and an upcoming congress. Graham: it "reinforce[s] our conviction that divarasib has best-in-class potential."
- At ASCO, Phase II Krascendo 170 (first line, divarasib plus pembrolizumab) showed strong efficacy in both PD-L1 positive and negative cohorts, supporting the ongoing Phase III Krascendo 2. The RAS program includes divarasib in first-line, second-line and adjuvant NSCLC, a G12D-specific asset and several pan-mutation assets.

### Hematology (CHF 4.6 billion, +14%)

- Hemlibra is the main growth driver, with adoption rising in non-inhibitor patients and older adults. Full-year growth outlook was raised to mid-single digit from low single digit. Graham called this "conservative" because it includes headwinds from competitor launches later in the year. An in-development Hemlibra autoinjector will be shown at Pharma Day; a demo at the ISTH conference was so popular that staff had to stop people prying it off the wall.
- Polivy: over 120,000 patients treated globally in first-line DLBCL (diffuse large B-cell lymphoma), up from 95,000 at Q1.
- Lunsumio: US filing achieved for SUNMO (Lunsumio plus Polivy, second-line-plus DLBCL), PDUFA 9 February. Graham said Lunsumio has a best-in-class safety profile with significantly less CRS (cytokine release syndrome), preferred by US advisers for outpatient and community settings. Columvi (STARGLO) has mature survival data that many advisers regard as curative, a first-mover advantage of 2+ years outside the US, and is standard of care post-reimbursement.
- Gazyva faces increased competition in CLL and follicular lymphoma, which masks the immunology launch. Roche will show a sales split by therapeutic area in future.
- Nurix deal (closed): bexobrutideg ("bexdeg"), a BTK degrader (a drug that removes the BTK protein rather than just blocking it). Graham said degradation removes all BTK functions, can overcome inhibitor-resistance mutations, is highly selective and potent, and crosses the blood-brain barrier. A Phase III in first-line-plus CLL has started, head-to-head against pirtobrutinib. A basket combination trial in CLL is planned, plus Phase II trials in MS and CSU (chronic spontaneous urticaria). Schinecker said fenebrutinib is a BTK inhibitor and the degrader could be a next-generation molecule with a proven mechanism.
- NXT007, the next-generation Hemlibra, moved into Phase III. Schinecker said it is about 30 times more potent and very safe.

### Neurology (CHF 5.0 billion, +9%)

- Ocrevus growth is led by the subcutaneous version (Zunovo in the US): about 44,000 patients, up from about 24,000 at Q1. It is now the fastest-growing anti-CD20 MS brand in the US and the only anti-CD20 with six-monthly subcutaneous dosing.
- Ocrevus outlook: previously high-single to low-double-digit growth for 2026; now the lower end of that range because competition in anti-CD20 is "at the upper end of our previous assumptions". Peak sales assumptions are unchanged at CHF 9 billion by 2029, including CHF 2 billion incremental from subcutaneous (plus some IV-to-subcutaneous switching).
- An on-body injector for the subcutaneous formulation is on track and will be discussed at Pharma Day.
- Fenebrutinib: US filing to be completed within weeks, with acceptance later in Q3 to be confirmed by press release.
- Trontinemab (Alzheimer's): long-term Phase I/II data at AAIC (London) confirmed amyloid clearance speed and depth and a strong safety profile (Brainshuttle technology). Study design of PrevenTRON, a Phase III in preclinical Alzheimer's, was shared.

### Immunology (CHF 3.3 billion, +8%)

- Xolair: more than 130,000 food allergy patients treated. Outlook of about 20% growth for 2026 is unchanged and includes a first biosimilar expected around September in the second half.
- Actemra sales fell 9% at half-year from biosimilars, as expected.
- Gazyva in lupus nephritis (LN): growth is faster in the US, Germany and UK, where LN is reimbursed; patient shares are approaching 10% in those markets. Graham sees a real inflection in the US. Momentum drivers include three New England Journal publications (SLE, LN and INS), two FDA breakthrough designations, LN access growth, and guideline inclusion. Roche is confident in the "up to CHF 2 billion" opportunity across Gazyva's immunology kidney indications.
- MAJESTY (membranous nephropathy, MN): Gazyva beat tacrolimus on complete response. US and EU filings are done; FDA granted breakthrough designation and priority review with PDUFA 15 November. All immunology indications are now filed in the US and EU, with decisions in H2 starting with INS in September.
- Sefaxersen Phase III interim readout in IgAN is due later this year.

### Ophthalmology (CHF 2.1 billion, +6%)

- Vabysmo: over 60% of US patient starts are treatment-naive. Positive Phase III POYANG in myopic choroidal neovascularization (a potential fourth indication, smaller but relevant especially in Asia). ASRS 2026 survey (about 1,000 retina specialists) supports its anatomic outcomes and durability.
- Regional outlook: the US branded market has stabilized at a lower level ("the new normal"), with Vabysmo US growth in the low single to mid-single-digit range. In the EU, strong volume growth overcompensates price effects. Global Vabysmo growth is expected at low double digits, driven by ex-US. Roche considers consensus peak sales of about CHF 6 billion "very reasonable".
- Enspryng in TED: US filing with priority review, FDA decision due by 15 October. Vamikibart in UME (uveitic macular edema) is planned for filing later this year.

### CVRM and obesity [Graham]

- Petrelintide (amylin, with Zealand): positive Phase II ZUPREME-1 at ADA with double-digit weight loss and placebo-like tolerability; Phase III go decision taken, start in H2.
- Enicepatide (formerly CT-388): positive Phase II at ADA; Phase III ENITH-1 and 2 in obesity ongoing; Phase III in glycemic control and a CVOT (cardiovascular outcomes trial) planned. Weight loss at 24 mg had not plateaued at 48 weeks, and no tolerability ceiling was seen, so doses above 24 mg will be explored. At 24 mg, 48% of patients lost more than 20%, 38% more than 25%, and 26% more than 30%. Graham said this approaches triple-agonist (GGG) efficacy with the tolerability of dual GLP-1/GIP agonists, which she called a "clearly best-in-class weight loss profile".
- CT-868 was discontinued in type 1 diabetes (unrelated to safety or tolerability) because Roche believes enicepatide may have best-in-disease potential.
- H2 data: enicepatide Phase II in obesity with type 2 diabetes, CT-996 (oral) Phase II in obesity, ZUPREME-2 for petrelintide, and Phase II GYMINDA (emugrobart plus tirzepatide). The Phase II ZYNERGY study (enicepatide plus petrelintide) is about to start. CT-996 Phase III is scheduled for H2 after its Phase II readout.

## 3. Diagnostics division [Sause, H1 2026 call]

### Results

Diagnostics sales were CHF 6.7 billion, up 3% at CER (plus CHF 228 million), despite China's healthcare pricing reform. Excluding China reform, growth was 6%. The weak Northern Hemisphere respiratory season cost an additional CHF 50 million.

| Customer area | Growth (CER) | Notes |
|---|---|---|

| Molecular Lab | +3% | Transplant +18%, offset by mild respiratory season |
| Near Patient Care | -5% | Lower cobas liat sales; blood glucose monitoring +3% on competitive wins |

By region: North America +8%, EMEA +3%, LatAm +10%, APAC -5% (China -15%, APAC excluding China +6%).

### Outlook and costs

- 2026 ambition: mid-single-digit sales growth, with a lessened China price-reform impact. 2027 ambition: return to mid- to high-single-digit growth.
- Costs: cost of sales +5% (China reform, launch manufacturing ramp, US tariffs); R&D +3% (spend on AXELIOS sequencing and the i601 mass spec); SG&A +3% (distribution costs from volume and launch investment).
- Core operating profit was CHF 1.2 billion, up 6% at CER, benefiting from CHF 126 million of extraordinary income from patent licensing.
- Margin ambition: profit growing faster than sales over time, but for 2026 the core margin is to be broadly stable at CER versus 14.4% in 2025, absorbing China reform and US tariffs. Sause also noted a negative currency effect on the margin.

### AXELIOS 1 sequencing (SBX) [Sause, Schinecker]

This is the most relevant section for Illumina and sequencing.

- AXELIOS 1 launched on 29 June 2026. It is based on SBX (sequencing by expansion), which came from Roche's 2020 Stratos acquisition. Schinecker: "I've always promised that we will deliver, and we did deliver on the sequencing with really a differentiated product."
- Sause's market framing: Roche sees the next-generation sequencing (NGS) market at around USD 7.3 billion (assessed in 2025), growing as new clinical applications become routine.
- Two run modes: SBX-Duplex and SBX-Simplex, giving "flexibility, speed and scalability that is unprecedented" and "differentiated cost effectiveness".
- Duplex mode: 1.8 terabases of output in 4 hours of sequencing, equal to around 16 whole human genomes, with a Q score of 38 (about 99.98% accuracy).
- Simplex mode: ultra-high throughput and longer reads of up to 1,500 bases, enabling isoform detection and structural variant detection. In a 4-hour run it can generate 40 billion reads averaging 175 base pairs.
- Interest has been consistently high since SBX was unveiled at AGBT in 2025. Sause: AXELIOS 1 "is positioned to set a new standard of care in next-generation sequencing for a broad array of applications."
- Schinecker said that for the industry, a company might have one launch of this scale, but Roche had three major launches (including mass spec and sequencing) in a close period, each of which "even for Pharma terms, would be a blockbuster".

### Oncology diagnostics strategy and M&A

- Sause: the strategy is an end-to-end oncology portfolio covering early detection, precise diagnosis, therapy selection and disease monitoring, via internal innovation and purposeful M&A.
- SAGA Diagnostics (acquired): ultrasensitive minimal residual disease (MRD) testing. Schinecker explained that the workflow sequences the cancer (on AXELIOS), then monitors with specific primers on digital PCR, which is cost-effective, and Roche has a digital PCR system. He said Roche is "extremely well positioned here to really scale this on a global level, much better than some of the other players", and noted the market caps of other companies in the space.
- PathAI: definitive merger agreement signed earlier in July 2026. PathAI is described as the leading provider of digital pathology image management systems; it augments H&E and IHC workflows with AI algorithms. It will integrate with Roche's Ventana solutions, strengthen the companion diagnostics business and support pharma clinical trials and new biomarkers. Sause: "wholly complementary to our Ventana business."

### Other Diagnostics launches

- Elecsys pTau217 (Alzheimer's blood test): CE mark on 12 May. Described as the first blood-based biomarker intended to rule in or rule out Alzheimer's in primary and secondary care. Alzheimer's is expected to affect over 80 million people by 2030, and diagnosis currently takes about 3 years after symptom onset. Schinecker said Roche has a "significant head start versus our other major competitors", especially in combination with trontinemab.
- Elecsys IGRA TB (latent tuberculosis): CE mark in July. About 25% of the world's population is estimated to carry TB infection. The test offers high throughput, automated results and a 3-tube workflow. Plans to integrate cobas ultra to automate pre-analytics, helping entry into high-throughput markets such as the US. Sause: Roche has the broadest installed base of immunoassay analyzers in the world.
- Launch list: 7 of 11 key Diagnostics launches achieved by half-year 2026.

## 5. Q&A

Pairs of speakers are labelled by who answered. Michael Leuchten (Jefferies) was announced for the last question but had dropped off.

### Graham Parry (Citi)
- Asked whether Krascendo 2 (listed primary completion October 2028) could read out earlier given 19-month PFS in Phase II, and whether the CHF 1–2 billion divarasib peak sales estimate can go higher.
- Graham: Roche is re-evaluating peak sales in light of Krascendo 1, talking with thought leaders and the ultimate trial program, with an update at Pharma Day. For now it confirms CHF 1–2 billion: "stay tuned". On an earlier readout: the trial has just started and, as an event-driven trial, "the data will tell us."

### Simon Baker (Redburn)
- Lunsumio in SLE and interplay with Gazyva: Graham said SLE is heterogeneous; Lunsumio has strong B-cell depletion like Gazyva but a slightly different mechanism, and "deeper and deeper B-cell depletion is likely what these patients need."
- Patient assistance foundations (Vabysmo): Graham said these are charitable donations not meant to drive sales. The retinal market has not recovered; it is growing 2–3%, and she expects this to be the new normal.

### Sachin Jain (BofA)
- On giredestrant, asked about the group that stopped CDK4/6 inhibitors for toxicity. Graham gave the early breast cancer risk groups: low risk 55–60% of patients, intermediate about 10%, medium 15–20%, high 15%. Those not on CDK4/6 inhibitors are 90%+ of intermediate, 80%+ of medium and 50%+ of high risk. About 50% of CDK4/6 patients cannot tolerate treatment, a population she sees as potential users without conflicting with label; switch data and low-risk data are being generated, and Roche's own combination data are forthcoming. lidERA plus evERA already cover about 80% of the market.
- On Ocrevus competition (Jain guessed Briumvi): Graham said Roche does not comment on individual competitors, but is seeing higher competition. She cited Ocrevus's advantages: first and only twice-yearly MS therapy (RMS and PPMS), over a decade of experience, 80% of first-line RMS patients without progression, 90% not needing walking aids, robust family planning data, and a 10-minute subcutaneous dose. Competitive bursts "aren't driven on clinical differentiation."
- Hippe on other revenue rising from 3% to 5% of sales (Jain's arithmetic: about $2 billion to $4 billion, all EBIT): it makes a difference until the end of the decade; Roche still sticks to "either we stabilize the margin or we grow it", and this "gives even more substance to our statement".

### Colin White (UBS)
- On Vabysmo and biosimilars, Graham said biosimilars in ophthalmology take share from the molecule they copy (a Lucentis biosimilar took Lucentis share), with little bleed-over. Vabysmo sales grew 8% in the half while volume grew 30% globally. She cited the ASRS data and said physicians choose Vabysmo for most new patients. Stabilization view is based on six months of watching the market and talking to physicians; 2–3% retina growth can accommodate biosimilars.
- On Epkinly's coming first-line DLBCL readout: the competitor is about a year ahead, but Graham sees POLARIX (Polivy) as a much better tolerated first-line regimen, so does not expect much impact. Eschli added Roche's own study (SKYGLO) comes next year.

### Justin Smith (Bernstein)
- Asked why 360 patients powers the NXT007 head-to-head vs Hemlibra. Graham: statistical analysis was done based on Hemlibra performance and NXT007's high potency. Eschli: first patient is in; data expected around end-2027 or early 2028.

### Sarita Kapila (Morgan Stanley)
- On divarasib, Graham said KRAS mutations are in about 25% of lung cancers and G12C is 40% of them, or roughly 12–13% of NSCLC. Divarasib's potency, selectivity and safety position it well, and patients with the mutation would likely use a direct targeted therapy. There will be a place for pan-RAS inhibitors, and Roche has several. Schinecker added upside in other RAS-mutated cancer types.
- On giredestrant (heredERA, evERA), Graham said lidERA plus evERA cover 80% of ER+/HER2- and other trials could cover nearly 100%; peak sales already reflect a broad program and further programs could be upside. A fuller update comes at Pharma Day. (The CHF 9 billion "peak" in the question was the analyst's wording.)

### Richard Vosser (JPMorgan)
- Ocrevus/Zunovo: Graham said "we have delivered the hockey stick", more conversion is "quite probably" possible, the CHF 2 billion remains, switching could make the pie bigger, and the on-body injector would add protection.
- Vabysmo pricing: price effects outside the US are largely related to volume gains.
- Other revenue: Hippe said the 3% to 5% includes everything; Schinecker said it is mostly royalty-driven, with a few milestones. Schinecker added that fenebrutinib and other pipeline items could extend the Ocrevus franchise.

### James Gordon (Barclays)
- Giredestrant launch: Graham said Roche is working with KOLs; they are excited by an option given 50% CDK4/6 intolerance and giredestrant's better tolerability. Ex-US, Roche is assembling the data package for a European filing and sustainable reimbursement. Bloomberg consensus of about CHF 300 million next year: Roche does not comment, but this is a chronic therapy, patients are expected to stay on for 5+ years, and big numbers build over time.
- Fenebrutinib: on track to file first in the US and use that as a springboard; EU data are being collected with intent to file. Nothing indicates a problem with the US filing. Schinecker: the FDA allowed open-label extensions (PPMS and RMS) with control-arm patients switched to fenebrutinib, which he called a good indicator.
- Obesity: Graham declined to pick a favorite. Enicepatide serves those needing deep weight loss; petrelintide serves those wanting 10–15% weight loss with placebo-like tolerability; the fixed-dose combination could boost both; an oral option (CT-996) is important. Schinecker added that giredestrant has a potency and label/safety advantage over another SERD.

### James Quigley (Goldman Sachs)
- BTK degrader: Graham is excited about efficacy, safety, tolerability and combinability, especially in oncology; in immunology a degrader versus an inhibitor could give incremental efficacy that is meaningful. More at Pharma Day.
- Gazyva peak (up to CHF 2 billion): Graham said membranous and INS are small, LN is medium, SLE is bigger but with entrenched therapies, and Gazyva's pricing is anchored to oncology pricing and is lower than other immunology drugs.

### Luisa Hector (Berenberg)
- Advisory committee for giredestrant: Graham said close contact with the FDA and no indication of an advisory board at this time.
- Capital allocation and BD: Schinecker cited the 93% pipeline value increase since end-2022 and R&D reallocation of about CHF 1.3 billion (including CRO contract renegotiation worth a couple of hundred million, and Flatiron going from losses of close to CHF 250 million to profit). He said Roche remains disciplined on deals (detailed due diligence, material transfer agreements), "not with the back against the wall", and was usually not the highest bidder but wins on expertise and trust.

### Emmanuel Papadakis (Deutsche Bank)
- Giredestrant pricing (SERDs launched above $300,000 in metastatic): Graham said Roche does not comment on pricing before launch and will make "a reasonable pricing decision based on the clinical benefit."
- NXT007 (zemocimig) Phase III: Hemlibra's label allows several dosing frequencies (Q2 and Q4 weeks). Graham did not say whether the head-to-head is for superiority or non-inferiority, and sees durability and convenience as meaningful but not the only advantage. Anti-drug antibodies in Phase I/II had no impact on pharmacokinetics, efficacy or safety and no cross reactions, so they are not seen as clinically meaningful.

### Peter Verdult (BNP Paribas)
- On pricing in a post-MFN (most favored nation) world, Graham gave no figures but said Roche's US/ex-US corridors are tighter than some competitors', and Roche prices on clinical value, ability to pay, and enough return to fund innovation. Roche intends to bring giredestrant to patients worldwide.
- On obesity differentiation, Graham said CT-996 data will come later this year and Roche expects a competitive profile. On petrelintide, 50% of patients on standard treatments don't complete therapy or reach top doses, which is the promise of amylin. Eschli on the missing dose response: this has been seen before with other gut hormones, so there is probably a biology Roche doesn't fully understand.
