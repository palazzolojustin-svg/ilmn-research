# Barclays MedTech & Life Sciences Investor Trip Feedback – Roche Diagnostics CEO Matt Sause (China, ALEXIOS sequencing, mass spec, Liat, IGRA TB, acquisitions)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ROCHE/research/20261002_Barclays_ROP-SW_Roche-_MedTech_-_Life_Sciences_Investor_Trip_Feedback_.md` · Date: 2026-09-28 · Barclays Equity Research (European Pharmaceuticals, Biotechnology & Life Sciences); analysts James Gordon, Charles Pitman-King CFA, Sidhartha Modi; meeting with Roche Diagnostics CEO Matt Sause and Head of Investor Relations Birgit Marjost · sell-side research*

**Summary.** Barclays summarises a virtual meeting with Roche Diagnostics CEO Matt Sause during its 2026 European MedTech & Life Sciences investor trip. Management said China diagnostics growth should return to single digits in FY28 after reform headwinds in FY27, while the new ALEXIOS sequencer and mass spectrometry launches are gaining early traction. Barclays reiterates Overweight on Roche with a CHF430 price target (18x FY28 core EPS) and describes the near-term Diagnostics outlook as balanced but the long-term outlook as positive.

### Valuation methodology

Barclays sets the Roche price target at CHF430, based on 18x 2028E core EPS (core earnings per share, Roche's adjusted profit per share, estimated for 2028). Details:

- The 18x target multiple is about a 34% premium to the current market-cap-weighted large-cap 2028E sector multiple of roughly 14x.
- It is about a 20% premium to the roughly 15.0x multiple Barclays expects the sector to trade at over the next 18 months.
- Barclays argues for the premium for three reasons: (1) Roche's strong 2028–2032E core EPS compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 5.1% versus a sector median of 4.0%; (2) more sustainable growth beyond 2031 given more limited loss-of-exclusivity (LOE, when drug patents expire and generics or biosimilars arrive) pressure; and (3) the longevity of growth in the Diagnostics business, which "isn't subject to LOEs in the same way a Pharma business is."

### Why Barclays is Overweight

Barclays bases its Overweight on Roche's "strong long-term growth outlook, limited patent expiry headwinds, giredestrant breast cancer launch potential, and potential for margin expansion." The strong Diagnostics growth outlook is named as one factor.

### Risks to the price target (Barclays)

Downside risks listed are:
1. Disappointing pipeline data for key pipeline assets.
2. Slower-than-expected launches of on-market products because of competition and/or payer pushback. Examples given: Vabysmo sales declining further as funding issues continue and as it loses market share to HD Eylea, or Hemlibra losing share to Mim8 from Novo Nordisk.
3. Faster erosion than modelled for key assets as they face loss of exclusivity.

No estimate changes (old to new) are given in this note.

## Five key takeaways from the meeting

### 1. China stabilising while underlying developed-market demand remains healthy

- Margin target: Management is targeting stable margins for the Diagnostics division in FY26 as Roche navigates the impact of China, supported by operational efficiency initiatives. [Roche Dx CEO, via Barclays, Sep 2026]
- China timeline: The impact of Chinese healthcare reforms will continue to affect the Chinese market in FY27, followed by a return to single-digit growth in FY28. However, Roche's in-market volume growth in China is already positive.
- Pricing reform: Pricing reform headwinds are viewed as manageable and are already reflected in prior guidance.
- Commitment: The Dx CEO reiterated that Roche remains committed to the Chinese market, with no intention of reducing its presence.
- Rest of world: Beyond China, underlying market growth remains broadly in line with a low-single-digit to mid-single-digit percent (LSD–MSD%) range. Management noted that Roche has continued to outperform competitors despite a weak respiratory season so far.
- PAMA: On the proposed cuts under PAMA (the US Protecting Access to Medicare Act, which governs Medicare lab test pricing), announced by CMS (the US Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) on 21 September 2026, Barclays believes ("we believe") Roche is insulated because it sells directly to laboratories, which are in turn reimbursed by Medicare. This is Barclays' view, not stated as a management claim.

### 2. ALEXIOS sequencing and mass spectrometry launches building momentum

ALEXIOS (Roche's next-generation sequencing, or NGS, platform; NGS reads DNA in bulk):
- Management maintained its ambition of more than CHF1bn in sales for ALEXIOS.
- Roche is "progressing well" in the first 60 days against its initial order target of 100 systems in 12 months.
- Early demand has come from both clinical laboratories and academic centres, supported by long-read capabilities (reading longer continuous stretches of DNA), clinical accuracy and competitive economics.
- Additional data are expected to be presented at ASHG (the American Society of Human Genetics meeting). Management aims for further launch progress to be data-driven.

Mass spectrometry (an instrument technique that identifies molecules by mass; Roche's cobas mass spec systems):
- The roll-out is progressing well.
- Roche still targets a "stretch target" of 100 placements by year-end.
- Engagement is particularly strong from large reference laboratories (big central testing labs), despite validation cycles that are lengthier than expected.

### 3. Confidence in Liat (nucleic acid testing) despite new competition and an uncertain flu outlook

- Liat is Roche's point-of-care PCR platform (rapid molecular tests, here mainly for respiratory infections). In management's view, the existing Liat platform compares favourably with newer market entrants, supported by Roche's broader test menu, its real-time PCR platform and an established installed base of "around one thousand" Liat systems this year.
- New Liat placements continue to be largely additive. The Dx CEO suggested there has not been any meaningful competitive impact to date.
- Respiratory demand remains weak in the 2025/26 season (as written in the note). It is too early to predict the size of the full season.
- Management noted that data such as declining vaccination rates and increasing Flu-A activity can typically suggest a more severe season, but forecasting the magnitude is difficult at this point.

### 4. IGRA TB launch focused on evidence generation and leveraging the installed base

IGRA TB is Roche's interferon-gamma release assay for tuberculosis (a blood test for TB infection).
- European launch: CE mark received July 2026. Roche is targeting known laboratory customers through its existing immunoassay footprint (its installed base of immunoassay analysers).
- Early feedback was described as encouraging but still at an early stage. Some tender-driven contracts (public purchasing processes) take more time for validation.
- US launch: the Dx CEO gave no update on timing, saying only that investors would be kept informed as plans develop.
- Evidence: Roche remains confident in its assay, but the lack of a gold standard (an accepted definitive reference test) means it cannot substantiate superiority or compare the specificity of its IGRA TB test versus incumbents. Roche is generating additional data and large-scale analyses to further demonstrate the strength of the assay.

### 5. Recent acquisitions strengthen the end-to-end oncology and development diagnostics position

- Management highlighted SAGA Diagnostics and PathAI as important additions that expand Roche's molecular diagnostics offering across oncology, MRD (minimal residual disease, detection of tiny amounts of remaining cancer after treatment) and companion diagnostics (tests that determine which patients suit a particular drug).
- Combined with Digital LightCycler (Roche's digital PCR platform, as named here), ALEXIOS sequencing and the broader Elecsys immunoassay portfolio, Roche believes it can increasingly offer a comprehensive end-to-end solution to both laboratories and pharmaceutical partners.
- The strategic objective is to become the preferred development partner, for both Roche and other pharma companies, across the oncology ecosystem by combining diagnostics, sequencing, pathology and pharma capabilities within a single platform.

## Barclays' overall assessment

Barclays sees a "balanced outlook" for Roche Dx near-term growth, citing four factors:
1. Strong but early launch outlooks for ALEXIOS (NGS) and cobas mass spec systems.
2. Limited incremental insight into a potential US launch for the IGRA TB assay.
3. Continued China top-line growth headwinds to 2028, despite stable margins.
4. Low flu rates seen in the quarter to date.

Over the mid and long term, Barclays remains positive on: Roche Dx leveraging its installed base to keep gaining share; NGS and mass spec systems becoming large, differentiated revenue drivers; and Roche Dx benefiting from its competitive advantage as a preferred development partner for laboratories and pharma partners through comprehensive end-to-end solutions.
