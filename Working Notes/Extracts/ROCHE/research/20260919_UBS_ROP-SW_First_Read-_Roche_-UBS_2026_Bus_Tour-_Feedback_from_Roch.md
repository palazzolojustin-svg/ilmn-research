## Key takeaways from the meeting (UBS summary)

1. Roche believes mid-term sales growth momentum can be sustained to the end of the decade without any additional Phase 3 readouts (Phase 3 is the large final-stage clinical trial).
2. Roche remains confident in its obesity strategy and sees CT-388 as a "truly differentiated asset".
3. Roche is excited by the launch potential of giredestrant and sees it hitting the consensus figure of about CHF 500m in 2027E. UBS notes the early launch may need to lean on free drug programs, especially in Medicare Part D, but early patient access should drive significant revenue in 2027 and support multi-billion-franc mid-term potential.
4. UBS sees current consensus for fenebrutinib as reflecting the primary progressive MS (PPMS) opportunity, with any sales in relapsing MS (RMS) as potential upside.
5. Roche is excited about several pipeline assets in neurology and immunology.

## Sales growth outlook

UBS asked whether Roche can maintain mid-single-digit sales growth in the mid-term. Roche said it believes it can sustain mid-single-digit growth momentum to the end of the decade without additional Phase 3 readouts [Roche management via UBS, 2026-09]. It gave two reasons: the potential of giredestrant, and Roche's lower "LOE burden" (loss of exclusivity, meaning the revenue lost when patents expire and cheaper copies arrive) compared with many European pharma peers.

## Obesity strategy

- Roche believes that at the current dose, CT-388 (a drug that activates both the GIP and GLP-1 gut-hormone receptors, as does Eli Lilly's tirzepatide) could deliver higher mid-term weight loss than tirzepatide, with potential approaching "triple G" (a reference to triple-hormone-agonist drugs).
- Roche is also testing higher doses of CT-388 that could have even stronger efficacy, and potential combinations with CT-388.
- Management expects obesity-market prices to decline continuously, but expects the volume opportunity to more than offset the price decline.
- Roche is "making every effort" to make manufacturing as cost-efficient as possible at its new site at Holly Springs (the note spells it "Holy Springs").

## Giredestrant launch (HR+ breast cancer)

- Roche is excited about giredestrant in HR+ (hormone-receptor-positive) breast cancer.
- It will have to work through payor access at launch, especially in Medicare Part D (the US government's outpatient prescription drug benefit). This may require some free drug programs to build patient volume.
- Because patients will likely stay on the drug for about five years, Roche sees the free-drug effect as temporary.
- In the first few quarters, the key metric to track will be the number of patients on the drug. Revenue is expected to "inflect strongly later in the year" (that is, later in 2027).
- Roche is confident of reaching the consensus 2027E expectation of about CHF 500m.
- UBS's headline for this section: the launch "will initially focus on building patient volume."

## Fenebrutinib and multiple sclerosis

- UBS says the current risk-adjusted consensus for fenebrutinib of about CHF 1bn by 2032E may represent only the opportunity in PPMS (primary progressive MS, a form with steady worsening from onset). Sales in RMS (relapsing MS) could be upside to those estimates.
- Roche management looks forward to seeing the full remibrutinib data (Novartis's competing BTK inhibitor, a drug class that targets immune cells in MS) in late October 2026, to compare relative efficacy, with "all the caveats of cross trial comps" (comparing results from different trials is unreliable).
- Roche noted there were no liver toxicity issues in the fenebrutinib studies once liver monitoring was introduced.

## Pipeline highlights

- Management highlighted the "breadth and depth of late stage innovation" in oncology and immunology, including trontinemab (Alzheimer's), prasinezumab (Parkinson's disease) and TL1A (an immunology target).
- It also called out B7H3 (an oncology target), its BTK degrader, and an anti-CD20 brain shuttle in multiple sclerosis.
- UBS expects to hear more on the late- and mid-stage pipeline at Roche's Pharma Day on 28 September 2026.

## Company description (UBS)

Roche is a Swiss global pharmaceuticals (about 75% of sales) and diagnostics (about 25%) company. Its main areas are oncology, neuroscience, ophthalmology, immunology and rare diseases, and it is committed to investing R&D in larger disease areas including obesity. Roche Diagnostics focuses on biomarkers and companion diagnostics, alongside next-generation DNA sequencing, continuous glucose monitoring and mass spectrometry. Roche Pharma has three business units: Roche, Genentech (100% owned) and a majority stake in Chugai.

## Risks (UBS)

General biopharma risks: pipeline success or failure, regulatory decisions, PBM (pharmacy benefit manager) and country drug-pricing negotiations, competitor products, patent challenges and product liability.

Roche-specific downside risks:
1. Lower Vabysmo (eye drug) penetration given the launch of the Eylea 8mg competitor.
2. More competition for Ocrevus (MS drug) from Kesimpta and BTK inhibitors.
3. Negative pipeline newsflow on key late-stage assets.
4. More competition in HER2 (Herceptin, Perjeta, Kadcyla) from new entrants.
5. Faster biosimilar (copycat biologic) erosion.

Upside risks:
1. Stronger Vabysmo and Ocrevus penetration.
2. Positive trontinemab data in Alzheimer's.
3. Diagnostics sales growth ahead of expectations on new technology launches (NGS, CGM, mass spec).
4. Lower-than-expected competition for Ocrevus, Vabysmo, Hemlibra and HER2.
