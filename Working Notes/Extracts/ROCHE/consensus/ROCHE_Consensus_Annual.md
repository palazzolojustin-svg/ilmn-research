**Summary.** Comprehensive Bloomberg consensus sheet for Roche covering actual results FY2022–FY2025 and estimated FY2026–FY2031, with full product-level detail across pharmaceuticals and diagnostics. Consensus expects total revenue to grow from CHF 61.5 billion (2025) to CHF 76.7 billion (2031), driven largely by pharmaceutical growth (particularly newer oncology and specialty care products like Vabysmo, Polivy, Columvi, and Phesgo) and diagnostics stabilization after near-term contraction. ## Pharmaceutical Product Portfolio: Major Franchises by Therapeutic Area

The pharmaceutical division is organized into five therapeutic franchises: **Oncology** (largest, ~30% of pharma revenue), **Neurology** (~24%), **Immunology** (~15%), **Haemophilia** (~11%), and **Ophthalmology** (~10%), plus smaller franchises in infectious diseases and other therapeutics.

### Oncology Portfolio

Roche's oncology franchise is worth ~CHF 14–15 billion annually and is characterized by a clear patent cliff: iconic franchises (Herceptin, Avastin, Perjeta, Kadcyla) are in steep decline, offset by newer products (Phesgo, Polivy, Columvi, and emerging candidates).

**Oncology Growth Narrative**: The consensus reflects a portfolio in transition. The HER2 complex (Herceptin, Perjeta, Kadcyla, Phesgo) collectively falls from CHF 6.5 billion (2025) to CHF 5.5 billion (2031), a 16% decline. However, newer agents—particularly the bispecific antibody Columvi (glofitamab), which enters the forecast at CHF 288 million (2025) and grows to CHF 1.2 billion (2031)—and expanded use of Polivy and Gazyva offset much of this erosion. Tecentriq (PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitor), the franchise's largest oncology asset at CHF 3.6 billion, is forecast roughly flat through 2029 (with growth in select indications like hepatocellular carcinoma and metastatic non-small-cell lung cancer), then declining in 2030–2031 as biosimilar competition intensifies.

Columvi growth is the most striking: it enters at CHF 288 million and reaches CHF 1.2 billion by 2031, a compound annual growth rate of 25%. This reflects new indication approvals and market expansion beyond its initial 2025 launch indication (relapsed/refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma).

The franchise is anchored by **Ocrevus** (anti-CD20 for relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis) and **Evrysdi** (onasemnogene abeparvovec for spinal muscular atrophy), both growth drivers, alongside legacy products.

### Infectious Diseases and Other Therapeutics

This portfolio is minimal. **Ronapreve** (casirivimab/imdevimab COVID combination) fell from CHF 1.7 billion (2022) to essentially zero by 2026E, reflecting the end of the COVID-19 pandemic emergency. Other drugs like **Activase/TNKase** (thrombolytics, CHF 1.1–1.2 billion) and **Xofluza** (baloxavir, for influenza, rising from CHF 407 million in 2025 to CHF 379 million in 2031) are stable but small.

## Notable Observations and Assumptions

1. **Patent Cliff Severity**: The consensus reflects acute patent cliff pressure on iconic franchises. Herceptin falls 51% by 2031; Perjeta falls 72%; Actemra falls 62%; Avastin falls 54%. Collectively, these four franchises lose CHF 4+ billion in annual revenue. This is a material structural headwind.

2. **New Product Growth Offsets**: Polivy, Columvi, Vabysmo, Phesgo, and Enspryng are expected to collectively grow from CHF 2.1 billion (2025 combined) to CHF 4.4 billion (2031), adding ~2.3 billion in incremental revenue. This partially but significantly offsets the patent cliff, but does not fully compensate.

3. **Pipeline Optionality Not Conservative**: The consensus assigns meaningful revenue to early-stage pipeline candidates (Giredestrant, Divarasib, fenebrutinib, and others), with cumulative pipeline revenue reaching CHF 5–10 billion annually by 2031. This is optimistic and assumes successful development, approvals, and commercial uptake for multiple candidates simultaneously. Clinical and regulatory risk is substantial.

4. **Diagnostic Division Recovery Assumption**: The D&D division is forecast to recover strongly from a 2023–2025 trough, growing 5–6% annually through 2031. The source does not explain the drivers; this may reflect assumption of pent-up testing demand, new test launches, or market stabilization post-COVID.

5. **Operating Leverage and Margin Expansion**: The consensus assumes revenue growing at 2.9% CAGR (2025–2031) while operating expenses grow at ~0.8% CAGR, delivering 700+ basis points of operating margin expansion. This high operating leverage requires disciplined cost management and/or assumes productivity gains, and may be optimistic if R&D investments ramp for pipeline programs.

6. **Tax Rate Assumption**: The consensus applies a relatively stable ~19.7% tax rate throughout the forecast, suggesting no material tax-law changes or geographic mix shifts. This is conservative in the context of rising international revenue (which may face higher tax rates in some markets).

7. **Share Count Decline**: The consensus assumes share buybacks reducing diluted share count from 803 million (2025) to 776 million (2031), a 3.4% reduction. This is modest and consistent with the strong FCF generation and improving balance sheet.

8. **Ocrevus Cliff in 2029–2031**: The sharp Ocrevus decline (CHF 8.3 billion in 2028 to CHF 5.9 billion in 2031) is unexplained but suggests either biosimilar entry, loss of market share, or a modeling assumption of commercial maturation. Ocrevus is Roche's largest single product, so this assumption is critical to the entire forecast and bears close monitoring.

## Summary

The Bloomberg consensus forecast reflects Roche as a mature, profitable pharmaceutical and diagnostics company with **modest revenue growth (2.9% CAGR 2025–2031), strong operating leverage (margin expansion from 35.5% to 39.8%), and robust cash generation (free cash flow of CHF 16.7–21.2 billion annually)**. The consensus is built on the assumption that new product launches (Polivy, Columvi, Vabysmo, Enspryng, Elevidys) and pipeline optionality sufficiently offset patent cliff erosion on legacy franchises (Herceptin, Avastin, Perjeta, Actemra, Xolair). The balance sheet is forecast to strengthen dramatically, with net cash turning positive by 2027 and reaching CHF 29.3 billion by 2031. Dividends are expected to grow modestly at 2.8% CAGR, with increasing capital returned via share buybacks as cash generation accelerates. **Key risks include the unexplained Ocrevus cliff assumption, pipeline execution risk, biosimilar competition, and the sensitivity of the entire forecast to assumed diagnostics recovery.**
