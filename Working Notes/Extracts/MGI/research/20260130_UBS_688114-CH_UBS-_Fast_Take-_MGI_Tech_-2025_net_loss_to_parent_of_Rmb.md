**Summary.** A short UBS note on MGI Tech's preliminary 2025 results. ## Headline and UBS's take

**Headline [UBS, 2026-01-26]:** MGI Tech's 2025 net loss to parent (profit or loss attributable to the shareholders of the listed company, excluding minority interests) is Rmb221-273m, versus a loss of Rmb601m in 2024. The note presents this as a range, which indicates a preliminary company figure rather than final audited results. The document does not say so explicitly.

**Our Take [UBS]:**
- The result is higher than UBS's estimate of a Rmb126m loss. This means the loss is larger than UBS forecast, even though it is much smaller than in 2024. UBS's own table shows 2025E net earnings of (126), matching that figure.
- UBS's view: "Cost savings, FX gain and tax reversal helped narrow losses YoY, though partially offset by impairment loss in our view." In plain terms, three items helped the loss shrink versus 2024: lower costs, a foreign-exchange gain, and a reversal of tax charges. UBS believes an impairment loss (a write-down of an asset's value) partly offset them.
- UBS gives no figures for the size of each item and no quarterly breakdown. It does not say whether the rating or the price target is being changed. It does not show old versus new estimates.

## Company description [UBS]

UBS describes MGI Tech as "China and the world's leading genome sequencer and lab automation equipment producer." It focuses on R&D and on the production and sales of life sciences and biotech-related instruments and reagents. Its main businesses are:
- Genome sequencing, the major revenue contributor and the one that "showcases the company's competitive edge."
- Lab automation, which complements the sequencing business.
- A "new business" segment exploring digitalization opportunities in life sciences.

## Risks [UBS]

Major downside risks:
1. IP (intellectual property) litigation and geopolitical tensions.
2. Technical road map shifts.
3. Macro pressure dragging research and clinical funding.
4. Slower genome sequencing downstream adoption.
5. Tightening regulations in the life science industry.
6. Intensifying competition from new entrants.

Major upside risks, which mirror the downside list:
1. Reduced geopolitical tensions.
2. Faster tech upgrades and product iterations.
3. Macro stimulus boosting research and clinical funding.
4. Wider genome sequencing downstream adoption.
5. Loosening regulations in the life science industry.
6. Less competition from new entrants.
