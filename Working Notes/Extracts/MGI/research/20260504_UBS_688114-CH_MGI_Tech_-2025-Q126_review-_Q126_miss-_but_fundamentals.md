# UBS review of MGI Tech 2025 and Q1 2026 results: Q1 miss, estimates and price target cut, Neutral maintained

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/MGI/research/20260504_UBS_688114-CH_MGI_Tech_-2025-Q126_review-_Q126_miss-_but_fundamentals.md` · Date: 2026-04-29 · UBS Securities Co. Limited (UBS Global Research, China Advanced Medical Devices); analysts Henry Liu, CFA, Chen Chen, PhD, and Judy Xie (associate) · sell-side research*

**Summary.** UBS reviews MGI Tech's full-year 2025 and Q1 2026 results. Q1 revenue grew 24.8% from a very low base, but profit missed UBS forecasts. UBS cut its 2026-28 operating forecasts for weak demand and tougher competition, cut its price target to Rmb58.00 from Rmb73.80, and kept a Neutral rating. The note also details MGI's out-licensing and divestment deals and its acquisition of nanopore and spatial-omics platforms, and says fundamentals "may have bottomed."

## Overview and bottom line

This is a UBS note dated 29 April 2026, written after MGI Tech reported full-year 2025 and Q1 2026 results. MGI makes DNA sequencers (machines that read genetic code) and the reagents (chemical kits) they consume, and it also makes lab automation equipment. The note is in question-and-answer form, with a "thesis map" and detailed estimate tables.

UBS's conclusions:
- Q1 2026 results "missed our forecasts" [UBS, 2026-04-29].
- The report's subtitle says fundamentals "may have bottomed."
- UBS cut its 2026-28 operating assumptions, and its price target from Rmb58.00... precisely, from Rmb73.80 to Rmb58.00.
- The rating stays **Neutral**.

## Q: How did results compare with expectations?

- **FY2025 revenue** was Rmb2.78bn, down 7.73% year on year.
- **FY2025 net loss to the parent** was Rmb222m, and the **recurring net loss** (excluding one-offs) was Rmb390m. A year earlier (2024) these were Rmb601m and Rmb653m. UBS says the full-year figures were "largely in line with the preliminary results."
- **Q1 2026 revenue** rose 24.8% year on year to Rmb585m, from a low base in Q1 2025.
- **Q1 2026 recurring net loss** narrowed to Rmb88.5m from Rmb144m in Q1 2025. UBS attributes this, "in our view," to a year-on-year improvement in gross profit margin (GPM, the share of revenue left after direct costs) and to cost control.
- Q1 2026 results still missed UBS's own forecasts.

## Q: What were the most noteworthy areas?

### 1) The "seq-all" segment (sequencers, reagents and related)
- 2025 revenue was Rmb2.20bn, down 8.04% year on year. Growth rebounded to about 23% in Q1 2026 from a low base.
- **By region, 2025:** domestic China revenue -11%; Asia-Pacific (APAC) -29%; Europe, Middle East and Africa (EMEA) +4%; Americas +21%.
- **By product:** instrument revenue fell 17% in 2025 and 4% in Q1 2026. Reagent revenue fell 6% in 2025 but rose 23% in Q1 2026.
- **Volumes:** sequencer sales volume rose to over 1,470 units in 2025, from over 1,260 in 2024. UBS thinks the average selling price (ASP) was dragged by pricing pressure and product mix changes.
- **Market share:** MGI said its share of public bids in China's sequencing market rose to about 70% in 2025.

### 2) Other segments
In 2025 and Q1 2026, the omics segment (new products and services) grew a rapid 48%, driven by new products. Generative Lab Intelligence (GLI, the lab-automation segment) revenue was -32% in 2025 and +10% in Q1 2026.

### 3) Corporate actions
In February, MGI announced acquiring nanopore-sequencing and spatial-omics platforms and divesting its US subsidiary (details below).

## Q: Has the outlook changed?

UBS lowered its 2026-28 operating assumptions "to reflect weak market demand and intensified competition." It still expects:
- Revenue growth in the "teens" percent range, mainly from seq-all and omics.
- Net profit breakeven in 2026.
- Margin expansion from a higher revenue mix of reagents and continued cost cutting.
- Further profit upside from the possible closing of the out-licensing and divestment deals in 2026-27.

UBS argues this should raise product competitiveness, reduce MGI's exposure to geopolitical risk, and improve long-term profitability. This is UBS's outlook, not company guidance; no company guidance is quoted.

## Valuation and price target

- UBS cut its target to Rmb58.00 from Rmb73.80 and kept Neutral.
- The target uses a discounted cash flow (DCF, valuing future cash flows in today's money). The WACC (discount rate) is 8.7%, up from 8.5%, after updating the risk-free rate and beta.
- UBS assumes the US divestment completes in early 2027, booking a milestone payment and an asset disposal gain in 2027.
- The target implies 7.0x 2026E price-to-sales, a 23% premium to the medtech peer average, which UBS says reflects MGI's "stronger global positioning."
- At the current price MGI trades at 6.9x 2026E P/S, a 21% premium to the China medtech peer average.

## Thesis map

### Pivotal questions
- **Can MGI keep gaining share in domestic and international next-generation sequencing (NGS)?** UBS says yes: even amid rising geopolitical tensions, MGI has gained share globally thanks to product competitiveness and an overseas sales network. But with "lacklustre demand and geopolitical uncertainties, the process could take some time."
- **Can NGS companies stay technologically competitive long term?** UBS thinks high-throughput sequencing (NGS) "will remain the mainstream technology for the foreseeable future," based on costs, stability and applications.

### Evidence UBS cites
1. **13 Oct 2025:** MGI out-licensed its CoolMPS technology outside APAC and Greater China to Swiss Rockets, for a US$20m upfront payment, a US$20m milestone payment, and future tiered sales royalties.
2. **24 Feb 2026:** MGI announced the sale of its US subsidiary, Complete Genomics Inc, for US$50m, plus out-licensing of StandardMPS in the US and Canada to Swiss Rockets for future sales royalties.
3. **1 Feb 2026:** MGI announced buying nanopore-sequencing and spatial-omics platforms from BGI Tech for Rmb365.7m. Earnings commitments run over 2028-30 for the nanopore platform and 2026-28 for the spatial platform.
4. **Import substitution:** at end-2025 MGI had shipped about 60% of last year's orders tied to China import-substitution demand, per management.

### What is priced in
- The share price has underperformed the SW Medtech index year to date, because consensus cut revenue growth and margin forecasts on worse-than-expected demand and gross margin deterioration from intensifying competition.
- UBS thinks it will take time for consensus to judge how the nanopore/spatial acquisition and US divestment affect financials, short and long term.
- At 6.9x 2026E P/S (21% premium to peers), UBS believes MGI's "higher tech barrier and stronger competitive positioning are fairly priced in," hence Neutral.

### UBS's reasons for the changes
- **Revenue cuts (2026-28):** (1) lower ASP, from a higher mix of lower-throughput sequencers and competitive pressure; (2) lower reagent consumption on weak end-market demand; (3) divestment of the US subsidiary (assumed to complete early 2027).
- **Gross margin cuts:** lower pricing and reagent consumption assumptions, partly offset by higher-margin sales royalties.
- **Operating profit:** cut from 2028, but operating margins are slightly raised over 2026-28 to reflect company cost-saving initiatives and the divestment of the loss-making US subsidiary.
- **Non-operating items raised in 2026-27:** to reflect upfront payments, milestone payments and an asset disposal gain, assuming the out-licensing and divestment deals close.

## Company description

UBS describes MGI as "China and the world's leading genome sequencer and lab automation equipment producer," focused on R&D and the production and sale of life-sciences and biotech instruments and reagents. Its main businesses are genome sequencing (the major revenue contributor and competitive edge), lab automation and a new business that explores digitalisation opportunities in life sciences. The sequencer and lab automation businesses complement each other.

## Risks (UBS)

- **Downside:** (1) IP litigation and geopolitical tensions; (2) technical roadmap shifts; (3) macro pressure dragging research and clinical funding; (4) slower adoption of genome sequencing downstream; (5) tightening life-science regulation; (6) intensifying competition from new entrants.
- **Upside:** (1) reduced geopolitical tensions; (2) faster tech upgrades and product iteration; (3) macro stimulus boosting research and clinical funding; (4) wider downstream adoption; (5) looser life-science regulation; (6) less competition from new entrants.
