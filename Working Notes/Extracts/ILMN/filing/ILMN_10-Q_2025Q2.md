# Illumina Q2 2025 10-Q — Return to profitability post-GRAIL, revenue decline amid China headwinds and tariff pressures

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-Q_2025Q2.md` · Date: 2025-06-29 · SEC filing, Illumina Inc. Form 10-Q for quarter ended June 29, 2025 · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina returned to profitability in Q2 2025 with net income of $235M and $366M YTD, up from prior-year losses driven by GRAIL goodwill impairment. Total revenue declined 5% to $1.06B in Q2 and 4% to $2.1B YTD, reflecting the June 2024 GRAIL spin-off and pressure from Chinese regulatory restrictions prohibiting instrument exports and reduced NIH funding. Gross margins improved to 65.6% despite a $23M intangible asset impairment and higher tariff-related freight costs. The company announced a $350M acquisition of SomaLogic (proteomics) and authorized $1.5B in share repurchases, of which $696M has been deployed YTD.

## Financial Performance

### Revenue and Segment Results

**Consolidated revenue** declined 5% in Q2 2025 to $1,059 million (from $1,112M in Q2 2024) and 4% year-to-date to $2,100 million (from $2,188M). The decline reflects the June 24, 2024 spin-off of GRAIL and weakness in Core Illumina's core business.

**Core Illumina** (the sole reportable segment as of Q2 2025) generated revenue of $1,059 million in Q2, down 3% from $1,092 million in Q2 2024, and $2,100 million YTD, down 2% from $2,148 million. The segment drives nearly all consolidated results.

**Product revenue** of $912 million in Q2 2025 (down 2% from $927M) and $1,793 million YTD (down 1% from $1,803M) comprises two categories:

*Sequencing consumables* (reagent kits, which drive recurring revenue), the largest product category, reached $740 million in Q2 2025, up slightly from $729 million in Q2 2024, and $1,437 million YTD, up from $1,420 million. Growth reflects transition by customers to the NovaSeq X high-throughput platform. *Microarray consumables* declined to $71 million in Q2 from $78 million, and to $143 million YTD from $149 million, as this older technology category continues to shrink.

*Sequencing instruments* revenue fell sharply to $96 million in Q2 from $116 million (down 17%) and to $204 million YTD from $226 million (down 10%), driven by "fewer shipments of our high- and mid-throughput instruments" due to customer capital and cash-flow constraints. The decline was partially offset by increased MiSeq i100 Series shipments following its Q4 2024 launch. *Microarray instruments* remained flat at approximately $5 million.

**Service and other revenue**, comprising instrument service contracts, genotyping and sequencing services, and development agreements, fell to $147 million in Q2 from $185 million and to $307 million YTD from $385 million. The primary driver was loss of GRAIL cancer-detection testing revenues post-spin-off; additionally, Core Illumina's own service revenue declined "primarily due to decreased revenue from our strategic partnerships."

**GRAIL revenue** (retained pre-spin-off for comparison purposes) contributed $29 million in Q2 2024 and $55 million YTD 2024, comprising only service revenue. The business is now entirely separate.

### Revenue by Geography

Illumina's geographic exposure shows material divergence. In Q2 2025, the Americas accounted for $586 million (55% of revenue), down 9% from $640 million (57% of revenue) in Q2 2024, and down 7% YTD to $1,156 million from $1,243 million. This largest market faces headwinds from reduced National Institutes of Health (NIH) funding and customer capital constraints.

Europe grew to $310 million in Q2 (29% of revenue), up 7% from $289 million, and to $603 million YTD, up 6% from $568 million—the sole regional bright spot.

Greater China collapsed to $63 million in Q2 (6% of revenue), down 16% from $75 million, and down 12% YTD to $135 million from $153 million. This deterioration reflects Chinese regulatory authorities' January 2025 decision to add Illumina to the "unreliable entities list" and prohibit the export of sequencing instruments into China. Management stated this will be a "significant headwind" for 2025 and a material revenue reduction.

Asia-Pacific, Middle East, and Africa (APMEA) fell to $100 million in Q2 (9% of revenue), down 7% from $108 million, and down 8% YTD to $206 million from $224 million.

### Gross Margin

**Gross profit** declined modestly to $695 million in Q2 from $721 million and to $1,378 million YTD from $1,388 million, a decline of only 1% despite revenue falling 4%. **Gross margin** improved to 65.6% in both Q2 and YTD 2025 from 64.8% and 63.5%, respectively.

The improvement is largely attributable to the removal of GRAIL's negative-margin cancer testing business following the June 2024 spin-off. However, Core Illumina's own gross margin declined in both periods. Management attributes this decline to several factors: a $23 million impairment of a finite-lived intangible asset (related to a change in planned use of technology), higher freight and customs duties from recently imposed tariffs (notably on imports from Illumina's Singapore manufacturing facility), and increased field service costs. These headwinds were partially offset by lower strategic partnership revenues (which carry lower margins) and a more favorable product mix skewing toward consumables.

**Product gross margin** is materially higher (approximately 70% in both periods) than **service gross margin** (approximately 48–52%), due to the high fixed-cost nature of service delivery.

## Operating Expenses

### Research and Development

**Core Illumina R&D expense** rose to $247 million in Q2 2025 from $241 million in Q2 2024 (+2%), and to $499 million YTD from $479 million (+4%). The increases were driven by higher restructuring charges ($4M Q2, $13M YTD) and increased employee-related compensation and lab supply costs, partially offset by lower share-based compensation (part of a broader optimization initiative) and reduced outside professional services.

### Selling, General and Administrative

**Core Illumina SG&A expense** surged to $234 million in Q2 from $60 million, but the comparison is distorted by the treatment of contingent consideration liabilities. Excluding the effect of gains recognized on GRAIL contingent value rights (CVRs), SG&A decreased, driven by the absence of the $37 million in GRAIL spin-off transaction costs incurred in Q2 2024, lower share-based compensation, reduced outside professional services, a $7 million decrease in accrued interest on a prior European Commission fine, and lower employee compensation costs. Year-to-date, SG&A increased to $501 million from $396 million, but again excluding CVR impacts, the underlying business saw SG&A decline due to the absence of $52 million in spin-off costs incurred in YTD 2024, a $16 million decrease in restructuring charges (primarily from Q1 2024 facility impairments), and the $14 million decrease in Commission fine interest.

**GRAIL contingent value rights (CVRs)** represent the largest driver of SG&A volatility. These instruments entitle holders to quarterly cash payments equal to a pro-rata share of GRAIL's eligible revenues: 2.5% of annual revenues up to $1 billion, and 9% of revenues above $1 billion, continuing through August 2033. The liability is remeasured quarterly at fair value using a Monte Carlo simulation; changes in fair value are recorded in SG&A. In Q2 2025, Illumina recognized only $21 million in gains compared to $271 million in Q2 2024 (a swing of $250 million), and YTD recognized $32 million in gains versus $255 million YTD 2024 (a swing of $223 million). The shift reflects the higher stock price at which GRAIL was trading at the time of the Spin-Off versus current levels, and changed volatility assumptions, which increased the estimated GRAIL CVR liability from $73 million at year-end 2024 to $40 million at June 29, 2025. Management noted that the liability remains sensitive to revenue forecasts and discount rate assumptions; a 20% change in GRAIL's projected revenues would swing the liability by approximately $10–$12 million, while a 250-basis-point change in the discount rate would swing it by approximately $7–$9 million.

### Restructuring Initiatives

Illumina recorded $39 million in restructuring charges YTD 2025 (versus $40 million YTD 2024), comprising $99 million in cumulative employee separation costs (with an estimated $3 million remaining through end of Q4 2025), $146 million in asset impairments since inception, and $8 million in other costs. The Q1 2025 restructuring program targeted $100 million in cost reductions for the full year, driven by the need to mitigate impacts from the China regulatory restrictions and NIH funding uncertainty. Activities included optimization of stock-based compensation, non-labor spending reductions, and workforce reductions. In Q1 2024, the company had recorded $32 million in asset impairments related to right-of-use assets and leasehold improvements for its Foster City, California campus and another San Diego property, reflecting a strategic shift away from those facilities.

### Goodwill and Intangible Asset Impairments

**Q2 2025**: No impairment charges.

**Q2 2024**: $1,886 million in goodwill and intangible asset impairment charges, entirely attributable to GRAIL. This consisted of a $1,466 million goodwill impairment and a $420 million in-process R&D (IPR&D) intangible asset impairment, triggering the multi-billion dollar swing from profitable operations in historical periods.

**YTD 2025**: $23 million intangible asset impairment (recorded in cost of product revenue), reflecting a change in the planned use of a finite-lived intangible asset whose carrying value exceeded estimated fair value, determined via discounted cash flow analysis.

**YTD 2024**: $1,889 million impairment (substantially all GRAIL), plus $3 million Core Illumina IPR&D impairment.

## Other Income (Expense)

### Strategic Investments

Illumina maintains a portfolio of strategic equity investments whose fair value fluctuates materially period-to-period. **Marketable equity securities** (short-term investments) totaled $221 million as of June 29, 2025, up from $93 million at year-end 2024, reflecting gains realized during the period.

In Q2 2025, Illumina recognized **$97 million in net gains** on marketable equity securities (versus $330 million in losses in Q2 2024), and **$128 million YTD** (versus $329 million in losses YTD 2024). The prior-year losses were substantially driven by the $328 million loss on Illumina's retained 14.5% stake in GRAIL following the Spin-Off, when GRAIL's stock price declined sharply. The Q2 2025 gains reflect gains on other strategic positions as GRAIL's equity value recovered somewhat.

**Non-marketable equity securities** (without readily determinable fair values) totaled $46 million, up from $26 million at year-end.

**Venture capital fund investments**, accounted for under the equity method, totaled $224 million in carrying value, up from $201 million. Illumina has outstanding capital commitments of $77 million across three funds: $3 million remaining in Fund I (callable through April 2026, with ~$10 million in recallable distributions), $39 million in Fund II (callable through July 2029), and $35 million in Fund III (callable through December 2034). Venture fund results generated $6 million in gains in Q2 2025 (versus $2 million loss in Q2 2024) and $8 million YTD (versus $3 million gain YTD 2024). Revenue from transactions with strategic investees was $8 million in Q2 and $15 million YTD 2025 (versus $4 million and $7 million, respectively, in the prior year).

### Interest and Debt

**Interest income** was $10 million in Q2 2025 (down from $13 million) and $21 million YTD (down from $25 million), reflecting lower money-market rates. **Interest expense** was $25 million in Q2 2025 (up from $20 million) and $50 million YTD (up from $39 million), reflecting the September 2024 issuance of $500 million in 2026 Term Notes.

### Overall Other Income

Total other income (expense), net, swung dramatically to $92 million in Q2 2025 from a loss of $339 million in Q2 2024, and to $110 million YTD from a loss of $337 million YTD 2024. The turnaround is attributable entirely to the shift from GRAIL-driven investment losses to gains on the broader equity portfolio, as noted above.

## Debt and Capital Structure

### Outstanding Debt

Illumina maintains a four-tranche term debt portfolio totaling $2,000 million in principal:

- **$500 million 2025 Term Notes** (5.800% coupon), maturing December 12, 2025, with semi-annual interest payments in June and December. These are callable at par plus accrued interest after November 12, 2025.

- **$500 million 2026 Term Notes** (4.650% coupon), maturing September 9, 2026, issued in September 2024. These carry make-whole call provisions prior to maturity.

- **$500 million 2027 Term Notes** (5.750% coupon), maturing December 13, 2027. Callable at 100% of par plus accrued interest after November 13, 2027, and at make-whole prices prior.

- **$500 million 2031 Term Notes** (2.550% coupon), maturing March 23, 2031, issued in March 2021. Callable at make-whole prices prior to December 23, 2030, and at par plus accrued interest thereafter.

The net carrying amount of debt was $1,991 million as of June 29, 2025 (down $2 million from year-end 2024 due to amortization of discounts), with $499 million classified as current and $1,492 million as long-term. The fair value of outstanding debt was approximately $1,950 million. Interest expense for the six months was $50 million, including $1 million in amortization of debt issuance costs.

### Revolving Credit Facility

Illumina maintains a $750 million senior unsecured revolving credit facility, established in January 2023, maturing January 4, 2028, with two optional one-year extensions subject to lender consent. The facility includes a $40 million sublimit for swingline borrowings and a $50 million sublimit for letters of credit. As of June 29, 2025, no amounts were drawn, and the company was in compliance with all covenants. The facility's pricing is based on SOFR (or alternate base rate) plus a spread varying with Illumina's debt rating, plus a 0.10% credit spread adjustment. Illumina may request an additional $250 million in incremental commitments or term loans, subject to lender consent. The agreement includes leverage and operating covenants; the leverage ratio (total debt to adjusted EBITDA) may not exceed 3.50x, increasing to 4.00x for the fiscal quarter following a qualified acquisition and the three subsequent quarters.

## Liquidity and Cash Flow

### Cash Position

As of June 29, 2025, Illumina held $934 million in cash and equivalents, of which $407 million was held by foreign subsidiaries, and $221 million in short-term investments (marketable equity securities), for total liquid resources of approximately $1.2 billion. Cash decreased by $193 million in the first half of 2025, driven primarily by share repurchases. Additionally, the $750 million revolving credit facility remained entirely undrawn.

### Operating Cash Flow

Net cash provided by operating activities was $474 million YTD 2025, up substantially from $157 million YTD 2024, driven by return to profitability. The operating cash flow reconciliation began with net income of $366 million, added non-cash charges including $142 million in share-based compensation expense, $137 million in depreciation and amortization, $41 million in deferred tax benefits, and $23 million in intangible asset impairment, and subtracted $133 million in net gains on strategic investments and a $32 million reduction in fair value of contingent liabilities. Working capital changes reduced cash by $79 million, with decreases in accounts receivable (favorable; $49M) and accrued liabilities ($91M) partially offset by an increase in inventory ($28M) and decreases in accounts payable ($26M).

### Investing Cash Flow

Net cash used in investing activities was $112 million YTD 2025, compared to $89 million YTD 2024. This comprised $62 million in capital expenditures, primarily for facilities and manufacturing infrastructure, and $43 million in purchases of strategic investments, plus $7 million in capitalized intangible assets.

### Financing Cash Flow

Net cash used in financing activities was $566 million YTD 2025 (versus $191 million YTD 2024), driven by $570 million in share repurchases and $23 million in taxes paid related to net share settlement of equity awards, partially offset by $28 million in proceeds from the Employee Stock Purchase Plan.

### Capital Allocation and Commitments

In August 2024, Illumina's Board authorized a new share repurchase program for up to $1.5 billion of outstanding common stock, canceling all prior authorizations. During Q2 2025 (including unsettled repurchases), the company repurchased 4.489 million shares at an average price of $84.66 per share for a total cost of $384 million. YTD 2025 repurchases totaled 6.217 million shares for $585 million. As of June 29, 2025, approximately $804 million of authorization remained. Subsequent to quarter-end and through August 1, 2025, an additional 230,300 shares were repurchased for $22 million. Management stated the company intends to repurchase incremental shares over the course of 2025.

Illumina committed to acquire SomaLogic (and certain other assets of Standard BioTools) for $350 million in cash, plus potential milestone payments of up to $75 million based on revenues of certain products and services, plus royalty streams. The transaction was announced June 22, 2025, is expected to close in the first half of 2026, and is subject to receipt of required regulatory approvals, including under the Hart-Scott-Rodino Act. If regulatory approval is not obtained by March 23, 2026 (subject to three automatic three-month extensions), Illumina must pay a $14.5 million termination fee. The acquisition is expected to enhance Illumina's position in the "expanding proteomics market" and advance its multiomics strategy.

Illumina has outstanding capital commitments of $77 million to three venture capital investment funds, with the bulk remaining callable through July 2029 and December 2034.

## Taxes

### Effective Tax Rate

Illumina's effective tax rate was 23.4% in Q2 2025 (compared to (0.6)% in Q2 2024) and 25.1% YTD 2025 (compared to (1.4)% YTD 2024).

The variance from the 21% U.S. federal statutory rate in Q2 and YTD 2025 was primarily driven by the $10 million (Q2) and $20 million (YTD) income tax expense impact of capitalizing research and development expenses for tax purposes, required under U.S. tax law. This was partially offset by a favorable mix of earnings in lower-tax-rate jurisdictions, particularly Singapore. The company noted that new U.S. tax legislation signed July 4, 2025, which eliminates the requirement to capitalize R&D expenses beginning in future years, is expected to reduce the effective tax rate starting in Q3 2025.

In contrast, the prior-year periods were severely disrupted by the $1,886 million GRAIL goodwill impairment, which is non-deductible for tax purposes ($308 million of tax expense impact), as well as the $99–$116 million tax expense impact from pre-acquisition net operating losses on global intangible low-taxed income (GILTI) and Pillar Two global minimum tax considerations, plus $41–$63 million in R&D capitalization impacts.

As of June 29, 2025, prepaid income taxes were $75 million.

## Pending Acquisition and Strategic Initiatives

### SomaLogic / Standard BioTools Acquisition

On June 22, 2025, Illumina entered into a Stock Purchase Agreement to acquire SomaLogic (a proteomics company) and other specified assets from Standard BioTools for $350 million in cash, subject to customary adjustments. The agreement provides for additional consideration in the form of (i) royalty streams tied to specified product and service revenues and (ii) up to $75 million in potential milestone payments to Standard BioTools. The transaction is expected to close in the first half of 2026, subject to customary closing conditions and receipt of required regulatory approvals, including review under the Hart-Scott-Rodino Act and by applicable foreign authorities. The Purchase Agreement contains termination rights if closing has not occurred by March 23, 2026, subject to three automatic three-month extensions related to regulatory approvals. Upon termination under circumstances relating to failure to obtain regulatory approvals, Illumina would owe a $14.5 million termination fee. The agreement contemplates ancillary agreements post-closing, including a transition services agreement and a license agreement granting Standard BioTools a non-transferable, non-sublicensable license to certain Illumina intellectual property.

### GRAIL Spin-Off and Contingent Value Rights

On June 24, 2024, Illumina completed the spin-off of GRAIL, distributing approximately 85.5% of GRAIL's outstanding common stock to Illumina shareholders pro rata (one GRAIL share per six Illumina shares held as of the June 13, 2024 record date). Illumina retained 14.5% of GRAIL's shares immediately following the spin-off. In connection with the separation, Illumina contributed $974 million in cash to GRAIL (the "Disposal Funding") to cover 2.5 years of operations, and incurred $52 million in separation-related transaction costs (recorded in SG&A).

As part of the GRAIL acquisition that preceded the spin-off, Illumina issued contingent value rights (CVRs) to former GRAIL shareholders. These entitle holders to receive quarterly "Covered Revenue Payments" representing a pro-rata share of certain GRAIL-related revenues (the "Covered Revenues"). Under the CVR terms, holders receive 2.5% of annual Covered Revenues up to $1 billion per year, and 9% of Covered Revenues above $1 billion per year, for a 12-year period through August 2033. 

As of June 29, 2025, the estimated fair value of Illumina's contingent consideration liability related to the CVRs was $40 million, down from $73 million at year-end 2024. Illumina uses a Monte Carlo simulation to estimate fair value, incorporating forecasts of GRAIL revenues, a revenue risk premium, revenue volatility, operational leverage, and counterparty credit spread. Since the spin-off, Illumina no longer has access to GRAIL management forecasts and relies on publicly available GRAIL information and analyst reports. In Q2 2025, Illumina employed a 4% revenue risk premium, derived using the Capital Asset Pricing Model and comparable company betas, reflecting the heightened volatility of GRAIL's market capitalization.

Sensitivity analysis: a 20% increase or decrease in forecasted GRAIL revenues would increase or decrease the liability by approximately $12 million or $10 million, respectively; a 250-basis-point increase or decrease in the revenue risk premium would decrease or increase the liability by approximately $7 million or $9 million, respectively.

During YTD 2025, Covered Revenues for Q4 2024 through Q1 2025 totaled $70 million, generating Covered Revenue Payments of $660,000, which were paid in H1 2025.

## Legal Proceedings and Settlements

### Shareholder Derivative Litigation

Multiple stockholder derivative complaints have been filed in Delaware Court of Chancery against Illumina's current and former directors and officers, alleging breach of fiduciary duty in connection with the GRAIL acquisition. These include:

**Icahn Partners LP et al. v. deSouza et al.** (filed October 17, 2023): On April 11, 2025, the parties agreed to a settlement in principle of this action, involving a release of claims with no payment by any party.

**City of Omaha Police and Firefighters Retirement System v. deSouza et al.** (filed February 26, 2024): Motion to dismiss filed March 26, 2024; action consolidated with others.

**City of Roseville General Employees Retirement System et al. v. deSouza et al.** (filed April 16, 2024): Motion to dismiss filed May 16, 2024; action consolidated.

**Thomas P. DiNapoli v. John Thompson et al.** (filed August 16, 2024): Stipulated dismissal of this action occurred March 14, 2025.

**The Pavers and Road Builders Benefit Funds v. deSouza et al.** (filed December 23, 2024): Motion to dismiss filed January 21, 2025; action consolidated.

On March 27, 2025, parties to the Icahn, Omaha, Roseville, and Pavers actions filed a stipulation to consolidate. On April 17, 2025, the court directed parties to refile the consolidation once the Icahn settlement was finalized and to proceed with briefing on motions to dismiss. Opening briefs were filed June 2–3, 2025; a stipulation on July 17, 2025 extended the plaintiff's deadline to file an amended complaint to August 8, 2025, with further briefing scheduled through December 2025 and a hearing on the motion to dismiss set for February 13, 2026. Illumina denies all allegations and intends to vigorously defend.

Additionally, in May and August 2024, stockholders Michael Warner and Jane Davidson, respectively, sent litigation demand letters to the Board requesting that the Board pursue claims on behalf of Illumina against GRAIL-acquisition participants. In May and October 2024, the Board determined to defer final decisions, pending the outcome of the pending stockholder lawsuits, and stated it will monitor and revisit as the lawsuits progress.

### Federal Securities Class Action

**In re Illumina, Inc. Securities Litigation** (consolidated from three separate cases filed November 2023 – January 2024 in U.S. District Court for the Southern District of California): The consolidated action was established April 11, 2024, with three lead plaintiffs appointed. A second amended consolidated complaint was filed September 13, 2024, alleging violations of Sections 10(b) and 20(a) of the Securities Exchange Act and Rule 10b-5 in connection with the GRAIL acquisition. The defendants filed a motion to dismiss November 12, 2024; plaintiffs' opposition was filed December 20, 2024; and defendants' reply was filed February 3, 2025. No hearing date has been set. Illumina denies the allegations and intends to defend vigorously.

### State Securities Class Action

Two separate securities class actions were consolidated in California Superior Court, County of San Mateo, alleging material misstatements and omissions in November 2020 and February 2021 registration statements and prospectuses relating to the GRAIL acquisition. The consolidated complaint was filed August 12, 2024. The court declined to stay the litigation on October 4, 2024. Defendants filed demurrers February 28, 2025; plaintiffs' oppositions were filed April 30, 2025; replies were filed May 30, 2025. An initial hearing on demurrers was held June 20, 2025, with further argument scheduled for August 29, 2025.

### DOJ Settlement

On January 18, 2024, Illumina received a Civil Investigative Demand (CID) from the U.S. Department of Justice as part of a False Claims Act investigation regarding allegations that the company misrepresented its compliance with cybersecurity requirements to the FDA and federal agencies in connection with the sale of sequencing instruments, causing false claims to be submitted to Medicare and other federal government programs.

On July 22, 2025, while denying the allegations and any liability, and to avoid litigation costs and distraction, Illumina entered into a settlement agreement with the Department of Justice. Under the settlement, the company will make a one-time payment of $9.8 million plus interest in exchange for a release from civil monetary claims relating to the sale of certain sequencing instruments sold to federal agencies between 2016 and 2023.

## Risk Factors and Business Challenges

### China Regulatory Restrictions

In Q1 2025, Chinese regulatory authorities added Illumina to the "unreliable entities list" and issued notice prohibiting the company from exporting sequencing instruments into China. This represents a material blow to the Greater China business, which contributed $63 million in revenue in Q2 2025 (6% of total), down 16% YoY. Management expects these restrictions to result in "a reduction in revenue and related operating income from our Greater China business" and represents a "significantly uncertain" headwind extending through 2025. As a result, Illumina implemented an incremental $100 million cost reduction program in Q1 2025 to help mitigate the impact.

### Tariffs and Trade Policy

Beginning in April 2025, the U.S. government and several other countries enacted tariffs affecting Illumina's manufacturing and supply chain. Management identified the largest impact stems from importation from its manufacturing facility in Singapore, with additional exposure from parts and subassemblies imported into the U.S. and imports into China. Tariff-related freight and duties costs were a material headwind to gross margin in Q2 2025. Management stated the company has undertaken "several actions to fully mitigate" tariff impacts, expecting "partial mitigation" in 2025 and "more full mitigation" in 2026, through supply chain optimization, cost measures, and pricing actions. The uncertainty regarding future trade policy and the efficacy of mitigation efforts represents a material risk.

### NIH Funding and Customer Capital Constraints

U.S. government reductions in NIH funding, and uncertainty regarding potential cancellations or targeted reductions of certain grants or contracts, represent a material headwind to demand, particularly among research customers. Additionally, research customers are "managing inventory and capital more conservatively," delaying projects due to funding concerns. These dynamics drove declines in instruments revenue (capital equipment purchases are discretionary) in Q2 and YTD 2025.

### Macroeconomic Uncertainty

Macroeconomic factors including inflation, exchange rate fluctuations, and concerns about economic downturn continue to impact customer behavior and Illumina's cost structure. The company explicitly incorporated these uncertainties in its critical accounting estimates.

## Products and Markets

Illumina serves three primary markets: research, clinical, and applied (agriculture, consumer genomics, and other emerging segments).

### Sequencing Platform Portfolio

The company's sequencing platform portfolio ranges across throughput levels. The **NovaSeq X** platform addresses high-throughput applications and is driving customer migration, with strong consumable adoption observed in Q2 2025. The **NextSeq** platform serves mid-throughput research applications. The **MiSeq i100 Series**, launched in Q4 2024, is generating incremental instrument sales. Instruments revenue declined sharply in Q2 and YTD 2025 due to customer capital constraints, but consumables revenue benefited from the transition to higher-throughput platforms.

### Array-Based Products

Microarray-based solutions represent approximately 8% of revenue and are in structural decline, falling to $87 million in Q2 from $96 million YoY. This category comprises both consumables (chips) and legacy instruments and serves mainly research and quality control applications.

### Services

Service and other revenue comprises instrument service contracts, genotyping services, sequencing services provided to third parties, development and licensing agreements, and (prior to the June 24, 2024 Spin-Off) GRAIL cancer detection services. Service revenue fell to $147 million in Q2 (down 21% YoY) and to $307 million YTD (down 20% YoY), driven by the loss of GRAIL revenues post-spin-off and declines in strategic partnership revenues.

### Customers

Illumina's customer base includes leading genomic research centers, academic institutions, government laboratories, and hospitals, as well as pharmaceutical, biotechnology, commercial molecular diagnostic, and consumer genomics companies. The company noted continued customer wariness regarding capital expenditure and increased inventory management discipline in response to funding uncertainty and macroeconomic headwinds.

## Derivative Financial Instruments and Foreign Exchange Hedging

Illumina uses foreign currency forward contracts to manage exposure to currencies in which it transacts. As of June 29, 2025, the company had:

- **Non-designated hedges**: Foreign currency forward contracts with total notional amounts of $483 million, used to hedge monetary assets and liabilities denominated in currencies other than the U.S. dollar (euro, Japanese yen, Australian dollar, Canadian dollar, Singapore dollar, Chinese Yuan Renminbi, and British pound). These contracts have terms of one month or less. Changes in fair value are recorded in other income (expense), net, along with the remeasurement gain or loss on the hedged foreign currency-denominated assets or liabilities.

- **Designated cash flow hedges**: Foreign currency forward contracts with total notional amounts of $689 million (up from $621 million at year-end), with terms up to 24 months, used to hedge portions of foreign currency exposure associated with forecasted revenue transactions in the same seven currencies. Changes in fair value of effective hedges are recorded in accumulated other comprehensive (loss) income and reclassified to revenue in the same period the underlying transactions are recorded.

In Q2 2025, Illumina recognized a **$2 million loss** in revenue from ineffectiveness and remeasurement of cash flow hedges (compared to a $4 million gain in Q2 2024). YTD 2025 recognized a **$5 million gain** (compared to a $7 million gain YTD 2024). As of June 29, 2025, the fair value of foreign currency forward contracts recorded in assets and liabilities was $2 million and $32 million, respectively. Estimated losses in accumulated other comprehensive (loss) income expected to be recognized into earnings in the next 12 months are $22 million.

## Warranty Reserves

Illumina generally provides a one-year warranty on instruments and a six- to twelve-month warranty on consumables (through the expiration date). The company records warranty expense as cost of product revenue. As of June 29, 2025, the warranty reserve was $16 million, flat compared to a year ago, reflecting a balance between $14 million in additions to the reserve and $16 million in actual warranty replacements and repairs during YTD 2025.
