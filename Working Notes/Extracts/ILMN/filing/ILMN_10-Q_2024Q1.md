# Illumina Q1 2024 10-Q: Flat Revenue, Widening Losses, GRAIL Divestiture Proceeding, EC Fine Accruing

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-Q_2024Q1.md` · Date: 2024-03-31 · SEC filing (10-Q); filed May 3, 2024 · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina's first-quarter 2024 results show revenue essentially flat at $1,076 million (down 1% from $1,087 million in Q1 2023), with a widening operating loss of $111 million (vs. $64 million loss in Q1 2023) driven by higher restructuring charges and GRAIL operating losses. The company is pursuing divestiture of GRAIL following European Commission approval of its divestment plan on April 12, 2024, with target close by end of Q2 2024. The EC fine of €432 million imposed July 2023 now stands at $478 million accrued (including 5.5% annual interest since October) while Illumina appeals. Gross margins improved 170 basis points to 62.0% despite instrument shipment weakness, benefiting from favorable product mix and cost savings. Customer demand remains constrained by capital limitations and macroeconomic uncertainty.

## Financial Results and Revenue

### Revenue Performance

Illumina reported total revenue of $1,076 million in the first quarter of 2024, down $11 million or 1% from $1,087 million in the prior-year quarter ended April 2, 2023 [Q1 2024 10-Q]. The flat performance masks divergent business segments and product categories, with strength in services offset by weakness in instruments.

Core Illumina revenue declined 2% to $1,056 million from $1,076 million, driven primarily by a 29% drop in instruments revenue. GRAIL revenue increased 35% to $27 million from $20 million, primarily from Galleri test sales, though revenue from all GRAIL and Core Illumina eliminated intercompany transactions was $7 million in Q1 2024 [Q1 2024 10-Q].

### Product Revenue Composition

Product revenue (instruments and consumables), representing 81.4% of total revenue, declined 5% to $876 million from $922 million in Q1 2023 [Q1 2024 10-Q]. Within this category:

**Consumables** (which accounted for 87% of product revenue) were essentially flat, declining marginally to $762 million from $764 million. Sequencing consumables, the dominant category at $691 million, increased $5 million or 1% despite broader market headwinds, suggesting continued demand in that segment. Microarray consumables, a smaller but legacy product line, declined 9% to $71 million from $78 million, indicating customer shift away from that technology [Q1 2024 10-Q].

**Instruments** suffered a significant decline, falling 28% to $114 million from $158 million. Management attributed this primarily to a 28% drop in sequencing instruments revenue (down $44 million to $110 million), citing "fewer shipments of our high-throughput instruments, given we entered 2024 with a lower backlog of NovaSeq X instruments, as compared to 2023, given significant pre-orders following the launch, and fewer shipments of our mid-throughput instruments, primarily as capital and cash flow constraints continue to impact our customer's purchasing behavior" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. This reflects a normalization from the surge of advance orders that followed the NovaSeq X launch in 2023, as well as broader customer capital constraint.

### Service and Other Revenue

Service and other revenue, representing 18.6% of total revenue, grew 21% to $200 million from $165 million. Core Illumina service revenue increased 18% to $173 million from $146 million, driven primarily by increased revenue from strategic partnerships. GRAIL service revenue increased 35% to $27 million from $20 million, primarily driven by Galleri test sales [Q1 2024 10-Q].

### Geographic Decomposition

Revenue by geography shows mixed trends across Illumina's markets [Q1 2024 10-Q]:

- **Americas** (largest market at 56% of revenue): $603 million, down 2% from $616 million, indicating slight softness in the core North American market
- **Europe**: $279 million, up 7% from $261 million, the only major region showing year-over-year growth
- **Greater China** (China, Taiwan, and Hong Kong): $78 million, down 14% from $91 million, reflecting significant competitive pressures and macroeconomic headwinds in that region
- **Asia-Pacific, Middle East, and Africa**: $116 million, down 3% from $119 million, relatively stable

Management noted that "since 2023, macroeconomic factors such as inflation, exchange rate fluctuations and concerns about an economic downturn, competitive challenges in our China region, and the sanctions imposed on Russia as a result of the armed conflict between Russia and Ukraine have impacted both Illumina directly and our customers' behavior" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. Management further observed that "some customers experienced supply chain pressures that delayed their lab expansions and others are managing inventory and capital more conservatively" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

## Gross Margin and Cost of Revenue

Consolidated gross profit improved 2% to $667 million from $655 million despite a 1% revenue decline, with gross margin expanding 170 basis points to 62.0% from 60.3% [Q1 2024 10-Q]. This margin expansion despite flat revenue reflects favorable product mix and operational execution.

Total cost of revenue was $409 million, down from $432 million year-over-year, comprising:
- Cost of product revenue: $255 million (down from $285 million), a 10% improvement
- Cost of service and other revenue: $106 million (up from $99 million)
- Amortization of acquired intangible assets: $48 million (essentially flat, down from $48 million)

### Core Illumina Gross Margin

Core Illumina achieved gross margin of 65.7% in Q1 2024, up 190 basis points from 63.8% in Q1 2023 [Q1 2024 10-Q]. Management attributed this improvement to "a more favorable mix of sequencing consumables and execution of our operational excellence priorities that delivered cost savings, including freight, and improved productivity" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. These offsetting headwinds constrained margin further: "This was partially offset by certain strategic partnership revenue that is lower margin and increased warranty and field service costs" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

Core Illumina gross profit reached $693 million, only marginally higher than the $687 million in the prior year despite lower revenue, demonstrating improved unit economics.

### GRAIL Gross Margin

GRAIL continued to report negative gross margin, with a gross loss of $22 million (improvement from $25 million loss in Q1 2023). This substantial drag on consolidated margin is driven primarily by amortization of intangible assets of $34 million annually. GRAIL's gross loss reflects the high-margin business model challenged by amortization and ongoing operational losses [Q1 2024 10-Q].

## Operating Expenses

### Research and Development

Total consolidated R&D expense was essentially flat at $339 million in Q1 2024 compared to $341 million in Q1 2023, masking offsetting trends between Core Illumina and GRAIL [Q1 2024 10-Q].

**Core Illumina R&D** declined 7% to $241 million from $259 million. Management explained: "Core Illumina R&D expense decreased by $18 million, or 7%, in Q1 2024 primarily due to a decrease in headcount and employee related compensation costs, as well as a decrease in lab supply costs, as we continue to focus on our cost reduction initiatives in 2024" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. This decline reflects the cost reduction program initiated in Q2 2023. However, "the decrease was partially offset by impairment of an IPR&D intangible asset" [Q1 2024 10-Q], indicating some writedown of in-process R&D assets.

**GRAIL R&D** increased 17% to $101 million from $86 million. The increase was "primarily due to an increase in headcount and employee related compensation costs and an increase in lab and consumables spend" [Q1 2024 10-Q], reflecting ongoing investment in GRAIL's cancer detection platform despite the announced divestiture plan.

### Selling, General and Administrative

SG&A expense surged 16% to $439 million from $378 million, a $61 million increase that significantly impacted operating performance. This increase reflected several one-time and ongoing items [Q1 2024 10-Q]:

**Core Illumina SG&A** increased 17% to $336 million from $286 million, a substantial $50 million increase driven by:
- Restructuring charges of $33 million (primarily lease and asset impairments), reflecting continuation of the facility consolidation initiated in Q2 2023
- A $17 million loss recognized on contingent consideration liability related to GRAIL CVRs, representing a mark-to-market adjustment on the deferred acquisition consideration obligation
- $7 million in accrued interest on the European Commission fine, which began accruing at 5.5% per annum in October 2023

Management noted that "the increase was partially offset by a decrease in facility related costs, as we continue to exit certain of our facilities, and a decrease in headcount and employee related compensation costs" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

**GRAIL SG&A** increased 12% to $104 million from $93 million, due to "an increase in headcount and employee related compensation costs and a slight increase in professional services" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

## Operating Loss and Profitability

Consolidated loss from operations widened to $111 million in Q1 2024 from $64 million in Q1 2023, a deterioration of $47 million or 73% [Q1 2024 10-Q]. Operating margin contracted to negative 10.3% from negative 5.7%, reflecting the disproportionate impact of higher operating expenses on flat revenue.

Management's assessment of the quarter: "The increase in loss from operations was due to an increase in operating expense of $59 million, which included restructuring charges of $36 million, partially offset by a $12 million increase in gross profit. We continue to focus on our cost reduction initiatives to accelerate progress toward higher margins and create flexibility for further investment in high-growth areas" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

## Other Income and Expense

Total other income and expense swung to positive $2 million from negative $14 million, a favorable swing of $16 million [Q1 2024 10-Q].

**Interest income** declined to $12 million from $17 million, a $5 million decrease. Management noted: "Interest income consisted primarily of interest on our money market funds, which decreased primarily due to a lower cash balance in Q1 2024 as compared to the prior year" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

**Interest expense** improved to negative $18 million (a $2 million benefit compared to $20 million in Q1 2023), remaining "primarily on our outstanding Term Notes" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

**Other income/expense** improved dramatically to positive $8 million from negative $11 million. Management explained: "The fluctuation in other income (expense), net in Q1 2024 was primarily driven by a favorable impact related to our strategic investments. We recognized a net gain of $5 million in Q1 2024 compared to a net loss of $16 million in Q1 2023" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

## Net Loss and Earnings Per Share

Illumina reported a net loss of $126 million in Q1 2024 compared to net income of $3 million in Q1 2023, a swing of $129 million [Q1 2024 10-Q]. This reflected the combination of operating losses widening by $47 million and other expense changes partially offsetting this to improve profitability outside of operations by $16 million.

Loss per share on a basic and diluted basis was $0.79 in Q1 2024 compared to earnings per share of $0.02 in Q1 2023, calculated on weighted average shares of 159 million shares in both periods [Q1 2024 10-Q]. Antidilutive shares (3 million shares from equity awards and convertible securities) were excluded from the diluted calculation in the loss period.

## Tax Rate and Provision

The company recorded a tax provision of $17 million on a loss before taxes of $109 million, resulting in an effective tax rate of negative 15.3%, compared to a tax benefit of $81 million on a loss before taxes of $78 million, resulting in an effective tax rate of 103.9% in the prior year [Q1 2024 10-Q].

Management explained the variance: "The variance from the U.S. federal statutory tax rate of 21% for Q1 2024 was primarily because of the $21 million income tax expense impact of capitalizing research and development expenses for tax purposes, and the $18 million income tax expense impact of GRAIL pre-acquisition net operating losses on GILTI, the utilization of U.S. foreign tax credits, and the Pillar Two global minimum top-up tax" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. The rate was "favorably impacted by the mix of earnings in jurisdictions with lower statutory tax rates than the U.S. federal statutory tax rate, such as in Singapore" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

Notably, "we concluded for Q1 2024 that it was appropriate to determine the provision for income taxes utilizing the year-to-date effective tax rate method. Since minor changes in the estimated income/(loss) before income taxes would result in significant changes in the estimated annual effective tax rate, we determined the year-to-date effective tax rate method would provide a more reliable estimate of the provision for income taxes for Q1 2024" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

## GRAIL Acquisition, Divestiture, and Related Litigation

### Acquisition Background and Current Status

Illumina acquired GRAIL, a healthcare company focused on early detection of multiple cancers, on August 18, 2021 [Q1 2024 10-Q]. The acquisition has been subject to extensive legal and regulatory proceedings in the United States and European Union, effectively preventing integration of the two companies.

"On December 17, 2023, we announced that we will divest GRAIL" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. Subsequently, "on April 12, 2024, the European Commission formally approved our divestment plan with respect to GRAIL, which had been submitted pursuant to the EC Divestment Decision" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

The company stated: "The divestiture of GRAIL is expected to be executed through a third-party sale or capital markets transaction in accordance with the EC Divestment Decision, with the goal of finalizing the terms of the divestiture by the end of the second quarter of 2024. There can be no assurance regarding the ultimate timing of the divestiture of GRAIL" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

A critical funding requirement for divestiture: "The EC Divestment Decision requires us to ensure that GRAIL has access to sufficient funds to cover at least 2.5 years of operations according to its latest long-range plan. We expect the amount of such funding will be approximately $1 billion, which includes cash from GRAIL's balance sheet" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

### GRAIL Financial Performance

In Q1 2024, GRAIL generated service and other revenue of $27 million, up 35% from $20 million in Q1 2023, "primarily due to sales of Galleri" (its early cancer detection blood test) [Q1 2024 10-Q]. However, GRAIL reported an operating loss of $227 million compared to $204 million in Q1 2023, a deterioration of $23 million [Q1 2024 10-Q].

The gross loss of $22 million (prior year: $25 million loss) was driven by amortization of intangible assets of $34 million annually, which exceeds revenue and prevents the division from achieving profitability on an operating basis [Q1 2024 10-Q].

### European Commission Regulatory Proceedings

#### Initial Interim Measures and Jurisdiction

On October 29, 2021, the European Commission adopted an Initial Interim Measures Order requiring GRAIL to be held and operated separately from Illumina. This was renewed on October 28, 2022 as the New Interim Measures Order, which included "an express prohibition on Illumina selling, transferring, encumbering or otherwise disposing of GRAIL or any of GRAIL's assets" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

On April 19, 2021, the European Commission accepted a referral by France (joined by Belgium, Greece, Iceland, the Netherlands, and Norway) for European Union merger review under Article 22(1) of the EU Merger Regulation—notably unusual because "the acquisition has no revenue in Europe" at the time of referral [Q1 2024 10-Q].

Illumina challenged the EC's jurisdiction, but on July 13, 2022, the EU General Court ruled the EC did have jurisdiction [Q1 2024 10-Q]. Illumina appealed to the Court of Justice of the European Union on September 22, 2022 [Q1 2024 10-Q].

On March 21, 2024, "the Advocate General assigned to this case recommended, in a non-binding Opinion, that the EU Court of Justice annul the General Court's judgment and the European Commission's decisions accepting the referral of the GRAIL acquisition for EU merger review" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. This non-binding opinion supports Illumina's position but the final EU Court of Justice ruling remains pending.

#### Prohibition Decision and Related Litigation

On September 6, 2022, the European Commission announced its Prohibition Decision, finding that "our acquisition of GRAIL was incompatible with the internal market in Europe because it results in a significant impediment to effective competition" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

Illumina filed an action for annulment with the EU General Court on November 17, 2022 [Q1 2024 10-Q]. The proceeding continues with briefs filed: European Commission defense (April 20, 2023, served May 23, 2023), Illumina reply (August 31, 2023), and Commission rejoinder (December 22, 2023, served January 8, 2024) [Q1 2024 10-Q]. GRAIL has been granted leave to intervene [Q1 2024 10-Q].

#### EC Divestment Decision and Transitional Measures

On October 12, 2023, the European Commission adopted the EC Divestment Decision requiring divestiture and replacing interim measures with transitional measures [Q1 2024 10-Q]. These measures require that "Illumina and GRAIL will continue to operate as independent legal entities that transact at arms' length, no integration activity will take place, the day-to-day operation of GRAIL will remain the sole responsibility of GRAIL's management and our management will have no involvement in or influence over GRAIL" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

Additionally, the measures require that Illumina take "certain supportive measures to preserve GRAIL's viability, marketability and competitiveness, including with respect to the provision of resources to GRAIL and the retention and/or replacement of key personnel" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. An independent monitoring trustee has been appointed to oversee compliance [Q1 2024 10-Q].

Management assessment: "Such hold separate arrangement, and our obligations pursuant thereto, have imposed implementation and administrative processes and additional legal, financial advisory, regulatory and other professional services costs, which have been burdensome to implement and administer, and which we expect to continue for the duration of the hold separate arrangement" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

Illumina filed an action seeking annulment of the EC Divestment Decision on December 22, 2023 [Q1 2024 10-Q].

#### Article 14(2)(b) Fine Decision and Appeal

On July 12, 2023, the European Commission adopted a final decision under Article 14(2)(b) of the EU Merger Regulation, finding that Illumina "breached the EU Merger Regulation by, in its view, acquiring the possibility to exert decisive influence over GRAIL and exerting such influence during the pendency of the European Commission's review" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

The Commission imposed a fine of approximately €432 million, "representing the maximum fine of 10% of our consolidated annual revenues for fiscal year 2022" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

Rather than pay cash, "we provided guarantees in October 2023 to satisfy the obligation in lieu of cash payment while we appeal the European Commission's jurisdictional decision and fine decision" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. As of March 31, 2024, the company accrued $478 million in legal contingency liabilities, "including related accrued interest and foreign currency fluctuations" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. The fine accrues interest "at a rate of 5.5% per annum, beginning in October 2023, while it is outstanding" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

Illumina appealed the Article 14(2)(b) Decision on September 26, 2023 [Q1 2024 10-Q]. The European Commission lodged its defense on February 2, 2024 [Q1 2024 10-Q]. On March 7, 2024, the Council of the European Union was granted permission to intervene in the case [Q1 2024 10-Q].

### Federal Trade Commission Proceedings (United States)

#### Initial Legal Actions

On March 30, 2021, the FTC filed an administrative complaint and motion for preliminary injunction in the US District Court for the District of Columbia, alleging that the acquisition would violate Section 7 of the Clayton Act [Q1 2024 10-Q]. Following transfer of venue and administrative proceedings, on September 1, 2022, the administrative law judge (ALJ) ruled in favor of Illumina and "found that the acquisition of GRAIL did not violate Section 7 of the Clayton Act" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. The ALJ determined that "the FTC's complaint counsel had failed to prove its prima facie case that Illumina's acquisition of GRAIL would result in harm to competition in a putative market for multi-cancer early detection (MCED) tests" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

#### FTC Appeal and Reversal

The FTC appealed the ALJ decision and on March 31, 2023, the full FTC issued an order requiring divestiture, "reversing the ALJ's ruling" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. Illumina then filed a petition for review with the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit on April 5, 2023 [Q1 2024 10-Q]. The FTC stayed its order "in its entirety pending resolution of Illumina's Fifth Circuit appeal" on April 24, 2023 [Q1 2024 10-Q].

#### Fifth Circuit Remand Decision

On December 15, 2023, "the Fifth Circuit issued its opinion and order, in which the Court ruled that the Commission applied the incorrect standard in assessing Illumina's open offer contract, and on that basis vacated the FTC Order and remanded the case to the Commission for reconsideration of the effects of the open offer contract under the proper standard as described in the Fifth Circuit's decision, and in all other respects upheld the Commission's decision" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

This decision does not resolve the antitrust issues but requires the FTC to reconsider the effect of Illumina's contractual open offer commitment under the proper legal standard. Further proceedings are anticipated.

### DOJ False Claims Act Investigation

## Contingent Liabilities and GRAIL CVRs

### Contingent Value Rights (CVRs) Valuation

As part of the GRAIL acquisition, Illumina issued contingent value rights ("CVRs") that entitle holders to "Covered Revenue Payments representing a pro rata portion of certain GRAIL-related revenues (Covered Revenues) each year for a 12-year period" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

The payment structure is tiered: "This will reflect a 2.5% payment right to the first $1 billion of revenue each year for 12 years. Revenue above $1 billion each year will be subject to a 9% contingent payment right during this same period" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

The fair value of the contingent consideration liability as of March 31, 2024 was $403 million (up from $387 million at year-end 2023), representing a mark-to-market adjustment of $16 million during the quarter [Q1 2024 10-Q]. Of this amount, $401 million was classified as a long-term liability (in other long-term liabilities), with $2 million in the current portion (in accrued liabilities) [Q1 2024 10-Q].

### Covered Revenue Performance

GRAIL's Covered Revenues for Q4 2023 were $30 million (compared to $23 million in Q4 2022), "driven primarily by sales of GRAIL's Galleri test" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. Related Covered Revenue Payments made in Q1 2024 for Q4 2023 revenues were approximately $284 thousand [Q1 2024 10-Q]. For Q1 2024, "we expect Covered Revenues for Q1 2024 to be approximately $27 million and for related Covered Revenue Payments to total approximately $250,000 in Q2 2024" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

### CVR Fair Value Measurement

The contingent consideration liability is measured using a Monte Carlo simulation incorporating "forecasted revenues for GRAIL, a revenue risk premium, a revenue volatility estimate, an operational leverage ratio and a counterparty credit spread" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. These are Level 3 fair value inputs (unobservable).

## Foreign Exchange Management

### Non-Designated Hedges

The company uses foreign exchange forward contracts with terms of one month or less to hedge foreign currency risks on monetary assets and liabilities denominated in non-U.S. dollars [Q1 2024 10-Q]. These are not designated as hedging instruments, and changes in fair value are recognized in other income (expense), net.

As of March 31, 2024, the company had foreign exchange forward contracts covering exposures in euro, Japanese yen, Australian dollar, Canadian dollar, Singapore dollar, Chinese Yuan Renminbi, and British pound, with total notional amount of $909 million (versus $926 million at year-end) [Q1 2024 10-Q].

In July 2023, the company "entered into forward contracts for a total notional amount of €432 million to hedge the foreign currency exposure for the fine imposed by the European Commission on July 12, 2023" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

### Designated Cash Flow Hedges

The company also uses foreign currency forward contracts to hedge portions of foreign currency exposure associated with forecasted revenue transactions [Q1 2024 10-Q]. These contracts, with terms up to 24 months, are designated as cash flow hedges. Changes in fair value are recorded in accumulated other comprehensive income (loss) and reclassified to revenue in the same period as the underlying hedged transactions.

As of March 31, 2024, the company had foreign currency forward contracts in place to hedge exposures associated with forecasted revenue transactions in euro, Japanese yen, Australian dollar, Canadian dollar, and Chinese Yuan Renminbi, with total notional amount of $633 million (versus $628 million at year-end) [Q1 2024 10-Q].

Fair value of these contracts and expected reclassifications [Q1 2024 10-Q]:
- Fair value recorded in assets: $14 million
- Estimated gains in AOCI to be reclassified into earnings within 12 months: $13 million
- Q1 2024 reclassifications to revenue: $3 million
- Q1 2023 reclassifications to revenue: $2 million
