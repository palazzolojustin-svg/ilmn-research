# Illumina Inc. Form 10-K – Fiscal Year 2023

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-K_FY2023.md` · Date: 2023-12-31 · SEC Filing; Ernst & Young LLP (auditor) · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina reported fiscal 2023 revenue of $4.5 billion, down 2% year-over-year, driven by declines in core sequencing consumables and instruments revenue amid macroeconomic headwinds and the NovaSeq X transition. The company recorded a net loss of $1.1 billion, a substantial improvement from the $4.4 billion loss in 2022, as goodwill impairment charges moderated ($827 million in 2023 vs. $3.9 billion in 2022). Gross margin contracted 390 basis points to 60.9%, reflecting lower manufacturing volumes and new-product launch effects. The GRAIL acquisition remains under regulatory constraint; the European Commission imposed a €432 million fine in July 2023, and Illumina announced in December 2023 its plan to divest GRAIL by mid-2024. Operating cash flow strengthened to $478 million from $392 million.

## Financial Results – Fiscal Year 2023

### Revenue and Product Mix

Consolidated revenue in fiscal 2023 reached $4.504 billion, down $80 million, or 2%, compared to $4.584 billion in 2022 [consolidated statements of operations]. The decline was driven primarily by decreases in sequencing consumables revenue and sequencing instruments revenue, partially offset by growth in service and other revenue.

Product revenue, which includes instruments and consumables, totaled $3.787 billion, or 84.1% of revenue, down from 86.2% in the prior year. Service and other revenue reached $717 million, or 15.9% of revenue, up from 13.8% in 2022. Within product revenue, consumables represented the largest component: sequencing consumables of $2.790 billion and microarray consumables of $293 million, for a combined $3.083 billion, down 4% from $3.225 billion in 2022. Sequencing instruments revenue was $685 million, down 3% from $709 million, and microarray instruments $19 million.

The decrease in Core Illumina consumables revenue of $140 million was driven principally by a $127 million decline in sequencing consumables revenue. Management attributed this to lower pull-through volumes of NovaSeq 6000 consumables as high-throughput customers transitioned to the newly launched NovaSeq X platform, as well as the impact of macroeconomic conditions on customer purchasing power and project planning [MD&A – Results of Operations]. Core Illumina instruments revenue declined $23 million, reflecting fewer shipments of NovaSeq 6000, NextSeq, and MiSeq instruments, partially offset by NovaSeq X shipments that began in early 2023 [MD&A].

GRAIL's service and other revenue increased $38 million, or 69%, driven by sales of Galleri, the multi-cancer early detection blood test [revenue section].

### Geographic Performance

International sales, which represent customer shipments outside the United States, comprised 48% of total revenue in 2023 ($2.145 billion), down from 50% ($2.294 billion) in 2022. The company provided revenue by region (based on destination): Americas $2.521 billion (including United States revenue of $2.359 billion); Europe $1.140 billion; Greater China (China, Taiwan, Hong Kong) $384 million; and Asia-Pacific, Middle East and Africa $459 million [note 2, Revenue by Geographic Area].

### Gross Profit and Margins

Consolidated gross profit declined $228 million to $2.744 billion, representing a gross margin of 60.9%, down 390 basis points from 64.8% in 2022. Core Illumina gross profit declined $251 million to $2.856 billion, with gross margin compressed to 64.4% from 68.2%. Management attributed the margin decline principally to less fixed cost leverage on lower manufacturing volumes, lower instrument margins due to the NovaSeq X launch (which is typical early in a new platform's life until manufacturing scales), and increased field services and installation costs, partially offset by lower freight costs [MD&A – Gross Margin].

GRAIL recorded a gross loss of $96 million, primarily attributable to amortization of intangible assets totaling $134 million [gross margin table note].

### Operating Expenses

Consolidated research and development (R&D) expense increased $33 million, or 2%, to $1.354 billion in 2023 from $1.321 billion in 2022. Core Illumina R&D rose $26 million, or 3%, to $1.030 billion, driven by increases in compensation-related expenses, including performance-based compensation, and by $18 million of restructuring charges primarily for employee separation costs, partially offset by decreases in lab supplies, recruiting, professional services, and travel. GRAIL R&D increased $8 million, or 2%, to $338 million, primarily due to increased headcount and employee-related compensation costs and higher lab and consumables spending, partially offset by decreased clinical trial costs [MD&A – Operating Expense].

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expense surged $315 million, or 24%, to $1.612 billion from $1.297 billion. Core Illumina SG&A increased $245 million, or 24%, to $1.248 billion. The increase was primarily attributable to a lower gain on the contingent consideration liability: the company recognized a net gain of $24 million in 2023 compared to $205 million in 2022 (both related primarily to GRAIL's contingent value rights, or CVRs); $94 million in increased restructuring charges for lease and asset impairments and employee separation costs; and $32 million in costs related to a proxy contest. These increases were partially offset by decreases in professional services, facility-related costs (as the company exited certain facilities), and recruiting costs. GRAIL SG&A increased $70 million, or 24%, to $366 million, driven by increased headcount and employee-related compensation, as well as higher professional services and marketing spending [MD&A].

Goodwill and intangible impairment totaled $827 million in 2023, down substantially from $3.914 billion in 2022. The 2023 impairment consisted of a $712 million goodwill impairment for the GRAIL reporting unit (recorded in Q3 2023 following a sustained decrease in company stock price and market capitalization) and a $109 million impairment of in-process research and development (IPR&D) related to GRAIL, both in the third quarter, and a $6 million IPR&D impairment in Q4 2023. The goodwill impairment was driven primarily by a decrease in consolidated market capitalization and a higher discount rate (24.0%) for the GRAIL fair value calculation. As of December 31, 2023, goodwill allocated to GRAIL was $1.466 billion [note 4 – Goodwill].

Legal contingency and settlement expense declined $599 million to $20 million, down from $619 million in 2022. The 2023 amount consisted of a $5 million adjustment to the accrual for the European Commission fine and accrued interest, plus gains and losses on two separate patent litigation settlements. The 2022 amount comprised primarily the accrual for the European Commission fine and a net loss of $145 million related to settlement of litigation with BGI [MD&A].

### Loss from Operations and Net Loss

Loss from operations improved substantially to $(1.069) billion in 2023 from $(4.179) billion in 2022, primarily due to the $3.087 billion decrease in goodwill and intangible impairment. Core Illumina recorded income from operations of $552 million, up 15% from $481 million in 2022. GRAIL recorded a loss from operations of $(1.621) billion, improved from $(4.657) billion in 2022, principally due to lower impairment charges.

Net loss for 2023 was $(1.161) billion, or $(7.34) per diluted share, compared to $(4.404) billion, or $(28.00) per diluted share, in 2022. The improvement reflects the lower impairment charges and reduced legal contingency accruals, partially offset by a $228 million decline in gross profit.

### GRAIL Contingent Consideration

The CVRs entitle holders to receive quarterly cash payments (Covered Revenue Payments) representing a pro rata portion of certain GRAIL-related revenues (Covered Revenues) for a 12-year period, structured as 2.5% of the first $1 billion of annual revenue and 9% of revenues above $1 billion. Covered Revenue Payments in 2023 for revenues earned in Q4 2022 through Q3 2023 totaled approximately $803,000. Covered Revenues for Q4 2023 were approximately $30 million. The company uses a Monte Carlo simulation to estimate the CVR fair value, incorporating assumptions for forecasted revenues, a revenue risk premium, revenue volatility, an operational leverage ratio, and a counterparty credit spread [note 3].

## The GRAIL Acquisition, Regulatory Status, and Planned Divestiture

### Acquisition Overview

On August 18, 2021, Illumina completed its acquisition of GRAIL, a healthcare company focused on early detection of multiple cancers, for total consideration of $9.745 billion. The purchase consideration comprised $2.862 billion in cash, $4.975 billion in Illumina common stock (9.8 million shares), $757 million in fair value of contingent consideration (CVRs), $1.149 billion for fair value of Illumina's previously held 12% investment in GRAIL, and $2 million for settlement of preexisting relationships. The company recorded a gain of $899 million in 2021 upon remeasurement of its previously held equity investment [note 4].

GRAIL is reported as a separate segment in Illumina's consolidated financial statements. GRAIL's principal product is Galleri, a multi-cancer early detection blood test designed as a screening test for adults at elevated cancer risk, such as those aged 50 or older. Galleri was commercially launched in 2021 as a laboratory-developed test. GRAIL is also developing solutions for blood-based detection of minimal residual disease and other post-diagnostic applications [business overview].

### Regulatory Proceedings and European Commission Decisions

The GRAIL acquisition has been subject to ongoing legal and regulatory proceedings in the United States and European Union.

**European Commission Actions**: On April 19, 2021, the European Commission accepted a request for referral of the acquisition for EU merger review under Article 22 of the EU Merger Regulation, requested by France and subsequently joined by Belgium, Greece, Iceland, the Netherlands, and Norway. This was unprecedented, as the Commission had never before solicited referral of a U.S. acquisition of a company with no European revenue.

On October 29, 2021, the European Commission adopted an Initial Interim Measures Order requiring GRAIL to be held and operated separately and independently from Illumina, with no integration activity, day-to-day operations to remain GRAIL's sole responsibility, and with an independent monitoring trustee appointed. The order was renewed on October 28, 2022, with certain operational modifications and an express prohibition on Illumina selling, transferring, or disposing of GRAIL. The order imposed "burdensome" implementation and administrative requirements and "substantial" additional costs for legal, financial advisory, regulatory, and other professional services [Business & Market Information – Risk Factors, GRAIL acquisition].

On September 6, 2022, the European Commission issued its Prohibition Decision, concluding that the acquisition was incompatible with the internal market because it results in a significant impediment to effective competition. On July 13, 2022, the EU General Court ruled that the Commission has jurisdiction to review the acquisition [note 8]. On October 12, 2023, the European Commission adopted the EC Divestment Decision, requiring Illumina to divest GRAIL and replacing interim measures with substantially equivalent transitional measures. The EC Divestment Decision requires GRAIL to have access to sufficient funds to cover at least 2.5 years of operations according to its latest long-range plan.

**European Commission Fine**: On July 12, 2023, the European Commission imposed a fine of approximately €432 million (representing the maximum fine of 10% of consolidated annual revenues for fiscal year 2022) for breach of the EU Merger Regulation. The Commission found that Illumina acquired the possibility to exert decisive influence over GRAIL and exerted such influence during the pendency of the review. Illumina provided guarantees in October 2023 in lieu of cash payment while appealing the decision. The fine accrues interest at 5.5% per annum beginning in October 2023. As of December 31, 2023, the company accrued $484 million, including related foreign currency losses and accrued interest [MD&A – Liquidity; note 8].

**FTC Proceedings**: In March 2021, the FTC filed an administrative complaint alleging the acquisition would violate Section 7 of the Clayton Act. An administrative law judge ruled in favor of Illumina on September 1, 2022, finding the acquisition did not violate the Clayton Act. The FTC appealed to the full Commission, which on March 31, 2023, issued an opinion and order (the FTC Order) requiring divestiture, reversing the ALJ's ruling. On April 5, 2023, Illumina filed a petition for review with the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit. On April 24, 2023, the FTC granted a motion staying its entire order pending resolution of the appeal. On December 15, 2023, the Fifth Circuit issued its opinion, ruling that the Commission applied an incorrect standard in assessing Illumina's open offer contract. The court vacated the FTC Order and remanded the case to the Commission for reconsideration under the proper standard, while upholding the Commission's decision in all other respects [note 8 – Legal Proceedings].

### December 2023 Divestiture Announcement

On December 17, 2023, Illumina announced that it will divest GRAIL. The divestiture is expected to be executed through a third-party sale or capital markets transaction in accordance with the EC Divestment Decision, with the goal of finalizing the terms of the divestiture by the end of Q2 2024. Management stated there can be no assurance regarding the ultimate timing of the divestiture, which remains subject to satisfaction of certain conditions, including approval by the European Commission and receipt of other regulatory approvals [business overview; note 8].

## Impairment Charges and Fair Value Assessments

### Goodwill Impairment

The company performs goodwill impairment testing annually in Q2 and whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate the fair value of a reporting unit is likely less than carrying value.

**2023 Impairment – Q3 2023 Interim Test**: In Q3 2023, the company concluded that the sustained decrease in its stock price and overall market capitalization triggered an interim goodwill test requirement. Based on a combination of income and market approaches, the company concluded that the GRAIL reporting unit's carrying value exceeded its estimated fair value. The company recorded a $712 million goodwill impairment for GRAIL, primarily due to decreased consolidated market capitalization and a higher discount rate (24.0%) for the GRAIL fair value calculation. No impairment was recorded for Core Illumina; its fair value exceeded carrying value. The discount rate of 24.0% was determined using a weighted average cost of capital for risk factors specific to GRAIL and other market and industry data [note 4 – Goodwill impairment].

In conjunction with this interim test, the company also evaluated the GRAIL IPR&D asset and recorded an impairment of $109 million, using an income approach with a discounted cash flow model and a discount rate of 19.0%, driven by decreased projected cash flows and higher discount rates.

**2023 Impairment – Q4 2023 Interim Test**: The company's December 2023 announcement of the GRAIL divestiture was determined to be a triggering event requiring an additional interim test. The test concluded that fair values for both GRAIL and Core Illumina exceeded carrying values by approximately $950 million and $19 billion, respectively. The selected discount rate for GRAIL in Q4 2023 was 23.0%; an increase of 100 basis points would not result in impairment. No impairment was recorded for either reporting unit.

As of December 31, 2023, total goodwill declined to $2.545 billion from $3.239 billion, with $1.466 billion allocated to GRAIL.

### IPR&D Impairment

As of December 31, 2023, the company's carrying value of indefinite-lived IPR&D intangible assets was $590 million. The company evaluated these assets for impairment using fair value measurements derived from discounted cash flow models (Level 3 inputs) and recorded total impairments of $115 million in 2023 ($109 million for GRAIL in Q3 and $6 million for a legacy asset in Q4).

### Operating Lease Right-of-Use Asset Impairments

During 2023, the company recorded $100 million in impairment charges related to operating lease right-of-use assets as it exited certain facilities. The company fully exited its i3 campus in San Diego, resulting in a $38 million right-of-use asset impairment and $16 million in leasehold improvement impairments. The company also exited a portion of its Foster City, California campus, recording a $21 million right-of-use asset impairment and $22 million in leasehold improvement impairments. Fair values were estimated using discounted future cash flows, incorporating estimates for future sublease rental rates reflecting current market conditions and a discount rate. As of December 31, 2023, remaining Foster City assets totaled approximately $136 million [note 7 – Supplemental Balance Sheet].

## Product Portfolio and Technology

### Sequencing Platforms and Revenue

Sequencing represented 91% of total consolidated revenue in all of 2023, 2022, and 2021 [business overview – Principal Products]. The company offers a family of sequencing systems including the iSeq (entry-level), MiSeq, NextSeq, and NovaSeq platforms, designed to address different throughput, complexity, and cost requirements.

In 2023, the company launched two significant products. The **XLEAP-SBS** chemistry, launched in 2023, represents a faster, higher-quality, and more robust version of the company's proprietary sequencing-by-synthesis (SBS) biochemistry, delivering the highest level of data accuracy and performance. The technology provides the ability to sequence more than 20,000 human genomes per year [business overview – Sequencing].

The **NovaSeq X Plus**, launched in 2023, is the company's new production-scale sequencing system capable of sequencing a human genome for as low as $200 [business overview]. This represents a significant reduction from prior cost points. The sequencing platforms can generate between 500 megabases and 16.0 terabases (equivalent to approximately 128 human genomes) in a single run, depending on the instrument and application, with different price points per gigabase depending on the instrument and application [business overview].

Sequencing revenue in 2023 totaled $4.112 billion, down from $4.171 billion in 2022. Sequencing instruments revenue was $685 million (96.9% of total instruments), and sequencing consumables revenue was $2.790 billion (90.5% of total consumables).

### Array Products and Revenue

Array revenue comprised 9% of total consolidated revenue in 2023, 2022, and 2021. The company's **BeadArray** technology uses microscopic beads and proprietary substrates to perform simultaneous analysis of millions of genetic markers. Arrays enable high-throughput, cost-effective analysis of genetic variation and biological function through various configurations and formats for different applications.

Array revenue in 2023 totaled $392 million, comprising microarray instruments of $19 million (2.7% of total instruments) and microarray consumables of $293 million (9.5% of total consumables).

### Bioinformatics Solutions

Illumina's portfolio includes several bioinformatics platforms designed to support sequencing applications and customer analysis needs. **BaseSpace Informatics Suite** integrates with sequencing instruments, enabling customers to manage biological samples, sequencing runs, process and analyze raw genomic data, and derive meaningful results. It provides data-sharing and storage solutions and streamlines analysis through applications developed by Illumina and the bioinformatics community.

The **DRAGEN Bio-IT Platform** is used for secondary analysis of sequencing data from various experiment types (whole genomes, exomes, germline and somatic datasets, RNA sequencing) with industry-leading accuracy, speed, and efficiency.

**Illumina Connected Analytics** is an integrated bioinformatics cloud-based data platform that enables customers to manage, analyze, and explore large volumes of multi-omic data in a secure, scalable, and flexible environment [business overview – Products].

### Services

The company provides support services, whole-genome sequencing, genotyping, noninvasive prenatal testing (NIPT), and product support. Human whole-genome sequencing and NIPT services are delivered through Illumina's CLIA-certified, CAP-accredited laboratory. Service revenue totaled $637 million from sequencing services and genotyping and $80 million from array services in 2023 [business overview – Services].

### GRAIL's Galleri Test

GRAIL's principal product is **Galleri**, a multi-cancer early detection blood test designed as a screening test for adults with elevated cancer risk, such as those aged 50 or older. Galleri was commercially launched in 2021 as a laboratory-developed test. The test is enabled by Illumina's sequencing technology and analyzes circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) to detect multiple cancer types before symptoms appear. GRAIL is also developing solutions for blood-based detection of minimal residual disease and other post-diagnostic applications [business overview – GRAIL; business overview – Principal Markets].

## Gross Margin and Product Mix Effects

Gross margin contracted significantly in 2023, declining 390 basis points to 60.9% from 64.8% in 2022. Management attributed this decline to several factors:

1. **Fixed Cost Leverage**: Lower manufacturing volumes resulted in less favorable absorption of fixed costs across the manufacturing footprint [MD&A].

2. **New Platform Launch Effects**: The NovaSeq X launch resulted in lower instrument margins early in the product lifecycle, which is typical until manufacturing scales and operating efficiencies are realized [MD&A].

3. **Operating Costs**: Increased field services and installation costs on sequencing platforms and instruments [MD&A].

4. **Offsetting Factors**: Lower freight costs partially offset the above headwinds [MD&A].

The company noted that gross margin depends on multiple factors, including market conditions affecting pricing; sales mix changes among consumables, instruments, and services; product mix changes between established and new products; excess and obsolete inventory; royalties; the cost structure of manufacturing operations relative to volume; freight costs; and product support obligations [MD&A – Financial Overview].

## Backlog and Order Pipeline

As of December 31, 2023, the company's backlog was approximately $653 million, down significantly from $1.030 billion at January 1, 2023. The company expects approximately 82% of the December 31, 2023 backlog to ship in 2024, approximately 13% in 2025, and the remainder thereafter. The backlog represents orders believed to be firm as of the balance sheet date; however, the company may allow customers to make product substitutions as new products are launched. The timing of shipments depends on several factors, including agreed-upon shipping schedules (which may span multiple quarters) and whether the product is catalog or custom [business overview – Backlog].

### Capacity Expansion and Automation

The company continued to increase manufacturing capacity in 2023 and expects to do so again in 2024 to meet customer demand. To address increasing product complexity and volume, the company continues to automate manufacturing processes to accelerate throughput and improve quality and yield. All key manufacturing and distribution facilities operate under a quality management system certified to ISO 13485 [business overview – Manufacturing].

### Supply Chain and Raw Materials

The company's manufacturing operations require a wide variety of raw materials, electronic and mechanical components, chemical and biochemical materials, and other supplies. Multiple commercial sources provide many of these components and supplies; however, some raw materials and components are obtained from single-source suppliers. While the company believes it could redesign products or develop internal supply capability if necessary, single-source dependencies create risk. The company maintains minimal inventory levels while purchasing incremental inventory as needed to protect the supply chain. If supplier capabilities are limited or stopped due to pandemics, disasters, quality issues, regulatory reasons, or other causes, it could negatively impact the company's ability to manufacture products [business overview – Raw Materials].

The company experienced some supply chain pressures in 2023 from global inflation and disruptions; management noted that some customers experienced supply chain pressures that delayed lab expansions and that others are managing inventory and capital more conservatively [MD&A – Financial Overview].

## Competition

The company competes with third parties that design, manufacture, and market sequencing, array, and diagnostic products. Competition is described as intense and ongoing, with competitors offering products and services for sequencing, SNP genotyping, gene expression, and molecular diagnostics markets.

The company noted that it competes in multiple ways: directly with competitors offering similar sequencing or non-sequencing products for the same use case, and indirectly for customers' capital allocation across a broad range of sequencing and non-sequencing products (some complementary or adjacent but not directly competitive). Some competitors have substantially greater financial, technical, research, and other resources than Illumina, along with larger, more established marketing, sales, distribution, and service organizations. In some cases, competitors have greater name recognition and a larger installed base of systems in clinical and reference laboratories, which could deter acceptance of Illumina's products. Some pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies, which are Illumina's potential customers and strategic partners, could also develop competing products. The company expects existing competitors to develop new or improved products, new competitors to enter the market, and competition intensity to increase [business overview – Competition; risk factors].

## Income Tax Matters

### Effective Tax Rate

The company's effective tax rate in 2023 was (3.9)%, or a provision of $44 million on a loss before income taxes of $(1.117) billion. This compared to an effective tax rate of (1.6)%, or a provision of $68 million on a loss before income taxes of $(4.336) billion, in 2022 [note 9 – Income Taxes].

The variance from the U.S. federal statutory tax rate of 21% in 2023 was primarily caused by:

- Income tax expense impact of goodwill impairment (non-deductible for tax purposes): $149 million
- Income tax expense impact of capitalizing R&D expense for tax purposes: $86 million
- Income tax expense impact of GRAIL pre-acquisition net operating losses on GILTI and utilization of U.S. foreign tax credits: $61 million
- Favorable impact of earnings in jurisdictions with lower statutory tax rates (e.g., Singapore at 17%): offset the above

## Regulatory Environment and Diagnostic Products

The company noted that as it expands its product line into diagnostic products intended for disease diagnosis, regulation by governmental authorities becomes increasingly significant. Products intended for the diagnosis of disease may be regulated as medical devices or in vitro diagnostic products (IVDs) by the FDA in the U.S. and comparable agencies internationally.

Some Illumina products may require FDA 510(k) pre-market notification clearance (typically 3–6 months but can take significantly longer) or pre-market approval (PMA) (typically 9–18 months but can take much longer and requires more extensive clinical studies). All products currently regulated by the FDA as medical devices and IVDs are subject to the FDA Quality System Regulation (QSR). For example, Illumina's FDA-regulated MiSeqDx instrument uses the 510(k) process, while its FDA-cleared RAS panel uses the PMA process [business overview – Government Regulation].

In Europe, products sold as medical devices or IVDs are regulated under the In Vitro Diagnostics Regulation (EU 2017/746 – the IVDR), which went into full enforcement in May 2022. The IVDR includes requirements for presentation and review of performance data and quality-system requirements [business overview].

Certain Illumina products are available through CLIA-certified laboratories and are called "laboratory-developed tests" (LDTs). For years, the FDA exercised enforcement discretion, not regulating LDTs as medical devices if created and used within a single laboratory. However, the FDA has indicated an increased intention to regulate LDTs in the future. The company cannot predict the nature or extent of FDA's final guidance or regulation of LDTs. Certification of CLIA laboratories includes standards for personnel qualifications, administration, proficiency testing participation, patient test management, and quality control. Illumina must maintain its CLIA certification and CAP accreditation. Loss of either could adversely affect the business [business overview – Government Regulation].

In 2023, the FDA published a proposed rule that would allow the FDA to regulate LDTs. Under the proposed rule, customers would be required to either submit their current test for FDA approval or find an IVD manufacturer to supply IVDs. This could impact customers' use of Illumina products [business overview – Government Regulation].

### Derivative and Class Action Complaint

On October 17, 2023, a stockholder derivative and class action complaint captioned *Icahn Partners LP, et al. v. deSouza, et al.* was filed in the Delaware Court of Chancery against certain current and former directors (including Illumina's former CEO). ## Cybersecurity and Data Security

The company acknowledges the importance of developing, implementing, and maintaining robust cybersecurity measures to safeguard information systems and protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data. Illumina's cybersecurity risk management strategy is integrated into its established enterprise risk management program, including defined risk, assessment, mitigation, and reporting processes.

The company's information security team has deployed multiple technical and operational processes to continuously identify and respond to cybersecurity threats and incidents, including use of third parties for external threat intelligence and evaluation of incident notifications. Illumina evaluates its cybersecurity program against the National Institute of Standards and Technology's Cybersecurity Framework.

For all suspected cybersecurity incidents, the information security team conducts a preliminary assessment to determine severity and impact extent, and a materiality assessment. Upon a confirmed incident, the information security team initiates a response process with goals to contain, respond, recover, protect, and minimize impacts. The company has established a third-party risk management program to monitor suppliers with access to Illumina's information.

The Audit Committee of Illumina's Board of Directors is responsible for governing management's review and assessment of cybersecurity and IT risks, controls, and procedures. The Chief Information Officer, supported by the Chief Information Security Officer (with over 20 years' experience) and a team of information security professionals, provides regular updates to the Audit Committee and Board, including reviews of security risk events and improvements in security controls.

The company maintains a cybersecurity insurance policy that may mitigate certain financial impacts of a cybersecurity incident. Illumina believes risks from prior cybersecurity threats have not materially affected its business to date but can provide no assurance that future incidents will not materially affect the business, business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition [business overview – Cybersecurity].
