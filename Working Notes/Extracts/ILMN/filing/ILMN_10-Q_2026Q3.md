# Illumina Form 10-Q for the quarter ended September 27, 2026 (Q3 2026)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-Q_2026Q3.md` · Date: 2026-10-30 · Illumina, Inc. SEC filing, signed by CEO Jacob Thaysen and CFO Ankur Dhingra · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina's Q3 2026 quarterly report shows revenue up 4% to $1,129 million and operating margin up to 27.0%, helped by a net $42 million benefit from tariff refunds. Year-to-date revenue rose 6% and net income reached $561 million, and the company still has about $1.77 billion of buyback authorization. The filing also covers the January 2026 SomaLogic acquisition, a new $1 billion revolving credit facility and commercial paper program, the GRAIL contingent payment rights, and ongoing shareholder and securities litigation.

## Overview

Illumina is the largest maker of DNA sequencing machines and the reagent kits used with them. This filing is its official quarterly report to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission for the third quarter of fiscal 2026, which covered the 13 weeks ended September 27, 2026. The company reports all of its results as one business segment.

Three things dominate the quarter. First, revenue and profit grew, but a one-time tariff accounting effect flatters the margin. Second, the company bought SomaLogic, a protein-measurement business, in January 2026, and it paid off most of the earnout obligations tied to that deal during the year. Third, the company refinanced its debt: it repaid a $500 million note, issued a new $300 million note, replaced its bank credit line, and set up a $1 billion commercial paper program.

The filing gives no numerical guidance for future revenue, margins or earnings. It does say the company "continue[s] to use the best information available" and that tariffs, NIH funding cuts, China's unreliable entities list and the Middle East conflict "continue to create additional uncertainty" [Q3 2026 10-Q].

### Our arithmetic (not stated in the filing)

We recalculated a few figures to show the effect of the one-time tariff item. These are our calculations from the filing's numbers, not the company's.

- Excluding the net $42 million tariff benefit, Q3 2026 revenue would have been about $1,166 million (the $37 million refund reduction added back to $1,129 million). That is roughly 7.6% growth over Q3 2025, compared with the reported 4%.
- Excluding the $42 million net benefit, Q3 operating income would have been about $263 million, or a 23.3% operating margin, compared with 21.0% a year earlier.
- Excluding the same benefit, Q3 gross margin would have been about 69.8%. The company's own words are that gross margin "excluding this net benefit, ... was relatively flat" [Q3 2026 10-Q, management]. Our arithmetic shows a gain of about 2 points against Q3 2025's 67.6%. The filing does not reconcile the two views, so the reader should check the figure against the company's own detail.

## Gross margin and the tariff refunds

Tariffs are import taxes. Beginning in April 2025, the U.S. government and several other countries imposed tariffs that raised Illumina's costs. The largest cost effect came from products shipped from its manufacturing plant in Singapore. In February 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA, the law the tariffs relied on) did not authorize them. In March 2026, the Court of International Trade ordered U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP, the federal import agency) to refund them [Q3 2026 10-Q].

The accounting worked like this, as described in the filing:

- **Refunds Illumina expects to receive:** The company recognized a $79 million reduction to cost of revenue in Q3 and YTD 2026. This is a cost recovery.
- **Refunds Illumina expects to pass to customers:** The company recorded a $37 million reduction to revenue, with a matching $37 million liability in accrued liabilities. Customers who bought products at tariff-inflated prices are being credited.
- **Net effect:** $79 million minus $37 million, or $42 million, added to gross profit.
- **Cash position:** As of September 27, 2026, Illumina had actually received $32 million of refunds and held a $47 million receivable for the rest ($32 million plus $47 million equals $79 million).

The $37 million revenue reduction was spread across the business: $30 million reduced consumables, $4 million reduced instruments, and $3 million reduced service revenue. Of the total, $35 million fell in sequencing and $2 million in microarray (see the revenue section).

The filing states that the company "partially mitigated the effects of tariffs through supply-chain optimization, cost measures, and pricing actions, and aim[s] to continue such efforts" [Q3 2026 10-Q, management].

Gross margin factors that the filing says are not one-time:

- **Mix shift:** The company sold a larger share of lower-margin products. The filing names "lower-margin SomaLogic revenue" as part of this shift [Q3 2026 10-Q].
- **Freight costs:** Higher fuel, energy and shipping costs linked to the Middle East conflict, including Iran, hurt gross margin in 2026 [Q3 2026 10-Q, management].
- **Memory chips:** "higher costs associated with memory chips used in our products" also hurt gross margin in 2026 [Q3 2026 10-Q]. The filing does not quantify this cost.

The YTD 2025 gross margin included a $23 million impairment (a write-down of an asset's value) of an intangible asset, recorded in cost of product revenue in Q2 2025. This makes the year-over-year comparison look better in 2026.

Our arithmetic (not stated in the filing): Removing the $42 million net tariff benefit and the 2025 impairment, YTD gross margin would be about 67.4% in 2026 (2,279 divided by 3,380) and about 67.0% in 2025 (2,134 divided by 3,184). That suggests a slight increase, while management says the underlying YTD gross margin "decreased." The filing gives no reconciliation of this difference.

### Explanations given for product trends

- **Consumables:** Management attributes growth "primarily [to] greater demand for high-throughput consumables, supported by continued growth of the NovaSeq X installed base, partially offset by a $30 million reduction for tariff refunds to be credited to customers and a decrease in mid-throughput consumables" [Q3 2026 10-Q, management]. The NovaSeq X is Illumina's high-throughput sequencer, meaning one that processes very large numbers of samples per run.
- **Instruments:** Q3 instrument revenue fell mainly because of the $4 million tariff refund credit. Excluding that, management says instrument revenue "remained relatively flat." For YTD, instrument revenue rose on "higher NovaSeq X and MiSeq i100 shipments," partly offset by fewer mid-throughput instruments. The MiSeq i100 is a smaller, lower-throughput instrument.
- **Services:** Growth is "primarily due to revenue attributable to SomaLogic, which we acquired in January 2026," along with informatics, genotyping services and extended maintenance contracts. These were partly offset by a $3 million tariff refund credit. The filing does not state how much SomaLogic added.

Our arithmetic (not stated in the filing): Pro forma tables in the filing assume SomaLogic was owned for all of 2025. Comparing those figures with Illumina's reported results implies SomaLogic contributed about $24 million of revenue in Q3 2025 ($1,108 million pro forma minus $1,084 million reported). The implied pre-acquisition revenue for 2026 is about $7 million. These are approximations.

Remaining performance obligations, meaning contracted revenue not yet recognized, are covered in the Other items section below.

### Revolving credit facility

On August 13, 2026, Illumina signed a new five-year, $1 billion senior unsecured revolving credit facility, arranged with Bank of America as administrative agent. It replaced the January 2023 credit agreement, which was terminated. The facility matures on August 13, 2031, with three one-year extensions available at Illumina's option. Sub-limits are $50 million for swingline (same-day) borrowings and $75 million for letters of credit.

Key terms:

- **Pricing:** Variable interest tied to SOFR (the Secured Overnight Financing Rate, a benchmark rate) or an alternate base rate, plus a margin that changes with the company's credit rating.
- **Accordion:** Illumina may increase commitments or add term loans of up to $500 million, subject to lenders agreeing.
- **Leverage covenant:** The company must keep its total net leverage (total debt minus unrestricted cash, with cash netting capped at $1 billion, divided by adjusted EBITDA over four quarters) at or below 3.50 to 1.00. After a "Qualified Acquisition," the limit rises to 4.00 to 1.00 for the quarter of the deal and the three quarters after it.
- **Other covenants:** Limits on subsidiary debt, liens on assets, and certain mergers or asset sales.

As of September 27, 2026, there were no borrowings and no letters of credit outstanding, and the company was in compliance with all covenants.

### Commercial paper program

On October 8, 2026, after quarter-end, Illumina set up a commercial paper program (short-term unsecured notes sold to investors) allowing up to $1 billion of notes with maturities of up to 397 days. Proceeds may fund general corporate needs. Management says it "plan[s] to use the Revolving Credit Facility as a liquidity backstop for borrowings under the Commercial Paper Program" [Q3 2026 10-Q, management]. In plain terms, the bank facility guarantees the company can repay commercial paper even if the market will not refinance it.

Management says that cash, short-term investments, operating cash flow and available borrowing capacity are "sufficient to fund our near-term capital and operating needs for at least the next 12 months" [Q3 2026 10-Q, management].

## GRAIL and the contingent value rights

Illumina acquired GRAIL in 2021 and divested it in 2024. As part of the GRAIL acquisition, Illumina issued contingent value rights (CVRs, payment rights tied to a future outcome). The CVR holders receive quarterly payments tied to GRAIL revenue through August 2033:

- 2.5% of the first $1 billion of GRAIL revenue each year, and
- 9% of revenue above $1 billion each year.

Covered revenue for Q4 2025 through Q2 2026 was $129 million, mainly from GRAIL's Galleri blood test. Covered revenue for the prior comparable period was $106 million. Payments were $1.2 million YTD 2026 (versus $1.0 million YTD 2025). These payments are small relative to Illumina's total revenue.

The filing says the CVRs and the commercial arrangements with GRAIL could "adversely affect us and our business and/or the market value of the CVRs" [Q3 2026 10-Q].

Illumina also remains on the hook for a performance-based award that GRAIL assumed at the divestiture, worth up to $78 million. Illumina must indemnify GRAIL for cash payments under that award through December 2026. The company has recorded a $1 million liability for this.

### Legal proceedings

The filing groups legal matters into three types. The company says it "deny[s] the allegations in the complaints and intend[s] to vigorously defend against the litigation matters." **Shareholder derivative suits (lawsuits filed on behalf of the company against its directors and officers, alleging harm to the company over the GRAIL deal):**

- *Icahn Partners v. deSouza:* Filed in October 2023 in Delaware Chancery Court. The parties agreed in August 2025 to dismiss with a full release of claims and no payment. The court set a settlement hearing for November 2, 2026, after quarter-end.
- *City of Omaha, City of Roseville, and Pavers and Road Builders Benefit Funds:* These suits were consolidated. Defendants filed motions to dismiss, and a hearing was held on February 13, 2026. The filing does not give a ruling.
- *Demands by shareholders Michael Warner and Jane Davidson:* Both asked the board to pursue claims against officers and directors involved in the GRAIL deal. The board deferred decisions (most recently for Warner on February 4, 2026). It said it "will monitor the stockholder lawsuits and revisit the demand as warranted."

**Federal securities class action (*In re Illumina Securities Litigation*, Southern District of California):** Investors allege that Illumina and some executives made false or misleading statements about the GRAIL acquisition, violating Sections 10(b) and 20(a) of the Securities Exchange Act. The court dismissed the Third Amended Complaint on September 30, 2026, with leave to amend. Plaintiffs filed a Fourth Amended Complaint on October 21, 2026, and the defendants' response is due December 4, 2026.

**State securities class action (*Mar* and *Zerzanek* v. Illumina, San Mateo County Superior Court):** Alleges misstatements in the 2020 and 2021 registration statements related to GRAIL. Key steps:

- The court overruled Illumina's demurrer (a request to dismiss on legal grounds) on September 3, 2025.
- The court certified the class on May 15, 2026.
- Illumina's request for appellate review of class certification was denied on September 3, 2026.
- The court denied Illumina's motion for judgment on the pleadings on June 26, 2026.
- Trial is set for March 20, 2028.

## Changes to risk factors and new disclosures

The filing does not restate its risk factors. It refers readers to the risk factors in the 2025 Form 10-K and the earlier 2026 10-Qs. But the forward-looking statements section and MD&A add or stress several points that appear new or more prominent in this filing:

- **China's unreliable entities list:** The filing lists "our inclusion on the 'unreliable entities list' by regulatory authorities in China" as a source of uncertainty. It appears in several places, including the critical accounting estimates section and the note on basis of preparation. It is cited as a reason for lower Greater China revenue. The filing does not describe the list's consequences beyond the revenue effect.
- **Middle East and Iran conflict:** The filing now cites "recent conflict in the Middle East and Iran" as a source of uncertainty and as a cause of higher freight, fuel and energy costs in 2026.
- **Memory chip costs:** Higher costs of memory chips used in the company's products are cited as a 2026 gross margin headwind.
- **Tariff refund programs:** The filing describes possible "additional refund or relief programs" and the risk that tariff refunds may be partly passed to customers.
- **NIH funding:** The filing repeats the risk of "reductions or potential reductions in funding for the National Institutes of Health (NIH), or targeted cancellations by the U.S. federal government of certain grants or contracts."
- **SomaLogic integration:** Named as a forward-looking risk, along with the "impact of recently launched or pre-announced products."
- **Legal and regulatory:** Risks around pending proceedings and liabilities.

The filing does not state any new numerical risk or quantify the effects of these items beyond the amounts already described.
