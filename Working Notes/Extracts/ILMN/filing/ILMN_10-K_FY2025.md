# Illumina, Inc. Form 10-K for Fiscal Year Ended December 28, 2025

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-K_FY2025.md` · Date: 2026-02-11 · SEC filing; auditor Ernst & Young LLP · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina's FY2025 10-K reveals a return to profitability with $850 million net income and core business revenue of $4.34 billion (essentially flat year-over-year). The company faced significant headwinds including tariffs beginning April 2025, its designation on China's List of Unreliable Entities (affecting $65 million in revenue), and reductions in US government NIH funding. Gross margins compressed 100 bps to 66.1% due to tariff impacts and intangible asset impairments, partially offset by favorable consumables mix. The company completed the GRAIL spin-off in June 2024 and acquired SomaLogic in January 2026 for $350 million to expand proteomics capabilities. Operating leverage improved significantly from FY2024's $833 million loss, driven by the absence of $1.9 billion in goodwill impairments and a $481 million gain from reversal of the European Commission fine.

## Financial Performance

### Revenue and Growth

**Total Consolidated Revenue:** $4,343 million in FY2025 vs. $4,372 million in FY2024, a decrease of 1% or $29 million. This decline was entirely attributable to the June 24, 2024 spin-off of GRAIL, which contributed $55 million in H1 2024 service revenue before separation.

**Core Illumina Revenue** (the ongoing business): $4,343 million, essentially flat compared to $4,332 million in FY2024, up slightly from $4,438 million in FY2023 when GRAIL was still consolidated. This represents stabilization after years of decline, though below peak revenue levels.

**Revenue by Product Category (FY2025):**
- Consumables: $3,227 million (74% of revenue), up $58 million or 2% from $3,155 million in FY2024 and $3,083 million in FY2023
- Instruments: $482 million (11% of revenue), down $19 million or 4% from $501 million in FY2024, significantly down from $704 million in FY2023 (prior to GRAIL separation)
- Services and other: $634 million (15% of revenue), down $28 million or 4% from $716 million in FY2024 (decrease driven by lower strategic partnership revenue)

**Sequencing vs. Arrays Split (FY2025):**
- Sequencing: $3,985 million (92% of revenue) up from $3,993 million (91%) in FY2024
- Arrays: $358 million (8% of revenue) down from $379 million (9%) in FY2024

The company states: "Core Illumina consumables revenue increased in 2025 primarily due to demand for high-throughput consumables as customers continue to transition to NovaSeq X."

**Geographic Revenue Distribution (FY2025):**
- Americas (55%): $2,406 million
  - US specifically: $2,243 million (52% of total revenue)
  - Down from $2,441 million (56%) in FY2024
- Europe (29%): $1,264 million, up from $1,185 million (27%) in FY2024
- Greater China (6%): $243 million, down from $308 million (7%) in FY2024
  - Decline of $65 million attributed to "our inclusion on the List of Unreliable Entities in the beginning of the year"
- Asia-Pacific, Middle East, and Africa (10%): $430 million, down from $438 million (10%) in FY2024

International sales accounted for 48% of total revenue in each of FY2025, FY2024, and FY2023, demonstrating consistent geographic exposure despite China setbacks.

### Backlog

Backlog grew to $738 million (December 28, 2025) from $657 million (December 29, 2024), representing a $81 million or 12% increase. The company expects 77% of backlog to ship in 2026, 13% in 2027, and the remainder thereafter, suggesting strong forward visibility.

### Gross Profit and Margins

**FY2025 Consolidated Gross Profit:** $2,870 million with gross margin of 66.1%, compared to $2,861 million (65.4%) in FY2024, representing a 70 bps improvement. However, this comparison is distorted by GRAIL's spin-off; Core Illumina gross margin actually declined.

**Core Illumina Gross Margin Analysis:**
- FY2025: 66.1% (down 100 bps from 67.1% in FY2024)
- FY2024: 67.1%
- FY2023 and prior: core segment margin data not separately reported

Management attributed the margin decline to:
1. Higher costs related to tariffs (quantified as beginning April 2025)
2. $23 million intangible asset impairment (charged to cost of product revenue)
3. Partially offset by lower strategic partnership revenue (which is lower-margin) and a more favorable product mix toward consumables (which carry higher margins)

The company's commentary on margin trends indicates significant pressure from tariffs: "We have and will continue to take several actions to fully mitigate the impact of these tariffs, and we partially mitigated the impact in 2025, through supply chain optimization, cost measures, and pricing actions. Based on the current tariff environment, our aim is to more fully mitigate the impact in 2026."

Cost of revenue declined slightly:
- FY2025: $1,473 million (33.9% of revenue)
- FY2024: $1,511 million (34.6% of revenue, including GRAIL)
- Decline of $38 million despite slightly higher revenue, demonstrating operational leverage

## Product Portfolio and Segments

### Sequencing Business (92% of Revenue)

**Technology Platform:**
- Proprietary reversible terminator-based sequencing by synthesis (SBS) chemistry
- Latest iteration: XLEAP-SBS - "our fastest, highest quality, and most robust version"
- Capability: Can sequence >20,000 human genomes per year per instrument

**Product Lineup:**
- **NovaSeq X Plus:** Production-scale system, launched 2023
  - Flagship product driving margin improvement and customer transition
  - Can produce as little as $200 per human genome, addressing cost pressures
  - Can generate 16.0 terabases (equivalent to 128 human genomes) in single run
  - Management noted: "Core Illumina consumables revenue increased in 2025 primarily due to demand for high-throughput consumables as customers continue to transition to NovaSeq X"

- **MiSeq i100 Series:** Benchtop system, launched 2024
  - Room temperature reagents, simplifying workflow
  - Target: "empowering every lab, everywhere"
  - Shipping volume increasing, partially offsetting decline in other instruments

- **NextSeq Series:** Production sequencing system for various throughput levels
- **HiSeq Systems:** Legacy but still revenue-generating

- **Consumables:** TruSeq library prep kits and reagents
  - Consumables revenue: $2,939 million in FY2025 (81% of sequencing revenue)
  - Key driver of margin improvement as installed base generates recurring revenue

### Consumables as Strategic Advantage

Consumables represent the most profitable and recurring revenue element:
- FY2025: $3,227 million (74% of product revenue)
- Growth of 2% YoY despite challenging macroeconomic environment
- Management focus: "a more favorable product mix towards consumables" is cited as margin offset

- Growing installed base of sequencing instruments
- Pricing pressure on instruments (competition) vs. pricing power on consumables (less commoditized, proprietary chemistry)
- Customer migration to high-throughput systems (NovaSeq X) driving incremental consumable purchases

## Regulatory Environment and Compliance

### FDA Regulation of Diagnostics

**Current Regulatory Status of Products:**
- Most Illumina products are "For Research Use Only" (RUO) and not subject to FDA clearance/approval
- Two specific products are FDA-regulated:
  - **MiSeqDx:** Uses 510(k) pre-market notification pathway (FDA-cleared assays run on this platform)
  - **NextSeq550Dx:** Uses PMA (pre-market approval) pathway (FDA-approved for TruSight Comprehensive Oncology panel)

**510(k) Pathway Characteristics:**
- "Usually shorter" regulatory process
- Typical timeline: 3-6 months after submission (but can take longer)
- Demonstrates substantial equivalence to predicate device
- Lower cost and uncertainty vs. PMA

**PMA Pathway Characteristics:**
- "Usually much more costly and uncertain"
- Typical timeline: 9-18 months after complete filing (can take longer)
- Requires "conducting clinical studies that are generally more extensive than those required for 510(k) clearance"
- Demonstrates safety and effectiveness

**Products in Development:**
- Oncology diagnostic products: planned for PMA pathway (higher bar)
- Non-invasive prenatal testing products: planned for PMA pathway
- Company states: "We cannot be certain which of our other planned molecular diagnostic products will be subject to the shorter 510(k) clearance process, or which of these will need to go through the PMA process"

### FDA Quality System Regulation (QSR)

**Applicability:**
- All FDA-regulated medical devices and IVDs subject to QSR
- Requires quality management system compliance

**Implementation Challenge:**
"Obtaining the requisite regulatory approvals, including the FDA quality system inspections that are required for PMA approval, can be expensive and may involve considerable delay"

## Risk Factor Summary

### Market and Demand Risks

1. **Genomic Market Emergence:** Dependent on continued emergence and growth of markets for genetic analysis; markets may not develop as expected
2. **Government Funding:** Reduction or delay in NIH and other government research funding could materially impact demand
3. **Cost Reduction Saturation:** Even if sequencing costs continue to decline, demand may not increase proportionately
4. **Ethical/Legal Concerns:** Ethical concerns about genetic testing could limit market acceptance or result in regulatory restrictions

### Competition Risks

1. **Intense Competition:** Expect continued intense competition from both existing competitors and new entrants
2. **Competitor Capabilities:** Some competitors have greater financial, technical, and AI capabilities
3. **Complementary Technologies:** Competitors may offer complementary technologies that attract customers
4. **Market Transitions:** If unable to be first to develop/supply new products, competitive position may suffer

### China-Specific Risks

1. **UEL Listing:** Company cannot predict duration of China's unreliable entities list inclusion or future penalties
2. **Export Restrictions:** Current case-by-case export approval process may result in lost business
3. **Customer Loss:** Potential material impairment if forced to curtail or exit China operations entirely

### Tariff and Trade Risks

1. **Ongoing Tariff Volatility:** Expect continued volatility in tariff rates and trade requirements
2. **Mitigation Limits:** Ability to pass cost increases to customers is limited
3. **Tariff Expansion:** Potential for additional sector-specific duties and licensing requirements

### Product Development Risks

1. **New Product Management:** Risks associated with launching new products and managing product transitions
2. **Market Acceptance:** New products may not deliver expected performance or market acceptance
3. **Transition Challenges:** Managing excess inventory, higher cost structures, different sales/support requirements

### Regulatory and Healthcare Risks

1. **FDA Approval Uncertainty:** Products in development may face delays or denials in FDA approval process
2. **RUO Labeling Risk:** Regulatory authorities may challenge interpretation of RUO product use
3. **Reimbursement Uncertainty:** Diagnostic product reimbursement not assured and may be withdrawn or reduced

### Financial and Operational Risks

1. **Tariff Impact:** Significant gross margin pressure from tariffs beginning April 2025
2. **China Revenue Loss:** $65 million decline in FY2025, potential for further deterioration
3. **Customer Capex Cycles:** Instrument sales subject to customer capital availability cycles
4. **Working Capital:** Changes in customer payment cycles and inventory requirements

### Strategic and Acquisition Risks

1. **GRAIL Litigation:** Ongoing shareholder lawsuits from GRAIL acquisition with potential adverse outcomes
2. **CVR Obligations:** Contingent value rights obligations create ongoing financial exposure
3. **SomaLogic Integration:** Recent acquisition requires successful integration and realization of synergies
4. **Tax Treatment:** GRAIL Spin-Off tax treatment could be challenged by IRS
