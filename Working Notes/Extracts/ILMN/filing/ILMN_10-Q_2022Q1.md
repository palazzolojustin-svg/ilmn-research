# Illumina Q1 2022 10-Q: Revenue Growth Offset by GRAIL Acquisition Drag and Tax Headwinds

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-Q_2022Q1.md` · Date: 2022-04-03 · SEC filing, Form 10-Q for quarter ended April 3, 2022, filed May 5, 2022 · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina's Q1 2022 results show 12% revenue growth to $1.2 billion, driven by strength in sequencing consumables and instruments, but net income fell 41% to $86 million due to GRAIL acquisition losses, higher amortization, and a sharp rise in the effective tax rate to 38.3% (from 13.1% a year earlier). GRAIL, acquired in August 2021, contributed $10 million in revenue but incurred a $172 million operating loss, primarily from $34 million in intangible asset amortization. The GRAIL acquisition remains subject to FTC administrative trial (with decision expected by September 2022) and European Commission merger review. The company ended the quarter with $1.4 billion in cash and no draw on its $750 million credit facility.

## Financial Results—Q1 2022

### Revenue: 12% Growth Driven by Sequencing

Total revenue grew 12% to $1,223 million in Q1 2022 from $1,093 million in Q1 2021. Illumina's core business (excludes GRAIL) generated $1,221 million; GRAIL contributed $10 million, less $8 million in inter-company eliminations.

**Product revenue** increased 12% to $1,070 million. Sequencing (which accounted for ~91% of product revenue) drove growth:
- Sequencing consumables: $778 million (up $89 million, or 13%, from $695 million), primarily from installed-base growth.
- Sequencing instruments: $211 million (up $36 million, or 20%, from $176 million), mainly from increased NovaSeq shipments.
- Microarray consumables: $75 million (down $4 million from $79 million).
- Microarray instruments: $6 million (up $3 million from $3 million).

**Service and other revenue** grew 3% to $153 million from $140 million, including genotyping, sequencing services, instrument maintenance contracts, and development licensing. GRAIL added $10 million from Galleri cancer detection test sales.

### Geographic Mix

| Region | Q1 2022 | Q1 2021 | Change |
|--------|---------|---------|--------|
| Americas | $648M (53.0%) | $562M | +15% |
| EMEA | $316M (25.8%) | $305M | +4% |
| Greater China | $127M (10.4%) | $127M | flat |
| Asia-Pacific | $132M (10.8%) | $99M | +33% |

All regions except Greater China showed growth. Asia-Pacific was the strongest performer.

### Gross Margin: Compression from Freight Costs and GRAIL Losses

Gross profit increased 7% to $815 million but gross margin compressed 330 basis points to 66.6% from 69.9%.

**Core Illumina gross margin** held flat at 69.7% (vs. 69.9%), with gains in consumables and instruments offset by **higher freight costs**—the primary margin headwind. Other margin factors cited: product mix between consumables/instruments/services, new vs. established products, excess/obsolete inventory, royalties, manufacturing cost structure relative to volume, and warranty obligations.

**GRAIL gross loss** of $29 million was driven primarily by $34 million in **amortization of acquired intangible assets**, with only $10 million in quarterly revenue and early-stage commercialization costs. GRAIL's Galleri test is being positioned as a multi-cancer early-detection blood assay.

### Operating Expenses: R&D Surge, SG&A Sharp Decline

**Research and development** surged 64% to $323 million (26.4% of revenue) from $197 million (18.0%). Core Illumina R&D increased 21% to $238 million, driven by headcount expansion, professional services, and performance-based compensation tied to development of new products and enhancements. GRAIL R&D of $85 million (new) includes headcount, clinical trials, and performance awards.

**Selling, general and administrative** fell 18% to $308 million (25.2% of revenue) from $374 million (34.2%), primarily because Q1 2021 included $105 million in GRAIL Continuation Payments (cash advances made post-deal signing while regulatory review proceeded). Core Illumina SG&A declined 33% to $251 million, despite adding headcount, professional services, and travel—offset by a $49 million **gain on fair value of contingent consideration liability** (the GRAIL earnout was remeasured lower). GRAIL SG&A of $58 million (new) covers headcount, services, and performance awards.

**Consolidated operating income** fell 5% to $184 million (15.0% of revenue) from $193 million (17.7%), reflecting gross margin compression and GRAIL's $172 million operating loss.

### Other Income and Expense

**Total other expense** widened to $44 million from $24 million. Interest expense on debt (primarily the $500 million 2031 Term Notes issued March 2021) was $6 million in Q1 2022, down from $19 million in Q1 2021 because Q1 2021 included amortization of debt issuance costs on the Bridge Facility terminated in Q1 2021 and amortization of the original discount on convertible senior notes (prior to ASU 2020-06 adoption on January 3, 2022). **Other expense, net** was $38 million, primarily from $42 million in unrealized losses on marketable equity securities, partially offset by $5 million in unrealized gains on the Helix contingent value right. Q1 2021 other expense was only $6 million, as it included $26 million in gains on derivative assets from the terminated PacBio acquisition.

### Income Taxes: Sharp Rate Increase to 38.3%

Net income fell 41% to $86 million from $147 million, despite a small 17% decline in pre-tax income to $140 million from $169 million. The effective tax rate jumped to **38.3% from 13.1%**, significantly above the 21% U.S. federal statutory rate.

The Q1 2022 variance from 21% was driven by:
- **$24 million unfavorable** impact from GRAIL's pre-acquisition net operating losses on global intangible low-taxed income (GILTI) and foreign tax credit utilization.
- **$4 million unfavorable** impact from the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act's R&D capitalization requirement (new in 2022), which requires multi-year capitalization of research expenses instead of immediate deduction. Management warns: if this requirement is not repealed, modified, or deferred, it will add approximately **$103 million to tax payments in 2022** and permanently reduce cash tax deductions.
- **Favorable** mix of earnings in lower-tax jurisdictions (Singapore, United Kingdom).

In Q1 2021, the lower 13.1% rate reflected discrete benefits from GRAIL Continuation Payment deductions and favorable geographic mix, partially offset by tax on foreign subsidiary earnings no longer treated as indefinitely reinvested.

## GRAIL Acquisition: Separate Segment Under Regulatory Scrutiny

### Deal Structure and Contingent Consideration

Illumina acquired **GRAIL, Inc.** on **August 18, 2021** for $4,986 million, comprising $4,749 million in equity issuance and $237 million in treasury stock, plus contingent earn-out rights. GRAIL is the developer of Galleri, a blood-based test designed to detect multiple cancer types before symptoms appear.

The contingent value rights issued in the acquisition entitle holders to **quarterly cash payments (Covered Revenue Payments)** equal to a percentage of GRAIL's annual "Covered Revenues":
- **2.5% of the first $1 billion** of annual GRAIL revenue for a **12-year period**.
- **9% of revenue above $1 billion** per year during the same period.

As of April 3, 2022, the fair value of contingent consideration was **$566 million** (down $49 million from $615 million on January 2, 2022, reflecting lower expected revenues). The change in fair value is recognized in selling, general and administrative expense. Covered Revenues in Q4 2021 (the first quarter of GRAIL as a consolidated subsidiary) were $10 million, driven by Galleri test sales. In Q1 2022, Covered Revenue Payments amounted to approximately $97,000, which were applied to reimburse Illumina for certain expenses under the agreement.

### Financial Performance: Early-Stage Losses

GRAIL contributed $10 million in revenue (from Galleri and related services) but incurred $172 million in **operating losses**. The loss is almost entirely attributable to $34 million in amortization of acquired intangible assets and approximately $143 million in operating costs (R&D $85 million, SG&A $58 million). The acquired intangible assets reflect Illumina's accounting for GRAIL's technology, brand, and contractual relationships at purchase. GRAIL is operated as a separate reportable segment.

### Regulatory and Legal Status

The GRAIL acquisition is subject to **ongoing merger review by the European Commission** and an **FTC administrative trial**:

**FTC Action (U.S.)**
- The FTC filed an administrative complaint and motion for preliminary injunction on **March 30, 2021**, alleging the acquisition violates Section 7 of the Clayton Act (15 U.S.C. § 18).
- The U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia **dismissed the FTC's complaint without prejudice on May 28, 2021**.
- **Administrative trial commenced August 24, 2021**; live testimony concluded September 24, 2021.
- As of April 15, 2022, parties filed opening post-trial briefs and proposed findings of fact.
- Reply briefs are due **May 25, 2022**; closing arguments scheduled for **June 8, 2022**.
- An administrative law judge decision is currently expected **by September 2, 2022**.

**European Commission Proceedings**
- The European Commission accepted a referral for review on **April 19, 2021**, following a request from France (joined by Belgium, Greece, Iceland, the Netherlands, and Norway) under Article 22(1) of Council Regulation (EC) No. 139/2004.
- Illumina filed an action in the **EU General Court on April 29, 2021**, challenging the Commission's jurisdiction, arguing the acquisition does not meet the jurisdictional criteria of the EU Merger Regulation or any Member State's laws.
- The EU General Court held a hearing on **December 16, 2021**; the court's judgment is pending.
- **Risk of Fines**: Because Illumina decided to close the acquisition while the European Commission's review was pending, the Commission may seek to impose fines of up to **10% of Illumina's consolidated annual revenues** under Article 14(2)(b) of the EU Merger Regulation. (For context, Illumina's trailing twelve-month revenue as of Q1 2022 was approximately $4.6 billion, making a 10% fine hypothetically worth ~$460 million.)

**Operational Structure During Review**
- GRAIL is being held and operated as a separate, independent company.
- An independent, court-appointed **monitoring trustee** oversees GRAIL's compliance with interim measures imposed by the European Commission (described in the forward-looking statements section as "burdensome to implement and administer").
- Illumina warns that the interim measures may result in "material and adverse effects on synergies and other benefits" expected from the acquisition, additional costs/liabilities, and loss of revenue.

### Cash-Based Equity Awards to GRAIL Employees

Illumina has issued cash-based equity incentive awards to GRAIL employees under a Cash-Based Equity Appreciation Award Plan. These awards increase or decrease in direct correlation with changes in GRAIL's enterprise fair value. As of April 3, 2022:
- Aggregate cash value outstanding and unvested: **$241 million**.
- Estimated current liability (fair value adjustment): **$24 million** (included in accrued liabilities).
- Q1 2022 share-based compensation expense related to these awards: $13 million.

In addition, Illumina assumed a **performance-based award from GRAIL's prior equity plans** with vesting tied to GRAIL's future revenues. The award has an aggregate potential value of **up to $78 million** and expires (if unvested) in August 2030. As of April 3, 2022, management deemed it **not probable** that the performance conditions will be achieved, so no compensation expense or liability has been recognized.

## Intellectual Property and Legal Disputes

### BGI Genomics Patent Infringement Cases

Illumina and **BGI Genomics Co. Ltd.** (and its affiliate, **Complete Genomics, Inc.**, or "CGI") are engaged in multiple patent disputes in the United States District Court for the Northern District of California (two suits by Illumina) and the District of Delaware (one suit by CGI):

**Northern District of California—First Suit (Filed June 27, 2019)**
- Illumina claims infringement of U.S. Patent No. 7,566,537 ('537 patent) and U.S. Patent No. 9,410,200 ('200 patent).
- BGI counterclaimed alleging Illumina infringes U.S. Patent No. 9,944,984 ('984 patent). Illumina denies.

**Northern District of California—Second Suit (Filed February 27, 2020)**
- Illumina alleges infringement of U.S. Patent Nos. 7,771,973 ('973 patent), 7,541,444 ('444 patent), and 10,480,025 ('025 patent).
- On **June 15, 2020**, the Court granted Illumina's motions for preliminary injunctions, finding Illumina's patents were likely valid and infringed by BGI's chemistries. The injunction **prohibits the sale of infringing BGI sequencers and sequencing reagents in the U.S.**
- On **December 9, 2020**, BGI moved to amend its answer to assert an inequitable conduct defense (patent unenforceability). Illumina denies.
- As of April 12, 2021, BGI claimed **approximately $54 million in past damages and an ongoing royalty of 3.6%** on sales of the accused products in the U.S. until the '984 patent expires June 13, 2026.

**Summary Judgment (August 27 and September 9, 2021)**
The Court issued a series of summary judgment decisions:
- **Granted Illumina SJ**: Illumina does not infringe BGI's '984 patent.
- **Granted Illumina SJ**: Illumina's '444 and '973 patents are not unenforceable (rejected BGI's inequitable conduct defense).
- **Granted Illumina SJ**: BGI's standard MPS (massively parallel sequencing) products infringe **all** of Illumina's patents-in-suit ('537, '200, '973, '444, '025).
- **Granted Illumina SJ** (partial): BGI's "Cool MPS" sequencing products infringe the '973 and '444 patents; **Granted BGI SJ** (partial): Cool MPS does not infringe the '025 patent.
- **Denied BGI SJ**: The '973 patent is not invalid for lack of written description and enablement.

**Jury Trial (November 12–30, 2021)**
A jury found:
- The '537, '200, '973 patents and claims 9, 27, 31, 33, 34, 42, 47 of the '025 patent are valid and were **willfully infringed** by BGI.
- Claim 4 of the '444 patent and claim 1 of the '025 patent are invalid as obvious.
- **Jury awarded Illumina $8 million in damages.**

**Post-Trial (March 27, 2022)**
The Court:
- **Upheld the jury's $8 million award** and granted pre-judgment interest.
- **Upheld the finding of willfulness.**
- **Granted a permanent injunction** against BGI's infringing products, lasting until the relevant patents expire.
- **Upheld non-validity of claim 4 of the '444 patent** but reversed the invalidity of claim 1 of the '025 patent (now deemed valid).
- **Denied Illumina's request for enhanced damages** (treble damages for willful infringement).

**Appeal (April 27, 2022)**
BGI appealed the judgment to the **U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit**. Illumina cross-appealed, including on the denial of enhanced damages.

**Antitrust Countersuit (Filed January 11, 2021)**
CGI, BGI Americas Corp., and MGI Americas, Inc. filed an **antitrust and state unfair competition suit** in the Northern District of California, alleging:
- Illumina fraudulently withheld a prior art reference material to patentability of the '444 and '973 patents.
- Illumina's '025 infringement claims against Cool MPS were objectively baseless.

On **March 30, 2021**, the Court **stayed the antitrust action pending resolution of the underlying patent infringement case.**

**District of Delaware—CGI Suit (Filed May 28, 2019)**
CGI alleges that Illumina's two-channel sequencing systems (NovaSeq, NextSeq, MiniSeq) infringe **U.S. Patent No. 9,222,132**. Illumina counterclaimed for infringement of U.S. Patents 9,303,290, 9,217,178, and 9,970,055.

- **July 1, 2020**: CGI amended complaint to add claims of infringement of **U.S. Patent No. 10,662,473**.
- As of April 8, 2022, CGI is seeking **$334 million in past damages** and **an average ongoing royalty of at least 5.5%** on sales of the accused instruments and chemistry in the U.S. through January 28, 2029.
- **January 14, 2022**: Court denied both parties' motions for summary judgment.
- **April 25, 2022**: Trial began.

### Legal Positions and Expectations

Illumina states it "will continue to pursue claims against BGI and CGI, and vigorously defend against BGI's and CGI's claims." The company notes it "currently cannot estimate any possible loss or range of loss" from BGI's and CGI's claims against Illumina, indicating contingent liability disclosures do not yet assume an adverse outcome.

## Risk Factors and Operational Headwinds

### COVID-19 and Geopolitical Uncertainty

Illumina warns that the **COVID-19 pandemic** continues to curtail movement of people, goods, and services worldwide, with uncertain duration and magnitude of impact on 2022 sales and results. A significant forward tax headwind is the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act's mandatory **capitalization of research and development expenses** over multiple years rather than immediate deduction. Beginning in 2022, this is reducing Illumina's tax deductions. Management estimates the provision will increase **tax payments by approximately $103 million in 2022**. The company is monitoring legislative efforts to repeal, modify, or defer this requirement, potentially retroactively to 2022 start.

## Accounting and Regulatory Matters

### ASU 2020-06 Adoption (Convertible Debt Accounting)

On **January 3, 2022**, Illumina adopted **ASU 2020-06** (_Debt - Debt with Conversion and Other Options; Derivatives and Hedging - Contracts in Entity's Own Equity_), which eliminated the bifurcation of embedded conversion features from convertible instruments and changed diluted EPS calculations for convertible securities to the "if-converted method."

**Impact on the 2023 Convertible Senior Notes:**
- Prior to adoption, the $126 million equity component of the conversion option was classified in stockholders' equity, and the host debt was measured with a debt discount that was being amortized to interest expense at an estimated effective rate of 3.7%.
- Post-adoption, the conversion feature is no longer separated; the entire $750 million principal amount is measured as a single liability at amortized cost.
- On January 3, 2022 (adoption date), Illumina recorded a cumulative-effect adjustment:
  - Increase convertible senior notes: **$43 million**.
  - Increase retained earnings: **$61 million**.
  - Decrease deferred tax liabilities: **$11 million**.
  - Decrease additional paid-in capital: **$93 million**.
- **Benefit**: Going forward, interest expense will be lower because no debt discount amortization occurs; the notes accrue interest based on amortized cost.
- **Diluted EPS Impact**: In Q1 2022, diluted EPS decreased by less than $0.01 due to the change in the if-converted method calculation. Q1 2021 was calculated under the prior treasury stock method.

## Summary of Key Takeaways

1. **Revenue growth is solid** (12% YoY) but **masks GRAIL operational drag**: Core Illumina grew 12% in an isolated view; GRAIL's early-stage commercialization yielded only $10 million in revenue against $172 million in operating losses.

2. **Gross margin compression is real but modest for Core**: The 30 basis point decline in consolidated margin to 66.6% was driven by GRAIL losses ($29 million on $10 million revenue) and freight cost headwinds, not Core Illumina weakness. Core margins held flat at 69.7%.

3. **Tax rate spike to 38.3% is a significant headwind**: The R&D capitalization requirement alone is projected to add ~$103 million to 2022 cash tax payments. This is unlikely to reverse absent legislative action.

4. **GRAIL acquisition remains in regulatory limbo**: FTC administrative trial wraps in June 2022 with decision expected by September; European Commission review is ongoing with risk of material fines (up to 10% of consolidated revenues) if deemed a violation. Operations are hamstrung by monitoring trustee oversight.

5. **Core business remains strong**: Sequencing consumables grew $89 million (13%); instruments grew $36 million (20%), driven by NovaSeq demand. Installed base expansion is sustaining consumables pull-through.

6. **Liquidity is adequate**: $1.4 billion in cash, no credit facility draws, and normalized operating cash flow of ~$170 million per quarter provide runway for continued operations and R&D investment, despite GRAIL cash burn.

7. **Patent litigation against BGI yielded $8 million jury award** with permanent injunction, but appeal is pending; separately, CGI's $334 million damages claim in Delaware trial is in progress. Outcomes remain uncertain.
