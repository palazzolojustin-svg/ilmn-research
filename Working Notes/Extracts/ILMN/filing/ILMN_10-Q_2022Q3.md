# Illumina Q3 2022 10-Q — $3.9B GRAIL Impairment, EU Prohibition, BGI Settlement

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-Q_2022Q3.md` · Date: 2022-10-02 · SEC, Illumina Inc. Form 10-Q filing, November 3, 2022 · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina's Q3 2022 10-Q filing reveals a $3.9 billion goodwill impairment on the GRAIL acquisition, triggered by the European Commission's September 6 prohibition decision. The company also accrued $453 million for a potential EU fine, paid $325 million to settle patent disputes with BGI and Complete Genomics, and reported flat revenue growth amid ongoing regulatory turmoil, a declining gross margin, and near-term debt maturities of $1.25 billion.

## Overview

Illumina reported results for the quarter and nine months ended October 2, 2022, dominated by regulatory setbacks on the $7.1 billion GRAIL acquisition and a large non-cash impairment charge. Revenue was essentially flat, but the company faced a $3.9 billion goodwill write-down, a $453 million contingent liability for an anticipated European fine, and significant near-term debt repayment obligations.

## Revenue

### By Product and Geography

**Three months ended October 2, 2022** (Q3 2022):

Total revenue of $1,115 million was essentially flat compared to $1,108 million in Q3 2021 (+1%). The composition shifted slightly: product revenue (instruments and consumables) fell 1% to $963 million, while service and other revenue (including GRAIL's Galleri blood test and maintenance contracts) rose 17% to $152 million [10-Q, Item 2].

Core Illumina revenue (excluding GRAIL) reached $1,110 million, nearly identical to $1,106 million a year earlier. Within that:
- **Sequencing consumables** (the reagent kits customers buy per run) held flat at $720 million, as growth in the installed base of instruments was offset by "challenging macroeconomic factors on customers" [Item 2]. The company also faced a $27 million foreign exchange headwind, partially offset by $16 million in cash flow hedge gains reclassified to revenue [Note 6].
- **Sequencing instruments** (sequencers like NovaSeq and NextSeq) fell 9% to $162 million, driven by fewer NovaSeq 6000 shipments, partially offset by more NextSeq 1000/2000 units [Item 2].
- **Microarray products** (older array-based technology for genotyping) were flat at $81 million, comprising only 8% of product revenue.
- **Service and other revenue** for Core Illumina rose 11% to $142 million, primarily from "increased revenue from extended maintenance service contracts" [Item 2].

GRAIL, the cancer early-detection company acquired August 18, 2021, generated only $10 million in Q3 2022 (up from $2 million in Q3 2021 from the post-acquisition period). This is minimal relative to its acquisition cost because GRAIL remains operationally separate under a European Commission interim measures order [Item 2, Item 7]; its Galleri blood test is still in clinical validation and early commercialization.

**Nine months ended October 2, 2022** (YTD 2022):

Revenue rose 5% to $3,501 million from $3,327 million in the nine months ended October 3, 2021. Core Illumina drove the growth:
- **Sequencing consumables** up 5% to $2,237 million ($2,123 million prior year), reflecting "growth in the instrument installed base" despite the macroeconomic headwinds [Item 2].
- **Sequencing instruments** up 4% to $563 million ($544 million), led by increased NovaSeq shipments partly offset by fewer NextSeq units.
- **Service and other revenue** up 2% to $431 million ($422 million), somewhat dampened by a comparison to a $27 million patent litigation settlement revenue recognized in Q2 2021 [Item 2].

GRAIL contributed $32 million YTD (vs. $2 million in the comparable 2021 nine-week period beginning post-acquisition).

**Q3 2022**: A $11 million *gain* (credited to operating expenses). This reflected a net $6 million release of previously accrued litigation liabilities and a $5 million gain recognized on the BGI settlement [Item 2, Item 7].

### Goodwill Impairment

**$3,914 million impairment in Q3 2022 and YTD 2022** (no impairment in Q3 2021 or YTD 2021). This is a non-cash charge related entirely to GRAIL [Item 6].

Illumina performs annual goodwill impairment testing in May. In Q2 2022, the company ran a quantitative test on GRAIL using a discount rate of 16.0% and concluded fair value exceeded carrying value by $700 million, with no impairment [Item 6]. However, on July 13, 2022, the EU General Court ruled that the European Commission has jurisdiction to review the GRAIL acquisition, and on September 6, 2022, the European Commission issued its Prohibition Decision finding the acquisition incompatible with the internal market. Combined with "a continued and significant decrease in the Company's stock price and market capitalization," these constituted triggering events for an interim impairment test [Item 6].

In the interim test (performed in Q3 2022), Illumina recalculated fair value using both an income approach (discounted cash flows) and a market approach (comparable companies). The discount rate for GRAIL increased to 22.0%, reflecting heightened regulatory risk and capital market uncertainty. Fair value fell below carrying value, resulting in the full $3,914 million impairment [Item 6]. 

After the impairment, GRAIL's remaining goodwill balance is $2,178 million, roughly equal to estimated fair value, such that "changes in our future operating results, cash flows, share price, market capitalization or discount rates…could affect the estimated fair values of our reporting units and may result in additional goodwill impairment charges in the future" [Item 6]. Core Illumina's fair value exceeded its carrying value by more than $30 billion, so no impairment was recorded for that segment.

Illumina also evaluated the IPR&D (in-process research and development) intangible asset and definite-lived intangibles on GRAIL and Core Illumina; no further impairments were recorded [Item 6].

## Regulatory and Legal Matters

### GRAIL Acquisition

The $7.1 billion acquisition of GRAIL (announced June 2020, closed August 18, 2021) has become the dominant legal and financial risk for the company. GRAIL is a privately held early-stage company focused on a blood test called Galleri that aims to detect multiple cancers before symptoms; it has not yet commercialized at scale.

**FTC Proceedings (United States)**

On March 30, 2021, the U.S. Federal Trade Commission (FTC) filed an administrative complaint and lawsuit in federal district court, alleging the acquisition would violate Section 7 of the Clayton Act (antitrust law). Illumina transferred the federal case to San Diego District Court [Item 7]. The administrative trial began August 24, 2021. On September 1, 2022, the administrative law judge (ALJ) issued a decision in favor of Illumina, finding that "the FTC's complaint counsel had failed to prove its prima facie case that Illumina's acquisition of GRAIL would result in harm to competition in a putative market for multi-cancer early detection (MCED) tests." The FTC appealed on September 2, 2022, and filed its opening brief on October 4, 2022. Illumina stated it "intends to vigorously defend against the FTC action" [Item 7].

The FTC decision is favorable but not final; the full FTC Commission must rule on the appeal.

**European Commission Proceedings**

On April 19, 2021, the European Commission accepted a Member State referral (Article 22 of the EU Merger Regulation) for review of the GRAIL acquisition, with France leading and Belgium, Greece, Iceland, the Netherlands, and Norway joining [Item 7]. Unlike the U.S., where dealmakers can close before regulatory clearance, the EU standstill provision (Article 22(4)) purportedly prohibited closing. Illumina contested the EU's jurisdiction and filed an appeal in the EU General Court on April 29, 2021 [Item 7].

**Key decisions**:
- **July 13, 2022**: The EU General Court ruled that the European Commission *has* jurisdiction to review the acquisition [Item 7, Item 1A Risk Factors].
- **September 6, 2022**: The European Commission adopted its Prohibition Decision, finding the acquisition "incompatible with the internal market in Europe because it results in a significant impediment to effective competition" [Item 7, Item 1A].
- **September 22, 2022**: Illumina filed an appeal in the Court of Justice of the European Union asking for annulment of the General Court's July 13 decision [Item 7].

The Commission's Prohibition Decision "indicates that a subsequent decision is likely to be adopted by the European Commission that will order us to divest GRAIL" (the "EC Divestment Decision"), though the timing is unknown [Item 7, Item 1A].

**Interim Measures Orders**

On October 29, 2021, the European Commission imposed an interim measures order (updated October 28, 2022) requiring that:
- Illumina and GRAIL operate as independent legal entities with no integration
- No confidential information flows between the entities
- An independent monitoring trustee oversees compliance
- GRAIL remains viable and competitive [Item 1A]

Illumina states these restrictions "have imposed implementation and administrative processes and additional costs, which have been burdensome to implement and administer" and expects them to continue "for the duration of the hold separate arrangement" [Item 1A].

**European Commission Fine**

On July 19, 2022, the European Commission issued a Statement of Objections, alleging Illumina breached the merger regulation by proceeding with the acquisition despite the purported standstill. Under Article 14(2)(b) of the EU Merger Regulation, fines can reach up to 10% of consolidated annual revenues [Item 7, Item 1A]. Illumina's fiscal 2021 revenues were $4.531 billion, so 10% = $453 million. The company accrued exactly this amount as of Q3 2022 [Item 2, Item 6].

The company notes that "neither the Prohibition Decision nor such public statements indicate when any such EC Divestment Decision may be adopted" and that it "intends to appeal the Prohibition Decision to the EU General Court by the applicable deadline" and "intend[s] to appeal any EC Divestment Decision (if and when adopted)" [Item 1A]. However, the forward-looking statement section lists as risks the uncertainty around timing and terms of any required divestment, tax consequences if forced to sell (likely a taxable transaction with a $500M–$1B tax basis, resulting in huge tax liability), and loss of anticipated synergies [Item 1A].

### BGI Patent Litigation and Settlement

Illumina has been locked in patent disputes with BGI Genomics and Complete Genomics (acquired by BGI) for several years. On July 14, 2022, the parties settled all disputes [Item 7].

**Settlement Terms** [Item 7]:

- **Payment**: Illumina pays Complete Genomics $325 million (one-time, no ongoing royalties).
- **Allocation**:
  - $180 million capitalized as a **licensed technology intangible asset** (amortized over 6.5 years, ~$28M per year). This license grants Illumina fully paid-up rights to BGI's 2-channel sequencing technology patents, allowing Illumina to use 2-channel sequencing in "all its current and future platforms with no additional royalties owed."
  - $150 million allocated to release of past damages claimed (offset against the $156 million accrual Illumina had recorded).
  - $5 million gain recognized from damages awarded to Illumina.
- **Mutual licenses**: BGI received a fully paid-up license to three Illumina image-mix patents.
- **Litigation standstill**: Both parties agreed not to file new patent or antitrust actions until October 1, 2025, except regarding NIPT (non-invasive prenatal testing) and MCED (multi-cancer early detection) IP.
- **Injunction**: The permanent injunction against BGI's StandardMPS chemistry was extended to January 1, 2023 (later expired).

**Prior litigation verdicts** [Item 7]:
- November 30, 2021: Jury found Illumina's '537, '200, '973 patents and claims of the '025 patent were valid and willfully infringed by BGI; awarded $8 million damages.
- May 6, 2022: Jury in Delaware District Court found Illumina willfully infringed two Complete Genomics patents (9,222,132 and 10,662,473); awarded ~$334 million damages.

The settlement resolved both judgments (Illumina's $8M award and Complete Genomics' $334M judgment) for a combined $325 million payment. The company recognized a $6 million net gain (release of $156M accrual minus $150M allocated to damages liability), offset by the loss allocation for the BGI license ($180M capitalized asset was measured at fair value using DCF analysis) [Item 6].

### RavGen Patent Litigation

On December 3, 2020, RavGen filed suit against Illumina claiming infringement of patents related to sample stabilization, alleging use in Verifi, VeriSeq, and liquid biopsy products. Patents expire March 13, 2023 [Item 7].

Illumina filed an IPR (Inter Partes Review) petition with the U.S. Patent Trial and Appeal Board in July 2021; the Board instituted the IPR on January 26, 2022, with a final decision due January 26, 2023 [Item 7]. The District Court stayed the litigation pending IPR outcome on March 1, 2022 [Item 7]. Illumina states it "cannot estimate the possible loss or range of loss" [Item 7], suggesting material uncertainty.

## Conclusion

Illumina's Q3 2022 10-Q reveals a company at an inflection point. The core sequencing business (Core Illumina) remains sound—modest revenue growth, positive operating income, and a global installed base of instruments—but is facing margin compression from macroeconomic headwinds and operational leverage challenges.

The GRAIL acquisition, intended to accelerate entry into early cancer detection, has become a financial and strategic burden. The European Commission's prohibition of the acquisition, combined with declining market valuations and regulatory uncertainty, triggered a $3.9 billion goodwill impairment in Q3. The company also accrued $453 million for an anticipated EU fine, bringing total GRAIL-related charges to ~$4.4 billion in the quarter and nine months.

Meanwhile, a major patent settlement with BGI and Complete Genomics cost $325 million upfront but cleared a significant legal overhang and granted Illumina access to 2-channel sequencing patents.

**Near-term financial challenges**:
1. **Debt refinancing**: $1.25 billion in debt due within 12 months ($500M term notes in March 2023, $750M convertible in August 2023). With modest operating cash flow and a challenged stock price, refinancing will likely require a debt capital markets transaction and/or use of available liquidity.
2. **GRAIL resolution**: Regulatory proceedings (EU appeals, FTC appeal) could stretch into 2023–2024. If ordered to divest GRAIL, Illumina faces significant tax consequences and loss of anticipated synergies.
3. **Profitability**: YTD operating loss of $4.1 billion (excluding GRAIL, $411M operating income, a reasonable result, but offset by legal charges). Without the non-recurring items, Core Illumina is profitable and growing, but the company needs to return to consistent pre-tax profitability to service debt and fund growth.

**Strengths**: Market leadership in sequencing, growing consumables revenue, strong balance sheet (post-refinancing), and a diversified instrument and application base.

**Risks**: Regulatory uncertainty on GRAIL, debt maturity wall, margin compression, customer hesitancy amid economic slowdown, and competitive threats from Oxford Nanopore and others in long-read sequencing.
