# Illumina Q1 2023 10-Q — revenue decline, GRAIL divestiture ordered, $458M fine accrual

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-Q_2023Q1.md` · Date: 2023-05-05 · SEC Form 10-Q filing for quarter ended April 2, 2023 · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina's Q1 2023 results show 11% revenue decline to $1.09B and operating loss of $64M (vs. $184M profit in Q1 2022) driven by lower sequencing consumables and instruments amid customer inventory management and NovaSeq X launch pressures. The European Commission and U.S. FTC ordered divestiture of the GRAIL cancer-detection acquisition; Illumina accrued $458M (10% of annual revenue) for an anticipated EU fine and is appealing the FTC order. The company ended Q1 with $1.5B cash and raised $1B in debt in December 2022. Guidance maintained at 7–10% full-year revenue growth, and management plans $100M+ in cost reductions beginning H2 2023.

## Financial Performance

### Q1 2023 Revenue and Segment Breakdown

**Consolidated revenue** declined 11% to **$1,087 million** in Q1 2023 from $1,223 million in Q1 2022. [10-Q, MD&A, Results of Operations]

**Core Illumina** revenue (excluding GRAIL) was **$1,076 million** (–12% vs. Q1 2022's $1,221M):
- Product revenue: $930M (–14% YoY), comprising consumables $770M (–10%) and instruments $160M (–27%)
- Service and other revenue: $146M (+1%)

**GRAIL** segment revenue was **$20 million** (+100% YoY from $10M), consisting of service and other revenue.

**Eliminations** of intercompany transactions totaled $9 million.

The revenue decline in Core Illumina consumables was "primarily due to a decrease in sequencing consumables revenue of $92 million driven primarily by lower NovaSeq 6000 consumables pull-through as some of our high throughput customers transition to NovaSeq X, as well as customers managing tighter inventory given the continued impact of challenging macroeconomic factors." [MD&A] Instruments fell due to fewer NovaSeq 6000 and NextSeq 550 shipments, "partially offset by shipments of our NovaSeq X instrument that launched in Q1 2023." [MD&A] Core Illumina was also adversely impacted by $24 million in unfavorable foreign exchange fluctuations in Q1 2023. [MD&A]

### Revenue by Geography

For Q1 2023, Core Illumina's regional revenue was:
- **Americas**: $616M (Q1 2022: $648M, down $32M or 5%)
- **Europe**: $261M (Q1 2022: $286M, down $25M or 9%)
- **Greater China** (China, Taiwan, Hong Kong): $91M (Q1 2022: $127M, down $36M or 28%)
- **Asia-Pacific, Middle East and Africa (AMEA)**: $119M (Q1 2022: $162M, down $43M or 27%)

Management implemented a new global commercial structure in Q1 2023 "to improve operating efficiencies and better align with local markets," integrating Asia-Pacific and Japan with emerging markets across the Middle East, Turkey, and CIS. [Revenue note]

### Gross Margin

**Consolidated gross profit** was $655 million (60.3% margin) in Q1 2023, down from $815 million (66.6% margin) in Q1 2022. The 630 basis-point decline reflects:

- **Core Illumina gross profit**: $687M (63.8% margin) vs. $851M (69.7% margin) in Q1 2022
- **GRAIL gross loss**: $(25)M vs. $(29)M in Q1 2022

The Core Illumina margin compression was "driven primarily by less fixed cost leverage on lower manufacturing volumes and lower instrument margins due to the NovaSeq X launch in Q1 2023, which is typical with a new platform introduction until we scale manufacturing and gain operating efficiencies." [MD&A] Management noted that gross margin "depends on many factors, including: market conditions that may impact our pricing; sales mix changes among consumables, instruments, services, and development and licensing revenue; product mix changes between established products and new products; excess and obsolete inventories; royalties; our cost structure for manufacturing operations relative to volume; freight costs; and product support obligations." [MD&A]

GRAIL's gross loss was "primarily due to amortization of intangible assets of $34 million." [Results of Operations table]

### Operating Expenses and Loss from Operations

**Consolidated loss from operations** was $(64) million in Q1 2023 vs. income from operations of $184 million in Q1 2022, a $248 million swing.

**Core Illumina operating income** fell to $142 million from $362 million, while **GRAIL operating loss** widened to $(204) million from $(172) million.

**Research and development expense** increased to $341 million (31.4% of revenue) from $323 million (26.4%) in Q1 2022:
- Core Illumina R&D: $259M (+$21M or 9%), driven by "increases in headcount, as we continue to invest in the research and development of new products and enhancements to existing products"
- GRAIL R&D: $86M (+$1M), consisting primarily of expenses for headcount, performance-based compensation, and clinical trials

**Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expense** rose to $378 million (34.8% of revenue) from $308 million (25.2%):
- Core Illumina SG&A: $286M (+$35M or 14%), primarily due to "recognizing a lower gain on our contingent consideration liability related to our acquisition of GRAIL" ($1M gain in Q1 2023 vs. $49M in Q1 2022), partially offset by a decrease in professional services
- GRAIL SG&A: $93M (+$35M or 60%), driven by "increases in headcount, including an increase in performance-based compensation, and professional services"

Management stated: "We plan to reduce our annualized run rate expenses by more than $100 million beginning later in 2023 to accelerate progress toward higher margins and create flexibility for further investment in high-growth areas." [MD&A]

## Income Taxes

## Legal and Regulatory Proceedings

### GRAIL Acquisition — European Commission and FTC Actions

The GRAIL acquisition, closed August 18, 2021 for approximately $7.1 billion (including $2B earnout), remains subject to extensive regulatory challenge and hold-separate orders.

**European Commission Prohibition Decision and Divestiture Order**:
- September 6, 2022: EC completed Phase II review and issued a Prohibition Decision finding the acquisition "incompatible with the internal market in Europe because it results in a significant impediment to effective competition"
- November 17, 2022: Illumina filed action with EU General Court seeking annulment of the Prohibition Decision
- December 5, 2022: EC issued a Statement of Objections announcing an order requiring Illumina to divest GRAIL (EC Divestment Decision)
- January 16, 2023: Illumina filed response to the Statement of Objections
- As of April 2, 2023: No EC Divestment Decision has been formally adopted; timing remains uncertain

**EC Interim Measures (Hold Separate) Order**:
- October 29, 2021: EC imposed Initial Interim Measures Order
- October 28, 2022: EC renewed order (New Interim Measures Order) requiring GRAIL to be held and operated independently from Illumina, with no integration, with independent monitoring trustee oversight
- January 10, 2023: Illumina filed action with EU General Court seeking annulment of the New Interim Measures Order
- February 21, 2023: EU General Court stayed proceedings at EC's request
- The hold-separate obligations are described as "burdensome to implement and administer" and have imposed "implementation and administrative processes and additional legal, financial advisory, regulatory and other professional services costs"

**EC Article 14(2)(b) Fine**:
- July 19, 2022: EC issued Statement of Objections alleging Illumina breached the EU Merger Regulation by completing the acquisition during pendency of EC review
- EC stated intent to impose a fine under Article 14(2)(b) of up to 10% of consolidated annual revenues
- As of April 2, 2023: Illumina accrued **$458 million** (representing 10% of fiscal 2022 consolidated annual revenues of $4.578 billion) as its probable loss, in accordance with ASC 450 (Contingencies)
- Management acknowledged: "There can be no assurance, however, that any fine or our actual losses will not be higher than the amount accrued"

**Jurisdiction Appeal** (EU General Court):
- July 13, 2022: EU General Court ruled that the European Commission has jurisdiction under the EU Merger Regulation to review the acquisition (rejecting Illumina's prior argument that the EC lacked jurisdiction)
- September 22, 2022: Illumina filed appeal in the Court of Justice of the European Union seeking annulment of that jurisdiction ruling

**U.S. FTC Administrative Proceedings**:
- March 30, 2021: FTC filed administrative complaint and motion for preliminary injunction in U.S. District Court for D.C.
- April 20, 2021: District court granted motion to transfer venue to U.S. District Court for Southern District of California; FTC's preliminary injunction motion was later dismissed without prejudice
- August 24, 2021: Administrative trial commenced before an administrative law judge (ALJ)
- September 1, 2022: ALJ ruled in favor of Illumina, finding no violation of Section 7 of the Clayton Act; the ALJ found the FTC's complaint counsel failed to prove its prima facie case that the acquisition would result in harm to competition in a putative market for multi-cancer early detection (MCED) tests
- September 2, 2022: FTC's complaint counsel appealed the ALJ decision to the full FTC
- December 13, 2022: Oral argument before full FTC
- March 31, 2023: **FTC issued opinion and order (FTC Order) requiring Illumina to divest GRAIL, reversing the ALJ's ruling**
- April 5, 2023: Illumina filed petition for review of the FTC Order in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit
- April 24, 2023: FTC granted motion staying the FTC Order in its entirety pending resolution of Illumina's Fifth Circuit appeal
- April 25, 2023: Fifth Circuit granted expedited briefing schedule: Illumina brief due June 5, 2023; FTC opposition due June 26, 2023; Illumina reply due July 11, 2023
- Management stated: "We intend to continue to vigorously defend against the FTC action"

### RavGen Patent Litigation — Settled

**Prior Status**: RavGen filed patent infringement suit on December 3, 2020 against Illumina in U.S. District Court for Delaware, alleging infringement of U.S. Patents Nos. 7,332,277 and 7,727,720 through use of Streck sample collection tubes in Illumina's Verifi, Verifi Plus, and VeriSeq NIPT and liquid biopsy oncology products. The patents cover use of a sample-stabilizing agent that inhibits cell lysis. RavGen sought unspecified damages, injunction, and attorneys' fees.

**Procedural History**: 
- January 27, 2021: Illumina filed answer and counterclaims seeking declaratory judgment of non-infringement and invalidity
- July 20, 2021: Illumina filed Petitions for Inter Partes Review (IPR) of both patents with the U.S. Patent Trial and Appeal Board
- January 26, 2022: PTAB instituted the IPRs
- January 25, 2023: PTAB issued Final Written Decisions finding no challenged claim unpatentable due to anticipation or obviousness
- Patents expired March 13, 2023

**Settlement (April 12, 2023)**: "The Company and RavGen executed a confidential settlement agreement resolving the litigation. Under the settlement, among other terms, the parties denied liability and stipulated to the dismissal of the lawsuit with prejudice."

**Streck Indemnification**: The company had requested indemnification from Streck, Inc. (supplier of the tubes used in Illumina's products) on December 15, 2020. Streck denied any obligation on January 6, 2021. The parties executed a tolling agreement effective April 2, 2021, staying Illumina's indemnification claim pending resolution of the underlying patent suit. The settlement status of the Streck indemnification claim was not disclosed in this 10-Q.

Illumina is "a provider of sequencing- and array-based solutions, serving customers in the research, clinical and applied markets. Our products are used for applications in the life sciences, oncology, reproductive health, agriculture and other emerging segments. Our customers include leading genomic research centers, academic institutions, government laboratories, and hospitals, as well as pharmaceutical, biotechnology, commercial molecular diagnostic laboratories, and consumer genomics companies." [Organization and Business Overview]

### Segment Reporting

Illumina operates two reportable segments:
- **Core Illumina**: All operations excluding GRAIL
- **GRAIL**: Healthcare company focused on early detection of multiple cancers, acquired August 18, 2021

GRAIL must remain held and operated separately and independently from Illumina under interim measures ordered by the European Commission (hold-separate agreement, monitored by independent trustee).

## Intellectual Property and Competition Risk

Management highlighted the complexity of protecting proprietary technologies in genomics: "The proprietary positions of companies developing tools for the life sciences, genomics, forensics, agricultural, and pharmaceutical industries, including our proprietary position, generally are uncertain and involve complex legal and factual questions." Risks include reliance on patent protection (including expiring patents on sequencing-by-synthesis technology), challenges enforcing IP outside the U.S., risk of independent development or design-around efforts by competitors, and uncertainty regarding AI-based software's potential to inadvertently disclose confidential information. [Risk Factors]
