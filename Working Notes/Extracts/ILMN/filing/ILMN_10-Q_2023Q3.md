# Illumina Q3 2023 10-Q: Severe GRAIL Impairment, EC Divestiture Order, Revenue Decline, Margin Compression

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-Q_2023Q3.md` · Date: 2023-10-01 · SEC Filing (Form 10-Q), filed 2023-11-09 · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina's Q3 2023 quarterly report shows revenue down 3% year-to-date to $3.38B (vs. $3.50B in 2022), driven by declines in sequencing consumables and instruments amid macroeconomic headwinds and product transitions. The company took a massive $821M goodwill and intangible impairment charge on GRAIL in Q3, following a $3.9B GRAIL impairment in Q3 2022. On October 12, 2023, the European Commission issued a Divestment Decision requiring Illumina to divest GRAIL and imposing transitional "hold-separate" measures; the company is appealing. Illumina faces a €432M ($458M) fine for proceeding with the GRAIL acquisition during the regulatory review period (appeal pending). Gross margin compressed to 61.2% from 65.7%, driven by fixed-cost deleverage, unfavorable product mix, and lower instrument margins during the NovaSeq X ramp. The company reported net losses of $754M (Q3) and $986M (YTD), offset losses on strategic investments and contingent consideration liability gains. Cash fell $1.08B to $927M due to repayment of $500M in 2023 Term Notes and $750M in convertible notes.

## Financial Summary

### Revenue

**Q3 and YTD Performance**

Total revenue was essentially flat in Q3 at $1,119M (Q3 2022: $1,115M), but declined 3% year-to-date to $3,382M (YTD 2022: $3,501M, a decrease of $119M). 

Product revenue (consumables and instruments) fell to $941M in Q3 2022 from $963M, down $22M or 2%. Year-to-date, product revenue declined to $2,864M from $3,039M, a drop of $175M or 6%. Service and other revenue increased to $178M in Q3 from $152M (up $26M or 17% year-to-date to $518M from $462M). GRAIL service and other revenue grew sharply to $21M in Q3 (vs. $10M in Q3 2022, up 110%) and to $62M year-to-date (vs. $32M, up 94%), driven primarily by sales of its Galleri multi-cancer early detection blood test.

**Core Illumina Segment Revenue Breakdown**

In Q3 2023, Core Illumina—the company's core sequencing and array business, excluding GRAIL—generated $1,106M in revenue (flat vs. Q3 2022 $1,110M). Year-to-date, Core Illumina revenue declined to $3,341M from $3,487M, a 4% decrease of $146M.

Within Core Illumina, sequencing consumables (the recurring reagent kits required for each sequencing run) declined $30M in Q3 and $127M year-to-date. Management attributed this primarily to lower "pull-through" of NovaSeq 6000 consumables as high-throughput customers transition to the newly launched NovaSeq X (launched Q1 2023), combined with reduced customer purchasing power and delayed project planning due to macroeconomic conditions.

Sequencing instruments revenue grew $17M in Q3 (driven by NovaSeq X shipments) but fell $38M year-to-date, as shipments of legacy instruments (NovaSeq 6000, NextSeq, MiSeq) declined faster than new X platform adoption could offset. Core Illumina service revenue rose $16M in Q3 and $27M year-to-date, reflecting expanded maintenance service contracts on a growing installed base.

**Geographic Performance**

Americas revenue strengthened to $663M in Q3 (Q3 2022: $597M), up 11%, and grew to $1,920M year-to-date (YTD 2022: $1,885M). Europe was flat in Q3 at $260M and essentially flat year-to-date at $825M (vs. $819M).

Greater China deteriorated significantly, falling 26% in Q3 to $98M (from $133M) and 20% year-to-date to $302M (from $378M). Asia-Pacific, Middle East, and Africa (APME) also declined, dropping 22% in Q3 to $98M (from $126M) and 20% year-to-date to $335M (from $419M). These declines reflect competitive pressures in China and broader macroeconomic challenges in emerging markets. Management notes that impacts from COVID-19 mitigation measures, the Russia-Ukraine conflict (restricting shipments to affected regions), inflation, foreign exchange volatility, and concerns about economic downturn are expected to continue through the remainder of 2023 with significant uncertainty around duration and magnitude.

### Gross Profit and Margin

Gross profit and margin deteriorated substantially. In Q3 2023, gross profit fell to $684M from $717M, a decline of $33M, and gross margin compressed to 61.1% from 64.3%. Year-to-date, gross profit fell to $2,070M from $2,300M (down $230M), and gross margin compressed to 61.2% from 65.7%.

Management attributed the margin decline to multiple factors: (1) **fixed-cost deleverage** on lower manufacturing volumes; (2) **lower instrument margins** from the NovaSeq X launch, which is typical during new platform ramp until manufacturing scales and efficiencies are gained; (3) **unfavorable product mix**; (4) **increased field services and installation costs**; partly offset by (5) **lower freight costs**.

GRAIL generated a gross loss in both Q3 2023 ($27M loss) and Q3 2022 ($32M loss), primarily due to $33M in quarterly amortization of acquired intangible assets (product technology and trade names). Year-to-date, GRAIL's gross loss was $77M (vs. $91M in YTD 2022), reflecting the substantial amortization burden ($100M YTD 2023).

## Operating Expenses and Operating Loss

### Research and Development

Core Illumina R&D expense decreased $15M (6%) in Q3 2023 to $315M, due to reductions in lab supplies, professional services, recruiting, and travel as the company executed cost reduction initiatives. Year-to-date, Core Illumina R&D rose $31M (4%) to $1,013M from $975M, driven primarily by higher compensation expenses (including performance-based compensation) and $13M in restructuring charges related to employee separations, partially offset by the cost reductions noted above.

GRAIL R&D grew $5M (7%) in Q3 and $9M (4%) year-to-date, reflecting increased headcount, employee compensation, lab and consumables spending, though partially offset by lower clinical trial costs.

Consolidated R&D (Core Illumina and GRAIL combined, net of eliminations) was $315M in Q3 (down $10M) and $1,013M year-to-date (up $38M).

### Selling, General and Administrative

SG&A expense spiked dramatically. Core Illumina SG&A rose $150M (227%) in Q3 to $216M, driven by two main items: (1) lower gains on the contingent consideration liability related to GRAIL's Contingent Value Rights (CVRs), which fell from $219M gain in Q3 2022 to $110M in Q3 2023; and (2) $55M in Q3 restructuring charges (primarily lease and asset impairments from exiting the San Diego i3 campus), partially offset by lower professional services and travel. 

Year-to-date, Core Illumina SG&A rose $201M (31%) to $857M from $656M, reflecting (1) lower CVR gains ($82M gain YTD 2023 vs. $230M gain in YTD 2022), a $148M swing; (2) $74M in restructuring charges (lease/asset impairments and employee separations); and (3) $29M in costs related to a proxy contest, partially offset by lower professional services and recruiting costs.

GRAIL SG&A rose $6M (7%) in Q3 and $61M (29%) year-to-date, due to higher headcount, employee compensation, and increased professional services and marketing spend.

Consolidated SG&A was $303M in Q3 (up $157M or 108%) and $1,127M year-to-date (up $262M or 30%).

### Goodwill and Intangible Impairment

GRAIL goodwill and intangible assets were severely impaired in Q3 2023. The company recorded $821M in total impairment, consisting of:
- **Goodwill impairment: $712M**
- **In-process research & development (IPR&D) impairment: $109M**

This was triggered by a "circumstance indicating" impairment risk: sustained decline in Illumina's stock price and consolidated market capitalization during Q3 2023. The company performed an interim quantitative impairment test using a blend of income approach (discounted cash flow) and market approach (comparable companies). The income approach employed a **24.0% discount rate for GRAIL** (reflecting the higher risk profile of a stand-alone, divesting subsidiary). The company performed sensitivity analysis: an increase of 50 to 100 basis points to the discount rate would have resulted in an additional $200M to $350M of goodwill impairment for GRAIL.

As of October 1, 2023, **remaining GRAIL goodwill stood at $1,466M**, down from $3,239M at January 1, 2023 (which had already reflected a $3,914M GRAIL goodwill impairment in Q3 2022). The company noted that Core Illumina's fair value exceeded its carrying value by more than $20B, so no impairment was recorded for Core Illumina. The company also indicated that future changes in operating results, cash flows, share price, market capitalization, or discount rates—as well as regulatory decisions regarding the GRAIL acquisition—could trigger additional impairment charges.

### Legal Contingency and Settlement

Q3 2023 recorded a $1M **gain** from a patent litigation settlement. Year-to-date, the company recorded a $14M **net expense**, consisting of adjustments to the European Commission fine accrual and a patent litigation loss in Q1 2023 (discussed below under Legal Proceedings).

In Q3 2022, the company had recorded a $11M gain from releasing prior litigation accruals and gain contingencies related to a BGI settlement. In Q2 2022 (part of YTD 2022), the company had accrued $453M for the European Commission fine and incurred a $145M net loss from the BGI settlement.

### Consolidated Operating Loss

The consolidated loss from operations was $(754)M in Q3 2023 (vs. $(3,657)M loss in Q3 2022) and $(905)M year-to-date (vs. $(4,052)M in YTD 2022). The improvement from prior year was entirely due to the absence of the massive $3,914M GRAIL goodwill impairment recorded in Q3 2022, which had not recurred. The more modest $821M impairment in Q3 2023, combined with lower revenue and compressed margins, resulted in Core Illumina reporting an operating income of $262M in Q3 and $519M year-to-date, down from $445M and $411M, respectively.

## Income Taxes

The company reported an income tax **benefit** (reduction in tax expense) of $28M in Q3 2023, resulting in an effective tax rate of 3.6% on a pre-tax loss of $(782)M. Year-to-date, the company reported a **provision** (tax expense) of $36M on a pre-tax loss of $(950)M, resulting in a negative effective tax rate of (3.8)%.

The variance from the U.S. federal statutory tax rate of 21% is primarily attributable to non-deductible charges: (1) $149M in tax expense impact from the non-deductible goodwill impairment; (2) $20M (Q3) and $84M (YTD) in tax expense from capitalizing R&D expenses for tax purposes (a difference between book and tax accounting); and (3) $38M (Q3) and $63M (YTD) in tax expense from GRAIL pre-acquisition net operating losses subject to GILTI (Global Intangible Low-Taxed Income) limitations and utilization of U.S. foreign tax credits. These headwinds were partially offset by favorable impacts from earnings in lower-tax jurisdictions (Singapore, United Kingdom).

The company changed its method for calculating the interim effective tax rate during 2023. Historically, it used an estimated annual effective tax rate applied to interim results; in Q2 2023, it switched to a year-to-date rate method; and in Q3 2023, it reverted to the estimated annual rate method, which management deemed more reliable given the magnitude of the pre-tax loss.

## GRAIL Contingent Value Rights (CVRs)

### Structure and Valuation

Illumina issued Contingent Value Rights to GRAIL acquisition shareholders as part of the August 2021 acquisition. The CVRs entitle holders to quarterly cash payments (Covered Revenue Payments) for 12 years, calculated as:
- 2.5% of the first $1B of annual "Covered Revenues" (GRAIL-related revenues, primarily from the Galleri test)
- 9% of annual Covered Revenues above $1B

Fair value is remeasured each quarter using a Monte Carlo simulation with Level 3 inputs including forecasted GRAIL revenues, revenue risk premium, volatility, operational leverage ratio, and counterparty credit spread.

### Activity and Current Obligations

Covered Revenues for the aggregate period Q4 2022 through Q2 2023 were $65M, generating Covered Revenue Payments of $609k (net of certain expense reimbursements). Expected Q3 2023 Covered Revenues were approximately $21M, with expected Q4 2023 Covered Revenue Payments of approximately $194k.

Fair value as of October 1, 2023 was $330M (down from $412M at January 1), of which $329M is included in other long-term liabilities and the remaining $1M in accrued liabilities. Changes in fair value during YTD 2023 resulted in an $82M gain recognized in SG&A expense. 

The EC Divestment Decision (discussed below) requires Illumina to ensure that GRAIL has access to sufficient funds, at the time of divestiture, to cover at least 2.5 years of operations according to its latest long-range plan, which impacts management's liquidity planning.

## Legal Proceedings and Regulatory Matters

### GRAIL Acquisition Proceedings

Illumina's August 2021 acquisition of GRAIL remains subject to extensive legal and regulatory proceedings in the United States and the European Union. The company faces extraordinary pressure to divest GRAIL and has incurred substantial costs and impairments as a result of regulatory challenges.

**FTC (U.S. Federal Trade Commission)**

- March 30, 2021: FTC filed administrative complaint in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia and an administrative complaint alleging the acquisition violates Section 7 of the Clayton Act (15 U.S.C. § 18).
- April 20, 2021: Venue transferred to U.S. District Court for the Southern District of California; FTC motion to dismiss granted without prejudice.
- August 24, 2021: Administrative trial commenced.
- **September 1, 2022: Administrative Law Judge (ALJ) ruled in favor of Illumina**, finding the FTC failed to prove the acquisition violates Section 7; no competitive harm to a market for multi-cancer early detection (MCED) tests established.
- September 2, 2022: FTC's complaint counsel appealed to full FTC.
- December 13, 2022: Oral argument before full FTC.
- **March 31, 2023: Full FTC reversed ALJ decision**, issuing an opinion and order (FTC Order) requiring Illumina to divest GRAIL.
- April 5, 2023: Illumina petitioned the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit for review of the FTC Order.
- **April 24, 2023: FTC granted a motion staying the entire FTC Order pending resolution of Illumina's Fifth Circuit appeal.**
- August 16, 2023: Fifth Circuit briefing completed.
- September 12, 2023: Oral argument before Fifth Circuit.

Illumina intends to continue vigorously defending against the FTC action.

**European Commission (EU)**

- April 19, 2021: EC accepted a referral request from multiple EU Member States (France, Belgium, Greece, Iceland, Netherlands, Norway) under Article 22(1) of the EU Merger Regulation, taking jurisdiction over the acquisition—unprecedented for a U.S. company with no EU revenue.
- April 29, 2021: Illumina filed an action in the EU General Court seeking annulment of the EC's assertion of jurisdiction.
- **July 13, 2022: EU General Court ruled the EC has jurisdiction under the EU Merger Regulation.**
- **September 22, 2022: Illumina appealed to the Court of Justice of the EU**, seeking annulment of the General Court's decision.
- October 29, 2021: EC imposed an Initial Interim Measures Order (hold-separate), requiring Illumina and GRAIL to operate as independent entities, no integration, and preventing sale of GRAIL assets.
- **October 28, 2022: EC renewed the interim measures via the New Interim Measures Order**, with similar requirements.
- December 1, 2021: Illumina filed an action with the EU General Court seeking annulment of the Initial Interim Measures Order; hearing stayed pending the appeal on EC jurisdiction.
- January 10, 2023: Illumina filed an action with the EU General Court seeking annulment of the New Interim Measures Order.
- January 20, 2023: EC requested the proceedings be stayed pending Illumina's appeal on jurisdiction; the court stayed proceedings on February 21, 2023.
- **September 6, 2022: EC announced completion of Phase II review and adopted the Prohibition Decision**, finding the acquisition is incompatible with the EU internal market and results in a significant impediment to effective competition.
- November 17, 2022: Illumina filed an action with the EU General Court seeking annulment of the Prohibition Decision.
- **October 12, 2023: EC adopted the Divestment Decision**, requiring Illumina to divest GRAIL and imposing transitional measures substantially equivalent to the interim measures previously ordered (replacing the New Interim Measures Order). Illumina intends to appeal.

**EC Fine for Breach of Merger Regulation**

- **July 12, 2023: EC adopted a final decision** finding that Illumina breached the EU Merger Regulation (Article 14(2)(b)) by acquiring the ability to exert "decisive influence" over GRAIL and exerting such influence during the pendency of the EC's review.
- **EC imposed a fine of approximately €432 million**, representing the maximum 10% of Illumina's consolidated annual revenues for fiscal year 2022.
- September 26, 2023: Illumina appealed the Article 14(2)(b) Decision.
- October 2023: Illumina provided guarantees in lieu of cash payment while appeals proceed.
- **As of October 1, 2023: $458M accrued** (includes foreign currency gains) in accrued liabilities. The fine accrues interest at 5.5% per annum while outstanding.

**Hold-Separate and Transitional Measures**

Under the interim measures and now the transitional measures imposed by the EC (effective October 12, 2023), Illumina is required to:
- Ensure Illumina and GRAIL operate as independent legal entities transacting at arm's length
- Implement hold-separate arrangements with no integration activity
- Have GRAIL's day-to-day operations remain solely the responsibility of GRAIL management, with no Illumina management involvement or influence
- Take "supportive measures" to preserve GRAIL's viability, marketability, and competitiveness, including provision of resources and retention/replacement of key personnel
- Implement measures to prevent Illumina from obtaining confidential GRAIL information and vice versa
- Appoint an independent monitoring trustee (already appointed) to monitor compliance

The company describes these obligations as imposing "burdensome" implementation and administrative processes and substantial legal, financial advisory, regulatory, and other professional services costs, which are expected to continue for the duration of the transitional measures. Failure to comply may result in fines or other penalties.

### SEC Investigation

In July 2023, the SEC informed Illumina that its staff was conducting an investigation relating to Illumina and the GRAIL acquisition, requesting documents and communications primarily regarding the acquisition, certain statements and disclosures about GRAIL and its products, and related compensation of certain members of Illumina and GRAIL management. Illumina is cooperating.

### Stockholder Litigation

On October 17, 2023, a stockholder derivative and class action complaint (_Icahn Partners LP, et al. v. deSouza, et al._) was filed in the Delaware Court of Chancery against certain current and former directors (including Illumina's former Chief Executive Officer). Illumina is named as a nominal defendant. The complaint alleges the named directors breached their fiduciary duties by:
- Knowingly causing Illumina to unlawfully close the GRAIL acquisition
- Concealing material facts related to the acquisition
- Making inadequate disclosures

The complaint seeks damages, costs and attorney fees, certification and consolidation of a putative class, issuance of amended disclosures, removal of conflicted directors, and other equitable relief. The lawsuit is in an early stage, and the company intends to defend vigorously.

### Other Intellectual Property Litigation

The company settled a patent litigation matter in Q1 2023, recording a $1M loss. An earlier patent litigation settlement with BGI in Q4 2022/Q1 2023 resulted in a net loss of $145M.

- Inability to pursue future transactions
