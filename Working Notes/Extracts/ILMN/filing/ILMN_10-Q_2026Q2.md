# Illumina Q2 2026 Earnings – Revenue Growth and Margin Expansion Despite Tariffs and China Headwinds

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-Q_2026Q2.md` · Date: 2026-07-31 · SEC filing (Form 10-Q), Illumina, Inc. · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q2 2026 revenue of $1,159 million (+9% YoY) and YTD 2026 revenue of $2,251 million (+7% YoY), driven by strong demand for NovaSeq X instruments and consumables. The company completed its January 2026 acquisition of SomaLogic (proteomics) for $460 million and narrowed net income due to GRAIL divestment losses and tariff headwinds. Gross margin improved YoY but declined sequentially due to unfavorable product mix, higher memory chip costs, and freight pressures from Middle East conflict; China revenue fell 20% YoY due to the "unreliable entities list" designation.

## Business Overview and Strategic Moves

Illumina is a global leader in DNA sequencing and array-based genomic analysis. Its products serve the research, clinical, and applied markets—including life sciences, oncology, reproductive health, and agriculture. Customers include genomic research centers, academic institutions, government laboratories, hospitals, and pharmaceutical, biotechnology, molecular diagnostics, and consumer genomics companies.

On January 30, 2026, Illumina acquired SomaLogic, a proteomics company, from Standard BioTools for a total purchase price of $460 million: $382 million in cash, $81 million in fair-value contingent consideration, less $3 million for settlement of a preexisting relationship. SomaLogic provides high-throughput protein measurement technology (SOMAmer-based assays) and data analysis services. Management stated the acquisition will enhance Illumina's presence in the proteomics market and advance its multiomics strategy. Results of SomaLogic are consolidated from the January 30, 2026 acquisition date.

### Segment and Product Mix

**YTD 2026 product/service breakdown:**
- Sequencing consumables: $1,500 million (67% of product revenue)
- Microarray consumables: $149 million
- Sequencing instruments: $243 million
- Microarray instruments: $7 million
- Total product revenue: $1,899 million (84% of total revenue)

### Geographic Revenue (YTD 2026)

Illumina implemented a new global commercial structure in Q1 2026, reclassifying all prior periods:
- United States and Canada: $1,243 million (+10% vs. $1,125M YTD 2025)

The 20% decline in Greater China was the most significant geographic headwind. Management disclosed that "uncertainty regarding the impact of our inclusion on the 'unreliable entities list' by regulatory authorities in China" is an ongoing risk factor.

## Profitability and Margins

### Gross Profit and Margin

**YTD 2026:**
- Gross profit: $1,491 million vs. $1,378 million, +8%
- Gross margin: 66.2% vs. 65.6%

**Q2 2026:**
- Gross profit: $770 million vs. $695 million, +11%
- Gross margin: 66.4% vs. 65.6%

The YoY improvement was primarily attributable to a $23 million intangible asset impairment charge recorded in Q2 2025. Excluding that one-time item, gross margin declined in both periods due to several persistent headwinds:

- **Higher tariff costs:** Effective April 2025, the U.S. and other countries enacted tariffs. The largest cost impact relates to importation from Illumina's Singapore manufacturing facility. In February and March 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court and Court of International Trade ruled that tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) were improper and ordered the U.S. Customs and Border Protection to process refunds. Illumina stated it has begun pursuing refunds but cautioned: "The timing and amount of recoveries remain uncertain and will depend on the scope and timing of future phases, any further court or administrative developments, and completion of applicable administrative steps." Recoveries to date are immaterial. Management said it has "partially mitigated the effects of tariffs through supply-chain optimization, cost measures, and pricing actions." In Q2 2026, tariff costs were lower than the prior quarter due to "ongoing operational initiatives."

- **Unfavorable sales mix:** The shift toward lower-margin instruments (vs. consumables) and the inclusion of lower-margin SomaLogic revenue both pressured margin.

- **Rising component costs:** Memory chip costs have increased, particularly in 2026.

- **Freight and energy costs:** The conflict in the Middle East and instability involving Iran have driven higher fuel, energy, and shipping costs.

- **Partially offset:** Decreases in field service and warranty costs provided some offset.

Management emphasized: "Gross margin depends on many factors, including: market conditions that may impact our pricing; sales mix changes among consumables, instruments, services, and strategic partnership revenue; product mix changes between established products and new products; excess and obsolete inventories; royalties; our cost structure for manufacturing operations relative to volume; freight costs; tariffs; and product support obligations."

## GRAIL Investment and Contingent Value Rights

### Background

Illumina divested GRAIL, a liquid biopsy oncology company, in 2024 but retained a marketable equity investment. The investment is accounted for at fair value with unrealized and realized gains/losses flowing through other income (expense).

### Financial Performance

As of June 28, 2026, Illumina held $88 million in GRAIL equity, down from $215 million at December 28, 2025. YTD 2026 results:
- Net losses recognized: $(23) million (realized and unrealized)
- Realized losses on $104 million of shares sold
- Unrealized losses on remaining holdings

This contrasted sharply with YTD 2025, when Illumina recognized a $128 million net gain.

### Contingent Value Rights Obligations

In the original GRAIL acquisition agreement, Illumina issued Contingent Value Rights (CVRs) that entitle holders to quarterly cash payments based on GRAIL-related revenues:
- **Rate:** 2.5% of the first $1 billion in annual Covered Revenues for 12 years (through August 2033)
- **Above $1B annually:** 9% of revenues above $1 billion during the same 12-year period

**Recent Payments:**
- Q4 2025 – Q1 2026: Covered Revenues of $84 million generated CVR payments of $794,000
- Q4 2024 – Q1 2025: Covered Revenues of $70 million generated CVR payments of $660,000

The increase in covered revenues (+$14M or +20% YoY) was "driven primarily by sales of GRAIL's Galleri test," a cfDNA-based cancer screening assay.

**Liability Valuation:**
- CVR liability as of June 28, 2026: $40 million (down from $54 million at December 28, 2025)
- $39 million in other long-term liabilities; $1 million in current liabilities
- Fair value estimated using Monte Carlo simulation with Level 3 inputs (forecasted GRAIL revenues through August 2033, revenue risk premium of 3%, revenue volatility, operational leverage, counterparty credit spread)
- Estimates derived from GRAIL's public market capitalization (using 60-day trailing average, CAPM, and comparable company betas) and analyst reports

## Risk Factors and Management Discussion

### Tariff and Trade Impacts

Effective April 2025, the U.S. and other countries imposed tariffs. Illumina's largest exposure is importation from its Singapore manufacturing facility. The company also faces tariffs on parts and sub-assemblies imported into its U.S. operations and on imports into China.

In February 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act does not authorize tariff imposition. In March 2026, the U.S. Court of International Trade ordered the U.S. Customs and Border Protection to process refunds of IEEPA-related tariffs, implementing them in phases. Illumina has incurred "significant costs" under IEEPA tariffs and intends to seek refunds, having begun the process. However, **"the timing and amount of recoveries remain uncertain and will depend on the scope and timing of future phases, any further court or administrative developments, and completion of applicable administrative steps."** To date, recoveries recognized have been immaterial.

Management stated mitigation actions include "supply-chain optimization, cost measures, and pricing actions" and aims to "continue such efforts in the future."

### China Operations and Regulatory Risk

Illumina disclosed that it was placed on China's "unreliable entities list" by regulatory authorities. This designation contributed to a 20% decline in Greater China revenue YTD 2026 ($108M vs. $135M) and a 7 million negative impact in Q2 2026. The company identified **"uncertainty regarding the impact of our inclusion on the 'unreliable entities list' by regulatory authorities in China"** as a material forward-looking risk.

### Macroeconomic and Operational Headwinds

Management cited continued headwinds from "macroeconomic factors such as tariffs, inflation, exchange rate fluctuations and supply-chain disruptions, and concerns about an economic downturn." Additionally, "in 2026, we have experienced higher fuel, energy, and shipping costs related to the conflict and instability in the Middle East and Iran and higher costs associated with memory chips used in our products, both of which have negatively impacted gross margin in 2026."

### U.S. Government Funding Risk

Management identified as a risk factor "any reductions or potential reductions in funding for the National Institutes of Health (NIH), or targeted cancellations by the U.S. federal government of certain grants or contracts that could negatively impact our customers and reduce demand for our products and services." In Q1 2025, Illumina cited uncertainty about NIH funding as one reason for implementing an incremental cost reduction initiative.

### ERP System Implementation

In Q3 2025, Illumina began implementation of a global enterprise resource planning (ERP) system upgrade, expected to be operational in H1 2027. Management acknowledged that process changes could "materially affect our internal controls over financial reporting" and committed to monitoring and reporting impacts as they occur.

## Derivative Instruments and Hedging

Illumina uses foreign exchange forward contracts to manage currency exposure:
- **For cash flow hedges** (designated hedges of forecasted revenues): Up to 24-month terms, with gains/losses recognized in accumulated other comprehensive income and reclassified to revenue when the hedged transaction occurs.
- **For operating exposures** (non-designated hedges): One-month or less terms, with fair value changes recognized directly in other income (expense).

As of June 28, 2026:
- Total notional value of non-designated forwards: $415 million (principal currencies: euro, yen, Australian dollar, Canadian dollar, Singapore dollar, Chinese yuan, British pound)
- Total notional value of cash flow hedge forwards: $723 million (principal currencies: euro, yen, Australian dollar, Canadian dollar, Chinese yuan)

**YTD 2026 cash flow hedge results:**
- Losses reclassified to revenue: $6 million
- Expected reclassification from accumulated other comprehensive income in next 12 months: $6 million net gain

As of June 28, 2026, fair value of all forward contracts: $14 million in assets, $5 million in liabilities.
