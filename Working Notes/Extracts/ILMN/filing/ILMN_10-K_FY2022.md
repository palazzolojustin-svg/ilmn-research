# Illumina, Inc. Form 10-K for Fiscal Year Ended January 1, 2023

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-K_FY2022.md` · Date: 2023-02-17 · SEC filing; auditor Ernst & Young LLP · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina's annual report for fiscal 2022 shows revenue of $4.58 billion (up 1% from $4.53 billion), but the company reported a net loss of $4.4 billion (vs. income of $762 million) due to a $3.91 billion goodwill impairment on its GRAIL acquisition, a $458 million accrual for an expected European Commission fine for completing the GRAIL acquisition in breach of merger review, and a $145 million loss from settlement with BGI. The acquisition of GRAIL—a multi-cancer early detection company—remains subject to regulatory prohibition by the European Commission and ongoing litigation, with GRAIL required to operate separately. Core Illumina operating income of $481 million offset GRAIL's $4.66 billion operating loss. Cash and investments totaled $2.04 billion; the company has no material customer concentration and derives 50% of revenue from international markets.

## Revenue

### Total Revenue and Growth

Consolidated revenue increased marginally to $4.584 billion in fiscal 2022, up just $58 million or 1% from $4.526 billion in fiscal 2021. This represents a substantial deceleration from prior growth, reflecting macroeconomic headwinds, customer inventory management, and deferral of capital purchases ahead of new product launches [MD&A Overview].

Core Illumina revenue grew $34 million (1%) to $4.553 billion, while GRAIL (acquired August 18, 2021) contributed $55 million in revenue in 2022 compared to $12 million in the partial-year 2021 period, resulting in $24 million of inter-company eliminations [Segment Information].

### Product and Service Mix

**Sequencing dominates the portfolio.** In 2022, sequencing-related revenue (consumables, instruments, and services combined) totaled $4.171 billion, or 91% of consolidated revenue, consistent with 91% in 2021 and 89% in 2020. Microarray revenue (consumables, instruments, and services) represented $413 million, or 9% of revenue.

**Consumables comprised the largest category** at $3.225 billion, or 70% of total revenue. Within this:
- Sequencing consumables: $2.919 billion (up from $2.911 billion in 2021), representing $26 million or 1% growth. This increase was driven by growth in the installed base of sequencing instruments, partially offset by challenging macroeconomic factors that constrained customer spending [Segment/Revenue Discussion].
- Microarray consumables: $306 million (unchanged from 2021).

**Instruments represented** $728 million, or 16% of revenue, essentially flat with $751 million in 2021 (a decrease of $23 million or 3%). The decline was driven by Core Illumina sequencing instruments revenue falling $26 million due to reduced shipments of high-throughput instruments as customers deferred capital purchases in response to macro uncertainty and anticipated the availability of the NovaSeq X series in 2023. This was partially offset by increased shipments of NextSeq 1000/2000 mid-throughput instruments [Results of Operations - Revenue].

**Services and other revenue totaled** $631 million, or 14% of revenue, up from $558 million in 2021. Core Illumina service revenue increased $32 million (6%), driven by higher extended maintenance service contracts and sequencing services revenue, partially offset by a decrease related to 2021 patent litigation settlement revenue. GRAIL service revenue of $55 million in 2022 (vs. $12 million in 2021) relates primarily to commercial sales of the Galleri multi-cancer early detection blood test [Results of Operations - Revenue].

### Geographic Distribution

International markets remain material. Shipments to customers outside the United States represented 50% of total revenue in 2022 ($2.294 billion), compared to 52% in 2021 and 49% in 2020. This reflects both the company's global presence and the importance of overseas research and clinical markets.

By region, 2022 revenue was distributed as follows [Revenue by Geographic Area]:
- **Americas: $2.479 billion (54% of total)**, down from $2.358 billion in 2021. Within this region, US revenue was $2.290 billion (50% of total), up from $2.195 billion (48%) in 2021. The US increase reflects underlying Illumina growth offset by customer conservation.
- **Europe, Middle East & Africa: $1.215 billion (26%)**, down from $1.289 billion (28%) in 2021.
- **Greater China (China, Taiwan, Hong Kong): $472 million (10%)**, down from $502 million (11%) in 2021.
- **Asia-Pacific: $418 million (9%)**, up from $377 million (8%) in 2021.

Core Illumina revenue was adversely impacted by $84 million due to unfavorable foreign exchange rate fluctuations in 2022, partially offset by $53 million of favorable FX reclassified to revenue from cash flow hedges [Results of Operations - Revenue].

## Gross Profit and Margins

### Overall Gross Profit

Consolidated gross profit declined $182 million (6%) to $2.972 billion in 2022 from $3.154 billion in 2021. Gross margin compressed to 64.8% from 69.7%, a decline of 490 basis points. This deterioration reflects both Core Illumina margin pressure and the impact of GRAIL's gross losses.

**Core Illumina gross profit decreased** $88 million (3%) to $3.107 billion, with gross margin declining to 68.2% from 70.7%. The margin compression was driven by [Results of Operations - Gross Margin]:
- Less fixed cost leverage on lower manufacturing volumes.
- Higher freight costs.
- These unfavorable factors were partially offset by favorable product mix.
- An additional headwind was the 2021 inclusion of a patent litigation settlement revenue that had no corresponding cost of revenue.

**GRAIL gross loss widened** to $(117) million in 2022 from $(41) million in 2021. The $76 million deterioration was primarily attributable to amortization of intangible assets of $134 million in 2022 (vs. $45 million in 2021). GRAIL's Galleri test, being commercially launched, has not yet achieved gross profitability [Results of Operations - Gross Margin].

## Operating Expenses

### Research and Development

Consolidated R&D expense increased $136 million (11%) to $1.321 billion in 2022 from $1.185 million in 2021. This represented 28.8% of revenue, compared to 26.2% in 2021.

**Core Illumina R&D** rose $119 million (13%) to $1.004 billion. This increase was primarily driven by increased headcount to support research and development of new products and enhancements to existing products. The company also incurred restructuring charges of $6 million in Q4 2022, consisting primarily of employee severance costs. These increases were partially offset by a decrease in licensing fees related to co-development agreements and lower performance-based compensation [Results of Operations - Operating Expense].

**GRAIL R&D** increased $30 million (10%) to $330 million. Expenses relate to headcount, performance-based compensation, and clinical trials supporting the Galleri test and development of additional products. In 2021, GRAIL's R&D of $300 million included $167 million of accelerated share-based compensation expense related to equity award acceleration as part of the acquisition, plus transaction-related costs [Results of Operations - Operating Expense].

### Selling, General and Administrative

Consolidated SG&A expense decreased $795 million (38%) to $1.297 billion from $2.092 billion in 2021, representing 28.3% and 46.2% of revenue, respectively. The decrease, despite integration expenses continuing, reflects the absence of one-time acquisition-related charges and changes in contingent consideration valuations.

**Core Illumina SG&A** decreased $499 million (33%) to $1.003 billion. Primary drivers included [Results of Operations - Operating Expense]:
- Decrease in fair value of contingent consideration liabilities of $205 million, primarily related to the GRAIL acquisition (with reduced probability of achieving revenue targets).
- Decrease in expenses related to the GRAIL acquisition, which in 2021 included $245 million in monthly Continuation Payments made to GRAIL through the acquisition close. Post-close, no further Continuation Payments were required.
- Decrease in performance-based compensation.
- These favorable items were offset by increased headcount and restructuring charges of $24 million in Q4 2022, consisting primarily of employee severance costs and a lease impairment charge.

**GRAIL SG&A** decreased $294 million (50%) to $296 million, primarily because 2021 included $448 million of accelerated share-based compensation expense from equity award acceleration and other transaction costs related to the acquisition.

### Legal Contingency and Settlement

**Core Illumina recorded a legal contingency and settlement charge of $619 million in 2022** (compared to $0 in 2021), consisting of:

**European Commission Fine Accrual: $458 million.** As a result of Illumina's decision to complete the GRAIL acquisition during the pendency of the European Commission's review (in breach of the EU Merger Regulation), the company expects the EC will seek to impose a fine. On July 19, 2022, the EC issued a Statement of Objections alleging breach. Pursuant to ASC 450 (Contingencies), the company accrued $458 million, representing 10% of its consolidated annual revenues for fiscal year 2022 ($4.584 billion), which is the maximum penalty under Article 14(2)(b) of the EU Merger Regulation. This accrual reflects the company's assessment of the probable outcome, though the final EC decision on the fine has not yet been issued [Legal Proceedings].

**BGI Litigation Settlement: $145 million net loss.** On July 14, 2022, Illumina entered into a Settlement and License Agreement with BGI Genomics and Complete Genomics, Inc., resolving all claims in several patent infringement and antitrust litigations that had been ongoing since June 2019. The company agreed to pay CGI $325 million one-time consideration. The company capitalized $180 million of this payment as an intangible asset (licensed technology) to be amortized over 6.5 years, allocated $150 million to release of past damages claimed, and recorded a $5 million gain on damages awarded to the company. The net effect was a $145 million loss in Q3 2022. In return, the company received fully paid-up licenses to BGI's 2-channel technology patents (with no additional royalties required) and provided reciprocal licenses to its image mix patents. A litigation standstill for patent and antitrust actions in the US is in place until October 1, 2025, except for NIPT and multi-cancer early detection IP [Legal Proceedings - BGI Genomics].

### Goodwill Impairment

**GRAIL goodwill impairment charge of $3.914 billion** was recorded in Q3 2022 (compared to $0 in 2021), representing a substantial write-down of the value originally assigned to the GRAIL business in the August 2021 acquisition.

**Triggering Events.** The impairment was prompted by several regulatory and market developments that occurred in the three months ended October 2, 2022 [Critical Audit Matter - Goodwill Impairment]:
- On July 13, 2022, the EU General Court ruled that the European Commission has jurisdiction to review the GRAIL acquisition under the EU Merger Regulation.
- On September 6, 2022, the EC issued its Prohibition Decision, finding the acquisition to be incompatible with the internal market due to a significant impediment to effective competition.
- Additionally, Illumina's stock price and market capitalization declined substantially during this period, falling from highs in excess of $400 per share in mid-2022 to below $200 by the end of the quarter.

**Fair Value Assessment.** Management performed an interim goodwill impairment test using both income and market-based approaches. For the GRAIL reporting unit, the income approach used a discounted cash flow model with management's projections of GRAIL revenues. In the annual Q2 2022 impairment test, management had used a discount rate of 16.0% and determined fair value exceeded carrying value by $700 million. In the interim Q3 test, the discount rate was increased to 22.0%, reflecting the heightened uncertainty and regulatory risk. This materially reduced the estimated fair value. Comparisons to market multiples of comparable companies also indicated reduced valuations. As a result of the analysis, carrying value was found to exceed fair value by the impairment amount [Legal Proceedings and Goodwill Impairment Assessment].

**Post-Impairment Carrying Value.** The goodwill impairment reduced total goodwill from $7.113 billion (Jan 2, 2022) to $3.239 billion (Jan 1, 2023), with GRAIL goodwill declining to $2.178 billion. The company noted that after the impairment charge, the carrying value of GRAIL approximates its fair value, and therefore GRAIL remains at elevated risk of further impairment if operating results, stock price, or the regulatory environment change [Goodwill - Impairment Assessment].

## Income (Loss) from Operations

Loss from operations deteriorated sharply to $(4.179) billion in 2022 from $(123) million in 2021. This $4.056 billion swing was driven primarily by:
- Goodwill impairment of $3.914 billion.
- Legal contingency and settlement charges of $619 million.
- Gross profit decrease of $182 million.
- Partially offset by SG&A decrease of $795 million.
- And R&D increase of $136 million.

Core Illumina operating income declined to $481 million from $808 million, while GRAIL operating loss widened dramatically to $(4.657) billion from $(931) million.

## Income Taxes

### Pre-Tax Income (Loss)

Loss before income taxes was $(4.336) billion in 2022, compared to income of $884 million in 2021 and $856 million in 2020.

### Provision for Income Taxes

The company recorded a tax provision of $68 million in 2022, compared to $122 million in 2021 and $200 million in 2020. The effective tax rate was (1.6)% in 2022, compared to 13.8% in 2021. This unusually low (and negative) rate reflects the company's large pre-tax loss combined with several significant non-deductible expenses.

**Variance from 21% federal statutory rate in 2022** [Income Taxes - Provision Reconciliation]:
- **Goodwill impairment (non-deductible): $822 million tax impact.** The goodwill impairment charge of $3.914 billion is not deductible for federal income tax purposes, increasing the effective rate.
- **Potential EC fine (non-deductible): $96 million tax impact.** The $458 million accrual for the anticipated EC fine is also non-deductible.
- **R&D expense capitalization: $87 million tax impact.** Effective January 1, 2022, the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 requires the company to capitalize and amortize R&D expenses over 5 years rather than deduct them immediately. This creates a timing difference reducing current deductions.
- **GRAIL pre-acquisition NOLs and foreign tax credits: $60 million tax impact.** The company's tax liability is affected by GRAIL's pre-acquisition net operating losses, particularly in relation to GILTI (Global Intangible Low-Taxed Income) and utilization of foreign tax credits.
- **Mix of foreign operations: favorable offset.** The company benefited from the mix of earnings in lower-rate jurisdictions: Singapore (17% statutory rate) and the United Kingdom (19% statutory rate).

## GRAIL Acquisition, Contingent Consideration, and Regulatory Status

### Acquisition Overview

On August 18, 2021, Illumina acquired GRAIL, a private healthcare company focused on the development of blood-based tests for early detection of multiple cancers, for a total purchase price of $9.745 billion (as adjusted for measurement period adjustments) [Acquisitions - GRAIL Acquisition].

**Consideration Structure** [GRAIL Acquisition]:
- Cash: $2.862 billion.
- Fair value of common stock issued: $4.975 billion (9.8 million shares at acquisition-date fair value of $510.61 per share).
- Fair value of contingent consideration (contingent value rights): $757 million.
- Fair value of previously held investment: $1.149 billion (the company remeasured its pre-existing 12% equity interest at fair value, recording a gain of $899 million).
- Settlement of preexisting relationships: $2 million.

The transaction was subject to regulatory review in the United States (by the FTC) and European Union (by the European Commission), as well as various state and foreign regulatory approvals.

### GRAIL Business and Products

GRAIL's primary product, **Galleri**, is a blood-based multi-cancer early detection (MCED) test designed to detect various types of cancers before they become symptomatic. The test was commercially launched in 2021 as a laboratory-developed test (LDT) through CLIA-certified, CAP-accredited laboratories. GRAIL is also developing solutions for:
- Cancer diagnosis acceleration.
- Minimal residual disease detection (post-treatment monitoring).
- Other post-diagnostic applications.

### Contingent Consideration Arrangement

GRAIL stockholders who elected to receive contingent value rights (rather than additional shares or cash) are entitled to receive Covered Revenue Payments for 12 years commencing with the acquisition date. The payment structure is [GRAIL Acquisition - Contingent Consideration]:
- **2.5% of the first $1.0 billion** of annual Covered Revenues (primarily Galleri-related revenues).
- **9% of revenues above $1.0 billion** annually.

The fair value of the contingent consideration liability was $412 million as of January 1, 2023 (down from $615 million at Jan 2, 2022). The $203 million favorable change in fair value reflects management's revised (downward) assessment of GRAIL's future revenue trajectory and increased discount rates. The liability is remeasured quarterly using a Monte Carlo simulation model.

### Regulatory Status and Legal Proceedings

#### U.S. FTC Action

The FTC filed an administrative complaint on March 30, 2021 alleging that the acquisition would violate Section 7 of the Clayton Act by creating a significant impediment to effective competition in the putative market for multi-cancer early detection (MCED) tests. An administrative trial occurred from August 24, 2021 onward [Legal Proceedings - Acquisition of GRAIL].

**September 1, 2022 ALJ Decision:** The administrative law judge (ALJ) ruled in favor of Illumina, finding that the FTC complaint counsel had failed to prove its prima facie case that the acquisition would harm competition in the MCED market. The ALJ concluded that Illumina and GRAIL are not direct competitors in a properly-defined MCED market and that the acquisition would not cause competitive harm.

**FTC Appeal:** On September 2, 2022, FTC complaint counsel appealed the ALJ decision to the full FTC. The appeal was fully briefed as of November 10, 2022, with oral argument occurring December 13, 2022. A decision from the full FTC is pending. Illumina intends to vigorously defend against the FTC action.

#### European Commission Action

The regulatory proceedings in the European Union have been more challenging for Illumina.

**April 19, 2021 - EC Assumes Jurisdiction:** The European Commission accepted a referral from a Member State (France, joined by Belgium, Greece, Iceland, the Netherlands, and Norway) for EU-wide merger review under Article 22 of the EU Merger Regulation.

**April 29, 2021 - Illumina Challenges Jurisdiction:** Illumina filed an action in the General Court of the European Union challenging the EC's assertion of jurisdiction, arguing the transaction does not meet jurisdictional thresholds under EU Merger Regulation or national laws.

**July 13, 2022 - EU General Court Rules Against Illumina:** The EU General Court upheld the European Commission's jurisdiction, holding that the acquisition falls within the EC's authority to review under the EU Merger Regulation. Illumina appealed to the Court of Justice of the European Union on September 22, 2022.

**September 6, 2022 - Prohibition Decision:** The European Commission completed its Phase II review and issued the **Prohibition Decision**, finding that Illumina's acquisition of GRAIL would be incompatible with the internal market in Europe because it results in "a significant impediment to effective competition." This was the ultimate unfavorable outcome for Illumina. On November 17, 2022, Illumina filed an action with the EU General Court seeking annulment of the Prohibition Decision.

**December 5, 2022 - Statement of Objections (Divestment Order):** The European Commission issued a Statement of Objections informing Illumina of its intention to adopt a **Divestment Decision requiring Illumina to divest GRAIL**. The timing and specific terms of the Divestment Decision have not been announced. Illumina filed a response to the Statement of Objections on January 16, 2023 and intends to appeal any Divestment Decision and, if necessary, seek interim relief.

#### Interim Measures Orders (Hold Separate)

On October 29, 2021, the European Commission adopted an **Initial Interim Measures Order** requiring that [Legal Proceedings]:
- Illumina and GRAIL operate as independent legal entities transacting at arm's length.
- No integration activities occur.
- Day-to-day operations of GRAIL remain the sole responsibility of GRAIL management, with no involvement or influence by Illumina management.
- Necessary supportive measures be taken to preserve GRAIL's viability, marketability, and competitiveness.
- Confidential information sharing between Illumina and GRAIL be restricted.
- An independent monitoring trustee be appointed to monitor compliance.

As this order was set to expire November 3, 2022, the European Commission adopted the **New Interim Measures Order** on October 28, 2022, which [Legal Proceedings]:
- Renews Illumina's hold-separate obligations with certain operational modifications.
- **Prohibits Illumina from selling, transferring, encumbering, or otherwise disposing of GRAIL or GRAIL's assets**, effectively requiring Illumina to retain the asset pending final resolution.

On December 1, 2021, Illumina filed an action seeking annulment of the Initial Interim Measures Order, though the hearing was stayed pending resolution of the jurisdiction challenge. On January 10, 2023, Illumina filed an action seeking annulment of the New Interim Measures Order.

The hold-separate arrangement has imposed burdensome administrative processes and costs, including payments to the independent monitoring trustee. Management expects these burdens to continue for the duration of the hold-separate period. These administrative constraints and costs, combined with uncertainty, have had and may continue to have adverse effects on GRAIL's ability to operate efficiently and achieve its growth objectives [Management's Discussion & Analysis - Forward-Looking Statements].

#### Expected European Commission Fine

On July 19, 2022, the European Commission issued a Statement of Objections alleging that Illumina **breached the EU Merger Regulation by completing the acquisition in violation of the standstill obligation** (the prohibition on implementing a merger during the EC's review period). As a result, the EC is expected to seek a fine under Article 14(2)(b) of the EU Merger Regulation of **up to 10% of consolidated annual revenues**.

For fiscal 2022, 10% of revenues of $4.584 billion equals $458 million. As of January 1, 2023, Illumina accrued the full $458 million in accrued liabilities, reflecting its assessment that a fine at or near this maximum is probable [Accrued Liabilities and Results of Operations - Legal Contingency].

This represents a highly significant potential liability. While the company disputes the allegations and intends to defend vigorously, the amount accrued reflects ASC 450 guidance that when a range of loss is probable and an amount within that range can be reasonably estimated, the accrual should be at the lower end of the range—though management's assessment is that the upper end ($458 million) is more likely than not to be the outcome.

#### Contingent Effects on Business Operations

The regulatory uncertainties have created substantial business risks and constraints:
- **Hold Separate Impediments:** The prohibition on integration and required operating independence limits the company's ability to achieve expected synergies (particularly in informatics, R&D, and commercial operations).
- **Customer and Supplier Uncertainty:** During the pendency of regulatory proceedings, customers, suppliers, and partners may delay purchasing decisions, defer adoption, or seek to renegotiate contracts, particularly given uncertainty about GRAIL's ultimate ownership or disposition.
- **Employee Retention:** Key employees of both Illumina and GRAIL may depart due to uncertainty about the future of the combined entity.
- **Divestiture Risks:** If required to divest GRAIL, Illumina would likely face:
  - Difficulty achieving favorable valuation terms (given that a buyer would know Illumina was forced to sell).
  - Potential tax consequences of non-taxable treatment not being available, resulting in recognition of taxable gain (estimated $500M-$1B in tax basis).
  - Complications relating to contingent value rights obligations, which may be difficult to satisfy post-divestiture or may result in a change of obligor.
  - Management distraction and transaction costs.

Management states it cannot predict what other adverse consequences may result from the decision to proceed with the acquisition, including impacts on reputation and future ability to complete acquisitions or divestitures [Forward-Looking Statements].

## BGI Litigation Settlement

On July 14, 2022, Illumina and BGI Genomics Co., Ltd., along with Complete Genomics, Inc. (CGI), entered into a **Settlement and License Agreement** resolving all patent infringement and antitrust litigation that had been pending since June 2019 [Legal Proceedings - BGI Genomics].

**Settlement Terms:**
- **One-time payment by Illumina to CGI: $325 million**, with a determination that the aggregate judgments in the prior litigations are satisfied in full.
- **License grants:**
  - Illumina received a fully paid-up license to BGI's **2-channel technology patents** (US Patents 8,617,811, 9,222,132, 9,523,125, 10,662,473, 11,098,356, and 11,214,832, along with related patents and applications) with no additional royalties required.
  - BGI received a reciprocal fully paid-up license to Illumina's **image mix patents** (US Patents 9,217,178, 9,303,290, and 9,970,055, along with related patents and applications).

**Litigation Standstill:** The parties agreed to a standstill provision preventing patent and antitrust actions in the US and its territories until October 1, 2025. **Carve-outs:** The standstill does not apply to NIPT (noninvasive prenatal testing) patents or IP related to multi-cancer early detection, allowing both parties to continue developing and protecting their respective technologies in these key areas.

**No Admission of Liability:** Neither party admits liability in entering into the settlement.

**Accounting Treatment:** The $325 million payment was allocated on a relative fair value basis [Results of Operations - Legal Contingency]:
- **$180 million capitalized as an intangible asset** for the 2-channel technology license (amortized over 6.5 years at ~$28 million annually).
- **$150 million** for release of past damages claimed (expensed).
- **$5 million gain** on damages awarded to Illumina (income effect).
- Net effect: $145 million loss in Q3 2022.

The 2-channel technology acquired is a sequencing-by-synthesis chemistry relevant to Illumina's ongoing product development, so the capitalized amount is expected to provide benefits over future periods.

## Risk Factors - Newly Disclosed or Significantly Changed

The company identifies extensive risk factors in its Form 10-K. Among the most material newly disclosed or significantly changed risks [Risk Factors]:

### GRAIL Acquisition Risks (Extensively Updated)

- **Regulatory prohibition and divestiture requirement:** The EC's Prohibition Decision and expected Divestment Decision create substantial risks of forced divestiture on unfavorable terms, with implications for shareholder value, tax consequences (estimated $500M-$1B in unrecognized gains), contingent value rights management, and business disruption.
- **Hold-separate burdens:** The interim measures order has created burdensome administrative processes and costs with significant financial and operational impacts.
- **Expected fine:** The anticipated EC fine of up to 10% of revenues ($458M accrued) is a material potential liability.
- **Uncertainty effects:** Regulatory uncertainty may cause customers, suppliers, partners, and employees to delay or terminate relationships, reducing expected GRAIL benefits and Illumina's overall growth.
- **Market capitalization impact:** The stock price decline and regulatory setbacks may further impair GRAIL's valuation and trigger additional goodwill impairments.
- **Loss of synergies:** Inability to integrate GRAIL's operations may prevent realization of expected cost savings, innovation benefits, and operational efficiencies.

### Macroeconomic and Inflation Risks

- **Customer conservation:** Customers are managing capital purchases and inventory more conservatively in response to economic uncertainty, inflation concerns, and potential downturn risks.
- **Input cost inflation:** Supply costs and freight costs have increased materially, and if the company cannot pass these costs to customers, margins may face further pressure.
- **Interest rate risk:** The company has a variable-rate credit facility, and future borrowing costs may increase as rates rise. (Though no amounts are currently drawn.)

### Russia-Ukraine Armed Conflict Risks

- **Sanctions and export restrictions:** The US and other countries have imposed sanctions on Russia and export restrictions, affecting Illumina's ability to ship products into the region and collect receivables.
- **Spillover effects:** Civil, political, and economic disturbance in surrounding areas may affect sales and operations.
- **Uncertain impact:** While currently not expected to be material, the broader economic impact of the conflict is unknown.

### COVID-19 Pandemic Ongoing Effects

- **Continued operational impacts:** Movement restrictions, supply chain disruptions, and workforce impacts may continue to affect demand, sales operations, customer support, and R&D.
- **Remote work arrangements:** Continued remote work may impact employee productivity and morale.
- **Volatile capital markets:** Economic volatility resulting from COVID-19 may affect the fair value of the company's marketable securities and strategic investments.

### Regulatory and Approval Risks

- **FDA regulation of diagnostics:** As the company expands into clinical diagnostic markets, products may face FDA clearance requirements (510(k) or PMA processes) that are costly and time-consuming. The specific regulatory pathways for novel products like Galleri are not well-established, creating uncertainty.
- **LDT regulation:** The FDA has historically exercised enforcement discretion not to regulate laboratory-developed tests, but has indicated it may change this policy. Such changes could require regulatory approval for CLIA-certified products currently marketed as LDTs.
- **IVDR in Europe:** EU IVDR requirements (effective May 2022) impose performance and quality system requirements on in vitro diagnostic products.

### Competitive and Market Risks

- **Technology obsolescence:** Competitors may develop superior or less expensive alternatives, rendering Illumina's products uncompetitive. The company's competitive position depends on continuous innovation and first-mover advantages.
- **Pricing pressure:** Reimbursement constraints and customer cost sensitivity may limit pricing flexibility.
- **Large competitor advantages:** Some competitors have greater financial, technical, and market resources, as well as larger installed bases and customer relationships.

### Intellectual Property Risks

- **Patent expirations:** As Illumina's foundational patents expire (sequencing-by-synthesis technology patents expire at various dates through 2043), competitors may enter and erode market position.
- **Patent disputes:** The RavGen litigation (now apparently concluding with unfavorable PTAB decisions) exemplifies ongoing patent litigation risks that could block product sales or require costly royalty obligations.
