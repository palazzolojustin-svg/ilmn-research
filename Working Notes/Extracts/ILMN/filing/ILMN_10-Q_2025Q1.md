# Illumina Q1 2025 earnings (Form 10-Q)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-Q_2025Q1.md` · Date: 2025-03-30 · SEC Form 10-Q filing, signed May 8, 2025 · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina's first-quarter 2025 results show stabilization after the GRAIL spin-off (completed June 2024): revenue declined 3% to $1,041 million, but operating income swung to +$164 million from a loss, driven by lower operating expenses and margin expansion. The company faces headwinds from China regulatory action (inclusion on unreliable entities list, sequencing instrument export ban), tariff impacts (~$85 million estimated), and NIH funding uncertainty; management launched a $100 million cost reduction program for 2025 to offset these pressures and mitigate Greater China revenue loss.

## Business Overview

Illumina is a provider of sequencing and array-based genetic analysis solutions serving the research, clinical, and applied markets. Its customers include genomic research centers, academic institutions, government laboratories, hospitals, pharmaceutical companies, biotech firms, molecular diagnostic labs, and consumer genomics companies. The company offers products for life sciences, oncology, reproductive health, agriculture, and emerging segments [10-Q, Q1 2025].

## GRAIL Separation (Completed Q2 2024)

On June 24, 2024, Illumina completed the spin-off of GRAIL into an independent publicly traded company, distributing approximately 85.5% of GRAIL's outstanding shares to Illumina shareholders on a pro-rata basis (one GRAIL share per six Illumina shares held as of the June 13, 2024 record date). Illumina retained 14.5% of GRAIL's shares post-spin-off. The separation was required by the European Commission's October 2023 divestment decision [Q1 2025, 10-Q]. 

The European Commission's jurisdictional assertion and divestment order were subsequently annulled: on September 3, 2024, the EU Court of Justice ruled in Illumina's favor, confirming the European Commission had unlawfully asserted jurisdiction. On September 6, 2024, the Commission withdrew all prior decisions, including its €432 million fine (which accrued 5.5% annual interest while outstanding). Illumina recognized a net gain of $481 million in Q3 2024 from the fine reversal: $489 million gain in operating expense offset by an $8 million loss in other income from foreign currency revaluation [10-Q, Q1 2025]. On August 15, 2024, the U.S. Federal Trade Commission dismissed its administrative complaint in light of the spin-off completion [10-Q].

Post-spin-off, Illumina reports one segment: Core Illumina. GRAIL's results are no longer consolidated in Illumina's Q1 2025 and Q1 2024 comparisons reflect GRAIL's operations only through June 24, 2024 in Q2 2024 results (not presented in this quarterly filing). For Q1 2025, there is no GRAIL revenue.

## Revenue Analysis

### Total Revenue and Trend

**Q1 2025 total revenue: $1,041 million, down 3% versus Q1 2024 ($1,076 million).** The decline reflects the absence of GRAIL revenue (Q1 2024 included $27 million from GRAIL before the June 2024 spin-off) and a Core Illumina service and other revenue decrease of $12 million [10-Q].

**Remaining performance obligation (backlog): $891 million as of March 30, 2025.** Of this, approximately 83% is expected to be recognized within 12 months, 7% in months 13–24, and the remainder thereafter. This represents contractually committed but not yet recognized revenue and indicates the timing of customer shipments and service delivery [10-Q].

### Core Illumina Product Revenue

**Q1 2025 product revenue: $880 million, flat versus Q1 2024 ($876 million).**

**Sequencing consumables (the reagent kits customers purchase for each sequencing run): $696 million, up slightly from $691 million.** The increase was driven by higher NovaSeq X consumables volume (the company's latest high-throughput sequencer, launched in late 2023). This gain was partially offset by decreases in consumables for other high-throughput and mid-throughput instruments as customers transition to NovaSeq X. The company notes intercompany consumables revenue was higher in Q1 2024 (prior to GRAIL spin-off) but this intercompany elimination is now absent [10-Q].

**Microarray consumables: $72 million, essentially flat ($71 million).** Microarray is a lower-throughput technology for gene expression and copy number analysis; it remains a smaller revenue component [10-Q].

**Total sequencing + microarray consumables: $768 million versus $762 million, a $6 million or 1% increase [10-Q].**

**Instruments revenue: $112 million, down 2% from $114 million.** Sequencing instruments contributed $109 million (versus $110 million); microarray instruments contributed $3 million (versus $4 million). The company shipped more MiSeq i100 Series units (launched Q4 2024) and NovaSeq X units, but this was outweighed by fewer shipments of other high- and mid-throughput instruments, reflecting customer capital and cash flow constraints [10-Q].

### Geographic Revenue Distribution

**Q1 2025 geographic revenue [in millions]:**
- **Americas: $570 million (54.8% of total), down from $603 million in Q1 2024 (56.1%)**—a $33 million or 5% decrease. This is the largest market [10-Q].
- **Europe: $293 million (28.1%), up from $279 million (26.0%)**—a $14 million or 5% increase [10-Q].
- **Greater China (China, Taiwan, Hong Kong combined): $72 million (6.9%), down from $78 million (7.3%)**—a $6 million or 8% decrease. This reflects early impact of China regulatory actions announced in early 2025 (see China regulatory section below) [10-Q].
- **Asia-Pacific, Middle East, and Africa: $106 million (10.2%), down from $116 million (10.8%)**—a $10 million or 9% decrease [10-Q].

**Full year 2024 Greater China revenue: $308 million** (disclosed as context for the potential impact of China regulatory restrictions) [10-Q].

### Service and Other Revenue

**Q1 2025 Core Illumina service and other revenue: $161 million, down $12 million or 7% from Q1 2024 ($173 million).** This decline was primarily due to decreased revenue from strategic partnerships (lower-margin development and licensing agreements), and was partially offset by sequencing and instrument service contracts [10-Q].

**Q1 2024 GRAIL service revenue: $27 million** (cancer detection testing, absent in Q1 2025 post-spin-off) [10-Q].

### Revenue Performance Obligations and Contract Liabilities

**Contract assets (revenue recognized in advance of customer billing): $15 million (Q1 2025) versus $16 million (Q4 2024).** These are recorded in prepaid expenses and other current assets [10-Q].

**Contract liabilities (deferred revenue and customer deposits): $329 million (Q1 2025) versus $327 million (Q4 2024).** Of this, $256 million is short-term (recorded in accrued liabilities) and $73 million is long-term (recorded in other long-term liabilities). Revenue recognized in Q1 2025 that was previously deferred totaled $97 million [10-Q].

## Profitability and Margins

### Gross Profit and Margin

**Q1 2025 consolidated gross profit: $683 million (65.6% margin) versus Q1 2024 gross profit of $667 million (62.0% margin).** This represents a 40 basis point increase in gross margin percentage and a $16 million increase in absolute gross profit [10-Q].

**Core Illumina gross margin: 65.6% (Q1 2025) versus 65.7% (Q1 2024), essentially flat.** Management attributes the stability to:

- **Positive factors**: Execution of operational excellence initiatives delivering cost savings and improved manufacturing productivity; and lower-margin strategic partnership revenue that was included in the Q1 2024 base [10-Q].

- **Negative factors**: Lower product margins, primarily due to reduced pricing (likely reflecting competitive pressure and customer negotiating leverage); and an increase in field service costs (after-sales support expenses) [10-Q].

**Q1 2024 GRAIL gross loss: $(22) million (negative margin).** GRAIL was pre-revenue on its Galleri cancer detection test and carried high R&D and commercial expenses, resulting in negative gross margins. GRAIL is absent from Q1 2025 [10-Q].

### Cost of Revenue Breakdown

**Q1 2025 total cost of revenue: $358 million (34.4% of revenue) versus Q1 2024 $409 million (38.0% of revenue).** The $51 million decrease reflects both lower absolute costs and the absence of GRAIL [10-Q].

- **Cost of product revenue: $253 million (24.3% of revenue) versus $255 million (23.6%)**. Slight increase in cost percentage despite lower volume reflects product mix and pricing pressures [10-Q].
- **Cost of service and other revenue: $88 million (8.5%) versus $106 million (9.9%)**. Decrease reflects lower GRAIL service costs and lower overall service volume [10-Q].
- **Amortization of acquired intangible assets: $17 million versus $48 million**. The substantial $31 million decrease is due to GRAIL no longer being consolidated (GRAIL carried significant intangible assets from acquisitions). Illumina's remaining intangible assets are being amortized at a slower rate [10-Q, Note 3].

## Operating Expenses

**Q1 2025 total consolidated operating expense: $519 million (49.8% of revenue) versus Q1 2024 $778 million (72.3% of revenue).** A $259 million or 33% decrease in absolute expense [10-Q].

### Research and Development (R&D)

**Q1 2025 Core Illumina R&D: $252 million (24.2% of revenue) versus Q1 2024 $241 million + $101 million GRAIL = $342 million total consolidated R&D (31.5% of revenue).** Core Illumina R&D increased $11 million, or 5%, driven by $10 million in incremental employee separation costs as part of 2025 cost reduction initiatives (see Restructuring section below) [10-Q].

The $90 million consolidated decrease (from $342 million to $252 million) reflects the absence of GRAIL's R&D expense post-spin-off [10-Q].

### Selling, General and Administrative (SG&A)

**Q1 2025 Core Illumina SG&A: $267 million (25.6% of revenue) versus Q1 2024 $336 million + $104 million GRAIL = $440 million total consolidated SG&A (40.8% of revenue).** Core Illumina SG&A decreased $69 million, or 21%, driven by [10-Q]:

1. **GRAIL contingent value right (CVR) liability revaluation: $27 million favorable swing** (recognized a $11 million gain in Q1 2025 versus a $16 million loss in Q1 2024). The CVRs entitle GRAIL shareholders to receive quarterly cash payments equal to a pro-rata portion of "Covered Revenues" (defined as GRAIL-related revenues) for 12 years through August 2033, at a rate of 2.5% on revenues up to $1 billion per year and 9% on revenues exceeding $1 billion. In Q1 2025, Illumina paid $360,000 in Covered Revenue Payments based on Q4 2024 Covered Revenues of $38 million (driven primarily by GRAIL's Galleri cancer detection test). The fair value of this liability is estimated using Monte Carlo simulation and is sensitive to GRAIL revenue forecasts and discount rates (see detailed assumptions in Note 3) [10-Q, Note 3].

2. **Acquisition-related costs decrease: $27 million reduction**, primarily legal and transaction expenses associated with the GRAIL acquisition dispute resolution and spin-off, which are now largely complete [10-Q].

3. **Restructuring charges decrease: $17 million reduction**, primarily due to the absence of the Q1 2024 right-of-use asset and leasehold improvement impairments ($18 million and $14 million, respectively) recognized on the Foster City campus and San Diego property [10-Q].

The $173 million consolidated decrease (from $440 million to $267 million) reflects the absence of GRAIL's SG&A post-spin-off [10-Q].

## Operating Income

**Q1 2025 income from operations: $164 million (15.8% of revenue) versus Q1 2024 loss from operations: $(111) million (-10.3% of revenue).** A $275 million swing in operating performance driven by a $51 million increase in gross profit and a $259 million decrease in operating expense. This represents a significant operational turnaround [10-Q].

### Other Income, Net

**Q1 2025 other income, net: $32 million versus $8 million in Q1 2024.** A $24 million increase, primarily driven by:

- **Net gains on marketable equity securities: $31 million** (recognized in Q1 2025 on securities still held; no securities sold). This reflects mark-to-market gains on equity holdings, likely strategic investments in genomics and life sciences companies [10-Q, Note 3].
- **Net gains on venture capital fund investments: $2 million** (versus $6 million in Q1 2024). Illumina holds equity-method investments in three venture capital funds with total carrying value of $205 million (versus $201 million in Q4 2024) and remaining capital commitments of $92 million callable through December 2034 [10-Q, Note 3].

These gains contributed $33 million of the $32 million net other income figure (the remaining $1 million adjustment relates to foreign exchange and other items) [10-Q].

## Income Taxes

**Q1 2025 income before income taxes: $182 million versus Q1 2024 loss before income taxes: $(109) million [10-Q].**

**Q1 2025 provision for income taxes: $51 million; effective tax rate: 27.9% versus Q1 2024 provision: $17 million; effective tax rate: (15.3%).** The negative rate in Q1 2024 reflects a loss before taxes; the positive rate in Q1 2025 reflects profitability [10-Q].

**Q1 2025 effective rate variance from 21% U.S. federal statutory rate:** The rate was higher than the statutory rate primarily due to a $10 million income tax expense impact from capitalizing R&D expenses for tax purposes (a timing difference where R&D is expensed for financial reporting but capitalized for tax, delaying deductions). This was partially offset by a favorable impact from earnings mix in lower-tax jurisdictions such as Singapore [10-Q].

**Q1 2024 effective rate variance:** The rate was lower than the statutory rate due to the $21 million R&D capitalization tax expense and $18 million expense related to GRAIL pre-acquisition net operating losses on Global Intangible Low-Taxed Income (GILTI) calculations, utilization of foreign tax credits, and Pillar Two global minimum top-up tax. These were partially offset by favorable earnings mix in lower-tax jurisdictions [10-Q].

**Net income: Q1 2025 $131 million (EPS: $0.83 basic, $0.82 diluted) versus Q1 2024 loss of $(126) million (loss per share: $(0.79)).** A $257 million swing in net profitability [10-Q].

## China Regulatory Actions and Impact

### Unreliable Entities List Inclusion

**February 4, 2025:** Illumina was added to China's "List of Unreliable Entities" (UEL) under the Provisions of the List of Unreliable Entities. Potential penalties under the UEL Provisions include monetary fines, restrictions or prohibitions on sale of goods in China, restrictions on import/export, denial of market access investments, denial of entry or work permits for company personnel, and other measures. Illumina states that the duration of listing and ultimate penalties remain uncertain [10-Q].

### Sequencing Instrument Export Ban

**March 4, 2025:** Illumina received notice that it is no longer permitted to export sequencing instruments into China. This is a direct prohibition on a core product category [10-Q].

### Financial Impact

**Greater China 2024 revenue: $308 million** (out of approximately $4.3 billion total annual revenue, or ~7%). The company expects this to decline materially in 2025 [10-Q, Risk Factors].

**Greater China Q1 2025 revenue: $72 million** (down $6 million or 8% from Q1 2024 $78 million), likely reflecting early impact of the regulatory actions announced in February and March 2025 [10-Q].

### Management Guidance

Management has stated that these factors will "continue to have an impact on our sales and results of operations in 2025, the size and duration of which is significantly uncertain." The company is not providing specific guidance on the quantified impact beyond the $100 million 2025 cost reduction plan [10-Q].

## Tariff Impact and Mitigation

### April 2025 Tariffs (Post-Quarter End)

**Announced in April 2025: New U.S. government and international tariffs on cross-border goods movement.** Management estimates the cost impact to Illumina at approximately $85 million, comprised of [10-Q]:
- **Largest component:** Importation from Singapore manufacturing facility
- **Remainder:** Mix of parts/sub-assemblies imported to U.S. manufacturing and China importation

### Mitigation Plan

Management states it is taking "several actions" to mitigate tariff impact, expecting to **partially mitigate in 2025** and **more fully mitigate in 2026** through [10-Q]:
- Supply chain optimization (potential nearshoring or onshoring of manufacturing)
- Cost measures (operational efficiency improvements)
- Pricing actions (pass-through to customers, subject to competitive constraints)

### Context

Singapore is Illumina's primary offshore manufacturing hub. This tariff exposure may accelerate reshoring of production if tariffs remain elevated [10-Q].

## Legal Proceedings and Contingencies

### GRAIL Acquisition Litigation Overview

Multiple shareholder derivative complaints have been filed alleging that Illumina directors breached fiduciary duties by knowingly closing the GRAIL acquisition in violation of the European Commission's prohibition order. These disputes remain in early-stage discovery and pleading phases [10-Q, Note 7].

**Delaware shareholder derivative actions (consolidated):** As of March 27, 2025, four Delaware derivative actions are pending consolidation:
1. _Icahn Partners LP, et al. v. deSouza, et al._ (filed October 17, 2023)
2. _City of Omaha Police and Firefighters Retirement System v. deSouza, et al._ (filed February 26, 2024)
3. _City of Roseville General Employees Retirement System, et al. v. deSouza, et al._ (filed April 16, 2024)
4. _The Pavers and Road Builders Benefit Funds v. deSouza, et al._ (filed December 23, 2024)

A fifth action by _Thomas P. DiNapoli v. John Thompson et al._ (filed August 16, 2024) was voluntarily dismissed on March 14, 2025 [10-Q, Note 7].

All complaints allege breach of fiduciary duty for causing the GRAIL acquisition to close in violation of the European Commission order and seek damages, attorney fees, and equitable relief. Defendants have filed motions to dismiss [10-Q, Note 7].

**Federal securities class actions:** Three securities class action complaints were consolidated in the Southern District of California. Lead plaintiffs (three German investment vehicles) filed a second amended complaint on September 13, 2024, alleging Securities Exchange Act violations (Sections 10(b) and 20(a)) and Rule 10b-5 fraud claims. Defendants' motion to dismiss was filed November 12, 2024; plaintiffs' opposition on December 20, 2024; and defendants' reply on February 3, 2025. No hearing date has been set [10-Q, Note 7].

**State securities class actions (California):** Two cases consolidated in San Mateo County Superior Court alleging fraud in the November 2020 and February 2021 registration statements and prospectuses for the GRAIL acquisition. Defendants filed demurrers on February 28, 2025; plaintiffs' oppositions due April 30, 2025; defendants' replies due May 30, 2025; hearing scheduled for June 20, 2025 [10-Q, Note 7].

### SEC Investigation Conclusion

**May 1, 2025 (post-quarter):** The SEC staff issued a termination letter to Illumina stating it had concluded its investigation into GRAIL, its products, and the acquisition and did not intend to recommend enforcement action. This closes the SEC's civil investigative demand (CID) issued January 18, 2024 [10-Q, Note 7].

### DOJ False Claims Act Investigation

**January 18, 2024:** Illumina received a Department of Justice Civil Investigative Demand under the False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. § 3729) alleging that the company may have submitted false claims to Medicare and other federal programs by misrepresenting compliance with cybersecurity requirements to the FDA and other federal agencies that purchase Illumina devices. Illumina is cooperating; the investigation remains ongoing [10-Q, Note 7].

### Shareholder Litigation Demands

Two shareholders submitted litigation demands to the Board requesting civil actions against GRAIL acquisition-related officers and directors for breach of fiduciary duty and damages:
- **Michael Warner** (May 1, 2024): Board deferred decision on July 30, 2024
- **Jane Davidson** (August 21, 2024): Board deferred decision on October 29, 2024

In both cases, the Board cited pending shareholder lawsuits with overlapping issues and stated it would monitor and revisit the demands as litigation progresses [10-Q, Note 7].

Management acknowledges multiple headwinds affecting 2025 outlook [10-Q]:

### GRAIL Contingent Value Rights

**Fair value of GRAIL CVR liability: $61 million (March 30, 2025) versus $71 million (December 29, 2024).**

**Terms:** Quarterly Covered Revenue Payments (CRP) representing pro-rata portion of GRAIL-related revenues for 12 years through August 2033:
- **2.5% on revenues up to $1 billion per year**
- **9% on revenues above $1 billion per year**

**Q1 2025 payment:** $360,000 in CRPs based on Q4 2024 Covered Revenues of $38 million (driven by Galleri test sales). This implies an annualized Covered Revenue run rate of approximately $152 million based on Q4 2024 results [10-Q, Note 3].

**Fair value estimation:** Uses Monte Carlo simulation with assumptions including:
- Forecasted GRAIL revenues through 2033 (derived from public disclosures and analyst reports post-spin-off)
- Revenue risk premium: 5% (derived by reconciling forecasted revenues to GRAIL's market capitalization; shifted to 10-day trailing average in Q1 2025 from 60-day average due to volatility in early 2025) [10-Q, Note 3]
- Revenue volatility, operational leverage ratio, and counterparty credit spread

**Sensitivity analysis:** A ±20% change in forecasted revenues would impact the liability by ±$15 million; a ±250 basis point change in revenue risk premium would impact by ±$10 million to ±$13 million, respectively [10-Q, Note 3].
