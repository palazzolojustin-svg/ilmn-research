# Illumina Q2 2024 10-Q Quarterly Report — GRAIL Spin-Off, Massive Goodwill Impairment, Revenue Decline

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-Q_2024Q2.md` · Date: 2024-06-30 · SEC filing (Form 10-Q, filed August 7, 2024) · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina completed the spin-off of GRAIL into an independent public company on June 24, 2024, distributing 85.5% of shares to stockholders while retaining a 14.5% stake. The quarter was dominated by a $1.9 billion goodwill and intangible asset impairment charge ($1.5 billion goodwill, $420 million in-process R&D) reflecting the steep decline in GRAIL's valuation following its IPO filing. Core business revenue declined 3–6% year-over-year, driven by sharp drops in sequencing instrument sales, while gross margins improved on better consumables mix and cost savings. The company faces significant ongoing legal and regulatory headwinds, including a €432 million fine imposed by the European Commission (accrued at $481 million with 5.5% annual interest), multiple shareholder lawsuits, SEC investigation, and DOJ civil investigative demand.

## Financial Performance

### Revenue

Consolidated total revenue declined across both the quarter and year-to-date period.

**Q2 2024 vs. Q2 2023**: Revenue fell to $1,112 million from $1,176 million, a 5% decrease. **YTD 2024 vs. YTD 2023**: Revenue declined 3% to $2,188 million from $2,263 million.

Product revenue fell 7% in Q2 to $927 million (Q2 2023: $1,001 million), while service and other revenue rose 3% to $185 million (Q2 2023: $175 million).

**Core Illumina segment** (non-GRAIL operations) generated $1,092 million in Q2 2024 revenue, down 6% from $1,159 million in Q2 2023, and $2,148 million YTD 2024, down 4% from $2,235 million in YTD 2023.

**GRAIL segment** contributed $29 million in Q2 2024 revenue (up 32% from $22 million) and $55 million YTD 2024 (up 31% from $42 million), driven primarily by sales of its Galleri cancer detection test.

### Product Revenue Breakdown

**Consumables** (reagent kits used in sequencing and array assays) remained essentially flat: $807 million in Q2 2024 versus $804 million in Q2 2023, and $1,569 million YTD 2024 versus $1,567 million in YTD 2023. Management attributed the stability to consistent customer demand, though some customers continue to manage inventory and capital expenditures more conservatively due to macroeconomic uncertainty.

**Instruments** (sequencers and array analyzers) declined sharply 39% in Q2 to $120 million from $197 million, and 34% YTD to $234 million from $356 million. The company entered 2024 with a lower backlog of NovaSeq X instruments (its high-throughput flagship product) following heavy pre-orders after the 2023 launch. Sequencing instrument revenue fell $77 million in Q2 and $121 million YTD, with declines in both high-throughput and mid-throughput products as customers face capital and cash flow constraints [Q2 2024 call]. Mid-range sequencers saw steeper reductions as research customers delay lab expansions.

### Service and Other Revenue

**Core Illumina service and other revenue** rose 3% in Q2 to $157 million from $153 million, and 10% YTD to $330 million from $299 million. Growth was driven by increased revenue from strategic partnerships (some at lower margins) and extended instrument maintenance service contracts, partially offset by declines in development and licensing agreements and genotyping services.

### Geographic Revenue

Americas held steady at $640 million in Q2 2024 (flat versus Q2 2023). Europe declined to $289 million from $303 million. Greater China fell significantly to $75 million from $115 million, reflecting competitive pressures and the impact of economic constraints and sanctions-related disruption in the region. Asia-Pacific, Middle East and Africa declined to $108 million from $118 million.

## Gross Profit and Margins

**Consolidated gross profit** remained relatively flat at $721 million in Q2 2024 versus $732 million in Q2 2023, but as a percentage of revenue (gross margin) improved substantially to 64.8% from 62.2%. **YTD gross margin** improved to 63.5% from 61.3%.

**Core Illumina gross margin** expanded to 68.0% in Q2 2024 from 65.5% in Q2 2023, and to 66.9% YTD from 64.7%. Management attributed this improvement primarily to more favorable mix within sequencing consumables and execution of operational excellence initiatives that delivered cost savings in freight and improved manufacturing productivity. Improvements were partially offset by lower-margin strategic partnership revenue and increases in warranty and field service costs.

**GRAIL gross margin** remained negative: the segment recorded a gross loss of $(16) million in Q2 2024 versus $(24) million in Q2 2023. This loss is driven principally by amortization of acquired intangible assets ($31 million in Q2 2024, $33 million in Q2 2023). Excluding intangible amortization, the underlying GRAIL business contribution improved modestly.

## Operating Expenses

Operating expenses surged due to the goodwill and intangible asset impairment charge related to the GRAIL spin-off, though core operating expenses (R&D and SG&A excluding impairments) declined modestly.

### Research and Development

**Core Illumina R&D** declined 12% in Q2 to $241 million from $274 million, and 10% YTD to $479 million from $532 million. Decreases stemmed from workforce reductions and lower employee-related compensation costs, as well as reduced lab supply spending, reflecting the company's ongoing cost reduction initiatives announced in Q2 2023.

**GRAIL R&D** was essentially flat in Q2 at $88 million versus $89 million, but rose 8% YTD to $189 million from $175 million as the company increased lab and consumables spending, employee compensation, and facility costs in preparation for anticipated product advancement and the independent operation post-spin-off.

### Selling, General and Administrative

**Core Illumina SG&A** declined 84% in Q2 to $60 million from $371 million, a dramatic improvement driven primarily by a $271 million gain recognized on the company's contingent consideration liability related to GRAIL contingent value rights (CVRs), compared to a $29 million loss in Q2 2023. The gain resulted from upward adjustment of the revenue risk premium assumption reconciling GRAIL's long-range forecast to market values observed in GRAIL's when-issued trading activity in June 2024. Additional Q2 improvements included $25 million reduction in proxy contest charges and $12 million decrease in legal contingency accruals. These gains were partially offset by $37 million in GRAIL spin-off transaction costs (comprised of financial advisory, legal, regulatory and professional services fees) and $7 million in accrued interest on the European Commission fine (accruing at 5.5% per annum since October 2023).

**Core Illumina SG&A** declined 40% YTD to $396 million from $656 million, driven by the $255 million gain on GRAIL CVR fair value adjustments, $31 million reduction in proxy contest charges, $15 million decrease in legal contingency matters, facility exit savings, and headcount reductions. These were partially offset by $52 million GRAIL spin-off costs YTD, $21 million increase in restructuring charges (primarily asset impairments), and $14 million accrued interest on the EC fine.

**GRAIL SG&A** remained relatively flat: Q2 declined slightly 3% to $88 million from $91 million, but YTD rose 4% to $192 million from $184 million as the company built out operating infrastructure ahead of independence.

### Goodwill and Intangible Asset Impairment

The company recorded **$1.9 billion in goodwill and intangible asset impairment charges in Q2 2024**, entirely within the GRAIL segment:

- **Goodwill impairment**: $1,466 million. During May 2024, the company performed its annual goodwill impairment test using investment banker valuations prepared for GRAIL's IPO pricing. GRAIL's carrying goodwill exceeded the estimated fair value of $580 million. Core Illumina's goodwill had no impairment; its fair value exceeded carrying value.

- **In-process R&D (IPR&D) impairment**: $420 million. GRAIL's IPR&D asset was tested for impairment in May 2024 and again in June following GRAIL's when-issued trading activity. The company used a discounted cash flow model with a 46.5% discount rate (derived from reconciling GRAIL's long-range plan containing FDA approval assumptions and 15-year cash flow projections to GRAIL's observed market trading values). An increase of 300 basis points to the discount rate would have resulted in only $20 million additional impairment, indicating high sensitivity to valuation assumptions.

Management noted that the dramatic markdown reflected changes in external factors: analyst and banker valuations of GRAIL shifted from initial estimates of $3–4 billion (December 2023 through spring 2024) to $400–770 million by May 2024 following disclosure of GRAIL's amended Form 10 filings, management presentations, and the $974 million Disposal Funding amount disclosed in connection with the spin-off. The impairment reflects substantially higher risk in R&D forecasting, including uncertainties around FDA approval timing, resource requirements, and the operational changes inherent in the spin-off transition.

**YTD 2024 also included $3 million IPR&D impairment in Q1** related to Core Illumina.

## GRAIL Spin-Off

### Completion and Structure

On **June 24, 2024**, Illumina completed the separation of GRAIL into an independent, publicly traded company (now trading as GRAIL, Inc. on NASDAQ). The spin-off was effected through distribution to Illumina stockholders of **26,547,021 shares of GRAIL common stock** on a pro-rata basis (one GRAIL share per six Illumina shares held on the record date of June 13, 2024).

The distribution constituted approximately **85.5% of GRAIL's outstanding common stock**. Illumina **retained approximately 14.5%** of GRAIL shares immediately following the spin-off, valued at $397 million at the time but declining to $74 million fair value by June 30, 2024 due to market price decline.

### Disposal Funding and Transaction Costs

**Disposal Funding**: As part of the spin-off, Illumina contributed **$974 million in cash to GRAIL** to cover 2.5 years of GRAIL's operations, as required by the European Commission's divestment order (see Legal Proceedings below). This funding was calculated to provide GRAIL with adequate working capital for operations through approximately mid-2026. The cash (less amounts retained by GRAIL) was deconsolidated from Illumina's balance sheet.

**Transaction costs**: Illumina incurred **$52 million in separation-related transaction costs during YTD 2024**, primarily consisting of financial advisory fees, legal expenses, regulatory filing costs, and other professional services directly related to the spin-off. An additional **$37 million was incurred in Q2 2024** specifically. These costs were recorded in selling, general and administrative expense.

### Tax-Free Reorganization

Illumina obtained a **private letter ruling (PLR) from the Internal Revenue Service** substantially confirming that the spin-off and certain related transactions qualified for non-recognition of gain and loss under Sections 355 and 368 of the Internal Revenue Code. Management also obtained a written opinion from tax counsel to the same effect.

However, the company disclosed a new risk factor: if the factual assumptions or representations made in the PLR request prove inaccurate or incomplete in any material respect, the ruling could be invalid. The IRS did not rule on whether all requirements for tax-free treatment under Section 355 were satisfied; that determination was based on representations by Illumina and GRAIL. Additionally, the opinion of tax counsel is not binding on the IRS or courts, and the IRS could challenge the conclusions.

### Accounting Treatment

The **disposition of GRAIL did not meet criteria to be reported as a discontinued operation**. Therefore, GRAIL's assets, liabilities, results of operations, and cash flows remain included (through the June 24 spin-off date) in Illumina's consolidated financial statements as a non-discontinued operation. This treatment differs from typical spin-offs of larger or more distinct operations and affects the comparability of period-to-period financial metrics.

### Contingent Value Rights (CVRs)

The company **remains the obligor on CVRs** issued in connection with the original GRAIL acquisition in 2021. These CVRs entitle holders to receive quarterly cash payments ("Covered Revenue Payments") representing a pro-rata portion of certain GRAIL-related revenues ("Covered Revenues") each year for a 12-year period:

- **2.5% of the first $1 billion** of GRAIL covered revenues per year.
- **9% of any covered revenues exceeding $1 billion** per year.

As of June 30, 2024, the fair value of the CVR liability was **$131 million** (down from $387 million at December 31, 2023). The $255 million favorable adjustment reflects reconciliation of GRAIL's long-range revenue forecast to market values observed in GRAIL's when-issued trading activity in June 2024 (the company increased the "revenue risk premium" assumption, effectively reducing the expected present value of future payments).

During YTD 2024, covered revenues for Q4 2023 and Q1 2024 totaled $57 million in aggregate, and the company paid **$535,000 in aggregate covered revenue payments**, indicating that covered revenues are tracking well below the $1 billion annual threshold at which the higher 9% rate would apply.

### Remaining Stake and Valuation Loss

Illumina's retained 14.5% stake in GRAIL was initially recorded at $397 million (representing Illumina's pro-rata share of GRAIL's net assets disposed of). The company is **measuring this equity investment at fair value**, with changes in fair value flowing through other income and expense.

Following GRAIL's IPO process and public trading in June 2024, the stock price declined significantly, resulting in Illumina's investment declining to **$74 million fair value as of June 30, 2024**. The company recorded an **unrealized loss of $328 million** on this investment during Q2 2024. This loss is reflected as a component of "Other (expense) income, net" and is a significant driver of the reported net loss for the quarter.

## Goodwill and Intangible Asset Impairments

### Goodwill Impairment

**GRAIL goodwill impairment: $1,466 million in Q2 2024.**

The company performed its required annual goodwill impairment test in May 2024. GRAIL's goodwill carrying value exceeded its estimated fair value of $580 million. Management used **enterprise value estimates prepared by investment bankers** for purposes of determining pricing for the GRAIL spin-off. These estimates incorporated (1) estimated GRAIL revenues for a two-year forward period based on assumed growth rates, and (2) implied revenue multiples for comparable public companies.

These enterprise value estimates represent a **Level 3 fair value measurement** (unobservable inputs with little market activity), reflecting management's own assumptions rather than quoted market prices. Management noted that "an increase in estimated enterprise values for GRAIL of 100% would still have resulted in a full impairment of goodwill," indicating extremely limited headroom.

**Core Illumina goodwill had no impairment**; the fair value of Core Illumina assets exceeded carrying value, indicating resilience of the core business valuation despite the overall revenue decline.

Prior impairment activity: In **Q3 2023**, the company recorded a **$712 million goodwill impairment** using a discounted cash flow (DCF) approach with a 24.0% discount rate. It estimated GRAIL's fair value at $3.6 billion using DCF methodology incorporating FDA approval and 15-year cash flow assumptions. By Q4 2023, no further impairment was indicated using the same DCF approach; however, initial analyst coverage (December 2023 into spring 2024) suggested valuations of $3–4 billion. By May 2024, following GRAIL's public filings and disclosure of the $974 million Disposal Funding, analyst and banker estimates shifted to $400–770 million, consistent with the Q2 2024 impairment.

### In-Process R&D (IPR&D) Impairment

**GRAIL IPR&D impairment: $420 million in Q2 2024.**

The company evaluated GRAIL's IPR&D asset (representing GRAIL's multi-cancer early detection technology pipeline) for impairment in May 2024 as part of the annual test, and again in June 2024 following GRAIL's when-issued trading activity as a triggering event. GRAIL's IPR&D carrying value exceeded estimated fair value.

The company used a **discounted cash flow model** to estimate fair value, incorporating (1) projected cash flows from GRAIL's long-range plan (which includes FDA approval assumptions and 15-year projections), and (2) a **46.5% discount rate** derived by **reconciling GRAIL's long-range plan to observed market values** based on GRAIL's when-issued trading activity in June 2024.

The discount rate represents an exceptionally high hurdle rate, reflecting the substantial risk profile: regulatory approval uncertainty for cancer detection assays, execution risk in scaling manufacturing and commercialization, uncertainty around the timing and magnitude of revenue inflection, and the inherent challenges of operating as an independent company post-spin-off. Management noted that "there is substantial risk inherent in forecasting revenues and spend associated with research and development, including assumptions around the timing and level of resources and investment to be made, which were made more challenging in light of the Spin-Off and related Disposal Funding" [Note 7, Supplemental Balance Sheet Details].

An increase of **300 basis points to the discount rate would have resulted in only $20 million additional impairment**, indicating the valuation is relatively robust to discount rate assumptions given the already-elevated 46.5% rate.

**Core Illumina IPR&D**: In Q1 2024, the company recorded a **$3 million IPR&D impairment** related to a Core Illumina development program.

## Legal Proceedings and Contingencies

### European Commission Fine

**July 12, 2023: European Commission imposed a fine of €432 million** (approximately $481 million as of June 30, 2024, including accrued interest). The fine was imposed pursuant to Article 14(2)(b) of the EU Merger Regulation, finding that Illumina acquired and exerted decisive influence over GRAIL in violation of the regulation's requirement for prior approval. The fine represents the **maximum permissible fine of 10% of the consolidated annual revenues for the fiscal year 2022** ($4.32 billion).

**Current status**: Illumina provided guarantees in October 2023 to satisfy the obligation rather than paying cash, allowing it to avoid the immediate cash outflow while pursuing appeals. As of June 30, 2024, the company accrued **$481 million** in accrued liabilities, including related accrued interest (which accrues at 5.5% per annum beginning October 2023 while the fine is outstanding). With no principal payment anticipated in the near term, interest continues to accrue.

**Appeal status**: Illumina appealed the Article 14(2)(b) Decision on September 26, 2023. The European Commission lodged its defense on February 2, 2024. Illumina filed reply comments on April 30, 2024, and observations responding to the Council of the European Union's intervention on May 17, 2024. The matter remains pending before the EU General Court.

### European Commission Divestment Decision and Prohibition Decision

**October 12, 2023: EC adopted the "EC Divestment Decision"** requiring Illumina to divest GRAIL (among other remedies). This decision effectively ordered Illumina to separate from GRAIL. The decision superseded interim measures that had been in place since October 2021 and October 2022, imposing operational and structural separation requirements (a "hold separate" arrangement).

**September 6, 2022: EC announced completion of its Phase II review and adopted the "Prohibition Decision,"** finding that the GRAIL acquisition was incompatible with the internal market in Europe because it significantly impedes effective competition. Illumina filed an action with the EU General Court on November 17, 2022 seeking annulment of the Prohibition Decision. The EC lodged its defense on April 20, 2023 (served on Illumina May 23, 2023). Illumina filed its reply on August 31, 2023, and the EC filed a rejoinder on December 22, 2023 (served January 8, 2024). GRAIL was granted leave to intervene in this action. The appeal remains pending.

**December 21, 2023: Illumina filed an action with the EU General Court** seeking annulment of the EC Divestment Decision. This action also remains pending.

### EU Court of Justice Jurisdictional Appeal

**April 19, 2021: EC sought and accepted referral of the GRAIL acquisition** from France for European Union merger review under Article 22(1) of Council Regulation (EC) No 139/2004. This was unprecedented: the EC had never before solicited referral to review an acquisition of a U.S. company with no revenue in Europe.

**April 28, 2021: Illumina filed action in the EU General Court** seeking annulment of the EC's assertion of jurisdiction under Article 22 of the EU Merger Regulation, arguing the acquisition did not meet jurisdictional criteria.

**July 13, 2022: EU General Court ruled in favor of the EC**, holding that the EC had jurisdiction to review the acquisition.

**September 22, 2022: Illumina filed appeal** in the Court of Justice of the European Union (EU Court of Justice).

**December 12, 2023: EU Court of Justice held hearing** on the appeal.

**March 21, 2024: Advocate General issued non-binding Opinion**, **recommending that the EU Court of Justice annul the General Court judgment** and the EC's decisions accepting the referral for EU merger review. This represents a significant development favoring Illumina's jurisdictional arguments.

**Timing**: The EU Court of Justice is scheduled to issue its judgment in early September 2024 (i.e., shortly after the June 30, 2024 quarter end and after the June 24 spin-off completion).

### Interim Measures Orders (Stayed)

**October 29, 2021 and October 28, 2022: EC issued "Initial Interim Measures Order" and "New Interim Measures Order,"** respectively, imposing interim remedies including the hold-separate arrangement described above. These interim measures ceased to apply with the completion of the GRAIL spin-off on June 24, 2024, as the structural separation is now complete.

### FTC Administrative Proceeding

**March 30, 2021: FTC filed administrative complaint** in the U.S. Federal Trade Commission and motion for preliminary injunction in the United States District Court for the District of Columbia, alleging that Illumina's acquisition of GRAIL would violate Section 7 of the Clayton Act.

**March 31, 2023: FTC issued opinion and order** ("FTC Order") requiring Illumina to divest GRAIL.

**April 5, 2023: Illumina appealed** to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit.

**December 15, 2023: Fifth Circuit issued opinion and order,** ruling that **the FTC applied the incorrect standard** in assessing Illumina's "open offer contract" (a contractual commitment Illumina had made regarding potential sale terms). The Fifth Circuit **vacated the FTC Order and remanded** the case to the FTC for reconsideration under the correct legal standard. The Fifth Circuit upheld the FTC's decision on all other grounds.

**July 30, 2024: Parties filed Joint Motion to Dismiss** the FTC's administrative complaint in light of Illumina's completion of the GRAIL spin-off.

### SEC Investigation

**July 2023: SEC informed Illumina** that the SEC staff was conducting an investigation relating to Illumina, requesting documents and communications primarily regarding (1) Illumina's acquisition of GRAIL, (2) statements and disclosures about GRAIL, its products, and the acquisition, and (3) conduct and compensation of certain Illumina and GRAIL management. **Illumina is cooperating** with the SEC inquiry.

## Risk Factors — Changes and New Disclosures

### New Risk Factor: GRAIL Spin-Off Tax Liability

**"The Spin-Off could result in substantial tax liability."** 

The company received a private letter ruling (PLR) from the IRS substantially confirming that the spin-off qualified for non-recognition of gain and loss under Sections 355 and 368 of the Internal Revenue Code. However, if the factual assumptions or representations made in the PLR prove inaccurate or incomplete, the ruling may be invalidated. Additionally, the opinion of tax counsel is not binding on the IRS, and the IRS could challenge and prevail against the tax-free treatment conclusions.

If the spin-off does not qualify for tax-free treatment, the resulting tax liability to the Company and shareholders "could be substantial."

### New Risk Factor: FDA Laboratory Development Test (LDT) Regulation

**"In April 2024, the FDA issued the Final Rule relating to Laboratory Development Tests (LDTs)."**

For many years, the FDA exercised enforcement discretion to not regulate LDTs created and used within a single CLIA-certified laboratory. On April 29, 2024, the FDA released final regulations (21 CFR Part 809) making explicit that LDTs offered as in vitro diagnostics (IVDs) are FDA-regulated devices under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, even when manufactured and used within a single laboratory.

The final rule includes enforcement discretion for certain IVDs, including **currently marketed LDTs that were first marketed prior to April 29, 2024**. The FDA also provided enforcement discretion for LDTs approved by New York State's Clinical Laboratory Evaluation Program (NYS CLEP).

**Impact on Illumina**: The company discloses that the majority of revenue from its laboratory-operated products do not fall within the scope of the LDT Rule. With one exception, the LDTs currently offered by Illumina's laboratories that fall within the scope of the rule (1) were approved by NYS CLEP and (2) were first marketed prior to the April 29, 2024 release date, and thus are eligible for the enforcement discretion provision.

**Remaining uncertainty**: The company states: "We cannot predict the specifics of how the FDA intends to implement the Final Rule and uncertainties remain as to whether and how newly developed LDT products that are now require regulatory clearance or approval may impact our business, financial condition, or results of operations." This represents a material new disclosure of regulatory risk for any future LDT product development.

### Updated Risk Factor: CVR Obligations Post-Spin-Off

**"Following the Spin-Off, we remain the obligor on the contingent value rights (the CVRs)."**

After the spin-off, Illumina continues to be the obligor on the CVRs issued in connection with the GRAIL acquisition. The company notes that because it no longer owns GRAIL, "it may be more difficult for us to estimate these future liabilities." Additionally, "we also may have difficulty complying with our obligations in respect of the CVRs if we are unable to obtain timely and accurate information from GRAIL." This represents a material increase in execution risk relative to the period when GRAIL was a consolidated subsidiary.

### Updated Risk Factor: GRAIL Business Deterioration

**"The Spin-Off could adversely affect the market value of the CVRs."**

The company discloses that the business of GRAIL may be adversely affected by the spin-off, which in turn could adversely affect the market value of the CVRs. This risk has materialized substantially: GRAIL's stock price declined sharply in June 2024 following its public listing, resulting in Illumina's retained 14.5% equity stake declining from $397 million to $74 million (a $323 million loss) in less than one month.

## Critical Accounting Policies and Estimates

### Income Tax Provision Methodology Change

Effective in Q2 2024, the company changed its methodology for calculating interim period income tax provisions. **Historically**, it applied an **estimated annual effective tax rate** to interim period pre-tax income, except in Q1 2024 and Q2 2023 when it used a **year-to-date effective tax rate method**.

**New methodology (Q2 2024 forward)**: The company now uses the **estimated annual effective tax rate method** because management determined it provides a more reliable estimate of the provision for income taxes in Q2 2024 and YTD 2024 periods, given that minor changes in estimated income/(loss) before income taxes would not result in significant changes in the estimated annual effective tax rate.

This change affects comparability of period-to-period tax expense and effective rates, particularly between Q1 (which used year-to-date method) and Q2 (which used estimated annual method). The company discloses this explicitly to ensure transparency.

### Goodwill and Intangible Asset Impairment Testing

The company tests goodwill for impairment annually in Q2 (its fiscal second quarter), or more frequently if triggering events occur. The GRAIL goodwill impairment in May 2024 and IPR&D impairment testing (with re-testing in June 2024 following when-issued trading) represent examples of both scheduled and event-triggered testing.

Fair value is estimated using **Level 3 inputs** (unobservable inputs with little market activity), primarily:
- **Enterprise value estimates** from investment bankers (for the 2024 annual test)
- **Discounted cash flow models** incorporating management's projected revenues, growth rates, and discount rates
- **Comparable company revenue multiples**

The company acknowledges substantial judgment and uncertainty in these estimates, particularly for GRAIL given the nascent commercial stage, regulatory approval risks, and the operational separation inherent in the spin-off transition.
