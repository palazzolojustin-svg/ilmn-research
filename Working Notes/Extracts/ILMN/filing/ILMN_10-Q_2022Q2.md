# Illumina Inc. Form 10-Q for Quarter Ended July 3, 2022

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-Q_2022Q2.md` · Date: 2022-07-03 · SEC filing (Form 10-Q), filed August 11, 2022 · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q2 2022 revenue of $1.16 billion, up 3% year-over-year, but swung to a net loss of $535 million (vs. $185 million profit in Q2 2021) primarily due to $609 million in legal contingencies—$453 million for an anticipated EU fine related to the contested GRAIL acquisition and $156 million for settlement of BGI patent litigation. GRAIL, acquired in August 2021, contributed only $12 million in revenue but $187 million in operating losses. The company faces ongoing regulatory scrutiny over the acquisition in both the EU (where the General Court ruled the EC has jurisdiction on July 13, 2022) and the US (FTC administrative trial concluded, decision expected by early September 2022).

## Financial Performance

### Revenue

Total revenue in the second quarter of 2022 was $1,162 million, up 3% from $1,126 million in Q2 2021. Year-to-date (first six months) revenue reached $2,386 million, up 8% from $2,219 million in the same period last year. 

The increase was driven primarily by sequencing consumables growth. Core Illumina (the company's sequencing and microarray business, excluding GRAIL) saw consumables revenue rise 5% in Q2 to $818 million and 8% year-to-date to $1,676 million. Core Illumina instruments revenue was essentially flat in Q2 at $193 million but up 10% year-to-date to $412 million. GRAIL contributed only $12 million in Q2 (primarily from sales of its Galleri multi-cancer early detection blood test) and $22 million year-to-date since its acquisition in August 2021.

### Revenue by Geography

In Q2 2022, the Americas accounted for $639 million (55% of total revenue), compared to $589 million in Q2 2021. Europe, Middle East and Africa contributed $308 million (27%), compared to $320 million. Greater China (including Taiwan and Hong Kong) declined to $118 million from $132 million, and Asia-Pacific rose to $97 million from $85 million. Year-to-date, the company experienced unfavorable foreign exchange effects of $33 million on revenue.

### Gross Profit and Margins

Gross profit declined to $767 million in Q2 2022 (66.0% margin) from $802 million (71.2% margin) in Q2 2021. Year-to-date gross profit rose slightly to $1,583 million (66.3% margin) from $1,566 million (70.6% margin). The margin compression was driven by three factors: lower fixed-cost leverage (reduced volume of instruments sold per dollar of revenue), higher freight costs, and GRAIL's negative gross margin of $29 million in Q2 due largely to intangible asset amortization of $33 million. These headwinds were partially offset by favorable product mix (greater share of higher-margin consumables).

## Operating Results

### Operating Loss

Illumina reported an operating loss of $579 million in Q2 2022 (a loss of 49.8% of revenue) compared to operating income of $187 million in Q2 2021. Year-to-date, the company posted an operating loss of $395 million (16.6% of revenue) versus operating income of $381 million (17.2% of revenue) in the prior year. This swing was entirely attributable to $609 million in legal contingencies recorded in Q2 2022.

### Research and Development

Core Illumina R&D spending increased 23% in Q2 to $249 million and 22% year-to-date to $486 million, driven by increases in headcount and professional services as the company invested in new product development and enhancements to existing platforms. GRAIL added $86 million in Q2 R&D ($171 million year-to-date), consisting primarily of headcount expenses and clinical trial costs.

### Selling, General and Administrative Expense

Core Illumina SG&A spending fell 18% in Q2 to $339 million and 25% year-to-date to $590 million, primarily because Q2 2021 included $105 million in Continuation Payments made to GRAIL during the regulatory review process (with $210 million made in YTD 2021). The decreases were partially offset by increases in headcount, travel expenses, and a $38 million fair-value charge on the contingent consideration liability related to GRAIL. GRAIL itself incurred $72 million in Q2 SG&A ($130 million year-to-date) for headcount, including performance-based compensation, and professional services.

### Legal Contingencies

The company recorded $609 million in legal contingencies in Q2 2022, consisting of two components:

1. **European Commission fine:** $453 million, representing 10% of fiscal 2021 consolidated annual revenues. On July 19, 2022, the EC issued a Statement of Objections alleging that Illumina breached the EU Merger Regulation by completing the acquisition of GRAIL without clearance. The company accrued the maximum potential fine under Article 14(2)(b) of the regulation. [Q2 2022 10-Q]

2. **BGI settlement:** $156 million, representing the company's preliminary estimate of past damages owed under a Settlement and License Agreement executed July 14, 2022 with BGI and Complete Genomics Inc. [Note 7. Legal Proceedings; Q2 2022 10-Q] The company paid the $325 million settlement amount on July 25, 2022. In addition, the company expects to record an estimated $177 million intangible asset (the value of licenses received from BGI) to be amortized over approximately 6.5 years, and to record an $8 million gain contingency from damages awarded to Illumina.

## Net Income (Loss)

Net loss in Q2 2022 was $535 million, or $(3.40) per diluted share, compared to net income of $185 million, or $1.26 per diluted share, in Q2 2021. Year-to-date, the company reported a net loss of $449 million, or $(2.85) per diluted share, versus net income of $333 million, or $2.26 per diluted share. The swing was driven primarily by the legal contingencies, lower gross margin, and higher R&D spending, partially offset by lower interest expense and reduced SG&A spending.

## GRAIL Acquisition and Regulatory Proceedings

Illumina acquired GRAIL on August 18, 2021 for approximately $7.1 billion. GRAIL is a clinical-stage company developing Galleri, a blood-based early detection test for multiple cancer types. The acquisition remains subject to extensive legal and regulatory review in both the United States and European Union.

### European Proceedings

On April 19, 2021, the European Commission accepted a referral of the acquisition for EU merger review under Article 22 of the EU Merger Regulation, requested by France and joined by Belgium, Greece, Iceland, the Netherlands, and Norway. On July 13, 2022, the EU General Court ruled in favor of the European Commission, holding that it has jurisdiction to review the acquisition. Illumina intends to appeal this decision to the Court of Justice of the European Union. [Q2 2022 10-Q]

On October 29, 2021, the EC adopted an interim measures order (referred to as a hold-separate arrangement) requiring Illumina and GRAIL to continue operating as independent entities, prohibiting integration activities and management involvement, and requiring appointment of an independent monitoring trustee. The order is burdensome to implement and administer and has imposed additional costs. On July 19, 2022, the EC issued a Statement of Objections alleging Illumina breached the regulation by completing the acquisition without approval, triggering the $453 million fine accrual. [Q2 2022 10-Q]

### United States Proceedings

On March 30, 2021, the Federal Trade Commission filed an administrative complaint alleging the acquisition violates Section 7 of the Clayton Act. The administrative trial commenced August 24, 2021, with live testimony concluding September 24, 2021. Opening post-trial briefs were filed April 15, 2022, reply briefs on May 25, 2022, and closing arguments occurred June 8, 2022. A decision by the administrative judge is due no later than September 2, 2022. Illumina is defending vigorously against the FTC action. [Q2 2022 10-Q]

### Goodwill and Impairment Considerations

As of July 3, 2022, the fair value of the GRAIL reporting unit exceeded its carrying value by $700 million based on a quantitative impairment test using a 16.0% discount rate derived from a weighted average cost of capital. However, the company noted that the EU General Court's July 13, 2022 jurisdictional decision constitutes a triggering event requiring an interim goodwill impairment test in Q3 2022, which could result in material intangible asset and goodwill impairment charges. [Note 6. Supplemental Balance Sheet Details; Q2 2022 10-Q]

### Contingent Consideration and Fair Value

GRAIL contingent consideration liabilities have a fair value of $605 million as of July 3, 2022 (down from $615 million at January 2, 2022). These are contingent value rights entitling holders to receive quarterly cash payments representing a pro rata portion of specified GRAIL-related revenues for 12 years: 2.5% of the first $1 billion of revenue each year, and 9% of revenue above $1 billion. Year-to-date Covered Revenues totaled $20 million (Q4 2021 and Q1 2022 combined), and Covered Revenue Payments totaled approximately $187,000 (with part applied to expense reimbursement). [Note 3. Investments and Fair Value Measurements; Q2 2022 10-Q]

### Equity Awards

The company granted GRAIL employees cash-based equity incentive awards with potential values tied to GRAIL's enterprise fair value. As of July 3, 2022, the aggregate cash value of unvested awards outstanding was $267 million, with an estimated liability of $40 million accrued. In addition, a performance-based award with vesting tied to GRAIL revenues has an aggregate potential value of up to $78 million and expires (to the extent unvested) in August 2030. As of Q2 2022, the company determined it was not probable that the performance conditions would be achieved. [Note 5. Stockholders' Equity; Q2 2022 10-Q]

## BGI Patent Litigation and Settlement

On July 14, 2022, Illumina entered into a Settlement and License Agreement with BGI Genomics Co. Ltd. (BGI), Complete Genomics Inc. (CGI), and their affiliates, resolving all patent infringement and antitrust claims between the parties. The agreement includes a one-time payment of $325 million by Illumina to an affiliate of BGI, with the parties acknowledging that this payment satisfies all prior jury judgments and damages claims. The company paid the $325 million on July 25, 2022. [Note 7. Legal Proceedings; Q2 2022 10-Q]

Under the agreement, Illumina received a fully paid-up, royalty-free license to BGI's 2-channel technology patents (including Patents 8,617,811, 9,222,132, 9,523,125, 10,662,473, 11,098,356, and 11,214,832, plus related patent applications) for use in all current and future Illumina platforms. BGI received a fully paid-up license to Illumina's image mix patents (Patents 9,217,178, 9,303,290, and 9,970,055). The parties agreed to a litigation standstill for patent and antitrust actions in the United States and its territories until October 1, 2025, excluding claims related to non-invasive prenatal testing and GRAIL's multi-cancer early detection intellectual property. [Note 7. Legal Proceedings; Q2 2022 10-Q]

Illumina's preliminary assessment allocated the $325 million payment as follows: approximately $156 million to the release of past damages claims (accrued in Q2 2022), and an expected $177 million to an intangible asset representing the fair value of the license (to be recorded in Q3 2022 and amortized over approximately 6.5 years). Additionally, Illumina expects to record an $8 million gain contingency from damages awarded to the company. [Note 7. Legal Proceedings; Q2 2022 10-Q]

Prior jury verdicts that were resolved include:
- A November 30, 2021 verdict in Illumina's favor finding willful infringement of Patents 537, 200, 973, and claims of Patent 025, awarding $8 million in damages plus pre-judgment interest and a permanent injunction against BGI's Standard MPS chemistry through January 1, 2023.
- A May 6, 2022 verdict in CGI's favor in the District of Delaware finding Illumina willfully infringed Patents 9,222,132 and 10,662,473 and awarding $334 million in past damages. [Note 7. Legal Proceedings; Q2 2022 10-Q]

## Balance Sheet and Liquidity

### Cash Position

Cash and cash equivalents totaled $1,289 million as of July 3, 2022, up $57 million from $1,232 million at January 2, 2022. Short-term investments (marketable equity securities) totaled $38 million, down from $107 million. Of the total cash, approximately $611 million was held by foreign subsidiaries. [Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheets; Q2 2022 10-Q]

### Debt

The company maintains the following debt obligations:

- **2023 Term Notes:** $500 million principal at 0.550% annual interest, due March 23, 2023. Classified as short-term as of July 3, 2022.
- **2031 Term Notes:** $500 million principal at 2.550% annual interest, due March 23, 2031. Classified as long-term.
- **2023 Convertible Notes:** $750 million principal at 0% annual interest (no coupon), due August 15, 2023. Reclassified to short-term as of July 3, 2022 because holders may convert on or after May 15, 2023 through August 11, 2023. The notes were not convertible as of the balance sheet date.

Interest expense in Q2 2022 was $6 million and $12 million year-to-date, a significant decrease from $16 million and $34 million in the prior year periods, due primarily to the adoption of ASU 2020-06 on January 3, 2022, which eliminated the requirement to separately account for the embedded conversion features of the convertible notes.

The company also maintains a $750 million senior unsecured revolving credit facility (the Credit Facility), entered into March 8, 2021, with no amounts outstanding as of July 3, 2022. The facility matures March 8, 2026, subject to two one-year extensions at the company's option. [Note 4. Debt; Q2 2022 10-Q]

## Cash Flow

Net cash provided by operating activities was $297 million in the first six months of 2022, compared to $535 million in the prior year. The $238 million decrease was attributable to the net loss of $449 million, partially offset by $406 million in net adjustments (including $185 million in depreciation and amortization, $183 million in share-based compensation, and $76 million in net losses on strategic investments), and $340 million in favorable changes in operating assets and liabilities (primarily driven by an increase in accrued liabilities due to legal contingencies). [Condensed Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows; Q2 2022 10-Q]

Net cash used in investing activities was $239 million in the first six months of 2022. The company invested $132 million in capital expenditures (primarily facilities), paid $85 million for acquisitions, and used $22 million for purchases of strategic investments. [Condensed Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows; Q2 2022 10-Q]

Net cash provided by financing activities was $16 million in the first six months of 2022, consisting of $33 million in proceeds from employee stock purchases under the ESPP, partially offset by $17 million paid for taxes related to net share settlement of equity awards. The company did not repurchase any shares in the period; approximately $15 million remained available under the $750 million share repurchase authorization. [Condensed Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows; Q2 2022 10-Q]

## Segment Results

Core Illumina (the company's base sequencing and microarray business) had revenue of $1,156 million in Q2 2022 and $2,377 million year-to-date, compared to $1,126 million and $2,219 million in the prior year periods. GRAIL had revenue of $12 million in Q2 and $22 million year-to-date. Eliminations of intercompany transactions totaled $6 million in Q2 and $13 million year-to-date.

Core Illumina reported an operating loss of $396 million in Q2 2022 (including the $609 million legal contingencies accrual) and a loss of $34 million year-to-date, compared to operating income of $187 million and $381 million in the prior year periods. GRAIL reported an operating loss of $187 million in Q2 and $359 million year-to-date, driven by R&D, SG&A, and intangible asset amortization. [Note 9. Segment Information; Q2 2022 10-Q]

## Taxes

The company's effective tax rate was 16.0% in Q2 2022 and 9.7% year-to-date, compared to 10.8% and 11.8% in the prior year periods. The lower effective rates in 2022 were negatively impacted by the $95 million non-deductible impact of the potential EC fine, and positively impacted by the mix of earnings in lower-tax jurisdictions (Singapore, United Kingdom) and discrete tax benefits from GRAIL pre-acquisition net operating losses. Additionally, the company faced a significant headwind from the mandatory capitalization of R&D expenses under the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (Section 174), which added $23 million in Q2 and $27 million year-to-date to tax expense. If the capitalization requirement is not repealed or deferred, the company anticipates approximately $142 million in additional tax expense for full-year 2022 and corresponding increases in cash tax payments. [Income Tax; Q2 2022 10-Q]

## Share Count and Equity

As of August 5, 2022, there were 157.3 million shares of common stock outstanding. Basic and diluted earnings per share were both $(3.40) in Q2 2022 and $(2.85) year-to-date, as the company was in a loss position and therefore dilutive shares are excluded. In the prior year periods, diluted EPS of $1.26 and $2.26 reflected the inclusion of potentially dilutive shares from convertible notes under the if-converted method (adopted January 3, 2022). [Earnings (Loss) per Share; Q2 2022 10-Q]

The company has approximately 1.9 million shares remaining available for future grants under the 2015 Stock and Incentive Compensation Plan. Year-to-date, approximately 0.1 million shares were issued under the Employee Stock Purchase Plan (ESPP) at a weighted-average grant-date fair value of $91.27 per share. [Stockholders' Equity; Q2 2022 10-Q]

## Management and Organization

On June 9, 2022, following the departure of Sam Samad as Chief Financial Officer, Joydeep Goswami, Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer, was appointed interim Chief Financial Officer while the company conducts a permanent search. On August 9, 2022, the Compensation Committee approved additional compensation for Goswami of $25,000 per month, retroactive to July 2022 and continuing through two months following the appointment of a permanent CFO. Goswami previously held senior management roles at Thermo Fisher Scientific and was a consultant at McKinsey & Company. [Other Information; Q2 2022 10-Q]

## Key Risk Factors and Outlook

Management emphasized that the acquisition of GRAIL and the ongoing legal and regulatory proceedings present material risks. The company faces potential significant financial penalties, operational restrictions, or divestiture requirements depending on the outcomes of FTC and EU proceedings. The hold-separate arrangement under the EC interim measures order has imposed burdensome administrative costs and may materially impair the anticipated benefits of the acquisition.

The company expects revenue to continue growing in 2022, but anticipates operating expenses will continue to grow on an absolute basis. Gross margin may face ongoing pressure from the cost structure of GRAIL and from high freight and logistics costs. The company's ability to achieve profitability and cash flow targets will depend significantly on the resolution of the GRAIL regulatory proceedings, which are inherently uncertain. [Management's Overview and Outlook; Q2 2022 10-Q]
