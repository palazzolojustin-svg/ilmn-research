# Illumina Q1 2026 Quarterly Report (10-Q): SomaLogic Acquisition, Tariff Impact, China Challenges, and Operating Margin Expansion

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/filing/ILMN_10-Q_2026Q1.md` · Date: 2026-05-01 · SEC filing (Form 10-Q), filed May 1, 2026 · SEC filing*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q1 2026 revenue of $1.091 billion (up 5% YoY), with operating margin expanding to 19.2% from 15.8%, driven by strong NovaSeq X demand and cost reduction efforts. The company closed its $460 million acquisition of SomaLogic, a proteomics platform, on January 30, 2026, to advance its multiomics strategy. Significant headwinds include U.S. tariffs (seeking CBP refunds), inclusion on China's unreliable entities list (costing ~$20M Q1 revenue), Middle East conflict raising freight costs, rising memory chip costs, and ongoing GRAIL-related litigation. Cash from operations was strong at $289 million, while capital returned to shareholders via $243 million in share repurchases. The company ended Q1 with $1.089 billion in cash and $1.989 billion in term debt.

## Financial Performance: Revenue and Operating Leverage

Illumina reported total revenue of **$1.091 billion in Q1 2026, a 5% increase from $1.041 billion in Q1 2025**, marking progress toward management's goal to return to revenue growth by end of 2027 [Q1 2026 10-Q]. Within this, product revenue comprised **$917 million (84.1% of total)** and service and other revenue **$174 million (15.9%)**.

**Product revenue breakdown**: Consumables (the reagent kits used in every sequencing run) reached **$797 million, up 4% from $768 million**, while instruments sales rose **$120 million, up 7% from $112 million**. The consumables growth reflected strong demand for high-throughput consumables as the NovaSeq X installed base continues to expand; instruments growth was driven by increased NovaSeq X shipments [MD&A, Results of Operations]. Service and other revenue increased **8% to $174 million**, primarily because SomaLogic contributed full revenue from its January 30, 2026 acquisition date forward [MD&A, Results of Operations].

By geographic region, the picture was mixed: **United States and Canada grew to $590 million (from $555 million)**, **Europe, Middle East, Africa and Latin America to $356 million (from $332 million)**, and **Asia-Pacific to $93 million (from $82 million)**. However, **Greater China contracted sharply to $52 million from $72 million, a decline of $20 million**, directly attributable to Illumina's inclusion on China's unreliable entities list by regulatory authorities; management stated this region experienced "competitive challenges" and said they expect these headwinds to persist [MD&A, Financial Overview, Risk Factors]. The company also implemented a new global commercial structure in Q1 2026 to improve operating efficiencies and align with local markets, reorganizing reporting into these four regions [Note 3, Revenue by Geographic Area].

**Gross profit grew to $721 million (66.1% margin) from $683 million (65.6% margin)**, a 6% increase. Management attributed the margin expansion to "decreases in field service and warranty costs and higher product margins," partially offset by "the addition of SomaLogic revenue that is lower margin and higher tariff costs" [MD&A, Results of Operations]. This indicates SomaLogic trades some margin for scale in the emerging proteomics market.

**Operating income expanded substantially to $209 million (19.2% of revenue) from $164 million (15.8%)**, a 27% increase. This was driven by the $38 million gross profit increase and a $7 million decrease in operating expense [MD&A, Results of Operations]. Operating expense (R&D plus SG&A) totaled **$512 million, down 1% from $519 million**, despite the acquisition: R&D fell **$12 million to $240 million** (a 5% decline, primarily from lower restructuring charges), while SG&A rose **$5 million to $272 million** (a 2% increase), reflecting higher legal expenses and professional services related to an enterprise resource planning system upgrade and the SomaLogic acquisition [MD&A, Results of Operations].

**Net income reached $134 million ($0.87 diluted EPS) compared to $131 million ($0.82 diluted EPS)** in Q1 2025, a modest 2% increase. Earnings per share reflected a lower share count: weighted average diluted shares outstanding fell to **154 million from 159 million**, the result of ongoing share repurchases [Earnings per Share table]. However, this bottom-line growth was tempered by a swing in other income/expense: **other (expense) income deteriorated to -$39 million from +$32 million**, a $71 million adverse swing, driven primarily by a **net loss of $45 million on strategic investments in Q1 2026 versus a net gain of $31 million in Q1 2025**, reflecting mark-to-market losses on the retained GRAIL equity stake (discussed below) [MD&A, Other Income (Expense)].

The **effective tax rate fell to 14.8% from 27.9%**, a favorable variance driven by "prior year tax return adjustments" and "the mix of earnings in jurisdictions with lower statutory tax rates than the U.S. federal statutory rate, such as Singapore" [MD&A, Provision for Income Taxes]. Management noted this was 630 basis points below the 21% U.S. federal statutory rate.

## Balance Sheet: Cash Deployment and Debt Position

As of **March 29, 2026, total assets were $6.559 billion, down from $6.644 billion at year-end 2025**, reflecting the SomaLogic acquisition deployment. **Cash and cash equivalents fell to $1.089 billion from $1.418 billion**, a $329 million decrease driven by the acquisition and share repurchases. **Short-term investments (consisting of marketable equity securities, primarily the retained GRAIL stake) plummeted to $66 million from $215 million**, as the company sold $104 million of GRAIL shares during Q1, recognizing net unrealized losses of **$57 million on the remaining GRAIL holdings** [Investments and Fair Value Measurements]. **Total current assets declined to $2.738 billion from $3.289 billion**, while **goodwill jumped to $1.284 billion from $1.113 million** (reflecting $171 million of SomaLogic goodwill, primarily attributable to assembled workforce and expected synergies), and **intangible assets net rose to $430 million from $210 million** (reflecting $186 million of SomaLogic intangible assets and a $50 million IP portfolio acquired during Q1 for 14-year amortization) [Note 2, Acquisitions, Intangible Assets and Goodwill; Note 7, Supplemental Balance Sheet Details].

**Stockholders' equity edged down to $2.676 billion from $2.723 billion**, despite $134 million of net income, because the company repurchased **2.0 million shares for $243 million** during Q1 and recognized **$20 million in net stock-based compensation adjustments from net issuances and repurchases** [Stockholders' Equity]. **Treasury stock increased to $4.931 billion (49 million shares) from $4.699 billion (48 million shares)** [Balance Sheet].

On the liability side, **term debt held steady at $1.989 billion net (principal of $2.0 billion less $11 million unamortized discount)**, comprising four tranches: **$500 million due 2026 (4.650% coupon, maturing September 9), $500 million due 2027 (5.750%, maturing December 13), $500 million due 2030 (4.750%, maturing December 12, issued November 2025), and $500 million due 2031 (2.550%, maturing March 23)** [Note 5, Debt]. **Accounts payable fell to $218 million from $240 million**, while **accrued liabilities held roughly flat at $849 million versus $846 million**, though the composition shifted: **contingent consideration liabilities (primarily SomaLogic) surged to $68 million current and $52 million long-term** (total $119 million, up from $54 million GRAIL-related at year-end) [Note 4, Fair Value Measurements]. The company also maintains a **$750 million undrawn revolving credit facility maturing January 4, 2028**, in compliance with all financial and operating covenants [Note 5, Debt].

## The SomaLogic Acquisition: Proteomics and Multiomics Strategy

Illumina closed its acquisition of **SomaLogic and other assets from Standard BioTools on January 30, 2026**, for a **total purchase price of $460 million**, consisting of **$382 million in cash, $81 million in contingent consideration, offset by $3 million for settlement of a preexisting relationship** [Note 2, Acquisitions, Intangible Assets and Goodwill]. Management stated the deal would "enhance our presence in the proteomics market and advance our multiomics strategy," signaling a broadening beyond sequencing into protein measurement [MD&A, About Illumina].

The acquisition is structured with substantial earn-out provisions: **Standard BioTools is eligible to receive up to $75 million in milestone payments, comprising $25 million for 2025 performance and $50 million for 2026 performance, payable upon achievement of specified net revenue targets for SOMAmer-based assay services and related products**. (The company paid the first $25 million milestone in April 2026, as the 2025 target was met.) Additionally, **Standard BioTools receives a 2% royalty on net revenues from sales of SOMAmer-based next-generation sequencing library preparation kits, paid quarterly for 10 years through January 2036** [Note 2]. The aggregate fair value of these contingent liabilities was estimated at **$81 million at acquisition**, with management still finalizing purchase price allocation one year out [Note 2].

The preliminary acquisition accounting shows: **goodwill of $171 million** (attributable to assembled workforce, expanded market opportunities, and expected synergies, expected to be fully tax-deductible); **$186 million in intangible assets**, comprising **$158 million in developed technology (9-year amortization), $16 million in trade name (7-year), and $12 million in customer relationships (8-year)**, all valued using income approach discounted cash flow analysis; **$57 million in inventory** (including **$22 million classified as non-current long-term inventory** due to manufacturing processes lasting over one year, a new accounting policy for the company); **$19 million in property and equipment**; **$14 million in operating lease right-of-use assets**; and other minor items, offset by $12 million in operating lease and other liabilities [Note 2]. Pro forma revenue for Q1 2026 would have been **$1.099 billion** (versus actual $1.091 billion, indicating minimal additional revenue from SomaLogic in Q1 itself, likely because operations began late in the quarter).

Transaction costs of **$7 million** were expensed as SG&A in Q1 2026 [Note 2].

## Tariff Impact and Mitigation

Tariffs imposed by the U.S. government and other countries beginning **April 2025** have emerged as a material cost headwind. Management stated the "largest cost impact to us relates to importation from our manufacturing facility in Singapore," with additional exposure from "importation of parts and sub-assemblies to our manufacturing operations in the United States and importation into China" [MD&A, Financial Overview].

A significant legal development occurred in February 2026: **the U.S. Supreme Court held that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) does not authorize the imposition of tariffs**. In **March 2026, the U.S. Court of International Trade ordered U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) to finalize or revise certain import duty determinations excluding IEEPA duties**, but suspended the order pending CBP's implementation of an administrative refund process [MD&A, Financial Overview]. Management disclosed that the company "has incurred significant costs under IEEPA-related tariffs since their implementation" and intends to seek refunds, but acknowledged that "the timing and amount of recoveries remain uncertain and will depend on the scope and timing of future phases, any further court or administrative developments, and completion of applicable administrative steps" [MD&A, Financial Overview].

To mitigate impact, the company has "partially mitigated the impact in 2025 through supply chain optimization, cost measures, and pricing actions and are aiming to more fully mitigate in 2026" [MD&A, Financial Overview]. No quantification of tariff costs was provided in the filing.

Additionally, the conflict and instability in Iran and the Middle East prompted management to flag that they "expect higher fuel and energy costs to increase freight and shipping costs throughout 2026" and that costs in 2026 will be "impacted by the rising costs to purchase memory chips" [MD&A, Financial Overview].

## China Regulatory Challenges and Revenue Impact

Illumina's inclusion on China's "unreliable entities list" by regulatory authorities has created material business uncertainty. Management highlighted this as a risk factor: "uncertainty regarding the impact of our inclusion on the 'unreliable entities list' by regulatory authorities in China" [Forward-Looking Statements, Risk Factors].

The financial impact is already visible: **Greater China revenue fell $20 million in Q1 2026 to $52 million from $72 million**, directly attributed to "our inclusion on the unreliable entities list" as well as "competitive challenges in our China region" [MD&A, Results of Operations, Financial Overview]. The company reports this region was impacted "across all products and services" [MD&A, Results of Operations], suggesting broad-based customer caution. Management expects these factors to "continue to impact our sales and results of operations in 2026, and beyond, the size and duration of which is significantly uncertain" [MD&A, Financial Overview].

## NIH Funding Uncertainty

Management identified potential reductions in U.S. government funding of the National Institutes of Health as a risk. The filing states: "any reductions or potential reductions in funding for the National Institutes of Health (NIH), or targeted cancellations by the U.S. federal government of certain grants or contracts, could negatively impact our customers and reduce demand for our products and services" [Forward-Looking Statements]. This is material because many of Illumina's research customers—academic institutions and government laboratories—depend on NIH funding for their genomic research.

## GRAIL Contingent Liabilities and Retained Investment

Following Illumina's 2024 divestiture of GRAIL (a liquid biopsy company), the company retains equity exposure and ongoing contingent value rights (CVRs). As of **March 29, 2026, the fair value of the GRAIL contingent liability was $35 million** (down from $54 million at year-end), with **$33 million in other long-term liabilities and the remainder in accrued liabilities** [Note 4, Fair Value Measurements].

The CVRs entitle holders to "future quarterly cash payments (Covered Revenue Payments) representing a pro rata portion of certain GRAIL-related revenues (Covered Revenues) each year for a 12-year period through August 2033." The structure is: **2.5% payment right on the first $1 billion of GRAIL revenue each year, and 9% on revenue above $1 billion** for the same period [Note 4]. In Q1 2026, Illumina paid **$410,000 in Covered Revenue Payments** (versus $360,000 in Q1 2025), based on **Covered Revenues of $44 million in Q4 2025** (versus $38 million in Q4 2024), "driven primarily by sales of GRAIL's Galleri test" [Note 4]. Management estimated fair value using a **Monte Carlo simulation with assumptions including forecasted revenues, revenue risk premium, operational leverage ratio, and counterparty credit spread** [Note 4].

The company also holds marketable equity securities (the retained GRAIL stake), which declined sharply in value. **Short-term investments fell from $215 million at year-end to $66 million by quarter-end**, after the company sold **$104 million of shares during Q1, recognizing $45 million in net losses on strategic investments** (comprising $57 million in unrealized losses on the remaining stake, partially offset by $12 million in gains on shares sold) [Note 4, MD&A, Other Income (Expense)].

Illumina faces substantial litigation related to the GRAIL acquisition. Multiple shareholder derivative complaints allege directors breached fiduciary duties by "knowingly causing Illumina to unlawfully close the GRAIL acquisition, concealing material facts related to the GRAIL acquisition and making inadequate disclosures" [Note 8, Legal Proceedings]. These include consolidated Delaware actions (_Icahn Partners LP v. deSouza_; _City of Omaha Police and Firefighters Retirement System v. deSouza_; _City of Roseville General Employees Retirement System v. deSouza_; _The Pavers and Road Builders Benefit Funds v. deSouza_). The _Icahn Partners_ settlement was approved (no payment by any party), but litigation in the other three continues, with briefing on a motion to dismiss completed and a hearing held February 13, 2026 [Note 8].

Additionally, federal securities class actions were consolidated in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of California (_in re Illumina, Inc. Securities Litigation_, No. 23-cv-2082-LL-MMP), with a **Third Amended Complaint filed October 27, 2025**. As of the Q1 filing date (May 1, 2026), the company had filed a motion to dismiss on December 11, 2025, plaintiffs filed an opposition on February 4, 2026, and the company filed its reply on March 6, 2026, with no hearing date set [Note 8]. State securities class actions in California also continue, with a **class certification motion filed January 26, 2026 and the company's opposition filed March 12, 2026**, a hearing scheduled for May 15, 2026, and a motion for judgment on pleadings filed March 27, 2026 with a hearing scheduled for June 26, 2026 [Note 8]. "In light of the fact that the lawsuits are in an early stage, we cannot predict the ultimate outcome of the suits. We deny the allegations in the complaints and intend to vigorously defend the litigation" [Note 8].

Finally, two shareholders—Michael Warner and Jane Davidson—have sent litigation demands to the board requesting it pursue civil action against officers and directors over the GRAIL acquisition. The board deferred final decisions on both demands, most recently on February 4, 2026, citing the pending shareholder lawsuits and stating "Our Board will monitor the stockholder lawsuits and revisit the demand as warranted as the lawsuits progress" [Note 8].

## Operating Expenses and Cost Structure

**Research and development expense fell to $240 million (22.0% of revenue) from $252 million (24.2%)**, a 5% decrease. Management attributed this to "a decrease in restructuring charges of $11 million," indicating the company's cost reduction initiatives are moderating restructuring charges [MD&A, Results of Operations]. This is important context for the margin expansion: some of the operating margin improvement comes from the wind-down of restructuring programs initiated in 2023 and 2025.

**Selling, general and administrative expense rose to $272 million (24.9% of revenue) from $267 million (25.6%)**, a 2% increase. The increase was driven by "an increase in outside professional services, primarily related to our global enterprise resource planning (ERP) system upgrade and legal expenses, and an increase in acquisition-related costs, primarily related to the SomaLogic acquisition," partially offset by "a decrease in restructuring charges of $17 million" [MD&A, Results of Operations]. The ERP system upgrade, begun in Q3 2025, is expected to become operational in H1 2027 [Controls and Procedures].

**Total operating expense (R&D plus SG&A) was $512 million (46.9% of revenue), down from $519 million (49.8%)**, a 1% decrease despite the acquisition and ERP investment, underscoring the impact of cost reduction and the wind-down of restructuring charges.

Restructuring charges themselves have moderated significantly: the company recognized only **$1 million in Q1 2026 versus $31 million in Q1 2025** [Note 7, Restructuring], indicating the 2023 and 2025 cost reduction initiatives are nearing completion.

## Internal Controls and ERP System Upgrade

In **Q3 2025, the company began implementation of a global enterprise resource planning (ERP) system upgrade** expected to "enhance the flow of financial information, facilitate data analysis, and accelerate information reporting," with expected operational launch in **H1 2027** [Controls and Procedures]. Management cautioned that "we may make changes to our processes and procedures which, in turn, could materially affect our internal controls over financial reporting" and committed to monitoring and reporting such impacts [Controls and Procedures].
