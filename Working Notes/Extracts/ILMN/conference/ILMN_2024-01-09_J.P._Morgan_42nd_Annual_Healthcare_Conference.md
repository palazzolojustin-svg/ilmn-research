**Summary.** In his first major investor conference as Illumina CEO, Jacob Thaysen pre-announced Q4 and full-year 2023 results that beat the company's own expectations, driven by NovaSeq X instruments and consumables. He said 2024 should look broadly similar to 2023 for the core business because customers remain cautious, and set three priorities: customers/commercial excellence, operational excellence, and resolving GRAIL (terms targeted by end of Q2 2024). Q&A covered the macro backdrop, price elasticity, China (about 10% of sales), competition, long-read versus short-read, and long-term targets, which he declined to reaffirm or quantify.

# Illumina at the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference, January 9, 2024

This was a 40-minute fireside session: about 20 minutes of prepared remarks from CEO Jacob Thaysen, then about 20 minutes of Q&A led by Rachel Vatnsdal of J.P. Morgan. CFO Joydeep Goswami joined for the Q&A. Thaysen had joined Illumina "a few months" earlier, so this was an early public statement of his view of the company. Wording in the transcript is sometimes garbled (marked "[indiscernible]" in places), and where meaning is unclear this is noted below.

## Opening and framing [JPM, 2024-01-09]

- Thaysen noted that GRAIL (the cancer early-detection test business Illumina had acquired) must be "held and operated separately and independently" from Illumina under the European Commission order that prohibited the acquisition. He listed four topics: an overview, preliminary Q4 and full-year 2023 results, 2024 priorities, and the path ahead.
- **Mission:** "to improve human health by unlocking the power of the genome." He said this is why he joined and that he is impressed by employees' commitment to customers and to driving genomics forward.
- **Scale of the "core" business (Illumina excluding GRAIL):**
  - More than $4.4 billion of revenue in 2023, with strong positive cash flow.
  - More than 9,000 employees serving more than 9,000 customers in more than 160 countries.
  - More than 9,800 patents, around 800 patent families.
  - Technology is regularly cited in scientific and medical publications.
- Thaysen described Illumina as having "unmatched scale, differentiation and diversification" across clinical (patient testing) and research customers. He pointed to its 25 years of infrastructure, global reach and partnerships. He said the core business is "really compelling to me" and that he is "confident that we're very well positioned to grow."

## Preliminary Q4 and full-year 2023 results [JPM, 2024-01-09]

Thaysen said Q4 results came in ahead of the company's own expectations, "primarily driven by the NovaSeq X instruments and our consumable sales." Consumables are the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every sequencing run, and they generate recurring revenue. The figures below are as stated in the transcript and are preliminary. The full-year results were on a slide that is not captured in the text.

"Non-GAAP" means adjusted to exclude certain items, so it differs from official accounting results. The transcript says consolidated revenue was "up approximately $1.12 billion," which reads as a transcription slip for "approximately $1.12 billion."

### High-throughput instruments and NovaSeq X

- After the NovaSeq X's late-2022 launch, 2023 was a "very strong year" for high-throughput instruments (the large machines used for the biggest sequencing programs), with "more than 400" placed across the NovaSeq X and NovaSeq 6000 combined.
- Thaysen called the NovaSeq X "the most successful launch in the history of Illumina."
- **Orders and shipments:** The transcript is garbled ("900 orders since launch, and we have shipped 350, 390 orders…"). What is clear is that Illumina shipped 352 NovaSeq X instruments in 2023 and entered 2024 with a backlog of 38 instruments (orders received but not yet shipped).
- **Versus plan:** In Q&A Thaysen said Illumina had expected 330 to 340 NovaSeq X placements for the year and "actually saw more than 350."
- **Consumables:** Illumina saw higher-than-expected growth in NovaSeq X consumables in Q4, especially after the launch of the 25B flow cell. Goswami said the NovaSeq X consumables were "finally" starting to flow through and that this is positive momentum for later quarters.
- **Installed base:** More than 25,000 instruments are installed worldwide, and Illumina placed more than 2,200 instruments across all platforms in 2023 (the transcript says "this year").

### Q4 drivers (Q&A)

- Asked what drove Q4 and whether budget flush (customers spending leftover annual budgets late in the year) or pull-forward demand played a role, Thaysen said it was "probably less about the budget flush; we only saw small of that."
- He credited a strong NovaSeq X pipeline, customers who bought one instrument starting to buy several, and better consumable pickup after the 25B launch. The 25B is a flow cell (the consumable on which sequencing happens) that customers had been waiting for so they could start their large programs.
- Goswami later added that Illumina also "revamped some of the capabilities in the 10B flow cell," and that together these produced a "nice healthy uptick" of NovaSeq X consumables in late 2023 that he expects to continue in 2024.

## Product, ecosystem and strategy themes

- **End-to-end ambition:** Thaysen sees a big opportunity to be a full "sample-to-insights" solution provider. Customers told him they want to focus on their applications and research rather than build workflows, and want productivity and ease of use. Illumina's offering spans the throughput range, with built-in informatics including DRAGEN (its data analysis software). He called Illumina's informatics "a well-kept secret" that differentiates it.
- **Clinical continuity:** Illumina's diagnostic-cleared (regulatory-cleared for clinical use) portfolio spans throughput levels, so clinical customers can scale without leaving cleared instruments. The ecosystem allows "seamless scaling" from low to mid throughput for clinical and research users.
- **25B flow cell:** Launched late in Q4 2023 and "highly anticipated"; Thaysen said it "truly unleashed the full power of the NovaSeq X."
- **XLEAP-SBS chemistry:** The company's newer sequencing chemistry, which Illumina will make available on the NovaSeq 6000 platform in 2024. (The transcript says "NovaSeq 1K and 2K," which appears to mean the 1000 and 2000 models of the NovaSeq 6000 family, not the NovaSeq 6000 model.) Thaysen said it will bring better performance, faster turnaround, higher quality and lower cost, and can be deployed on the entire installed base of those instruments.
- **Acquisition:** Illumina recently acquired Partek, a specialized multiomics software company (multiomics means combining several biological data types such as DNA, RNA and proteins). Thaysen said it expands the company's capabilities and raises the value of its software portfolio. He said informatics is "key to us" for both genomics and multiomics.
- **Alliance for Genomics Discovery:** In collaboration with Nashville Biosciences (the transcript says "Nashville Bioscience"), announced the prior week, three new members joined: BMS, GSK and Novo Nordisk. They join founding members AbbVie, Amgen, AstraZeneca, Bayer and Merck. The program will whole-genome sequence more than 250,000 samples and combine them with longitudinal health data (health records over time) so pharma companies can mine the data. Thaysen said this "will actually change how we think about drug discovery… but actually healthcare going forward," and that more such initiatives are coming. Goswami later said the alliance shows pharma is at "the cusp" of using genomics and multiomics in drug discovery. (He called it the "NASH bio alliance," which appears to be a transcription error.)

## 2024 outlook [JPM, 2024-01-09]

- **Preliminary view:** "Our preliminary view is that our results will look very similar to '23," repeating what he said on the Q3 2023 call. Formal guidance will come with Q4/full-year earnings on February 8, 2024.
- **Macro:** Thaysen is "very encouraged by the recent positive macroeconomic headlines," but until conditions improve he expects customers to remain "cautious and constrained," which means modest instrument purchases, smaller project scopes and slower launch of new tests. If the market improves, Illumina is "very well positioned to accelerate growth."
- **Clarification on what was guided:** Asked whether flat revenue and flat EPS (earnings per share) were still in the cards, Thaysen corrected the premise: "we didn't make any comments about EPS." The earlier remarks concerned top-line revenue growth and operating margins, which he said would be flat for the core business.
- **First half versus second half:** Illumina is not differentiating between the halves. Thaysen noted that a year earlier many companies expected a tough first half and a better second half, "and that didn't turn out to be the case." Goswami agreed that macro is the same in both halves, apart from normal seasonality (a typical pattern of different demand in different times of year). The one difference he cited is the continued ramp-up in NovaSeq X utilization (how heavily customers run the instruments), which will make the halves differ.
- **NovaSeq X placements in 2024:** Expected to be lower than 2023's, "primarily driven by the big backlog that we entered into '23 with," though Thaysen expects incoming orders to gain momentum during the year. He expects fleet expansion in 2024 as customers who placed initial orders now scale up. Over time he expects most NovaSeq 6000 customers to move to the X, and customers not doing high-throughput sequencing today to join as well.
- **Strategic focus:** While Illumina will keep placing instruments, the 2024 focus is "ensuring that our customers are successful" with instruments already bought and driving sequencing volume on them, which drives consumables in 2024 and beyond.

## Three priorities for 2024 [JPM, 2024-01-09]

1. **Customers first and commercial excellence.** Grow the installed base, especially NovaSeq X, support customers' sequencing activity, and continue partnerships and initiatives that drive more activity.
2. **Operational excellence.** Greater rigor throughout the P&L (profit and loss statement), better margins, more productivity and cost reduction.
   - **Cost savings:** In early 2023 Illumina committed to cutting $100 million of costs and, per Thaysen, achieved about $175 million. The actions were headcount reductions, process streamlining, rationalizing the global footprint, reducing third-party vendor spend, and accelerating IT optimization.
   - **2024:** Continued supply-chain productivity, plus a newly implemented "portfolio optimization strategy" focused on innovation with the highest customer impact. Thaysen said Illumina will be "deliberate about where we make our investments and where we don't," with effects visible in 2024, and that the aim is both productivity and being "more predictable in how we run the business."
3. **Resolve GRAIL quickly** (see below).

## NovaSeq X adoption and price elasticity

Price elasticity here means that as sequencing gets cheaper per sample, customers run many more samples, so volume rises.

- Thaysen said the X is still "very early in the adoption curve." Lower cost and the ability to run different workflows on different lanes of one flow cell are driving both more samples and more complex analysis.
- **Customer example [Thaysen]:** A research customer runs bulk RNA on one lane and single-cell RNA on another and combines them for deeper insight that "could not have been done before."
- **Clinical shift [Thaysen]:** Cancer-focused clinical customers are moving from sequencing tissue to liquid biopsy (testing tumor DNA in blood), which requires much higher sequencing intensity. He said greater demand is "a matter of when and not if."
- **Goswami on elasticity:**
  - The X has already produced the expected "spurt in demand and elasticity," and customers who adopted the X behave differently from those who have not.
  - Research customers transition faster than clinical ones, whose transition is more gradual because of validated assays (tests locked in and approved on a specific system).
  - The main difference between the NovaSeq 6000 launch era (2017-18) and now is macro conditions: no high inflation or interest-rate constraint then. Illumina placed close to 400 high-throughput instruments "in a high interest, high inflation environment," which he said shows the strength of demand. The ramp will be tempered until macro improves.
  - **Why revenue is not rising despite elasticity:** In a customer's first year on a new instrument there is a step change down in price, which offsets the growth in sequencing volume. After year one that price drop disappears, so elasticity keeps building over multiple years without the same offset.
- **Macro effects by segment [Thaysen]:** Since mid-2023, both segments have been affected. Profitable clinical customers who historically would accelerate spending onto new platforms (as with the 6000) are now more cautious, slowing the move to the X. In research, slower economies mean less funding. Both segments still show ramping sequencing activity, and he expects improvement.

## Long-term market view

- Asked whether the sequencing market is slowing, Thaysen said the field is still early in the "genomic transformation." Few patients with disease are sequenced today. Moving from solid-tumor to liquid biopsy significantly increases sequencing. More diseases are becoming relevant for sequencing, and he called MRD (minimal residual disease, detecting leftover cancer after treatment) a big upcoming opportunity where Illumina is "just scratching the surface." Early cancer detection will drive demand for "many, many years and likely decades." He said the industry may just be at an inflection while moving "from one platform to the other," with "absolutely no doubt" about long-term opportunity.
- Goswami added that sequencing will "disrupt industries," that pharma drug discovery will see a step change, and that clinical customers will need larger gene panels rather than single-gene tests as biology proves complex. Customers are already going after bigger panels. He agreed the transition is very early.
- **Long-term targets:** Asked about the last Analyst Day's long-term goals (mid-teens growth and a margin profile), Thaysen said he is still working with the team to "solidify and identify exactly our strategic direction" and will take time so the company has "great confidence" in what it commits to. "I'm not ready to provide any numbers at this point." He still believes there is "significant growth in front of us" and sees no reason Illumina could not return to its "historic margin numbers in the future."

## China

- Thaysen said China faces challenges that go "beyond sequencing" and that the market has been challenged for years.
- China is "approximately 10% of our business." He expects it to remain challenged in 2024 while Illumina realigns its strategy and becomes more "made in China," with improved performance expected in 2025 and beyond.
- He said Chinese companies want to work with Illumina because it drives innovation and offers certainty and high quality when patients are involved. He added that China increasingly requires local presence and clarity on what counts as a Chinese player. (The question also noted increased local competition; the answer did not address it directly.)

## GRAIL [JPM, 2024-01-09]

- **Background:** On December 17, 2023, Illumina announced it would divest GRAIL, consistent with the European divestiture order.
- **Options:** Either a third-party sale or a capital markets transaction (Goswami: a spin-off or "some sort of sponsored spin"). "All options" are on the table.
- **Timing:** The goal is to finalize terms by the end of Q2 2024.
- **Valuation:** Goswami said it is too early to discuss, and "let the market drive that process."
- **Balance sheet:** Outcomes could affect Illumina's balance sheet, and it is too early for specifics, but the company is working to minimize impacts. Illumina is evaluating sponsors that could provide financial support if needed. If Illumina has to fund anything, it would more likely issue debt while prioritizing its investment-grade credit rating.

## Competition

- Thaysen welcomed competition ("great for the industry… great for our customers, and it keeps us on our toes") and said Illumina has competed throughout its history and done well. Asked about new entrants in mid- and high-throughput short-read sequencing and pricing effects, he gave no pricing data.
- **His argument:** Illumina "trained the whole world" to focus on cost per sequencing over the last decade. As that cost falls, the full workflow becomes more important: sample prep automation, sequencing, informatics and pipelines. Customers now want lower end-to-end cost, productivity and ease of use. He said a singular focus on sequencing cost "is going to be less relevant." Building a full workflow takes big investment that "very few companies can do" at Illumina's scale and breadth.
- "I love winning. But what I more love than that is to go out there and make a difference for our customers every day."

## Long-read versus short-read

- Short-read sequencing reads short fragments of DNA, while long-read reads much longer stretches. Thaysen said "there's room for everyone" and that Illumina is investing in long-read, but argued the discussion should start with samples. Most growth comes from FFPE (preserved tumor tissue) solid-tumor samples and liquid biopsies, both of which are already fragmented DNA and so suit short-read. Short-read will be "the vast majority of sequencing right now, but also in the future."
- **Goswami on population genomics (PopGen):** In large programs with hundreds of thousands of samples, long-read is used only on a subset of about 10,000, "less than 10% of the overall population of analysis." The approaches are complementary, not replacement. He said existing long-read technologies need large quantities of DNA and cost several-fold more, so there is no need for long-read except for answers short-read cannot give.

## Order book and 25B outlook (closing Q&A)

Goswami said Illumina enters 2024 with a backlog of 38 instruments and a "very healthy pipeline" of orders to convert in 2024. He sees the 25B flow cell as very positive, and expects the late-2023 consumables pickup to continue into 2024.

## Tone and what was new

- **Tone:** Upbeat and confident but cautious on near-term demand. Thaysen repeatedly stressed "when, not if," avoided numeric long-term commitments, and framed 2024 as a year of discipline and customer success.
- **New in this session versus prior commentary:**
  - Preliminary Q4/FY2023 numbers beat the company's own expectations.
  - More than 350 NovaSeq X shipped in 2023 against an expected 330-340.
  - Backlog of 38 instruments.
  - Cost savings of about $175 million versus the $100 million target.
  - Partek acquisition.
  - Three new Alliance for Genomics Discovery members.
  - GRAIL terms targeted by end of Q2 2024, with debt as the likely funding route if needed.
  - China about 10% of sales, still challenged in 2024.
  - Clarification that no EPS guidance had been given.
  - Long-term Analyst Day targets not reaffirmed.
- **Next catalyst:** Q4 and full-year 2023 earnings call on February 8, 2024, where more guidance detail was promised.
