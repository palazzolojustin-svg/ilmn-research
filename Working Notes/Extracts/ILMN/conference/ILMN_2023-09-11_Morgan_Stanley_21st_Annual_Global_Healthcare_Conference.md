# Illumina (ILMN) at the Morgan Stanley 21st Annual Global Healthcare Conference, Sep 11, 2023 – CFO Joydeep Goswami fireside Q&A

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/conference/ILMN_2023-09-11_Morgan_Stanley_21st_Annual_Global_Healthcare_Conference.md` · Date: 2023-09-11 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Morgan Stanley 21st Annual Global Healthcare Conference; host analyst Tejas Savant (Morgan Stanley); company speakers Joydeep Goswami (CFO and Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer) and Salli Schwartz (VP Investor Relations) · conference*

**Summary.** Days after Jacob Thaysen was named Illumina's new CEO, CFO Joydeep Goswami defended the recently cut 2023 sequencing guidance, blaming China (macro weakness, local-supplier preference, and stronger competition from BGI) and a slower-than-expected NovaSeq X rollout. He said NovaSeq X has reached about 110 instruments (about 10% of the roughly 1,000 NovaSeq customers), that mid-throughput win rates are unchanged outside China, and that the 2024 25% operating-margin target is now a "stretch" that the new CEO will weigh in on. On GRAIL, Illumina is waiting for the European Commission's new divestiture order and is preparing for divestiture, which becomes a certainty if it loses either pending court case.

# Illumina at the Morgan Stanley Healthcare Conference, September 11, 2023

**Format and participants.** This is a fireside-style question-and-answer session. The host is Tejas Savant, who covers life science tools and diagnostics at Morgan Stanley. Illumina was represented by Joydeep Goswami (Chief Financial Officer and Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer) and Salli Schwartz (Vice President of Investor Relations). Almost all answers come from Goswami, and Schwartz adds a few points. There are no prepared remarks. The tone of management is confident and defensive about competition, candid about missed internal expectations, and cautious about 2024 margins and the GRAIL outcome.

## 1. New CEO: Jacob Thaysen

Savant opened by noting the "big news last week" that Jacob Thaysen had been appointed CEO. He said the pick seemed to signal a fresh start with renewed focus on the core business, and he pointed to Thaysen's margin track record as a point of emphasis in the press release. He asked whether Goswami agreed.

- Goswami said the company was "thrilled." He said that in earlier conversations with investors, Illumina had emphasized prioritizing a CEO with industry experience, hired as quickly as possible, to remove uncertainty. He was "glad that's behind us." [Goswami, MS conference, 2023-09-11]
- He said Thaysen has extensive experience in diagnostics and in life sciences tools generally.
- He agreed that Illumina needs to get back to better margins and a stronger record of profitable growth. He said Thaysen "definitely" brings some of that, "especially on the stronger margins front at a scale company like Agilent." [Goswami]

## 2. The recent sequencing guidance cut (China, macro, NovaSeq 6000 consumables)

Savant framed the recent guidance cut as having three pieces: China, the macro environment, and consumables (the reagent kits customers must buy for every run) for the older NovaSeq 6000.

### 2a. China: how much is competition versus macro

Savant asked how much of the weakness in mid- and low-throughput sequencing in China comes from competition from BGI (the Chinese sequencer maker) versus general macro weakness. He also asked how much comes from government pressure toward local sourcing versus deeper discounting, and what role China's anti-corruption crackdown plays.

- **No quantitative split.** Goswami said Illumina has not broken these factors out numerically, but all of them play a role.
- **Original 2023 assumption.** Illumina went into the year expecting China to recover to normal growth levels, with no stimulus baked in. It expected the first half to be consumed by "getting out of the COVID funk" and getting labs running again.
- **What happened.** The first half played out as expected. In the second half of Q2 and for the rest of the year, Illumina "just did not see" the recovery happening. Goswami said that since Illumina reported, "most companies have reported a very similar kind of outlook in China," and he sees no near-term recovery that can be predicted. [Goswami]
- **Structural restrictions.** The Chinese market is restricted for Illumina by:
  - enhanced competition;
  - government preference for local suppliers in certain tenders (competitive bidding processes);
  - the fact that Illumina, like most US companies, is not allowed to sell to all entities in China.
- **What the second-half guidance included.** Illumina's second-half guidance (set at Q2) included increased competitive intensity from BGI, which translates into more lost deals to BGI. Price also has an impact. Goswami said Illumina is "pretty directive" about which customers it will compete with on price and which it will not, and it assumes this will continue.
- **What it did not include.** The Q2 guidance did not include anything from the more recent hospital crackdowns in China. Goswami said Illumina is "very selective" about its direct customers and distributors and follows all Chinese and US government regulations, so it is "not concerned" about the crackdown itself. But if end customers are affected, Illumina has no control over that. He said he did not have an update on it yet.

### 2b. Why pressure shows up in mid-throughput but not high-throughput

Savant remarked that "it's tough to compete with free," and asked why competitive pressure appears only in mid-throughput and not in the high-throughput portfolio. He also asked why BGI could not gain traction in price-sensitive regions such as Asia and Latin America, even if the US and Western Europe are hard to crack.

Goswami's answer had several parts:

1. **Basis of competition differs by throughput and by customer.** In high throughput, price is one factor, but customers also value the workflow, back-end translation and data analysis, and data compression (enabled by the NovaSeq X and DRAGEN, Illumina's genomic data analysis software). Some customers also value backward compatibility of bioinformatics platforms and the newer "ambient ship" capabilities, meaning instruments and reagents that can ship at room temperature.
2. **Competition is not new.** Previously it was Thermo and, for a while, QIAGEN. He said "we know how to work and play with competition."
3. **A premium persists even in China.** Prices in China have been affected, but Illumina still extracts "a premium for the innovation and the quality of our products."
4. **Patent expiry in mid-throughput.** He said that when Illumina's original patents went off patent, more companies tried to copy the technology and came out with solutions. Illumina is aware of the price differential and still expects to extract a premium. It continues to innovate, citing the XLEAP chemistry coming to the NextSeq 1000/2000.
5. **Win rates.** As he said on the Q2 earnings call, Illumina has not seen a big change in mid-throughput win rates anywhere, including Asia, except China. What has changed, partly from competition and partly from macro, is that it takes longer to convert the funnel of opportunities. That is what drove the guidance cut.
6. **Schwartz addition.** Illumina is now selling the NovaSeq X, including into China, which she called "unparalleled or unrivaled," an offering "no one else can compete with" in high throughput. [Schwartz]

### 2c. China contagion to Europe

Asked whether he sees spillover to export-oriented European economies such as Germany, Goswami said "not as of this point."

- Germany is "one of our faster-growing markets in Europe." Like the UK, its government is investing in funding clinical applications and in moving research from translational (bench-to-clinic) work into clinical use, followed by reimbursement.
- Clinical strength continues. He noted press reports (he cited the Wall Street Journal) of German economic challenges. That has not translated into research plans so far, but Illumina will monitor it carefully.

### 2d. Year-end budget flush

Savant asked about confidence in the year-end budget flush (customers spending remaining annual budgets in Q4).

- Goswami said the guide assumed regular year-end seasonality. Illumina "has not assumed a very high level" of flush.
- Both sequencing and microarrays (chip-based genotyping products) typically see an uptick in the last quarter, including direct-to-consumer (DTC) arrays.
- The expected Q4-over-Q3 bump in guidance was tied to the launch of the 25B flow cell on the NovaSeq X, the highest-capacity flow cell. He expects an uptick as consumers adopt it.

## 3. NovaSeq X rollout

### 3a. Penetration

Savant noted a strong start for the NovaSeq X and Illumina's statement of about 10% penetration of the roughly 1,000 existing NovaSeq customers. He asked how penetration splits between about 750 single-instrument customers and about 250 multi-instrument customers.

- Goswami: customers have purchased about 110 NovaSeq X instruments, about 10% penetration.
- Single-instrument customers are at about 5% penetration, or roughly 50 customers. He called this logical because higher-throughput, multi-instrument customers are the most likely to switch early to higher capacity, so they have a higher switch rate. [Goswami]

### 3b. Technical issues and fixes

Savant asked about the technical issues disclosed during the rollout.

- Goswami said there were two types of issues in Q2, "above the norm of the tail of issues that come up." Fixes for both were deployed toward the end of Q2.
- A majority have been deployed. Those not yet deployed are mostly customer-related, such as vacations and lab readiness. He said Illumina is "well on our way."
- He said such issues are "not unprecedented" for an instrument like this, although Illumina had not anticipated them in Q2, and they were fixed quickly. A team is ready to address new issues as the X goes to new customers and applications.

### 3c. Smaller customers and sample access

Savant asked whether, as the upgrade cycle moves from power users to smaller customers, sample access is a concern: customers must either wait and batch samples, lengthening turnaround, or forgo the economics of the X.

- Goswami said different customers will choose differently. At the limit, the X's capacity is at least 2.5 times larger, so with the same sample count a customer would wait 2.5 times longer to batch. That is not what typically happens.
- Customers land "somewhere in the middle": they wait somewhat to batch but do not run at full capacity. As a result, they do not get the per-sample or per-gigabase price people assume. They pay a "slightly higher effective price that we recover."
- This was seen in the transition from HiSeq to NovaSeq 6000, and Illumina expects the same here. Customers generally never batch samples at the rate needed to fill a flow cell. [Goswami]

## 4. NextSeq 1000/2000 (mid-throughput), XLEAP, and competition from Element

### 4a. XLEAP timing and pricing

Savant noted XLEAP chemistry is coming to the NextSeq 1000/2000 in the first half of 2024. Given greater benchtop competition, and Element Biosciences' press release of 100 AVITI units, he asked about pricing and whether XLEAP could be pulled forward to Q1.

- Goswami: "we're on track" for the planned XLEAP launch "early next year." The exact date will be communicated in due course. He gave no pull-forward commitment.
- **Win rates.** Illumina is seeing neither acceleration nor deceleration in its mid-throughput win rate. It tracks this closely, and at least through the end of Q2 there was no noticeable change.
- **Element's claims.** He said Element and others talk about placements, not orders or purchases. He suggested they may be placing boxes at customers who have purchased an Illumina box. [Goswami]
- **Field evidence.** Illumina gets reports on how its and others' instruments perform in the field. It feels "fairly confident" that customers will still want its technology and that, on a total-cost-of-operation basis, it will keep winning and charging a premium.
- **Pricing direction.** The premium "remains the same," but he said dollar-per-gigabase prices will continue to come down in mid-throughput, and that is already in Illumina's model.

### 4b. New customers and pull-through

Savant said about 15% to 20% of NovaSeq X orders came from customers new to Illumina or new to high-throughput. (The question refers to "next weeks" in the transcript, but the context shows it refers to NextSeq-class and X orders. Goswami clarified he understood it as mid-throughput.) He asked about the replacement ratio for NextSeq upgrades and whether the upgrade dynamic would put downward pressure on the per-instrument annual consumables pull-through range.

- Goswami: no downward push expected. People upgrade or buy because they expect a volume that gives them the economics they want.
- The range given earlier is about $120,000 to $170,000 of consumables pull-through per instrument per year. Illumina still expects to stabilize within that range.
- Where it lands in any year depends on sequencing throughput growth. It is harder to predict because the same number of samples used for more single-cell or spatial analysis implies running the instruments harder.
- **Schwartz addition.** Some of the new-to-Illumina share is brand-new customers. The X's ambient-ship capability lets Illumina sell into places it had not historically placed boxes, which is "truly incremental." [Schwartz]

## 5. Pricing philosophy

Savant asked why Illumina, as market leader, does not become the price leader and pre-empt emerging competitors, stimulate demand elasticity, cut costs more aggressively and protect margins.

- Goswami said that to a large extent that is what Illumina has been doing. It is "fairly calculated" and looks at the value its technology delivers, not just price.
- Pricing is set based on the customer's total cost of operation. If workflow improvements radically cut customers' data storage or back-end analysis needs, Illumina would not "give away all the boundary of that capability" in consumable or instrument prices.
- He said Illumina is leading the market rather than following, but "it isn't always just going down the bottom level of price in every workflow." He said competitors' price comparisons do not capture the full value provided.
- On proactive versus reactive pricing: new innovations are priced proactively. Sometimes it has to be reactive. He said Illumina cannot say it will never match or adjust price, and it does not do that, but it is "very selective" about which customers get which prices, based on which customers can drive greater sequencing throughput and value for investors. [Goswami]

Savant then asked whether the whole industry is shifting from price to value, since a race to the bottom helps no one.

- Goswami said it will happen anyway. At some point sequencing is a smaller part of the end customer's overall cost base, so further compressing sequencing cost without addressing other pain points has "limited utility."
- Examples he gave of other needs: using the sequencer for more things, such as other multiomics (proteomics, methylation), long reads, or RNA. "We just dropped the price on DNA, it's not going to help."
- This is why Illumina invested in DRAGEN and in data compression, which were becoming a pain point especially for high-throughput customers who "start spending an inordinate amount of money" processing and compressing data.
- He expects more value-based competition "naturally" because that is what customers are asking for. [Goswami]

## 6. 2024 margin target and cost structure

Savant said that even before Thaysen's announcement, Illumina reiterated a 25% operating margin target for core Illumina (excluding GRAIL) in 2024 but called it "stretchy" given the implied year-over-year improvement. He asked for the pushes and pulls beyond real estate optimization, SG&A cuts and natural leverage as the NovaSeq X matures, and whether R&D cuts are possible or R&D is a "sacred cow."

- **R&D.** Goswami: "R&D has not been a sacred cow." Cost cuts last year and this year were "very surgical" across the organization. Real estate was a broader tool, but "if you don't get to labor-based costs, you don't get to the level of cost realignment that we needed."
- **History.** Illumina had not planned to be at current margins. It invested ahead of the curve based on growth seen in 2021 and early 2022. When markets turned, its cost structure was clearly inappropriate for post-2022 growth, at least in the short term.
- **What is protected.** Cuts will be made across the organization as required, but Illumina is "not stepping back from our commitment to innovation." Programs necessary for core instruments and end-to-end assays will be treated as sacred. [Goswami]
- **Productivity.** He said this does not preclude productivity initiatives. At the end of Q2 Illumina said it had completed a cost reset, but it is also looking broadly at productivity and business transformation. The goal is to deliver innovation and commercial expansion in countries without adding much operating expense (OpEx), perhaps even reducing OpEx, and to improve cost of goods sold (COGS). Many such plans are being put in place and he feels "confident."
- **Why "stretch."** He said he wanted to be honest with investors. When the targets were set, Illumina had expected 7.5% growth this year and, going into next year, at least 22% margins. Illumina is "at 20% and close to flat for this year." So it must find additional savings to get margin expansion, "and we will get the margin expansion."
- **Deferral to new CEO.** He wanted to give Thaysen a chance to form his own views before Illumina comes back to investors. [Goswami]

Note: the transcript is ambiguous on the exact 2023 figures. Goswami's words were "7.5% growth this year" and "at 20% and close to flat for this year," which appear to refer to originally expected growth and margin versus where Illumina now stands.

## 7. GRAIL (divestiture, court cases, options)

Savant said he did not expect the European Commission (EC) divestiture order anytime soon, expected a decision in the FTC case by year-end, and expected the EU jurisdiction appeal verdict at year-end or early next year. He said most investors believe divestiture under new leadership is the most likely outcome, though terms and timelines depend on the appeals. He asked whether mid-2024 is a fair expectation for GRAIL to be off the books.

- **Waiting on the order.** Goswami reiterated earlier statements: Illumina is waiting for the new divestiture order, which "gates the options." It has provided all information requested, and the EC is not waiting on anything from Illumina.
- **Readiness.** Once the order arrives, Illumina has "everything locked and loaded" to begin lining up the best divestiture route.
- **Two court cases in parallel.** (1) The Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals in the US, which looks at the FTC's objections to the deal. (2) The European Court of Justice, which is different from the EC and looks at the application of Article 22 and a few other factors.
- **If Illumina wins both.** It has far more freedom to maximize GRAIL's value for shareholders: divestiture, keeping GRAIL with a very different cost structure, licensing IP, and others. He said these are not completely lined up but options exist.
- **If Illumina loses either one.** "Divestiture is a certainty," and planning has begun.
- **Timing.** Timing depends on which divestiture option is picked, and receipt of the divestiture notice is the other gating item. He did not endorse or reject mid-2024. [Goswami]

## 8. M&A and multiomics

Savant said that after the PacBio and GRAIL regulatory tangles on both sides of the Atlantic, large M&A may be "a bit of a lightning rod" right now. He asked how open Illumina is to pushing into multiomics via proteomics or spatial biology, adjacencies where it would not compete with its own customers and where there are synergies.

- Goswami: "we're already doing that." Not all M&A is out; Illumina has been doing tuck-in deals (small acquisitions) throughout. Larger M&A will be harder in the short term "for most companies, given where the regulators are these days."
- A "very experienced" BD (business development) team looks at technology licensing and partnerships to bring in technologies and optimize them for an Illumina workflow and Illumina bench, to create a distinguished product.
- Filters for end-to-end workflow investments: they must improve the sequencer's utility, bring customers value beyond what is already available, and address a market that is large enough and growing faster.
- Proteomics, spatial and others offer many opportunities for Illumina to participate directly or indirectly. [Goswami]

## 9. Strategic vision: hardware leader versus application owner

Savant closed by describing "competing visions" of Illumina's future: a company that excels at selling valuable hardware versus one that pushes into select applications and captures more of the value, at near-term cost (including GRAIL dilution). He asked whether the organization is culturally ready for a more circumscribed vision after years of selling hard on screening.

- **GRAIL was different.** Goswami called GRAIL "a very different kind of bet," with different customers and timeframe. Illumina knew from the beginning it was long-term, but it was huge: if it played out, it would "create a market which would rival the size of our business in the outer year." It required capabilities different from Illumina's, a build-versus-buy decision where buy was chosen.
- **"And," not "or."** For everything else, he said it is "an and issue." Capital is allocated to the core business to keep the lead in the best sequencer, which is not only about dropping price per gigabase. Illumina is "much more sophisticated," as with the X and X chemistry, which address what are now customer pain points. This continues at all throughput levels with launches that bring step changes in value, which he defines as more than price.
- **Modular innovation funds workflows.** Illumina has invested in modular capabilities that let it bring the next innovations at much lower cost. Some of the capital freed up can go to end-to-end workflows, which improve share of wallet, let customers pursue applications on the installed base of sequencers, make money for Illumina in library prep and back-end analysis, and drive more sequencing.
- **Timeline.** Illumina "pivoted" in this direction more than two to three years ago. He said investors should see some of it come through in 2024 and 2025 product launches. [Goswami]
