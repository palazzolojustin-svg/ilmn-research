# Illumina at the 43rd Annual JPMorgan Healthcare Conference (CEO/CFO presentation and Q&A with JPMorgan's Rachel Vatnsdal), with preliminary Q4/FY2024 results and 2025 outlook

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/conference/ILMN_2025-01-14_43rd_Annual_JPMorgan_Healthcare_Conference.md` · Date: 2025-01-14 · Seeking Alpha transcript of JPMorgan Healthcare Conference session; speakers Jacob Thaysen (CEO), Ankur Dhingra (CFO); host analyst Rachel Vatnsdal (JPMorgan, Life Science Tools and Diagnostics) · conference*

**Summary.** Illumina previewed better-than-expected Q4 2024 results (about $1.1 billion core revenue, 91 NovaSeq X placements) and gave a preliminary 2025 outlook of low single-digit constant-currency revenue growth, roughly 23% non-GAAP operating margin and about 10% non-GAAP EPS growth. Management reiterated its 2027 targets (high single-digit growth, 26% margin, double-digit-to-teens EPS growth) and described the NovaSeq X transition, new multiomics products (Constellation Mapped Reads, proteomics), partnerships (NVIDIA, Truveta), and competition in mid-throughput sequencing. Tone was confident and upbeat, with measured caution on 2025 placements and the pricing headwind from the X transition.

# Illumina at the 43rd Annual JPMorgan Healthcare Conference (January 14, 2025)

## Context and format

This was a 40-minute session at the JPMorgan Healthcare Conference, held January 14, 2025. Rachel Vatnsdal of JPMorgan's Life Science Tools and Diagnostics team hosted. Illumina was represented by CEO Jacob Thaysen and CFO Ankur Dhingra. The first half was a prepared presentation by Thaysen. The second half was Q&A, with Thaysen and Dhingra answering. Illumina's next full earnings call (Q4 and full-year 2024) was scheduled for February 6, 2025, where full 2025 guidance would be provided. All 2025 figures given here are explicitly "preliminary."

## Presentation: Thaysen's overview

Thaysen said he would cover four areas: his view of the business and opportunity; preliminary Q4 and full-year 2024 results with a 2025 outlook; progress on the new strategy; and the path ahead. [JPM, 2025-01-14]

### Illumina's position and the long-term opportunity

- Illumina pioneered next-generation sequencing (NGS, the technology that reads DNA in massive parallel batches) over more than 25 years. Thaysen said it is now integral to genomic research and clinical applications worldwide. [JPM, 2025-01-14]
- In 2024, more than 480 petabases of data (a petabase is a quadrillion DNA letters) were generated on Illumina sequencers. Thaysen said this is equivalent to roughly 5 million whole genomes. [JPM, 2025-01-14]
- 56% of Illumina's sequencing consumables revenue (the reagent kits customers must buy for each run) now comes from clinical customers. [JPM, 2025-01-14]
- Management claim: revenue is highly diversified across products, markets and geographies, supporting a strong business model, healthy free cash flow and a solid balance sheet. [JPM, 2025-01-14]
- Thaysen's decade view: NGS becomes deeply embedded in health care for personalized care; sovereign nations embrace whole genome sequencing, moving "from sick care into health care"; multiomics (measuring many biological layers beyond DNA, such as RNA and proteins) unlocks deeper insights; and AI-powered cohorts of whole-genome and multiomic data help transform partners' drug discovery. [JPM, 2025-01-14]
- The company is trying to shift the industry from a sole focus on cost per gigabase (the cost of generating each billion DNA letters) toward "the highest quality insight for the lowest end-to-end cost." [JPM, 2025-01-14]
- Market size: more than a $125 billion total addressable market, of which only about $10 billion is currently served by NGS. [JPM, 2025-01-14]
- Clinical drivers: genomics becoming standard of care for therapy selection, early detection and MRD (minimal residual disease, testing for leftover cancer after treatment), plus expansion into other diseases. Research drivers: multiomics requiring high-intensity sequencing and many more samples. [JPM, 2025-01-14]

### Preliminary Q4 and full-year 2024 results

Thaysen said Q4 2024 exceeded Illumina's expectations, "primarily driven by greater and as expected NovaSeq X shipments." The NovaSeq X is Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer. [JPM, 2025-01-14]

| Metric (Q4 2024, preliminary) | Figure |
|---|---|
| Core revenue | About $1.1 billion, up about 1% from the prior year |
| Non-GAAP operating margin | About 19.7% |
| Non-GAAP EPS | $0.91 to $0.93 |

Thaysen said full-year results were also shown on a slide, but the transcript does not give those figures. He credited innovation, product launches, customer focus and operational excellence for progress on the refreshed strategy, and said improving margins "even under tough market conditions" showcases the new operating model. [JPM, 2025-01-14]

### Preliminary 2025 outlook

The outlook is based on current market and economic conditions and does not attempt to reflect potential legislative changes. [JPM, 2025-01-14]

- Revenue growth: low single-digit percent on a constant-currency basis (excluding exchange-rate effects).
- Reported revenue: $4.28 billion to $4.4 billion.
- Non-GAAP operating margin: about 23%, a 170 basis point improvement from 2024 (a basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point). Thaysen tied this to operational excellence.
- Non-GAAP EPS growth: about 10%, helped by focus "below the line" (items beneath operating income).

### Long-term 2027 targets (set at the August 2024 strategy update)

- High single-digit revenue growth, 500 basis points of operating margin expansion, and double-digit to teens EPS growth for 2025 through 2027. Thaysen later stated the 2027 operating margin goal as 26%.
- Thaysen said 2024 was a "solid initial step," 2025 gets closer, and the company is "well on track." He added that 2027 "is not the end of the journey, we're just getting started." [JPM, 2025-01-14]

### Capital allocation (four priorities, in order)

1. Invest in the business: a pipeline spanning sequencing platforms, multiomics technologies, and services, software and data offerings.
2. Bolt-on M&A that expands addressable markets, plus strategic investments and partnerships.
3. Maintain a solid balance sheet and an investment grade credit rating.
4. Share buybacks intended to be antidilutive over time, with potential additional opportunistic buybacks. Illumina resumed buybacks after the August 2024 strategy update and repurchased about $116 million of stock over the rest of 2024.

### Three growth drivers to return to high single-digit growth by 2027

**1. NovaSeq X.** Thaysen said the X is setting a new standard, enabling deeper, higher-quality sequencing and serving as the platform for multiomics. [JPM, 2025-01-14]
- Illumina placed 91 X systems in Q4 2024 alone.
- Pull-through (consumables revenue per instrument) averaged $1.3 million per X instrument in 2024.
- By the second half of 2025, roughly 75% of total high-throughput gigabases shipped and about 50% of total high-throughput consumables revenue should be on the X. Management says that as volume moves to the X, the impact of the reduced X pricing lessens and more volume translates into revenue growth.
- Thaysen said data sequenced over the past couple of years shows customers use the X for deeper and broader sequencing, which "underpins my confidence in our high throughput consumables growth."
- MiSeq i100 (a small benchtop sequencer): announced October 2024, shipping began in December, and more than 70 instruments were placed before year-end. Some customers plan to use it to test experiments before running at scale on the NovaSeq X. More detail was promised for the earnings call.

**2. Multiomics.**
- Constellation Mapped Reads: key opinion leaders (KOLs, influential researchers/clinicians) are "thrilled." It helps detect large structural variants (big rearrangements of DNA) that are hard to see with short reads, with a much simpler workflow. One KOL said that until Constellation, it had been impossible with any technology to detect the most common causes of hemophilia A. Thaysen said Illumina has now made that possible.
- Proteomics (measuring proteins): developed jointly with Standard BioTools and launching in the first half of 2025. Thaysen described it as a complete end-to-end solution with an easier, automated workflow than other on-market products. It uses a 9,000-protein panel, which he called the largest orthogonally validated NGS proteomics panel in the industry. Recent publications showed a median coefficient of variation (a measure of reproducibility; lower is better) under 5% across all 9,000 proteins, and that it significantly outperforms other on-market NGS proteomics products on precision and reproducibility. Thaysen said it "will become the standard going forward" and pointed listeners to Standard BioTools' Thursday presentation about the SOMAmer technology (the protein-binding reagents) behind it.
- These solutions run on Illumina's software stack, including the DRAGEN-led suite (DRAGEN is Illumina's genomic data analysis software/hardware).

**3. Services, software and data.** Thaysen said multiomic data from customers' population studies and from drug discovery support this driver. Illumina is partnering with leading players at the intersection of technology, healthcare and pharma, citing announcements made the day before (NVIDIA, Truveta, and others). [JPM, 2025-01-14]

### Closing message

- Thaysen: "We will return to growth in 2025." He said the NovaSeq X series will drive the majority of 2025 growth, with multiomics and a greater focus on services, software and data adding growth layers in 2026 and 2027. [JPM, 2025-01-14]

## Q&A with Rachel Vatnsdal (JPMorgan)

### NVIDIA collaboration (and Truveta)

Vatnsdal asked about the first phase and goals of the NVIDIA collaboration on multiomic data analysis, and how it fits long-term plans. Thaysen said multiomics generates enormous data that no human can analyze "with an Excel sheet," so AI will be essential. The relationship will (1) bring DRAGEN to GPU and CPU platforms, in addition to its current FPGA (hardware-accelerated chip) form, so any customer can use it, and (2) make NVIDIA's tools for building foundation models (large AI models) available on Illumina's platform, especially for research customers. He said "we will all sit here in a few years and… see this is the starting of a new era," changing health care, treatment and drug discovery. He said "we're just getting started." [JPM, 2025-01-14]

### What drove the 91 NovaSeq X placements in Q4

Vatnsdal noted that prior guidance implied roughly 60 placements in Q4 (second-half guidance assumed more than the first half's 117) and asked what drove the beat and whether reagent rental (providing instruments in exchange for reagent purchases) was involved.
- Thaysen: customers are planning for 2025 and continuing to transition to the X, which drives significantly more volume. Adoption is not only from new customers; existing customers who had one or two Xs are buying five-packs or 10-packs.
- Dhingra: 91 was "well beyond our expectations." Illumina had always said its instrument pipeline was robust, but its guide assumed deal closure had elongated through the first three quarters and it could not call year-end. At year-end, there was "pretty sizable interest" from clinical customers, several of whom bought multiple X units together. He read this as a capacity addition signal ahead of newer applications such as MRD. Deal closure rates were better than anticipated. He did not address reagent rental in his answer.

### Q4 consumables trend

Vatnsdal asked about intra-quarter consumables trends and whether back-of-the-envelope math of about $720–730 million of Q4 consumables revenue was right. Neither executive confirmed or corrected the number.
- Thaysen: Q4 is always a bit slower for consumables because of fewer working days and holiday breaks, and consumables are a run-rate business, so a sequential step down was expected.
- Dhingra: the transition from the NovaSeq 6000 to the X in high throughput went very well. Underlying sequencing activity stayed robust, described as up 30% to 40% in the first three quarters, and remained strong in Q4. Illumina had guided to lower Q4 volume, and utilization dipped between Thanksgiving and Christmas, landing "in the ranges of where we were anticipating." Specifics were deferred to the earnings call. He said instruments and underlying demand set up 2025 well.

### NIH funding and the new US administration

Thaysen said NIH budgets are set in the fall, so he does not expect much change in 2025. Genomics and multiomics are areas that "likely will see increased funding" even if the overall NIH budget is under pressure. He said he is "more positive today than maybe we were a few weeks ago, a few months ago," expects puts and takes, and noted "we have seen a lot of curveballs over the last five years," so this is not a bigger challenge than the company can overcome. Vatnsdal also asked whether customer behavior had shifted since the election; no specific change was cited.

### Assumptions behind low single-digit 2025 growth

- Thaysen: no changes assumed in the macroeconomic environment or legislation. 2025 is still a transition year for the X: the price reduction taken on the X is still visible while volume comes through, so consumables revenue growth will be positive but "still not to the level that corresponds to the actually underlying volume." By mid-2025, 75% of volume and 50% of revenue should be on the X, so volume converts to revenue more.
- Dhingra: the first half and part of the second half remain transition, embedded in guidance. New launches (Constellation Mapped Reads in core genome; single cell and proteomics in multiomics) position Illumina well on the research side regardless of funding shifts. Geopolitics is a factor to watch as policies under the new administration emerge. Strong Q4 X installs give confidence in the transition and in customers' belief in the multiomics strategy.

### Levers for 2025 margin expansion (about 23%, +170 bps)

Vatnsdal asked how much comes from gross margin and pricing versus operating expenses.
- Thaysen: 2024 proved Illumina can improve margins in tough markets. He credited "operational excellence," a company-wide program to do things better, and said it is not simply cost cutting. He stated that expected 2025 growth "is not strong enough to drive the overall margin improvement," so it will be driven by operational excellence.
- Dhingra: improvement will come across all lines of the P&L, in both gross margin and operating expenses. Illumina will keep investing in innovation and the ecosystem (Truveta, NVIDIA) while expanding margins.

### 2025 placement trajectory

Thaysen said it would be "getting a little ahead of ourselves" to multiply 91 by four and he does not expect that. Q4 is always the strongest quarter for instrument placements, and "we do expect maybe to see a lower level into '25." Dhingra said details would come with earnings and he was very happy with the full-year placements.

### Ramp to high single-digit growth by 2027

Thaysen: 2024 growth was "negative a few percent" and 2025 moves to low single digits, a good step up, with similar steps expected afterwards. Most of the lift comes from having moved most volume to the X by 2026, so consumables volume translates to more revenue. He called it "a continuous movement" and not a step function. Additional growth layers include multiomics (proteomics launching in 2025, single cell coming), which he said will not be meaningful before 2026–2027, and a new organization called SAGA that focuses on the array business and services, expected to have impact in those years. He repeated that 2027 is a milestone, not the end.

### Constellation Mapped Reads: what changed since the strategy update

Vatnsdal noted Constellation was not named in the August strategy slides and asked what changed and about pricing for the planned 2026 commercial launch.
- Thaysen: it was presented in August as "comprehensive genome." Illumina set out a few years ago to eliminate sample prep so customers can go from sample to loaded flow cell in minutes. He said "I've tried it. It actually works." Without fragmenting DNA, long DNA fragments (up to millions of bases) sit on the flow cell, and because the instrument has a camera, reads can be associated with their physical position on the DNA. This reveals structural variants and phasing (which variants sit on the same chromosome copy), which short reads could not show. He said "we believe this is going to be the standard of the genome going forward."
- Demand exceeds capacity to serve: Illumina "can't work with all the researchers and clinicians that actually want to look at this right now," and the technology needs to mature for a 2026 launch. He gave no pricing strategy.

### NovaSeq X transition and 2025 pricing impact

Dhingra: over 60% of volume has now transitioned. Pricing impact of the transition is higher in the first half of 2025 and should begin to abate in the second half as more volume moves to the X. Q4 pace was robust, and customer interest in taking advantage of higher volumes and lower sequencing cost is "very, very high," as evidenced by instrument placements.

### Competition in mid-throughput

Thaysen acknowledged "a little more competition now than we've seen maybe a few years ago," but said Illumina still wins many deals in mid-throughput and that competition "just sharpens your thinking." He named no competitors. Responses:
- XLEAP chemistry: new chemistry with higher performance at a lower price point, resonating with customers and repositioning the mid-throughput portfolio.
- MiSeq i100: technically low throughput but higher capacity, pushing into mid-throughput. It offers a simple workflow, fast turnaround, and reagents that ship and store at room temperature, eliminating freezer logistics and freeing lab space. He said this "is what we're going to do for all our platforms going forward."
- Commercial initiatives: he said many were underway but gave no detail.

### R&D priorities

Thaysen said Illumina spent the past year speaking with customers to align the roadmap with their needs. The shift from cost per gigabase to highest quality insight at lowest end-to-end cost means being more application-focused: "customers are not looking for a sequencer. They're looking for an application." Examples: the end-to-end proteomics solution, an end-to-end single cell solution, and an end-to-end whole genome solution with Constellation. Focus areas are both genomics and multiomics. He called informatics "almost still the best kept secret" and said Illumina's software stack is differentiated.

## Management tone and what was new

- Tone: confident and enthusiastic, with deliberate restraint about 2025 placements and about the continued X pricing headwind in the first half.
- New versus prior commentary: preliminary Q4/FY2024 results beat internal expectations; the 91 X placements compared with an implied guide of roughly 60; first 2025 outlook figures; 70+ MiSeq i100 placements before year-end; 56% clinical share of consumables; $116 million of buybacks in the rest of 2024; NVIDIA and Truveta partnerships; and Constellation Mapped Reads positioned for a 2026 commercial launch.
- Not provided in this session: full-year 2024 figures, Q4 consumables revenue, Q4 instrument or services revenue, Constellation pricing, and any specific competitor names.
