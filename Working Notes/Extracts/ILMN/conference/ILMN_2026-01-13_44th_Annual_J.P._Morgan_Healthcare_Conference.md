# Illumina at the 44th Annual J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference (Q4 2025 preannouncement, strategy and Q&A)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/conference/ILMN_2026-01-13_44th_Annual_J.P._Morgan_Healthcare_Conference.md` · Date: 2026-01-13 · Seeking Alpha transcript of J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference; Illumina speakers Jacob Thaysen (CEO) and Ankur Dhingra (CFO); host analyst Casey Woodring, JPMorgan Life Science Tools and Diagnostics · conference*

**Summary.** Illumina used JPM to preannounce Q4 2025 results that beat its own expectations, with revenue up 4% in constant currency and 7% excluding China, driven by instruments, consumables and China in roughly equal thirds. Management said clinical customers drove the strength, that the NovaSeq X transition is working as modeled, and that 2026 should look like the second half of 2025, with research and academic demand still muted. The Q&A covered the beat, placements, volume growth, whole genome sequencing, competition, China's sanctions and the unreliable entity list, multiomics and the new BioInsight business.

# Illumina at the 44th Annual J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference (January 13, 2026)

## Event and participants

This is a transcript of Illumina's presentation and fireside-style Q&A at the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference, held January 13, 2026 (10:30 AM EST per the header). Jacob Thaysen (CEO) delivered the presentation. Casey Woodring of JPMorgan's Life Science Tools and Diagnostics team hosted the Q&A, joined by Ankur Dhingra (CFO). Management said full-year 2026 guidance would come with the fourth-quarter and full-year 2025 earnings call on February 5, 2026. Tone throughout was confident and upbeat, with openly stated hope that 2026 would be a "more straightforward" year than 2025.

## Prepared remarks by Jacob Thaysen

### Company profile
- Thaysen described Illumina as the market leader in next-generation sequencing (NGS, the technology that reads DNA/RNA at high speed and scale), with the largest and most diverse installed base in the industry: more than 20,000 instruments globally across clinical, research and applied markets. [JPM, 2026-01-13]
- The flagship NovaSeq X launched in 2023 (the transcript reads "2025 -- 2023"). Since then, Illumina has "doubled our customer sequencing capacity." [JPM, 2026-01-13]
- Roughly 60% of sequencing consumables (the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every run) now come from clinical customers. Thaysen said this supports a durable financial profile with a high level of recurring revenue and healthy margins. [JPM, 2026-01-13]

### Preliminary Q4 2025 results (published the morning of the conference)
Management said the preliminary results were "well ahead of our expectations."

| Metric | Figure | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Q4 2025 revenue growth | 4% (constant currency, which strips out exchange-rate effects) | Management called it above expectations |
| Q4 2025 revenue growth excluding China | 7% | Ex-China is about 97% of revenue |
| Q4 2025 EPS (earnings per share) | $1.27 to $1.30 | Preliminary range |
| Full-year 2025 EPS | About $4.76 to $4.79 | Described as earnings growth of over 15% |
| NovaSeq X placements, Q4 2025 | More than 95 | Compared with 91 in Q4 2024 |

| Share of high-throughput gigabases shipped on X (year-end 2025) | About 80% | Was 75% (gigabases) at Q3 per the host/CEO |
| 2025 margin expansion | About 200 basis points (2 percentage points) | |

- Thaysen attributed the above-expectation performance to both instruments and consumables, the latter driven by continued strong NovaSeq X utilization. He noted "signs of year-end budget utilizations" (customers spending remaining annual budgets). [JPM, 2026-01-13]
- He said clinical markets were a key driver and that management expects clinical demand to remain an important source of strength.
- He said the company is on track toward its 2027 targets and that in 2025 it "returned the company to growth."

### NovaSeq X transition and 2026 framework
- X accounted for about 80% of high-throughput gigabases shipped by year-end 2025, which Thaysen said reflected steady customer adoption and the milestones Illumina set were achieved.
- He said "elasticity dynamics" (lower per-unit prices leading customers to run much more volume) are clearly taking hold: higher sequencing intensity from expanded content and new indications on a more powerful platform. This is most evident in clinical.
- He expects the transition and volume elasticity to continue near term as the largest cohort of clinical customers either transitions fully to the X or layers incremental volume onto X alongside existing NovaSeq 6000 workflows.
- Guidance: formal 2026 guidance to come at the February 5 earnings call. Thaysen said the framework shared on the Q3 2025 call, that 2026 looks like the second half of 2025, "still hold[s] well," and that the demand pattern, adjusted for Q4 budget-utilization effects, should carry into 2026.

### Vision and strategy
- Thaysen said that historically researchers believed only about 2% of the genome was relevant; Illumina's technology has expanded that understanding, moving NGS into everyday clinical decision-making, national-scale health infrastructure and discovery.
- He envisions the NGS ecosystem becoming the standard in moving health care from "sick-care" to personalized health care, via routine multiomics (measuring several biological layers such as DNA, RNA and protein) in research and clinical use, and faster drug discovery.
- Customers have become more sophisticated and focus less on dollars per gigabase and more on total workflow cost from sample to insight. Illumina is investing in complete end-to-end workflows, owned and partnered. In "a few real-world examples" customers saved 30% or more on total sample-to-answer cost versus alternative workflows. (Management claim.)
- Thaysen argued customers choose partners offering "more than just a box": breadth of applications, reliability, service and continued innovation, all in "one single open ecosystem." He said Q4 results reinforce this.

### Three growth pillars
1. **Core sequencing**, anchored by NovaSeq X, driven by complete application-specific workflows with better results and unit economics.
2. **Multiomics**, adding more data layers per sample and running larger experiments at lower cost.
3. **Services, data and software**, generating and integrating large genomic and proteomic datasets and translating them into insights for pharma using AI.

### Clinical market
- Clinical customers are about 60% of consumables revenue (the Q&A later says "more than 60%"). Adoption has grown across oncology, genetic and reproductive disease on new assay approvals, broader reimbursement and more clinical trials.
- Thaysen said adoption is still early: less than 20% of patients receive a relevant NGS test across disease areas.
- Customers are adopting larger panels and deeper sequencing for sensitivity, all powered by the X.
- Example: in rare genetic disease, many are converting from exome sequencing (reading only protein-coding regions) to whole genome sequencing, which needs roughly 15x more sequencing volume.
- These dynamics are driving clinical sequencing volume growth above 30%. Further drivers: approved and emerging tests in early cancer detection and MRD (minimal residual disease, testing for leftover cancer after treatment), and more sequencing-intense assays.

### Innovation pipeline
- **5-base genome** (launched 2025): embeds epigenetic information (chemical marks on DNA) in standard genomic runs; expected to benefit early cancer and genetic disease detection.
- **Constellation** (planned): reduces library prep (sample preparation) and captures information that was hard to get with short-read sequencing. Thaysen said it is "already getting rave reviews in early access" and should enable several new clinical application areas.
- Continued sequencer innovation: more flow cell offerings and software upgrades expanding applications and performance.

### Multiomics products
- Recent single-cell solution launch; entry into proteomics with Illumina Protein Prep (IPP), developed with SomaLogic; an upcoming spatial solution (measuring molecules in their location within tissue).
- Capabilities cited: millions of cells in single-cell workflows, and up to 9,500 proteins per sample with low coefficients of variation (low measurement variability).
- Illumina Connected Multiomics software lets customers analyze and visualize multiomics data. Thaysen said these extend the ecosystem beyond the genome and drive incremental sequencing demand.

### BioInsight (new business)
- Just launched. Thaysen said four capabilities are converging for the first time: sequencing at scale, genome-wide CRISPR perturbation (gene editing used to switch genes off and see the effect), dedicated compute, and AI to build predictive biological models. Pharma could build, test and refine models digitally instead of years of wet-lab iteration.
- Training such AI models requires massive high-quality datasets, which is the reason for BioInsight.
- First project: a "5 billion Cell Atlas" (a large cell-level dataset) with ambitions to go well beyond. Merck, AstraZeneca and Eli Lilly were named as engaged pharma partners.

### Capital allocation and cash
- Cash generation of approximately $1 billion per year, with free cash flow conversion of about 140% (per Thaysen).
- Priorities, in order: (1) invest in the business (clinical sequencing, multiomics, and now BioInsight datasets and software); (2) targeted bolt-on acquisitions; (3) return excess capital via disciplined, flexible share repurchases.
- In 2025, Illumina returned $740 million to shareholders through buybacks.

### Closing
Thaysen summarized: strong core business, growing clinical momentum, a strategy to expand through sequencing, multiomics, data and software, with priorities of innovation, disciplined execution and thoughtful capital deployment.

## Q&A with Casey Woodring (JPMorgan)

### Q4 beat versus expectations; China versus ex-China
**Question:** Revenue beat came from both China and ex-China; how did the quarter play out, and was the China beat just the removal of the export ban?

**Answer (Thaysen):**
- Ex-China is 97% of revenue and the most important part. He was "very pleased" with traction, especially clinical. Illumina placed 95 X instruments, many in clinical but also in research/academic.
- He saw "some level of rebound" in research/academic ("too early to call"), partly reflecting year-end budget utilization.
- Beat composition: "probably 1/3 on consumables, 1/3 on instruments and 1/3 on China."
- On China: the General Manager (referred to as "Jenny") and team kept the business intact, customers continue to prioritize Illumina, and Illumina worked with Chinese officials in Q4 on a path through the situation.

### Placements and budget flush
**Question:** On the Q3 call management did not expect Q4 placements to match Q4 2024's 91; why did 95+ occur, and was there a budget flush?

**Answer (Thaysen):** The X's performance, reliability and versatility resonate. Many clinical customers "doubled down" on the X as the platform on which they will scale new products. A good number also went to research, which remains "muted," but the primary strength was clinical. (No specific budget-flush quantification was given.)

### Sequencing consumables
**Question:** On the Q3 call Illumina assumed about 6% ex-China sequencing consumables growth; the slides show about 11%. What drove the upside?

**Answer (Thaysen):** The X transition is progressing beyond the Q3 marks. At Q3, more than 50% of revenue was on the X and 75% of volume; now approximately 80% of volume. The price impact of the transition is turning out lower relative to volume performance: "the math is working and it's working according to the expectations." Strength was mostly clinical.

**Addition (Dhingra):** Clinical volumes in Q4 "pick[ed] up much more than what we had built in our expectations and our forecast." That is where the bulk of the strength came from.

### Sequencing output (volume) growth
**Question:** Volume growth was above 30% in Q3; how did it trend in Q4, especially in research?

**Answer (Thaysen):** Volume was very strong again, continuing above 30%, which he expects to continue for a while at least from clinical customers. Over a long horizon, he believes volume growth for the whole business should be about 25% and is sustainable, as customers build larger, deeper panels. Most volume growth is clinical, though academic and research also grew.

**Addition (Dhingra):** Clinical strength was not limited to the large U.S. clinical players that analysts track from their announced results; it was "fairly broad-based," including Europe and the rest of Asia.

### Underlying market health in 2026
**Question:** After 2025 headwinds (the proposed 40% NIH budget cut hurting academic confidence, Chinese tariffs, the unreliable entity list), how does the end market progress in 2026?

**Answer (Thaysen):**
- 2025 was "challenging" and "very dynamic"; geopolitics was the biggest impact. He hopes 2026 is "more predictable."
- Illumina focused on controlling what it can, and 15% EPS growth was "impressive" in that environment.
- Clinical (more than 60% of consumables revenue) should keep driving growth.
- Academic/research: "a little more clarity now" and improving sentiment, but expected to stay muted in coming quarters; "'26 is going to be muted from a research academic environment," while optimistic long term.

### NovaSeq X transition pacing and new KPIs
**Question:** Pacing through 2026, and any new KPIs now that tracking targets (over 75% of gigabases, 50% of high-throughput revenue on the X) are met?

**Answer (Dhingra):**
- The key focus is the clinical business: number of reimbursements approved, number of new tests reaching market, and the resulting growth.
- Being past 75% and "into the 80s" is a milestone; the price effect of the transition should keep shrinking and be a lesser impact in 2026 than in 2025.
- 2026 KPIs center on clinical growth, close observation of research, and traction of newly launched or launching technologies: protein and spatial, interesting to academic and pharmaceutical research customers.

### Durability of elasticity (the 40 fully transitioned customers)
**Question:** In Q3, 78% of high-throughput volume and 51% of revenue were on the X, and 40 fully transitioned customers offset year-1 price declines, with revenue accelerating in year 2. How durable is that pattern?

**Answer (Thaysen):**
- The Q3 disclosure was meant to address concern that a "negative bump" would precede growth. That is not the case: year one may be "a little bit flattish," then year two shows real growth.
- Illumina does not intend to keep reporting individual cohorts, but growth continues "across the spectrum" of converting customers.
- Clinical customers launching products and updating panels sequence deeper; "that is only going one way, and that is deeper sequencing and larger panels." Rare disease moving from smaller panels or exome to whole genome (15x more volume) is one example; clinical volume should keep growing above 30%.

### Whole genome sequencing shift and potential headwind
**Question:** How long will the shift to larger panels and whole genome take, what share of clinical customers run WGS today, and could there be a headwind to quantify?

**Answer (Dhingra):**
- Whole genome is still a smaller fraction of overall clinical volumes; many validated tests were built on exomes. Newly launched whole-genome approaches are entering the calculus but remain "much, much smaller."
- He sees a long runway: higher-intensity whole genome approaches plus many newer tests in clinical trials that could reach market over the next 2, 3, 4 years. This implies significant volume growth, enabled by the X's throughput, quality and unit economics.
- No percentage of customers running WGS and no headwind quantification was provided.

### Competition (Roche, Element) ahead of AGBT
**Question:** Latest thoughts on competition as AGBT (the genomics conference) approaches, and how customers evaluate new entrants like Roche and Element; price alone has not driven switching.

**Answer (Thaysen):**
- Illumina has long been in a competitive space; competition "keeps us on our toes."
- Customers want partners who help them succeed, not "sell them a box and argue for a lower price."
- Pitch: the NovaSeq X is flexible with easy workflows and ease of use, combined with DRAGEN (Illumina's analysis compute/software) and interpretation software. "Customers do not want to pick and choose different components. They want to focus on outcomes."
- He expects "a lot of new things coming out at AGBT" and said Illumina spends heavily on innovation. He did not discuss competitor specifics.

### China
**Question:** Latest on government approval needed for customers to buy Illumina in China?

**Answer (Thaysen):**
- Timeline: about 10 months earlier, in February 2025, Illumina was placed on China's Unreliable Entity List; a month later it was sanctioned from placing new instruments in China, which dented performance.
- Chinese customers showed strong commitment to Illumina and its quality.
- "We are committed to China," but "we need to be welcomed into that market."
- At Q3 2025, Illumina announced it was allowed to import instruments again for OEM customers, about 20% of the opportunity. Later in Q4, Thaysen traveled to China and described the talks with officials as "very open and good."
- The sanctions have now been lifted and steady progress is starting, but it takes time to rebuild momentum and the instrument funnel after a period of no placements.
- Illumina remains on the Unreliable Entity List; removal is the ultimate goal and is being worked on, but "there's still no clear outcome."

**Addition (Dhingra):** Q4 results were not affected by the lifting of sanctions. China strength was entirely consumables, from customers using the existing installed base. No additional instrument imports were seen.

### Multiomics portfolio
**Question:** How do IPP, single cell and the Fluent acquisition fit into the long-term vision?

**Answer (Thaysen):**
- Key advantage: all run on the current sequencing platform, so one platform can span "the genome all the way up to the proteome."
- Expect academic research to adopt first, with clinical applications following.
- Biology is more complicated than the genome alone; understanding it requires "billions of cells experiments," huge proteomic studies and large-scale spatial work, which needs sequencing, compute and AI. Illumina can supply both instruments and BioInsight.

### BioInsight differentiation
**Question:** How will BioInsight differentiate Illumina?

**Answer (Thaysen):**
- Customers need sequencing at scale for experiments that are nearly cost-prohibitive individually. With single-cell, sequencing, compute and AI under one roof, Illumina can scale beyond what individual customers can do alone, and is building a consortium for billions-of-cells experiments.
- The goal is to go beyond data to cell models that interpret how drugs perform, a "huge shift in drug discovery." He called Illumina an "absolutely beautiful center" for this and a huge growth opportunity.
