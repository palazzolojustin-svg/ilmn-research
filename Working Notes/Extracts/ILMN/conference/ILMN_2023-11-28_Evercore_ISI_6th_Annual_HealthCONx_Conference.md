# Illumina CFO Joydeep Goswami at Evercore ISI 6th Annual HealthCONx Conference (fireside Q&A with Vijay Kumar)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/conference/ILMN_2023-11-28_Evercore_ISI_6th_Annual_HealthCONx_Conference.md` · Date: 2023-11-28 · Seeking Alpha transcript; Evercore ISI 6th Annual HealthCONx Conference. Host analyst Vijay Kumar (Evercore ISI). Illumina speakers: Joydeep Goswami (CFO) and Salli Schwartz (VP, Investor Relations) · conference*

**Summary.** Illumina's CFO defends the company against the bear argument that its new NovaSeq X sequencer cut the price per sample without producing enough extra demand (weak "price elasticity"). He attributes the weak 2023 consumables numbers to the macro environment and a temporary customer transition from the older NovaSeq 6000 to the X, and expects a pickup in the first half of 2024 as the new 25B flow cell is validated. He also covers slower instrument order conversion, gross and operating margin recovery paths, the new CEO's early priorities, 2024 revenue expectations (roughly flat in dollars versus 2023), and the status of the GRAIL divestiture and legal proceedings.

# Illumina at the Evercore ISI 6th Annual HealthCONx Conference, 28 November 2023

## Context and format

This is a fireside-style question-and-answer session. The host is Vijay Kumar of Evercore ISI, who covers life science tools and medtech. Illumina was represented by Chief Financial Officer Joydeep Goswami and Salli Schwartz, Vice President of Investor Relations. The transcript begins abruptly (the first seconds were not captured). The session is tied to the Q3 2023 earnings call, which the speakers refer to repeatedly. Illumina is the leading maker of DNA sequencing instruments and the reagent kits ("consumables") that customers must buy for each run.

Tone: Goswami is steady and defensive but constructive. He admits to a weak year, says "Clearly, we and others in our industry were wrong about how China would evolve in '23," but repeatedly frames the problems as timing ("a question of when rather than if") rather than structural. The host's questions are pointed and mostly bearish-debate framed.

## Opening framing from the host

- [Vijay Kumar, 2023-11-28] 2023 has been a "pretty remarkable year" for Illumina. He says the shares are down 50% for the year and 50% to 80% off their peak. He says the macro environment has been challenging but Illumina "has had its own set of issues."
- He sets up the main investor debate: Illumina launched a new system (the NovaSeq X) that cut the price per sample from about $600 to about $200, but there is no evidence of price elasticity of demand (the idea that a price cut produces a proportionally larger rise in volume). He says investors are not seeing 3x volumes, and that this explains the weak numbers.

## Price elasticity of demand and the NovaSeq 6000 to X transition

### Goswami's first answer
- [Goswami, 2023-11-28] The NovaSeq X is an innovation "really well received by the market." Interest continues with the launch of the 25B flow cell (a larger-capacity consumable chip that holds the sample during a run). The company believes it will spur more utilization of sequencing.
- Sequencing utilization is still growing, as shown by the sequencing gigabase growth figures Illumina has published, both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year.
- 2023 numbers were "muted" for two reasons: macroeconomics, and the transition from the NovaSeq 6000 ("6K") to the X, which has not fully wrapped up. Both are headwinds this year.
- Long term, he cites execution, continued innovation, and a market where next-generation sequencing (NGS) brings "unique value in terms of biology at scale" with penetration "under 10%." He says "it's a question of when rather than if that growth comes."

### Why this is transition, not lack of elasticity
- Kumar pressed: Illumina has not had a transition problem in prior product cycles, so why is this not an elasticity problem?
- [Goswami] Customers already adopting the X show more sequencing growth than customers that have not adopted it. He says this is evidence that elasticity is being spurred, and that it is not only price: speed, integrated bioinformatics, and upfront workflow simplification also drive demand.
- He says elasticity plays out over multiple years. Illumina sees "a five or six year cycle": most of the price decline hits up front, while the volume benefit continues over a longer period.
- In the first year after adopting an X there is a more immediate year-on-year price decline; this becomes much smaller in later years, while volume ramps year over year.

### Does more sequencing mean more consumables revenue?
Goswami split the answer between clinical and research customers.

**Clinical customers:**
- Growth is incremental. Assays (lab tests) validated on the 6K will not immediately switch to the X. They continue on the 6K, a baseline that is itself growing with more samples, more awareness and more reimbursement.
- What goes onto the X are newer assays that were marginally viable or not viable on the 6K, so the volume is truly incremental.

**Research customers:** more nuanced. X adoption mostly enables work that was difficult on the 6K:
- Expanding to much larger cohorts in population genomics studies.
- Moving a whole cohort into multiomic analysis (for example proteomics).
- Analyzing more single cells, or spatial analysis (mapping molecules within tissue), which would have been cost-prohibitive on the 6K.
- He says these reasons let research customers adopt the X and let Illumina see incremental revenue growth "despite the drop in prices."

### Salli Schwartz on offsetting trends
- [Schwartz, 2023-11-28] Some research customers moving work to the X are reducing 6K consumables purchases. This year the offset has been more intense. She says that next year "we should see those lines cross": X consumable uptake rises, only partially offset by falling 6K consumables.

### Clinical volumes (the "won't you need fewer chips?" challenge)
- Kumar's challenge: if the X can process twice the samples, wouldn't customers use fewer chips, whether the transition occurs next year or in three years?
- [Goswami] Clinical volumes have been ramping very quickly, even before the X. Growth drivers: increased reimbursement, greater awareness of NGS panels, and MRD (minimal residual disease testing, which checks for leftover cancer after treatment) and MCED (multi-cancer early detection tests). Oncology and genetic disease testing have grown for a long time and the trends "continue to be very strong."
- Validated assays stay on the 6K for a long time. As an example, some assays still run on the HiSeq even though the HiSeq-to-6K transition was more than six years ago, because customers are "locked into a validated assay" and avoid re-validation. Goswami calls this "astounding."
- Therefore the X brings incremental volume rather than swapping like-for-like volume onto fewer flow cells. He calls the clinical story "very strong and it's accretive."

## Consumables pull-through and the 2023 guidance

- Kumar noted the consumables pull-through guidance for the year (consumable revenue per 6K-class instrument) had been lowered from about $1 million to $700K-$900K. Bears argue this is because the X can do more per run.
- [Goswami, 2023-11-28] Illumina expected 6K pull-through to fall as customers transition to the X, so "no surprise." He corrected the range: the expectation for this year was $800K to $900K, not $700K. As of the Q3 projections the number stays in that range but toward the low end.
- Pull-through is a calculated metric (revenue divided by number of installed instruments). In a transition year, if customers reduce 6K inventory or usage before moving to the X, the metric takes a hit.
- Full switchover has not occurred because many early adopters, who are heavy users, are waiting for the 25B flow cell, "the real workhorse" on the X. It launched this quarter (Q4 2023, late October) and customers are just starting to validate workflows on it. Best guess: a ramp in X consumables in the first half of 2024.
- He expects 6K pull-through to decline over time, but as a mix of research and clinical so the reduction will "not be as sudden as people have expected."
- Asked whether pull-through on the 25B will exceed that on the 6K, he said yes: "We do expect it to exceed the 6K."

### Which customers are disrupted
- The reduction in 6K purchases is specific to customers who have ordered or already received an X. Customers that have not ordered an X are at trend-line levels, "barring macro."
- For a given customer, spending will decline for a period and then rise. The gap between decline and ramp should be shorter than this year, which was peculiar because the 25B was not available until Q4. There was a sustained decline before the Q4 ramp began.
- Customer pattern: a burn-down of 6K inventory before the X is installed; then a few validation runs that take roughly a quarter (somewhat less or more by customer). Illumina is trying to have service engineers and field application specialists available to shorten this. After validation, customers ramp experiments on the X (sustained pull-through) and initially stock up inventory; he cannot predict how much.
- Once normalized, the trend line of sequencing volume increase should be similar to before, "or maybe slightly accelerated given the higher capabilities of the instrument."

## Q4 2023 guidance assumptions

- Kumar noted the Q4 guidance implied sequencing consumables declining by mid-single digits against an easy comparison with Q4 2022, which suggests the 25B flow cell is not having a material impact in Q4.
- [Goswami] Reasons: (1) Q4 only includes validation runs of the 25B, which launched in late October and the quarter is cut by the holidays; (2) year on year there is more burn-down of 6K inventories, and Illumina does not expect a customer "budget flush" (year-end spending of unused budget) this year, though last year had a small one.
- What Illumina wants to see in Q4: a healthy market reaction, enough customers starting to validate and placing initial orders. "Both of those so far seem to be playing out."

### Software and 25B experience
- The software issues mentioned on the Q2 call (and in August) are resolved; the deployment of fixes "has gone off really well."
- With the 25B launch the software was updated again to support the 25B and also improve performance on the 10B flow cell. Both changes and the 25B launch have been "incredibly well received by customers" as far as the company can tell.
- Kumar asked how many customers had received a 25B flow cell; no number was given.

## Instrument orders, funnel and macro

- Kumar noted Q3 order intake slowed to 50 systems and asked why Illumina sees macro challenges that differ from peers.
- [Goswami] China has a similar impact on most companies (a liquidity issue), and for Illumina a bit more because one of its major competitors is Chinese. China was somewhat expected, but got worse in the second half than expected.
- The other macro element was customers slowing the timing of purchases. Interest and the size of the funnel (the pipeline of potential instrument sales) "remains incredibly strong." What surprised Illumina was that it expected the funnel to convert at higher rates in Q3 and Q4, and that did not happen in Q3. Conversion speed is slower. He believes that with high interest and the 25B, conversion will pick up over the next few quarters. Guidance was based on what was visible in the market.
- [Schwartz] In magnitude, orders were 50 instead of the 60 expected. She says 50 orders was "still a pretty decent quarter, all things considered," and the aim is to get back to 60 and beyond.

### Where the slowdown occurred
- [Goswami] Widespread.
  - Academic: more caution; customers wait to see 2024 funding before committing.
  - Clinical: "a tale of two cities." Well-funded customers are placing strong orders, including fleet expansion orders, with no slowdown. Customers with funding challenges, or a bleaker 2024 outlook, are converting orders much more slowly.

### Is the funnel stale?
- [Goswami] New opportunities keep being added. Sales reps go back to customers to confirm interest and budget visibility, and remove opportunities from the funnel if there is none. Funnel staleness is evaluated weekly by the sales team and leadership.
- Advanced pipeline (opportunities where customers have line of sight to budget and a timing for the instrument) also slowed. Illumina saw the probabilities of conversion in a given quarter shift, which is why it "rewound" that disclosure. He says the pipeline itself is not weak; the speed of conversion fell in Q3 and Q4.

### Are customers swapping two 6Ks for one X?
- [Goswami] Not to Illumina's knowledge. There are about 1,000 NovaSeq customers (the transcript says "customers"), of which about 75% have one X (as transcribed); the remainder have multiple, with a distribution of how many 6Ks they own.
- Clinical: customers keep using 6Ks on validated assays and add Xs for new, more sequencing-intensive assays (more samples or more sequencing per analysis).
- Research: demand comes from very high sequencing users with planned increases in activity, moving specific cohorts or analyses to the X.
- Possible that a customer buys an X for the same sequencing volume, but "that's never played out in our history." Given under 10% penetration in very large addressable markets, he says it is highly unlikely customers feel saturated.

## Margins

### Gross margin
- Kumar said gross margins are now about 65-66% and asked if they can return to about 70%.
- [Goswami] "Yes, it can. And it will." 2023 decline was anticipated because of (1) mix shift to more instruments in the first year of a major launch, and (2) lower margins on the X (instrument and consumables) while production ramps than on the 6K. An additional impact came from lower-than-expected volumes and revenue, which reduced absorption of fixed costs in the plants. He says "we have corrected a lot of that."
- In 2024 these factors start to turn, producing year-on-year and sequential margin improvement. Productivity measures should return gross margin to "high-60s to low 70s over time."
- [Schwartz] The same pattern happened in 2017-2018 when the NovaSeq 6000 launched (transcribed as "NovaSeq X 6000"): a fall in gross margin followed by recovery.
- Kumar noted the last cycle took about four quarters from the bottom to recover and asked whether Q4 2023 is the bottom. [Goswami] It depends on macro and the absorption factor will take a bit longer; expect sequential improvement through next year and subsequent years. Seasonally, Q4 tends to have the highest margins (most volume) and Q1 the lowest, so he would not promise sequential gains every quarter, but expects them for the next few quarters.

### Operating margin
- Kumar asked whether core Illumina (excluding GRAIL) operating margin can return to 30%, and whether that timeline is five or ten years.
- [Goswami] "There's nothing structurally that prevents us from getting back into that high 20s to 30% operating margin on the core business." Levers: R&D and operating expense productivity, and gross margin gains. Illumina commits to "strong operating leverage."
- Two constraints: it will not speed up R&D savings so much that it harms technological leadership (R&D as a percentage of revenue will fall over time, but not at a pace that hurts innovation), and it will keep investing to serve customers in its geographies. No timeframe given.

## New CEO

- [Kumar] Asked about the priorities of the new CEO, Jacob (Jacob Thaysen; only "Jacob" appears in the transcript) amid many management changes.
- [Goswami] Jacob has been there about 60 days, mostly ramping up. Coming from the industry, he knows NGS, life sciences, tools and diagnostics ("we speak the same language"); transition "very smooth."
- Activities: portfolio reviews of the pipeline, decisions on the X and near-term launches, and the budgeting process currently underway. He has also started thinking about modifying long-term strategy and which areas to focus on more or less. "Still work in progress" to be shared "at some point next year."

## Long-term growth and 2024 outlook

- Asked if Illumina is still a double-digit top-line growth company, Goswami did not commit to a number. He said the market is "very large with very little penetration," Illumina is bullish on growth and on taking its fair share, and NGS can transform biology in research and clinical.
- Kumar said most peers expect first half 2024 to look like second half 2023 with normalization in the second half of 2024, and asked about Illumina.
- [Goswami] Illumina sees 2024 playing out similar to 2023. Upsides: faster transition to the X, customers spending on more Xs once they see 25B data and have more funding certainty, clinical customer funding with MRD and MCED ramps, and China if it improves. Downsides: anything further clouding customers' ability to spend on high- and mid-throughput instruments, and China, which is hard to predict. Illumina is "evolving our strategy to play in China the right way going forward."
- On whether this meant a decline: [Goswami] This was "meant less to be a guidance." Illumina is not giving guidance now. Consensus at the time was 10%-plus growth in 2024, and the company wanted to ensure people were not running with that expectation. Jacob's remark that 2024 would look similar to 2023 was in dollar terms: revenue roughly in the range of 2023. Full guidance will come in February 2024.
- Operating margin 2024: fairly close to 2023. Gross margin should be higher and headcount lower (2023 cost reductions apply for a full year). Offsetting is variable compensation normalization: in 2023 a substantial portion of variable cash and stock-based compensation was not paid because of performance; in 2024 Illumina expects to pay it if it hits its numbers. Kumar summarized "flattish operating margins" and Goswami agreed the reset offsets gross margin gains and lower headcount.

## GRAIL, regulatory and legal timeline

- Court of Justice of the European Union: a hearing on jurisdiction is on track for mid-December 2023 [Goswami, Schwartz clarified it is the EU Court of Justice].
- FTC: a decision from the Fifth Circuit Court is expected by the end of 2023 [Schwartz; Goswami confirmed "The FTC"].
- **Special committee:** The Q3 press release mentioned a Board special committee on GRAIL. [Goswami] Its purpose is to expedite decision-making on complex, evolving GRAIL issues (European Commission, FTC, legal cases) by meeting more regularly. Composition and names are not disclosed; it includes new and old board members with experience in divestitures and financial issues of this magnitude.
- **Strategic interest in GRAIL:** Kumar relayed street skepticism about interest in a cash-burning asset, with Illumina having outlaid $1 billion-plus of cash. [Goswami] It is early. Illumina received inbound interest even before the divestiture order. It plans to file a Form 10 (the registration document needed for a spin-off) before the end of 2023, which begins its ability to engage parties interested in GRAIL via a sponsored spin solution or an outright sale. The Form 10 will be the first time a counterparty is noted.
- Asked if skepticism was unfounded: "our advisers are telling us there is interest in the market, and there is interest in what GRAIL brings to the market, what GRAIL brings in its leadership in the MCED space." He declined to say more because these are market-based transactions.
