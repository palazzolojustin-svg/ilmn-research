# Illumina fireside chat at the UBS MedTech, Tools and Genomics Summit 2023 (Salli Schwartz, VP Investor Relations, with UBS analyst John Sourbeer)

**Summary.** A fireside chat held about one week after Illumina's Q2 2023 earnings, in which Illumina's head of investor relations explained why 2023 guidance for core Illumina was cut from 6-9% growth to roughly flat, attributing it to China, a slower NovaSeq X consumables ramp and cautious customer budgets. She also covered the NovaSeq X order book (about 260 cumulative orders), pull-through (consumables revenue per installed instrument) cuts, cost savings, the 25% operating margin target, the GRAIL legal and regulatory decision tree (Fifth Circuit ruling expected by end of 2023; EU Court of Justice ruling late 2023 or early 2024; EU fine of €432 million), a new SEC inquiry, and Illumina's long-read plans. # Illumina at the UBS MedTech, Tools and Genomics Summit, 17 Aug 2023

## Background and format

This is a fireside chat (a moderated question-and-answer session) on the final day of the life science portion of the UBS summit. The host was John Sourbeer, a life sciences analyst at UBS. Illumina was represented by Salli Schwartz, Vice President of Investor Relations. The event took place about a week after Illumina reported second-quarter 2023 results ("Q2 earnings"). Sourbeer also relayed a few questions from the audience, submitted through an iPad and QR code. Schwartz's tone was upbeat about demand and products. She was frank that the guidance cut was "frustrating," which she described as "meaningful takedown, which is frustrating, but temporary in a lot of the drivers."

## Why 2023 guidance was lowered

Sourbeer opened by asking for the puts and takes behind the lower guidance, citing China, a slower NovaSeq X ramp and customer budget pressure.

**The size of the cut.** Schwartz said Illumina had previously guided "core Illumina" (the business excluding GRAIL, the cancer-detection company Illumina acquired) to grow 6% to 9% in 2023. It was lowered to approximately flat for the year [Schwartz, Aug 2023].

**Three drivers.** She said about 25% of the variance came from China. The other 75% was roughly evenly split between the NovaSeq X transition and macro-driven customer caution.

1. **China (about 25% of the shortfall).**
   - Illumina had expected a macro recovery in China in the second half of 2023, and "that's just not happened." She noted other companies have also had challenges there.
   - There is also a company-specific problem: a more competitive landscape, with "more steeply discounted pricing coming from our primary competitor." This has produced a lower win rate for Illumina in China.
2. **NovaSeq X transition (roughly half of the other 75%).**
   - The transition to the NovaSeq X (Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer, launched with new chemistry) "has been going really well, lots of demand, lots of placements" (placements are instruments installed at customers).
   - On the other side, some customers are running down ("bleeding down") their stock of NovaSeq 6000 consumables (the reagent kits needed for each run) faster than Illumina expected.
   - Rolling out a new instrument has also surfaced the usual technical issues earlier. Fixing them across all customers has taken longer, which pushed out some consumables purchases. She said this is "not a negative necessarily" but a delay plus a faster run-down.
3. **Macro caution (the other half of the 75%).**
   - Schwartz acknowledged some hopeful macro signs, citing Walmart's consumer trends, positive retail sales, better inflation data and hopeful central bank signals.
   - Even so, customers are "really careful and cautious": longer times to reach purchase decisions, more cautious spending and more disciplined expense management. This hurt what Illumina had thought would be a second-half recovery.

Despite the cut, she said Illumina is "still very excited" about the ramp starting in Q4 2023, particularly consumables on the X.

## NovaSeq X: orders, customers and geography

**Order count.** Illumina gives order updates once a quarter. At the Q2 call it said it had received about 260 cumulative NovaSeq X orders, with roughly 60 arriving in Q2 [Schwartz]. Sourbeer noted that guidance for NovaSeq X placements for the year had been raised.

**Clinical share.** About 40% of orders continue to come from clinical customers (those running patient-testing work). She said this differs from past rollouts, especially the NovaSeq 6000 high-throughput launch. These customers are generally buying the X to build a new test or process, not to replace 6000 work, so it is additive to existing 6000 business.

**New customers.** On the Q1 call, Illumina said roughly 15% of orders came from customers new to Illumina or new to high-throughput sequencing. Some of these come from the ambient shipping capability: consumables can now be shipped at room temperature, without dry ice and the heavy packaging used historically. She called this "a huge deal to our customers." It has opened new geographies and customers, and some customers are moving up from mid-throughput to high-throughput.

**Ambient shipping geography.** Illumina last cited about 35 countries it sells into, and she said it is early days. Newly accessible markets are "more far flung regions of the world, more emerging markets" where cold chain (refrigerated logistics) is not guaranteed and consumables could arrive expired. She added that COVID raised the visibility of genomics for healthcare broadly.

**Customer feedback.** Feedback was described as "very, very positive," and "pretty phenomenal." Illumina continues to facilitate industry conversations, building on its Illumina Genomic Forum from the previous fall. The learning: Illumina has "rolled out more Xs faster and to more places than we have done any launch before." That is positive, but support takes more time as customers bring instruments online. She tied this to part of the reason guidance came down.

## NovaSeq X pull-through and the 25B flow cell

**Pull-through (consumables revenue per installed instrument).** Schwartz explained pull-through as consumables purchases (numerator) divided by installed base (denominator). The installed base of X is growing very rapidly, so the figure is moving around a lot. When the X launched, Illumina talked about six to eight quarters to get stability. Illumina will give an outlook "as soon as we can get a little bit more stability." The 10B flow cell (a consumable that holds the sample during sequencing; "10B" denotes its capacity) is available now. The 25B flow cell is due in Q4 2023, which she called "very highly anticipated" by customers. Illumina expects the beginnings of a consumables ramp, especially in Q4.

**Possible budget flush.** She said there may be "a little bit of budget flush" (customers spending leftover year-end budget) but did not want to overstate it. The Q4 ramp is reflected in guidance.

**NovaSeq 6000 replacement math.** Illumina has about 1,000 NovaSeq 6000 customers. About 750 own only one 6000, so for them the decision is binary (buy an X or not). She said Illumina thinks over time everybody will replace their 6000. The other ~250 customers have multiple instruments (about 25% of the base). About 20% of X orders so far have been multi-unit purchases, roughly in line with that 25%. Some early-adopter customers have come back with follow-on orders, which gives Illumina confidence in the order book.

**NovaSeq 6000 pull-through.** The average 6000 pull-through was cut to a range of $800,000 to $900,000 on the Q2 call. It fell faster than expected this year, a reason for both the pull-through reduction and the lower guidance. Research customers are moving work to the X faster. Clinical customers will hold on to the 6000 longer because their tests and processes are already validated there, so moving is more effort. She said the decline would follow patterns seen in prior instrument transitions.

**Liquid biopsy and MRD.** Responding to an audience question about liquid biopsy customers switching to the X, she said there is real excitement, citing the ease of blood collection relative to tissue samples. MRD (minimal residual disease, testing for leftover cancer after treatment) comes up often in customer discussions. There was also news that morning on the single-cell side. She said that with better economic sentiment "there's a ton of opportunity here."

## Mid-throughput: NextSeq

**Sales cycles and competition.** Asked whether sales cycles are lengthening in mid-throughput, she said yes on both macro and competition. Customers take longer to commit and to get internal approvals ("that's true even at Illumina"). Procurement departments also like to evaluate alternatives. Illumina tracks win rate closely, and on the Q2 call said its win rate in this throughput segment accelerated in Q2 versus Q1 2023. Her conclusion: "It might take longer, but ultimately, we are still doing quite well."

**Pull-through changes.**
- NextSeq 550 average pull-through: lowered from a $100,000 to $150,000 range to $80,000 to $130,000. Causes: partly China, partly the absence of the COVID surveillance-driven demand seen historically.
- NextSeq 1000/2000: pull-through range held at $120,000 to $170,000, despite a growing installed base. The installed base grew 8% in Q2, and Q2 pull-through was toward the lower end of the range.

**XLEAP chemistry for NextSeq.** Illumina plans to bring XLEAP ("chemistry X," the chemistry used on the NovaSeq X) to NextSeq 1000/2000 in the first half of 2024. Customers need no new instrument and can effectively "download" it, gaining some NovaSeq X capabilities. She said anticipation has helped drive current sales. The NextSeq 1000/2000 also uniquely offers DRAGEN on board (Illumina's built-in informatics and analytics hardware/software), which customers want. She expects "a nice backlog" and strong purchases. Because no instrument swap is needed, the transition is "more contained": customers may run down current consumables to switch to the new ones, but there is less ordering disruption than with the NovaSeq X.

## Cost savings and margins

**Expense cuts.** Illumina is doing what many customers are doing. The approach was to downsize, delay or drop projects, then translate that into reduced spending by function. Results: some headcount reductions (several announced over the past year), plus lower vendor spend and travel. A big piece still in progress is real estate: Illumina has re-examined its global footprint and will announce amounts and P&L impact when done. The cuts are broad-based and include R&D, but R&D is "a key point of commitment." She expects significant R&D dollars and growth in that budget, with discipline.

**25% operating margin target.** At last fall's Investor Day (October 2022), Illumina gave margin and growth targets, and the Q2 call reiterated a 25% margin target for 2025 (per Sourbeer's framing, "that 25% margin target"). Schwartz said CFO Joydeep had called it more of a stretch. The gap to cover from 2022 to 2025 was 300 basis points and is now 500 (a basis point is 0.01 percentage point), so "purely from a numbers perspective, that's 100% true." Levers:
- **OpEx (operating expenses):** further fine-tuning.
- **Gross margin:** (1) as X and consumables production ramps, more of the fixed manufacturing cost base is absorbed, which helps; low absorption is one reason gross margin is lighter in 2023; (2) as consumables purchases ramp, mix shifts back to higher-margin revenue; (3) proactive efforts to reduce cost of goods sold.
- **Revenue:** needs more visibility through the rest of 2023 and into next year given macro and customer sentiment.

She said the aim is 25% for next year (as stated) and acceleration beyond. Historically Illumina has been in a 25% to 30% range, which she called "a very reasonable place for us to be going forward" structurally.

## Long-term growth: mid-teens target

Sourbeer noted China is about 10% of revenue and asked how core Illumina gets to the mid-teens growth target. Schwartz said mid-teens "still makes sense" as the long-term structural growth rate, based on Investor Day target addressable markets that are largely unpenetrated or lightly penetrated. She doubted growth would be linear. Pieces being put in place: reimbursement, adoption, and physician and patient awareness. Another source is elasticity (lower prices increasing volume): more samples, more analyses per sample (multiomics), and more data per sample, such as customers moving from exomes to whole genomes, expanding panels, or going straight to whole-genome sequencing ("PGP" in the transcript) or liquid biopsy, which is more sequencing-intensive. Lower price points enable this.

## Clinical markets and NovaSeq X Dx

**Clinical strategy.** Serving clinical customers required a multi-year, heavily invested transformation of the company. Illumina is now well positioned. She sees clinical markets as "bigger and faster growing than research," with a feedback loop between research and clinical.

**NovaSeq X Dx (the diagnostic-use version of the instrument).** It had "a nice rollout." China was one piece, which she described as a point of success alongside the challenges discussed. Europe has been a really important market because several countries have rules or requirements the Dx instrument helps meet. Illumina will selectively place Dx instruments with customers who need them.

## SEC inquiry and GRAIL

**SEC.** The 10-Q (filed after the earnings call) disclosed an SEC investigation. Schwartz said there is little to add: it is very early, Illumina received a letter in July requesting documents, and it is cooperating fully. She suggested the interest stems from the attention drawn by a "very public proxy fight," regulatory discussions in multiple countries, and the departure of the CEO earlier in 2023.

**GRAIL decision tree.** Illumina's acquisition of GRAIL has been challenged by regulators.
- **U.S.:** Illumina's appeal is at the Fifth Circuit Court. A decision is expected by end of 2023, which will show whether Illumina can proceed or must divest, at least under U.S. rules.
- **EU:** The focus is a jurisdictional appeal at the EU Court of Justice (likened to the EU's Supreme Court), on whether the EU has jurisdiction to review the deal at all. A decision is expected late 2023 or early 2024. If Illumina wins, the recently assigned €432 million fine "goes away" and it would have more flexibility on any divestment order.
- **Decision rules:** If Illumina loses one or both appeals, it will divest and will not pursue further appeals. If it wins both, it must decide whether to keep GRAIL "in some form or fashion" or divest anyway. The Board and management would reassess based on the forward business case and shareholder value: "if it's in the best interest of shareholders, we will finish the deal, and if it isn't, then we won't, we will divest."
- **EC divestment order:** Illumina is still awaiting one from the European Commission, hoped for in Q3 (she assumed September, since August is a summer break). It would let Illumina start the divestiture process and explore paths with potential buyers.

## Long-read sequencing

Long-read sequencing (reading much longer DNA fragments, an area where competitors such as Oxford Nanopore and PacBio operate; the competitors were not named in this chat) comes up less often in conversations but is an important part of the market. Schwartz called short-read and long-read complementary. Short-read covers most use cases at a price point long-read cannot match "by quite a bit."

Illumina's approach is a long-read offering on existing instruments, with no new instrument purchase:
- First offering announced March 2023, rollout began May 2023.
- An enrichment assay is coming "very soon." Combined with the 25B flow cell on the NovaSeq X, it enables a **$600 total genome with long reads**.
- More than 40 customers are doing evaluations and early work, with encouraging feedback so far. The initial product is not the full enrichment assay plus 25B flow cell, so enthusiasm should grow.
- The stated aim is delivering whatever customers need on Illumina instruments: easier sample prep (especially clinical), and informatics, analytics, data storage, compression and data management.
- She sees it as incremental revenue, "more of a complement than a replacement."

## Population sequencing programs

Responding to a question on whether macro headwinds hit national population sequencing programs, she said Illumina tracks a portfolio of "30 something" projects, with some finishing and new ones added. The X has enabled some very large programs in certain countries that Illumina hopes to add soon. There are no giant new ones to name now. Macro effects: purchasing impacts, inflation stretching budgets, and sample gathering difficulties at times. These were built into the original view and any intensification is reflected in revised guidance. The programs as a whole are **less than 5% of revenue**: "important but not a huge piece of the revenue base."
