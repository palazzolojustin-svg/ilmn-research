# Illumina CFO Ankur Dhingra at the Evercore ISI HealthCONx Healthcare Conference (fireside chat, Dec 3, 2024)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/conference/ILMN_2024-12-03_CFO_Ankur_Dhingra_Hosts_Annual_Evercore_ISI_HealthCONx_Healthcare_Conference.md` · Date: 2024-12-03 · Seeking Alpha transcript; Evercore ISI HealthCONx conference; host Vijay Kumar (Evercore ISI); speakers Ankur Dhingra (CFO) and Sallilyn "Salli" Schwartz (VP Investor Relations) · conference*

**Summary.** Illumina's CFO discussed the Q3 2024 results, a stable-but-weak macro backdrop, China stabilization at roughly $75-80M per quarter, and the transition of customers from the NovaSeq 6000 to the NovaSeq X. He targeted having at least 75% of high-throughput volume on X by the second half of 2025, expected Q4 consumables growth to moderate, and framed 2025 X placements around roughly 200-240 instruments. He reaffirmed the Strategy Day goals of returning to high-single-digit growth by 2027 and 500 basis points of margin expansion over three years, with ratable (steady) margin gains in 2025.

## Overview and context

This is a fireside chat (an on-stage question-and-answer conversation) at the Evercore ISI HealthCONx conference on December 3, 2024. The host was Vijay Kumar, a life science device analyst at Evercore ISI. Illumina was represented by CFO Ankur Dhingra and Sallilyn (Salli) Schwartz, VP of Investor Relations. The CFO said he had been at Illumina about six months, so Q3 2024 was his second complete quarter. Tone: confident and upbeat on demand, the balance sheet and execution, but cautious and explicitly non-committal on macro, China upside and 2025 guidance.

Key terms used throughout: the NovaSeq X ("X") is Illumina's newer high-throughput sequencer; the NovaSeq 6000 ("6000") is the older high-throughput model it replaces. Consumables are the reagent kits customers must buy for each sequencing run. "Gb" (gigabases) measures the amount of DNA data sequenced, so "Gb shift" is a measure of sequencing volume growth.

## Q3 2024 review: what stood out

Asked by Kumar whether anything surprised internally in Q3 (Kumar's view: consumables a bit better, instruments a bit softer), Dhingra said:

- **Overall progress.** He is "very pleased" with progress. The company is "still in transition" and "in transformation." He cited two positives: the transition of the high-throughput consumables business to X, and an "operating discipline culture" that is beginning to show up in margins. [Evercore, 2024-12-03]
- **Margins.** He was "very pleased with the overall margin expansion."
- **Organizational response.** Q3 was the quarter in which he judged how quickly the team would rally to his "call to action." He saw fast alignment rather than significant pushback on priorities.
- **Revenue.** Total revenue came in "right at the midpoint" of what the company expected. He noted Illumina always works in forecast ranges.
- **Consumables stronger**, driven by high throughput. Volume on X was higher and grew faster than anticipated. He called this a net positive because his "eyesight is towards goalpost in the second half of 2025," when a larger chunk of the transition should be complete.
- **Instruments weaker**, below expectations, largely due to macro conditions. The sales funnel (pipeline of prospective purchases) has held up well, but deal closing timing and size are highly variable (for example, whether a customer buys three X systems at once or one now and two next quarter), which makes them hard to predict.
- **Underlying demand.** Sequencing demand is "very robust," running closer to 40% on the Gb shift. Combined with a "very strong balance sheet" and strong cash generation, this gives him confidence in the business model. He framed the things a CFO does not want (such as products with no demand) and said Illumina does not have that problem.

## Macro environment

Asked how macro compares with three months earlier (peers that morning had said stable, improving, or both), Dhingra said he is in the "stable" camp, "stable at a pretty low level." He would call it improving only after seeing one to two quarters of sustained improvement. The weakness shows mostly on the capital side: instrument demand, capital expenditure, and CFOs conserving cash. He said conditions are the same as last quarter, and warned that if customers stay constrained for a long time, "the pain only increases."

### Year-end budget flush

The "budget flush" is the year-end spending of leftover customer budgets. Dhingra reminded the audience that when Illumina guided Q4 (a point guide, not a range), it "took optimism out" for any strong budget flush. Even though the funnel looks robust and customers talk about buying fleets of X systems (not just one or two), the timing is hard to pin down. He added that instruments are a smaller part of Illumina's business than for many peers; the bigger driver is consumables demand, making X successful, helping customers transition volumes, and talks about technology choices for tests they will launch in two years.

## How Dhingra judges funnel health

He now personally engages with roughly the top 20-25 customers about their choices, what Illumina offers, and early-access products. He segments the funnel as follows:

- The top ~30 customers in each region: their planned purchases and capacity needs over the next one to two years, driven by current sequencing usage, new tests coming online and newer clinical trials.
- A long tail of customers, driven by underlying Gb growth.
- Data points: Gb growth has been about 40% in each of the last two quarters, versus a 10-year average of about 25%. The midpoint of that range implies roughly a doubling of sequencing volume in about three years, which he called "pretty robust demand."

## China

Kumar noted China revenue has hovered around $75-80M per quarter for three straight quarters and asked whether it has bottomed and whether the US election changed anything.

- **Stabilization.** Dhingra said China has stabilized for two to three quarters at $75-80M per quarter, roughly $300M a year annualized. Before that, China had declined even sequentially (quarter to quarter), not just year over year, for three years.
- **Changes made.** Illumina replaced its China leader with someone with a strong pharma background and deep relationships with hospital systems, the broader genomics community and government. She took a fresh look at the market. Structural go-to-market changes were made, including participating directly in tenders (government-run competitive bids).
- **Competition.** The China competitive environment differs from the rest of the world, with many local players, large and small, and intensity remains strong. Dhingra said it has been stable for six months with no new dynamic. There are five to six local sequencing providers, now competing more among themselves; he thinks some larger Chinese players may be ceding share to newer, smaller ones. Illumina still works with pharma and hospital chains and is participating in new tenders, which gives him confidence it may add new customers.
- **Government relationship.** Over the last four to five months he has seen incremental improvement: Illumina has been able to participate in some tenders it previously could not, and there has been local government support for expanding Illumina's manufacturing and other local capabilities. He cautioned: "Not to say we're out of the woods, anywhere near that." Strategy is localized products and a better go-to-market.
- **Stimulus and growth in 2025.** Asked if China could grow in 2025 with stimulus, he said "We would love for that to happen. I'm not banking on that." Schwartz added that stimulus so far has targeted other areas of life sciences tools, not sequencing, and nothing is built into Illumina's forecast. Dhingra said the plan to return to high-single-digit growth by 2027 does not assume a meaningful China contribution.

## Tariffs

Asked about a possible 50-60% tariff scenario on China and exposure to Mexico and Canada, Dhingra said direct exposure to China is "very minimal." Illumina's manufacturing is global but centered in the U.S. (several sites) and Singapore, its two largest hubs. It makes nothing in China for the rest of the world. The team is examining second- and third-tier component supply chains for any China-sourced parts and seeking alternative sources. He did not address Mexico or Canada specifically.

## Long-range plan: sequencing growth assumptions

Kumar noted that at the (mini) Analyst Day, a medium-term assumption of about 25% sequencing activity growth was given, versus ~40% currently, and asked if 25% is conservative.

- Dhingra said the strategic focus is keeping sequencing activity as high as possible. Much comes from tests already on market clinically, but a large part comes from new research moving into clinical use.
- The 25% is the last 10-year average, "the best data point that we have."
- Each new high-throughput platform launch brings a bump because customers do extra validation work, but he would not attribute the whole move from 25% to 40% to that; use of sequencing has broadened in many areas.
- Emerging applications he cited:
  - **MRD** (minimal residual disease, testing for leftover cancer after treatment): yet to play out; he expects MRD volumes to become accretive during the three-year strategy period.
  - **Oncology therapy selection:** remains very strong and continues to drive volume.
  - **Early detection:** probably further out; not a major contributor over three years.
  - **Single cell / Perturb-Seq:** an "entirely new exciting area" for pharma in early drug discovery, viewed on a three-to-four-year horizon.
- His view: these new applications should at least sustain the 10-year average; "If any of them becomes larger or sooner, we'll take the upside."

## Population sequencing (POPSEQ)

Kumar asked about population genomics programs (national sequencing initiatives, which were a big driver at the 6000 launch) and a recent Illumina POPSEQ conference in the U.K.

- Dhingra said the business remains robust, with government interest in adding genomics to health care systems strong. Illumina is in discussions on roughly 40-50 programs ("give or take") at various stages. They are lumpy, which complicates forecasting, "but I'll still take the business."
- New: at least one proposal submitted to a country now includes proteomics (protein measurement) alongside genomics, reflecting a multiomic strategy. Governments are asking "so what?" about added value, and discussions have scaled from 50,000 patients in the past to programs of 1 million patients over five years, as well as software pipelines for ongoing monitoring.
- Services build-out (set out at Strategy Day): Illumina is building services in a structured way rather than as one-off programs. It hired a new SVP and is building a team for areas "only Illumina can" do, such as population sequencing and large-scale genomic data access, not competing with its customers. The aim is to support governments through the whole journey, from data collection to integration to decisions.
- Schwartz explained the conference: at the earlier Illumina Genomics Forum, a population sequencing segment let customers learn from each other, was well received, and prompted the follow-up gathering, which also let Illumina explain its innovation road map.

## NovaSeq X transition and the "clinical cliff"

Kumar noted that on the Q3 call 35% of high-throughput consumable revenue came from X (a bit above plan), and asked about concern that clinical customers have not yet transitioned, a "clinical cliff."

- **Goalpost.** Dhingra aims for at least 75% of volume on X sometime in the second half of 2025, when a larger share of volume benefits from "elasticity" (lower per-unit prices spurring more volume) and more revenue. He said it is "another three, four quarters or so."
- **Q3 2024 data.** Roughly 55% of volume, equal to 35% of revenue, was on X. In the clinical segment, about 40% of volume had already transitioned. There will be quarter-to-quarter variation.
- **Clinical dynamics.** Transition has two parts: moving current activity to X, and putting new activity on X. Many clinical customers bought fleets of X systems, and new clinical trials and new tests launch on X.
- **Pace.** Customers, including some who have said so publicly, plan a gradual move, for example one test per quarter, stabilizing each before moving the next. Nobody has told him of a wholesale one-month transition, and he has not seen anyone suddenly buy 12 X systems to move everything. He expects it to be gradual and within the run rates discussed.
- **Tipping point.** Asked if 50% of consumables on X is the tipping point, he said teams have modeled it, but "it is a curve," with no magic number. That is why he uses 75-80%, when volume growth is substantially on X.

## Q4 2024 consumables guidance

Kumar asked why consumables growth would moderate in Q4 from Q3's high-single-digit level. Dhingra gave these reasons:

1. **Tough comparison.** The NovaSeq X 25B flow cell was launched in Q4 2023, so its early launch is now annualizing (lapping).
2. **Holidays.** He assumes test volumes in Q4 will be lower than in Q3 because of holidays. "If it doesn't play out that way, we'll take it." Asked if customers are already showing this, he said "We're not in the holidays yet, so we'll see."
3. **Shorter shelf life.** X consumables carry a committed shelf life of three to four months, versus a minimum of six months for the 6000. Customers could buffer testing swings more easily with the 6000. If customers expect lower volumes, they will likely buy fewer X consumables and manage inventory; this is built into guidance.

## Instrument placements: Q4 and 2025

- **2024 run rate.** Illumina has sold roughly 60 X instruments per quarter this year (slightly above or below), about 240 for the year. Selling more than 60 in Q4 keeps the second half above the first half. He is also trying to determine the normal ongoing capacity customers will add.
- **2025.** Asked if systems could grow next year, he said "I would love for that to happen," but guidance will come in 2025. For 2024, placements are down 40% against the launch-year shipments.
- **Installed base.** 528 X systems placed as of the end of Q3 2024, with about 1,700 NovaSeq 6000s still active (approximate). He said this is roughly double the high-throughput capacity over three years since launch, consistent with sequencing activity roughly doubling (25-40% annual growth). If that framework holds for the next three years, "somewhere in that 200, 240 mark will probably be a good place to land," with macro as the swing factor. He said there is no reason to call macro either way yet.

## Mid-throughput

On the Q3 call Illumina flagged mid-throughput weakness. Kumar asked if it is macro or competition.

- **Competition is not new.** Dhingra said there has always been competition in mid throughput: "The name changes, the offering changes." It is attractive to entrants because high throughput demands deep customer confidence (customers run businesses on it) and low throughput requires commercial reach that is hard to build quickly. Mid-throughput customers are willing to "tinker with the box" and may add an 11th instrument from another vendor if they already have 10 Illumina boxes. He said most past entrants failed to build a viable, scalable business model; how it plays out remains to be seen.
- **Dynamic 1: volume shifting up to high throughput.** Customers are moving volume from mid to high throughput. He has spoken to core lab directors (shared university or institutional labs) receiving more send-out samples from biotechs that no longer want their own labs. Lab real estate is hurting (he noted moving from the Bay Area to San Diego) because companies built labs when IPOs and funding were available, and that has dried up. He thinks part of this reverses when macro and IPO markets improve, as therapeutic developers using genomic biomarkers want in-house labs.
- **Dynamic 2: XLEAP.** (XLEAP is Illumina's lower-priced chemistry used with its mid-throughput platform.) He sees very good adoption and good volumes, partly due to lower prices but also meaningful customer feedback. Specific percentages have not been shared; he will "see if we can disclose some."

## Margins, 2025 and the long-term plan

Kumar said Illumina has overexecuted on margins and asked about another ~200 basis points (2 percentage points) in 2025, with consumables sustaining momentum, flat high-throughput systems and uncertain mid throughput.

- Dhingra declined to give guidance ("You're almost writing the guidance there").
- Revenue context: guidance implies about a 3% revenue decline in 2024, and the company has not grown for three years. The aim is to return to growth, as CEO Jacob (Thaysen) said on the earnings call.
- Strategy Day guideposts still stand: high-single-digit revenue growth (by 2027, per his earlier statement in this chat) and 500 basis points of operating margin expansion over the next three years.
- For 2025 he expects "ratable or somewhere in that range" margin expansion, with "step function improvements every year" consistent with the Strategy Day.
