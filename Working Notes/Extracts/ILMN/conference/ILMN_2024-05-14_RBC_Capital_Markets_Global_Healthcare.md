# Illumina CFO Ankur Dhingra at RBC Capital Markets 2024 Global Healthcare Conference (fireside chat)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/conference/ILMN_2024-05-14_RBC_Capital_Markets_Global_Healthcare.md` · Date: 2024-05-14 · Seeking Alpha transcript of RBC Capital Markets Global Healthcare Conference; host analyst Conor McNamara (RBC, life science tools and diagnostics); speakers Ankur Dhingra (CFO, Illumina) and Salli Schwartz (Investor Relations, Illumina) · conference*

**Summary.** New Illumina CFO Ankur Dhingra, a month into the job, explains why he joined, why full-year 2024 guidance was not raised after a Q1 beat, and how pricing, the NovaSeq X transition and R&D spending fit together. He says sequencing activity still grew more than 35% in Q1 and promises a fall strategy and financial-targets event. He also lays out the maximum cash exposure of a GRAIL spin-off (about $1 billion) and the timeline of the European Commission fine appeals.

# Illumina at the RBC Capital Markets Global Healthcare Conference, May 14, 2024

This was a fireside chat (a moderated question-and-answer session on stage). The host was Conor McNamara, life science tools and diagnostics analyst at RBC Capital Markets. Illumina was represented by Ankur Dhingra, its new Chief Financial Officer (CFO), and Salli Schwartz of Investor Relations. Dhingra gave most answers, and Schwartz added detail on metrics and legal timelines. The tone from management was upbeat and confident but measured. Dhingra repeatedly stressed prudence on guidance and a need for returns on R&D spending. No analyst ratings, price targets or estimate changes were given in the transcript.

## The new CFO's background and why he joined

**Career history [RBC conf., 2024-05-14].** Dhingra said he spent 18 years at Agilent Technologies, including as CFO of its life sciences business. He then served as CFO at a molecular diagnostics company (clinical lab testing), where he used Illumina products and launched a test using Illumina technology. He later served as CFO of a biopharma company that used sequencing at smaller scale to profile the tumors of patients in its clinical trials. He said he watched Illumina for "a decade and a half" and saw the change it brought to the genomics revolution.

**Reasons for joining [Dhingra, RBC conf.].** He gave two main reasons:
- He sees a still-sizable opportunity, not only in genomics but in "multiomic" work (measuring several types of biological molecules, such as DNA, RNA and proteins, together). He believes Illumina is "very well positioned to capitalize on it, provided they do the right things."
- He knows CEO Jacob Thaysen. Dhingra was Thaysen's CFO at Agilent when Thaysen was President of the life sciences business. He called the CEO's strategy "the right thing to do" and said "he is wicked smart. I have a lot of respect for him." He said the two factors made the decision "pretty much a no-brainer."

## First weeks on the job and involvement in 2024 guidance

Dhingra joined in mid-April 2024, after Q1 had ended. He said he was deeply involved in the earnings process and in developing the full-year guidance (management's financial forecast for the year). That included taking it through the board and audit committee, in what he called an intense three-to-four-week "class." He said he was lucky that the previous CFO was available as an adviser, which was not the case in some of his earlier jobs.

## Early priorities and "low-hanging fruit"

**Three company priorities [Dhingra, citing Thaysen].** Dhingra restated CEO Thaysen's three priorities as:
1. Driving top-line (revenue) growth.
2. Driving operational excellence (running the business more efficiently).
3. Resolving GRAIL (Illumina's cancer-detection spin-out business, which regulators forced it to separate). He said the team is making good progress and is "getting close towards the timeline that we have communicated."

**Operational excellence [Dhingra].** This is "a strong part of my wheelhouse." He sees opportunity across every line of the income statement: gross margin (revenue minus cost of goods), R&D, and SG&A (selling, general and administrative costs). He said some opportunities are "low hanging," while others are "higher hanging" and require "developing the muscle."

**Early proof points [Dhingra, Q1 2024 results].** Operating margin expanded by 190 basis points (1.9 percentage points) in Q1. Operating expenses (OpEx) fell year over year. He said the executive team is aligned and energized. When he brings ideas, "they are being wrapped up very quickly," and the team's response is often that they have already thought of it and want to pursue it.

## Reporting and disclosure

McNamara asked whether Dhingra would change how Illumina segments revenue or what metrics it gives. Investors ask on every call about clinical versus research customers and high-throughput versus low-throughput instruments.

**Dhingra's philosophy.** He said he had not yet formed specific opinions on changes. He intends to provide the level of detail needed to understand the main business drivers and to show how strategies are being measured. He said Illumina has historically been "fairly open" about its metrics and that he sees good alignment with the intent to disclose.

**Metrics Schwartz shared:**
- Clinical sequencing consumables revenue (the reagent kits that clinical customers must buy for each sequencing run) rose high single digits in Q1 2024.
- About 35% of NovaSeq X shipments went to clinical customers in 2023, and Q1 2024 was roughly in line with that. NovaSeq X is Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer.
- Q1 shipments were "very well in-line" with the ranges Illumina gave in February 2024 for every instrument class.

## Q1 2024 beat and why full-year guidance was not raised

**Question.** Illumina beat its Q1 guidance on revenue and margin but kept full-year guidance unchanged. McNamara asked what drove the outperformance and why guidance was not raised.

**Q1 drivers [Dhingra].** He described a "pretty sizable revenue beat," driven mostly by consumables and a good NovaSeq X placement story (instruments placed with customers). Backlog (orders not yet shipped) built over the quarter, as it does seasonally, and he called it a good build. He also cited a broad beat on gross margin and on OpEx, producing "a pretty healthy operating margin start."

**Existing full-year framework [Dhingra, restating Thaysen].** Illumina expects 2024 to be similar to 2023 overall, with operating margin closer to 20%.

**Reasons for not raising [Dhingra]:**
- It is early in the year, with one quarter done and three to go.
- He asked whether anything material had changed in end markets. The capital investment environment (customers' willingness to buy expensive instruments) had not changed, and China had not changed materially.
- Illumina executed well, but market conditions had not improved. Banking the Q1 outperformance to "derisk" the full-year guidance "would probably be a prudent approach" while watching Q2.
- By the time Q2 guidance is given, "everyone can mathematically build every quarter in the full year."

He said he feels good about the operating margin pathway and the actions the team is taking.

## Pricing: price increases versus competitive pressure

**Annual price increases [Dhingra].** Illumina raises prices every year. In 2024 it saw "very good realization" versus plan on both instruments and consumables, which he called a net positive and a sign of Illumina's underlying market position regardless of competitive pressure.

**Transition to higher-throughput products [Dhingra].** The shift to NovaSeq X and to XLEAP chemistry (a newer sequencing chemistry) in both the mid- and high-throughput portfolios is "going as expected." Customers buy higher-volume instruments and generate more sequencing, which lowers cost per unit of sequencing and so lowers the net price Illumina realizes. He cited sequencing growth of over 35% in Q1. Illumina modeled this lower net price when it guided that 2024 would look similar to 2023: consumption volume rises while price per unit falls. It has progressed in line with expectations.

**Pull-through [Schwartz].** Pull-through (the consumables revenue generated per instrument placed) was in line with the ranges given at the start of the year across mid-, high- and low-throughput instruments. The ranges already took these moving parts into account.

**P4 flow cell [Schwartz].** Illumina launched the P4 flow cell (a consumable on which sequencing runs occur) in March 2024, so only a few weeks of data existed before earnings. Reception was "very well received." Roughly 25% of customers using the NovaSeq 6000 "1K, 2K" flow cells had adopted the software that lets them use it. She called this a good early indication of uptake.

## XLEAP chemistry and the mid-throughput customer pause

**Question.** McNamara asked whether customers paused equipment purchases ahead of the XLEAP launch on mid-throughput instruments, and what feedback and consumable impact to expect.

**Answer.** Dhingra said "a little bit," as the launch was in mid-March, near the end of the quarter. He said the impact was not very material relative to the past, though some customers did wait. Schwartz said early uptake of the new chemistry is good, citing work such as single-cell and spatial studies (techniques that profile individual cells or map where molecules sit in tissue).

**Lag from launch to adoption [Schwartz].** She said the lag is one quarter to maybe two, depending on the work, mainly on the research side. Clinical customers can take longer when moving existing work over, because of revalidation and redocumentation, though those building something new can start sooner. Illumina works closely with customers on upcoming products and may do more of that in future so changes are not a surprise.

**Research customers [Dhingra].** Excitement is visible on the research side, especially for multiomics. Customers are preparing or waiting, because they see either new research programs or the chance to run more samples within a constrained grant environment (limited research funding).

## R&D spending: is 20% of sales right?

**Question.** Illumina has historically spent about 20% of sales on R&D, funding products like NovaSeq X and XLEAP. McNamara asked if that is the right level.

**Answer [Dhingra].** He did not commit to a number. His stated philosophy: spending 20% of revenue on R&D requires a good return, and "if I can't justify a return on it, then it doesn't make sense." After time with the R&D teams he said he is "blown away" by the pipeline, which makes his job harder because he does not plan to grow R&D yet must keep everything in the pipeline alive. He called that "a good problem to have."

**Breadth of R&D focus [Dhingra].** It spans core instrumentation and front-end work, multiomics, and software. His stated goal is to keep innovation alive while finding cost efficiencies, for example by running R&D programs more efficiently and asking how often an entire instrument box must be changed and whether components can be reused without hurting innovation. He said he has more margin levers in gross margin and G&A (general and administrative costs).

**Pipeline details.** McNamara asked him to share exciting programs. Dhingra said "We will at some point." When McNamara said Illumina has historically been among healthcare's most innovative companies, Dhingra replied "It's still is."

## Long-term growth and margins

**Question.** Illumina historically grew faster than life science tools peers and had margins at the high end of the sector, with EBITDA margins around 35% (EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization). McNamara asked whether it can return to above-market growth and those margins.

**Dhingra's answer.** He called this top of mind, but said he is still forming his view as he thinks about resource allocation. He gave reasons for confidence:
- Sequencing activity in Q1 2024 still grew more than 35%, which he called "huge." He contrasted this with Agilent, where moving a company from mid- to high-single-digit growth took a substantial change.
- Fundamental demand and capacity utilization support growth. Product launches are changing the gigabase (GB) output per product, so there is a cycle to work through.
- The outlook does not yet include the multiomics offerings Illumina is discussing, whether through partnerships or otherwise.
- Customers he has spoken with, mostly clinical (he was once a customer himself), still anticipate needing significant capacity. They discuss tests requiring more compute power and speed. Newer instruments could let them bring new tests to market because they can now do so economically.

He said he and Thaysen "would love to have multiple shots at the goal" on growth, and that more detail will come over the rest of the year.

**Fall strategy event [Schwartz].** Illumina will lay out its strategy "in the fall." No exact date was given, but she said it would come "very soon." It will be a virtual event with executives presenting the strategy and the financial algorithm (the targets for growth and margins if the strategy is executed), plus time for Q&A.

## GRAIL: maximum capital exposure and the fine

**Question.** McNamara noted investor confusion over the appeals, next steps and the Form 10 filing (a regulatory registration for a spin-off). If a strategic buyer is not found and GRAIL is spun off, what is the maximum capital Illumina would have to commit, given the accrued fine and the roughly $1 billion to fund GRAIL's operations?

**Two parallel paths [Dhingra].** A sale if an offer "makes sense," and a spin-off. Both are being worked in parallel.

**Cash to GRAIL [Dhingra].** Under Illumina's agreement with the European Commission (EC), the maximum capital Illumina must put on GRAIL's balance sheet is roughly $1 billion, at the time of the divestiture. He called this the near-term item.

**EC fine [Dhingra and Schwartz]:**
- The fine is already accrued on Illumina's balance sheet and is under appeal. How much, if anything, must be paid depends on the outcome. Dhingra put the timeline in 2025, potentially 2026.
- Schwartz said a European Court of Justice appeal is underway, with a decision possibly later in 2024. If Illumina wins, the fine goes away because the EC lacked the right to assess it. If it loses, a separate appeal of the fine itself runs out to 2025.
- The fine is stayed (on hold) pending the second appeal if it is not extinguished first. Illumina currently has a guarantee on the line and is weighing options.
- Schwartz said that even if Illumina lost everything and had to pay, it would "not [be] this year."
