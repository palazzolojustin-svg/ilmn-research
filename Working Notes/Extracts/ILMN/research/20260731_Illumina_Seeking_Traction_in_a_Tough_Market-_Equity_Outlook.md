# Illumina: Seeking Traction in a Tough Market (Bloomberg Intelligence Equity Outlook)

**Publisher and author:** Bloomberg Intelligence, Jonathan Palmer, Senior Industry Analyst on the Health Care team. The report is dated 07/31/26. Some sections carry a later 08/10/26 date, and the PDF was printed on 10/11/2026. Contact: jpalmer81@bloomberg.net.

**What is not in the document:** There is no investment rating, price target, valuation method, or formal estimate-change table (old → new). The only changes to numbers are the guidance raises noted below. There are no channel checks or surveys. The only consensus comparisons are those in the 2Q26 results section.

## Core thesis [BI, Palmer, 2026-07-31]

- Illumina's flagship NovaSeq X sequencer (a high-throughput DNA reading machine) and its updated chemistry platform "should help the company defend its market position." Roche's return to sequencing adds a competitive challenge.
- The transition has been "painful." NovaSeq X commercialization is ramping amid weaker customer demand and intensifying competition in mid-throughput systems (mid-sized sequencers such as the NextSeq line).
- Illumina has framed 2026 as a year of steady, clinically led growth. It makes cautious assumptions on research (academic and government-funded labs) and projects 5% organic revenue growth excluding China.
- China sanctions have cut China's contribution in half. The timing of any resolution is unclear.

## Key topics summary [BI, Palmer]

- **Outlook under pressure:** Illumina aims to create long-term value through a multiyear systems-upgrade cycle, its partnership model, and momentum in population and clinical genomics. The 2023-24 results were underwhelming because of the NovaSeq X launch ramp-up. 2025 added pressure from China's unreliable-entity designation (a Chinese government listing that restricts a foreign company's business there). 2026 is shaping up as "a measured step forward" on 5% ex-China growth.
- **Long-term sequencing dominance:** Illumina's reach in genomic tools was "unparalleled" for more than a decade. It serves research and clinical customers globally with a broad lineup. New entrants Element Biosciences and Ultima Genomics are increasing competition, and Roche's return to the market is now "a reality."
- **Limited M&A targets:** Illumina's dominance typically precludes large-scale M&A, but the Grail purchase broke the norm and led to a years-long saga that ended in its sale. Illumina has shown a tendency to acquire early-stage technologies that aid or expand its sequencing workflows.
- **Cash choices subdued:** The Grail acquisition paused share repurchases, which resumed modestly in 2024. The divestiture order imposed a $476 million fine for closing the deal without approval, plus $35-$50 million of incremental charges. Illumina keeps exposure to the screening market through a 14.5% stake in Grail and a reestablished royalty agreement.

## 2Q26 earnings outlook: "Illumina Boosts Visibility of 2027 Growth Goal" [BI, Palmer, 2026-07-31]

**Main message:** Illumina's target of high-single-digit revenue growth in 2027 "appears increasingly visible" now that 2026 guidance was raised to 5% growth excluding China.

**Instrument placements:**
- NovaSeq X placements stayed robust: 95 in 2Q26, after 80 in 1Q26.
- These compare with a goal of 60 per quarter, which Palmer says is "increasingly less meaningful."

**Clinical mix:**
- Clinical customers still make up most system buyers.
- Clinical customers accounted for 65% of sequencing consumables in 2Q26 (consumables are the reagent kits customers must buy for every run). That is up 10 percentage points over two years.
- Palmer says this "adds ballast" to revenue because clinical workflows are sticky (customers rarely switch once a test is validated).

**Main negative:**
- The primary negative in management's discussion was the second-half cadence.
- 4Q26 will need a "more marked step-up" to reach full-year goals.
- 3Q26 guidance is "a touch softer than consensus."

## Long-term drivers: recovery timeline [BI, Palmer, 08/10/26]

**Dependence:** Illumina's financial performance is tied to demand for sequencing consumables and instruments.

**2026 guidance (as described):**
- Instrument sales are expected down low single digits to flat, excluding China.
- Consumables are expected up low to mid single digits, excluding China.
- Core revenue growth is expected above 5%, with China geopolitical friction and research-funding uncertainty still clouding the outlook.

**History:** Revenue grew significantly at the 2021 pandemic peak but has seen flat-to-modest declines since, despite the launch of a new flagship system.

**Management's 2027 targets:**
- 26% operating margin.
- High-single-digit core growth.
- EPS growth in the double digits to teens.

**Grail spinoff:** The June 2024 spinoff let Illumina return full attention to core operations and removed a massive drain on profitability.

## 2026 outlook: firmer, headwinds still there [BI, Palmer, 08/10/26]

**Recovery context:**
- Illumina must rebuild confidence in its ability to deliver longer-term goals after a choppy stretch following an outsized 40% gain in 2021.
- Core sales rose just 1% in 2022, then declined in 2023 and 2024 amid slower-than-expected NovaSeq X adoption.
- A hoped-for 2025 turnaround proved muted. 2025 ended with just 2% organic revenue growth ex-China, as tariff headwinds and weak research demand offset clinical strength.

**Mitigation:** Management expects mitigation actions (pricing, supply-chain shifts and cost cuts) to "increasingly take hold through 2026."

**Margin:** Operating margin guidance is 23.4-23.6%, which includes a 100 bp headwind from the SomaLogic acquisition. SomaLogic is a protein-measurement business, and the document does not describe it further.

**Research assumption:** The outlook assumes a soft research market, with potential upside if NIH (US National Institutes of Health) funding loosens.

## Customers, products and market [BI, Palmer, 08/10/26]

### Customer base
- The customer profile is diverse by geography and application.
- Sequencing is increasingly common in commercial markets such as oncology, agriculture and reproductive health, which brings new customers.
- Most sales are still domestic, but overseas is growing as more countries fund genomic research.
- Government funding accounts for almost half of sales. US government action can temporarily cap growth, as budget sequestration and a federal shutdown did in 2012.
- Covid-19 added a wrinkle. Academic and research labs' timelines were thought to dictate the pace of recovery, but 2021 saw record demand across instruments.
- Growth is shifting to clinical end-markets. Consumables are now 60% of sequencing revenue, driven by NovaSeq X volume and oncology demand.

### Revenue breakdown
A chart based on the 2025 Annual Report shows revenue by product and by geography. The chart is only partly legible. It shows consumables at about 75%, with instruments and service/other making up the rest (service/other appears to be about 6%), and geographic splits across the Americas, Europe, Greater China and Asia Pacific. Europe appears to be about 20%.

### Sequencer lineup
- Illumina leads DNA sequencing with a broad array of systems.
- The NovaSeq X series launched at the company's 2022 Genomics Forum at its lowest cost per gigabase (a unit of sequencing output). It is "a critical catalyst" for the portfolio, with 2.5x the throughput of existing offerings.
- For smaller labs, iSeq and MiSeq use Illumina chemistry in a small footprint at a competitive price.
- Demand for the mid-throughput NextSeq line is a key driver of the expansion into clinical applications.
- The HiSeq X Ten, first offered in 2014, broke the $1,000-per-genome barrier. The XLEAP-SBS chemistry upgrades (sequencing by synthesis) are projected to push costs below $200 per genome.

### Market opportunity
- Illumina divides its addressable markets into two categories totaling over $125 billion in potential sales.
- It significantly penetrates only the research market, its current core business. At the 2022 investor day it estimated 34% captured by 2027.
- Clinical penetration hinges on more biomarker profiling and physician use of downstream sequencing applications.
- The next opportunity is the $800 million liquid biopsy market (non-invasive blood tests for circulating tumor cells or DNA). It covers cancer screening and monitoring, where Illumina hoped to compete via Grail.
- A slide image (partly legible) frames Illumina as a biology platform company with a >$25B research market and an 18% forward CAGR for clinical.
- Palmer's view: Illumina "has clearly defined large-market opportunities as sequencing moves out of the lab and into the clinic."

## M&A history and Grail [BI, Palmer, 08/10/26]

**Grail:**
- Illumina formed Grail in 2016 to commercialize a sequencing-based liquid-biopsy cancer screen with the potential to unlock a massive market.
- Illumina closed the Grail acquisition in August 2021 without first gaining EU approval. After an arduous dispute, it was mandated to divest.
- Palmer calls early detection of cancer before symptoms "a holy grail for clinicians."
- Without Grail, Illumina "must now devise a new strategy to participate in screening growth."
- Other liquid-biopsy players include Guardant Health, Tempus AI and Exact Sciences.
- Grail was spun off on June 24, 2024. Illumina sees $35-$50 million of incremental charges from the spin-off. The divestiture and a fresh CEO help refocus on core operations.

**Wider strategy:**
- Acquisitions "haven't been key" to strategy, though Illumina pursues technology deals opportunistically.
- The terminated PacBio deal can be viewed through an innovation lens, since it would have added long-read technology.
- Palmer's view: the $480 million Solexa acquisition "may go down as one of the best health-care deals in history." Illumina's current sequencing business is based on the Solexa platform. Market cap peaked at $76 billion in 3Q21, from $1.8 billion when the Solexa deal was announced. "Almost all of Illumina's value stems from sequencing."

**Helix:**
- Helix was a consumer genomics platform, which Illumina funded with two venture investors providing $100 million.
- Illumina deconsolidated Helix effective 2019 after years of weighing on profitability. Palmer viewed this as "a prudent step to tighten the portfolio."
- Palmer notes the research market is maturing, and each new instrument generation provides smaller marginal performance gains than the last.
- Quote from the Illumina press release, Aug. 18, 2015: "Helix will enable individuals to acquire an unprecedented amount of genetic information by providing affordable sequencing and database services for consumer samples brought through third-party partners."

## Capital allocation [BI, Palmer, 08/10/26]

- Illumina was drawing questions about its rising cash balance until the failed PacBio acquisition in 2020. The cash was ultimately spent on Grail.
- Year-end 2025 cash was $1.6 billion, and 2025 free cash flow was $931 million. Palmer expects it will take time to regain pre-Grail flexibility.
- Grail's spin-off was completed in 2Q24, with Illumina retaining a 14.5% minority stake.
- Capital has historically gone to buybacks that largely offset dilution from employee stock options.
- Buybacks were zero in 2022 and 2023, $115 million in 2024 and $740 million in 2025. The most recent program has $1.8 billion remaining as of 2Q26.

## Chart book and peers [BI, Palmer, 08/10/26]

- **Company:** Illumina is "turning the page" on the Grail era. Consumables propel revenue while instruments drag. Management is navigating challenges in China and academic markets, but NovaSeq X is "likely to catalyze long-term growth." Charts (product revenue, sequencing and microarray revenue) are unreadable, and margins are described as climbing even as global headwinds persist.
- **Peers:** Illumina's genomics offerings "tower over peers." Palmer compares it with larger rivals such as Thermo Fisher and with niche players, and the strength comes from sustained innovation and technology leadership in its largest end market, research and applied.
- **Margins vs. peers:** Illumina's operating margins were aligned with peers until 2023, when Grail, the delayed NovaSeq X launch and the Icahn proxy battle hurt performance. Palmer calls this "a 2023 hiccup" with "ambitious goals ahead." A margin chart (Illumina about 23.7%, and peers Waters and Bruker among those shown) is only partly legible.
- **Stock:** A ten-year price chart versus peers shows ILMN last price of about $94.26, with Waters at about $278.67. The chart is titled "Amid choppy water post-pandemic, stability on the horizon."

## Risks and flags

- China: the unreliable-entity designation halved China's contribution, with no clear resolution timeline.
- Research demand and NIH funding uncertainty. Government funding is almost half of sales.
- Competition: Roche's return, Element Biosciences, Ultima Genomics, and intensifying mid-throughput competition.
- Back-end-loaded 2H26 (4Q step-up needed, and 3Q guidance below consensus).
- Tariffs, which hurt 2025.
- SomaLogic margin dilution of 100 bp.
- Possible stale or inconsistent items: the report is dated 07/31/26, but several sections are dated 08/10/26. The long-term drivers text says "more than 5%" core growth, while other places say "5%." The ten-year price chart's final price may reflect the print date.
