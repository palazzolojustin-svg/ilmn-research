# Bloomberg Intelligence – Sequencing Market Enters More Competitive Era With Roche Push (Axelios vs. NovaSeq X)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/research/20260626_Sequencing_Market_Enters_More_Competitive_Era_With_Roche_Push.md` · Date: 2026-06-26 · Bloomberg Intelligence; Jonathan Palmer (Senior Industry Analyst, Health Care), with contributing analyst Adeline Zandi · independent research*

**Summary.** A short Bloomberg Intelligence note arguing that the DNA sequencing market is becoming more competitive as Roche's new Axelios platform challenges Illumina's long-standing leadership. It says Illumina's NovaSeq X is entrenched because customers have built workflows around it, but Roche's differentiated chemistry and its reach into hospitals and labs give it a credible path. The analyst expects no immediate disruption, but Roche could win share where workflow or cost advantages are clear. A comparison table of Axelios and NovaSeq X is included, though parts of it are garbled in the source.

## Overview

This is a brief research note from Bloomberg Intelligence, published 2026-06-26 by Senior Industry Analyst Jonathan Palmer, with Adeline Zandi listed as a contributing analyst. It is tied to the firm's "Global Life-Science Technologies 2026 Midyear Outlook" report. The document contains no rating, price target, estimate changes, valuation method, channel checks or survey data. It is a qualitative competitive assessment plus one comparison table.

The headline claim is that the sequencing market (the business of selling instruments and chemicals that read DNA) is entering a more competitive phase. New platform architectures are beginning to challenge the durability of Illumina's long-standing leadership [Bloomberg Intelligence, 2026-06-26].

## Core thesis

**Axelios as a test of Illumina's grip.** The analyst says Roche's Axelios platform could test whether differentiated technology plus Roche's channel access (its existing sales and customer relationships) are enough to loosen Illumina's hold on high-value sequencing workflows [BI, 2026-06-26].

**Illumina's position.** The note describes Illumina's NovaSeq X, its current high-throughput sequencer, as "the entrenched standard," with scale, familiarity and workflow depth. Customers have built their pipelines, bioinformatics (software that analyzes sequence data), purchasing patterns, validation processes and staff expertise around Illumina. This makes switching platforms difficult [BI, 2026-06-26].

**Roche's path.** The analyst calls Roche's path to competing "credible" for two reasons [BI, 2026-06-26]:
- Axelios uses a differentiated "sequencing-by-expansion" approach, which is Roche's own chemistry for reading DNA.
- Roche has deep diagnostics reach across hospitals, labs and oncology (cancer) workflows.

**Potential consequences if Axelios performs as advertised** (the analyst's conditional view) [BI, 2026-06-26]:
- It could pressure Illumina's pricing power.
- It could accelerate sequencing adoption in routine diagnostics.
- It could give Roche greater exposure to high-growth oncology applications.

**Timing and scale of the threat.** In the analyst's words, disruption to Illumina "is unlikely to be immediate, but Roche could still chip away where workflow or economic advantages are clear" [BI, 2026-06-26]. This is opinion, not a forecast with numbers attached.

| Cost per genome (whole human genome) | $150 (duplex) | $200 or less |

Notes on reliability:
- The output, reads-per-run and Axelios instrument-cost cells are unclear in the extracted text, so treat those values as approximate readings, not confirmed figures.
- "Duplex" next to Axelios's $150 genome cost is the source's label; the note gives no further explanation.
- The chart's source line is partly garbled but appears to credit Bloomberg Intelligence and company sources.
- The note does not explain the "nanopore-style" label in the table, and it separately names the Roche approach "sequencing-by-expansion."

What the table suggests, per the figures as legible: Axelios offers longer average reads (about 230–250 bp versus a 150 bp per-read maximum for NovaSeq X) and a $150 duplex genome cost versus NovaSeq X's $200 or less. This is the analyst's chart, not a claim made by Roche in this document.
