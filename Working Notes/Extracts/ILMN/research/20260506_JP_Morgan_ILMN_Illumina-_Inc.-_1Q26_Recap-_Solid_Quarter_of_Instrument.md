## Headline takeaways

The analysts call the 1Q beat and the 2026 guidance update "a solid start to the year." Three points stand out:

1. Clinical consumables (the reagent kits used up in every sequencing run, here for medical testing) grew 20% year over year in the rest-of-world (ROW) group for the second quarter in a row. ROW means results excluding Greater China.
2. Illumina placed more than 80 NovaSeq X instruments (its newest high-throughput sequencer) against guidance of 50-60 per quarter. Management expects a similar level in 2Q.
3. Concerns remain: research is "a drag on growth," consumables pricing is uncertain, and Illumina's position in a "ramping competitive environment" is unclear.

Total sequencing consumables revenue of $726M was only "a touch above" the Street (Wall Street consensus) of $722M.

## Revenue by product group

The product breakdown shows that instruments beat clearly, consumables were roughly in line and service missed.

- **Sequencing instruments:** $118M versus Street $102M. Organic growth was +8% y/y (+10% ROW), driven by NovaSeq X.
  - Illumina placed more than 80 NovaSeq X units, versus more than 60 in 1Q25 and guidance of 50-60. Placements grew in both clinical and non-clinical markets.
  - The total active NovaSeq X installed base is now about 970.
  - Management said it saw several multi-unit orders, particularly from clinical customers, and was "supply-constrained" on how many units it could place because demand was elevated.
  - More than 60% of 1Q NovaSeq X placements went to clinical customers.
  - Some placements included volume discounts, and some were placed under reagent rental or lease-type arrangements (the customer pays through reagent purchases or lease payments rather than buying the instrument outright).
- **Sequencing consumables:** $726M versus Street $722M, up 2% y/y organic. ROW sequencing consumables were $687M, up 5% y/y organic, driven by volume from the NovaSeq X installed base. On a reported basis consumables fell 4% q/q (versus the prior quarter), "the largest sequential decline in 1Q since 1Q20."
  - Clinical consumables grew 20% y/y ROW, for the second consecutive quarter. Management attributed this to sequencing-based diagnostics gaining traction and customers shifting to higher-throughput applications.
  - Research and applied consumables fell 12% y/y ROW because of "ongoing muted customer demand as customers navigate funding pressures." Management said that as funding uncertainty eases, research and applied will return to growth.
  - Sequencing activity: total activity on high- and mid-throughput instruments rose more than 30% y/y (after more than 30% in 4Q25), with clinical volumes "well above that number."
  - Clinical consumables were more than 65% of sequencing consumables in the quarter.
- **Service and other:** $151M versus Street $169M, up 4% y/y organic (+5% ROW).

## 2026 guidance (updated at 1Q26)

The raise was small and driven by instruments. In dollar terms, revenue guidance rose by $20M while organic growth guidance was unchanged.

| Item | New guide | Prior guide | Street / JPMe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reported revenue | $4.52-4.62B | $4.50-4.60B | Street $4.53B |
| Organic growth | +1.0-3.0% | unchanged | JPMe +2.0% |
| ROW organic growth | +2.0-4.0% | unchanged | n/a |
| Adj. OPM | ~23.4-23.6% | ~23.3-23.5% | Street 23.4% |
| Adj. EPS | $5.15-5.30 | $5.05-5.20 | Street $5.12 |

The OPM raise was attributed to 1Q outperformance and higher revenue.

**Moving pieces within the guide:**
- Sequencing instrument revenue (ROW) is now expected to be flat to up low-single-digit percent (LSD), versus a prior guide of down LSD to flat. Drivers are continued NovaSeq X demand and a solid backlog, with performance obligations (contracted future deliveries) up more than 20% y/y in the quarter for both instruments and consumables.
- NovaSeq X placements: about 80 in 2Q, then more than 60 in each of 3Q and 4Q ("per our follow up"), versus the prior guide of 50-60 per quarter.
- NovaSeq X annual pull-through (consumables revenue generated per instrument) was reiterated at about $1.3M per instrument "or higher."
- ROW sequencing consumables revenue is expected to grow low- to mid-single digits (unchanged). That rests on clinical growth of double digits to mid-teens (+DD/mid-teens) and research and applied declines of mid- to high-single digits (-MSD/HSD).
- The company "feels good" about the clinical market growing double digits to mid-teens this year, and said growth above that would be upside to guidance.

**2027:** Illumina says it remains on track for its targets of high-single-digit (HSD) ROW revenue growth, continued margin expansion and double-digit to teens EPS growth.

**Other management commentary on the call:**
- Capacity: given strong demand, Illumina is investing to expand NovaSeq X manufacturing capacity, with progress expected through 2Q and into 3Q.
- Customer transition: management expects more than 80%-85% of clinical volumes on NovaSeq X by the end of 2026, versus 76% in 1Q26.
- Inflation: the company is taking multiple mitigation steps to "fully offset" inflationary pressure over the year, reflected in the implied margin ramp in the second half.
- Research funding: it started the year slowly but should improve through the year. This is not in guidance and is viewed as potential upside.
- Pricing: with more than 90% of research and applied volume already moved to NovaSeq X, management expects the associated pricing headwinds to ease through the year.

## 2Q26 guidance

Second-quarter guidance is above the Street on revenue but below it on margin.

- Organic revenue growth of +3-5% y/y (JPMe was about +1.4%); ROW organic +4-6%.
- Reported revenue of $1.12-1.14B (Street $1.11B), assuming about 80 NovaSeq X placements, similar to 1Q.
- Adj. OPM of about 22% (Street 23.5%), because of mix and inflation from component costs and freight, which the company plans to mitigate in 3Q and 4Q.
- Adj. EPS of $1.20-1.25 (Street $1.25).

## Risks to rating and price target

- **Upside risks:** (1) faster-than-expected recovery in the academic end market; (2) stronger clinical growth from increased adoption of sequencing-based clinical applications; (3) broader uptake of whole genome sequencing; (4) expanding use cases for multiomic offerings.
- **Downside risks:** (1) ongoing uncertainty or further pressure in academic research funding; (2) intensifying competition and potential pricing pressure in the sequencing market; (3) execution risks in the launch and adoption of new products.
