# J.P. Morgan upgrades Illumina to Overweight (PT $185) after customer survey shows sticky clinical base and test-volume runway

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/research/20260615_JP_Morgan_ILMN_Illumina-_Inc.-_Upgrading_to_Overweight_on_a_Sticky_Clin.md` · Date: 2026-06-10 · J.P. Morgan North America Equity Research; analysts Casey Woodring (lead), Marta Nazarovets Zaremba, Jaden Rismay, Sebastian Sandler · sell-side research*

**Summary.** J.P. Morgan raised its rating on Illumina from Neutral to Overweight and its December 2026 DCF-based price target from $125 to $185. The call rests on a survey of 62 customers (30 research, 32 clinical) that points to sticky users, above-trend 2027-2028 spending and rising Illumina share, plus a clinical test-volume runway driven by therapy selection, MRD and MCED. The analysts see Roche's Axelios 1 as a manageable near-term threat, U.S. academic funding as the biggest source of upside, and Illumina's valuation premium to Core Tools as well below its historical level.

# J.P. Morgan: Illumina upgrade to Overweight, PT $185 [JPM, 2026-06-10]

## Rating, price target and headline numbers

J.P. Morgan (lead analyst Casey Woodring, with Marta Nazarovets Zaremba, Jaden Rismay and Sebastian Sandler of Life Science Tools & Diagnostics) upgraded Illumina (ILMN) from Neutral to Overweight on 10 June 2026. Overweight means J.P. Morgan expects the stock to outperform the average total return of the analysts' coverage universe over the price-target period. [JPM, 2026-06-10]

- The December 2026 price target rises from $125 to $185. The stock closed at $160.03 on 9 June 2026, so the target implies roughly 15.6% upside (arithmetic on the report's figures).
- The target is derived from a discounted cash flow (DCF, a valuation that adds up the present value of projected future cash flows) using a CAPM-derived WACC (weighted average cost of capital, the discount rate) of 9.1% and terminal growth of 4.5%.
- The report notes that Illumina has been "one of the most highly debated stocks in our space over the last year and a half" since Roche introduced its Sequencing by Expansion (SBX) technology, which underpins its new Axelios 1 sequencer.
- The analysts had been Neutral, "looking for more visibility into the competitive landscape" and waiting on Academic & Government (A&G) budget constraints. Over the last few quarters they became more comfortable with customer stickiness, particularly among large, high-growth clinical customers.

## Core thesis in brief

J.P. Morgan's argument has five parts [JPM, 2026-06-10]:

1. Illumina's clinical franchise is anchored by major CLIA labs (U.S. clinical laboratories certified under the CLIA regulations) that have largely moved to the NovaSeq X, Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer. They face meaningful switching friction, so the user base is "stickier-than-appreciated."
2. A multi-year clinical test-volume runway, driven by therapy selection, MRD and MCED, plus rising "sequencing intensity" (more data per test as labs adopt whole genome, RNA and multiomic workflows), underpins a forecast of +HSD (high-single-digit) organic growth through 2030E.
3. Competitive risk from Roche's Axelios 1 looks manageable near term because of unresolved concerns about reusable flow-cell contamination and higher DNA input requirements.
4. Incremental upside comes from a recovering U.S. A&G end market into 2H26/2027 and from Illumina's expanding multiomics portfolio (Fluent, SomaLogic, StrataMap).
5. Valuation is attractive even after the recent run-up: +mid/high-teens adjusted EPS growth and a leading ~4-5% free-cash-flow yield, at a 4.1x EV/EBITDA premium to Core Tools versus a historical premium of ~15x.

Terms used: MRD is minimal residual disease testing (detecting leftover cancer after treatment). MCED is multi-cancer early detection (blood tests screening for several cancers at once). Therapy selection means sequencing a tumor to choose a drug. WGS is whole genome sequencing; WES is whole exome sequencing. "Core Tools" is J.P. Morgan's group of traditional life-science tools companies (the report names DHR, MTD, TMO and WAT as high-quality examples).

## Clinical franchise: why J.P. Morgan sees it as sticky

### Size of clinical exposure
- More than 40% of Illumina's revenue is exposed to clinical diagnostics consumables (consumables are the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every run). Clinical customers make up 65%+ of Illumina's sequencing consumables [JPM, 2026-06-10].
- This is described as the fastest-growing end market in J.P. Morgan's coverage, with the exception of "maybe semis." The Specialty Diagnostics (Spec. Dx) universe is expected to see +high-teens to >+20% annual test-volume growth from 2026 to 2028E.

### Customers have moved or are moving to NovaSeq X
- All of the major CLIA labs are Illumina users and have transitioned, or are transitioning, to NovaSeq X.
- 76% of clinical high-throughput gigabases (Gb, a measure of sequencing output) shipped on NovaSeq X as of 1Q26.
- The pricing headwinds associated with that transition are described as "largely gone exiting this year." Illumina has consistently seen underlying consumables volume growth of at least +30%, using Gb output as a proxy.

### What Spec. Dx management teams told J.P. Morgan
- There is "general intrigue" about Roche's platform, but customers are not urgently planning to switch. They are either actively moving to NovaSeq X or have just done so.
- Because pricing and reimbursement continue to be tailwinds across the space, the gross-margin payoff from moving to Axelios 1 is diminishing, particularly in regulated workflows with FDA-approved assays, where switching adds risk.

### Structural tailwinds
- TAM (total addressable market) expansion in therapy selection (see J.P. Morgan's ASCO note).
- Clinical adoption of MRD, with guideline support in the outer years described as a significant catalyst.
- Commercialization of MCED and single-cancer screening assays.
- Sequencing intensity per test is expected to keep rising as labs adopt bigger panels, including WGS (an ongoing trend, per J.P. Morgan's AMP note), and add RNA sequencing. Per Illumina, WGS requires about 15x as much data (Gb) per sample as WES.
- Expert feedback at AMP and ASCO has repeatedly indicated diagnostics companies are shifting to WGS and transcriptome sequencing.
- Combined, these factors are expected to drive sustained +low/mid-teens clinical sequencing consumables growth in the outer years.

### Terminal-value argument
The analysts acknowledge that "a longer-term question about the terminal value of ILMN in a more competitive environment remains." Their counterpoints are that:
- Clinical testing volumes have a long runway because major markets remain underpenetrated (MRD in solid and heme tumors, MCED, newborn screening).
- Lower sequencing costs will support new testing applications "for which companies may not exist today."
- Diagnostics are moving toward multiomic approaches, where NGS (next-generation sequencing) sequencers serve as the readout for proteomics and single-cell analysis. Single-cell and spatial experiments need 3-15x and 10-20x more sequencing per sample than WGS, respectively (company estimates).

## Competition: Roche Axelios 1 (SBX)

J.P. Morgan sees "unanswered questions around Axelios' workflow (specifically, the reusable flow cells)" and argues that "pricing alone does not seem to be enough to fully convert customers" [JPM, 2026-06-10].

- **Reusable cartridge / flow cell:** industry participants, quoted via AlphaSense Expert Insights, flagged elevated contamination and cross-sample carryover risk and questioned whether performance stays consistent across reuse cycles. This is an especially high bar for regulated, reproducible clinical workflows.
- **DNA input:** reportedly higher input requirements, which would limit compatibility with low-yield specimens.
- **Analysis stack:** described as fragmented, relying on multiple third-party tools, which may complicate integration and add "hidden" costs.
- The analysts concede that Illumina's "competitive standing is under more pressure now than in the past."

## Customer survey (62 respondents)

The survey covered 62 Illumina customers: 30 research and 32 clinical, 65% in North America and the remainder in Europe. The full slide deck is linked separately from the report and is not reproduced here. Headline conclusion: both groups expect instrument and consumables spending in 2027 and 2028 to run above Illumina's current organic growth trends and above its long-term +HSD organic growth target, and they expect Illumina's overall NGS share to increase over the next two years.

### Expected spending growth

This table shows the average year-over-year spending increases respondents said they planned. Clinical customers expect stronger growth than research customers, and instrument plans exceed consumables plans in both groups.

| Customer group | Instruments 2027 vs 2026 | Instruments 2028 vs 2027 | Consumables 2027 | Consumables 2028 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Research | +18.6% | +19.0% | +9.0% | +12.8% |

### Volume versus spend
Consumable volume growth exceeded spending growth in both groups, which implies favorable pricing for customers:
- Research: gap of +2.4 points in 2027 and +4.5 points in 2028.
- Clinical: gap of +5.2 points in 2027 and +5.1 points in 2028.

J.P. Morgan says this pricing pressure is smaller than the "-DD%" (negative double-digit) pricing pressure seen for Illumina recently.

### Share expectations
Research customers of all sizes generally expect a modest rise in Illumina's share of their NGS activity, about +1-3%. Among clinical customers the increase is concentrated in mid-sized customers, who expect about +7%.

### Switching friction (open-ended clinical responses)
Clinical respondents showed limited urgency to add a vendor, citing satisfaction with incumbents on core purchase criteria and the burden of changing. Quotes:
- "it is difficult to suddenly switch as we are using almost every product of Illumina and we do not need to add any more vendors" [Survey respondent, clinical, via JPM]
- "we are currently receiving good services and do not have any reasons to evaluate any other vendor" [Survey respondent, clinical, via JPM]

## Research end market: U.S. A&G is the biggest source of upside

- Research is about 40% of Illumina's revenue, including about 25% U.S. A&G (Academic & Government) exposure and 10-15% indirect and LSD% (low-single-digit) direct NIH exposure, as of April 2025. By comparison, Core Tools averages MSD% (mid-single-digit) U.S. A&G exposure and 3% indirect / 2% direct NIH exposure.
- A&G remains under pressure, but J.P. Morgan sees the most upside here in 2H26 into 2027 as grant disbursements reset and customers regain confidence in more normalized funding patterns.
- Survey: research customers plan consumables spending +9.0% in 2027 and +12.8% in 2028, and instrument spending +18.6% in 2027 and +19.0% in 2028.

## Multiomics as incremental upside

- Illumina has said multiomics plus BioInsight should contribute at least 100-200 basis points (1-2 percentage points) to total company growth by 2027 [Illumina guidance, as recalled by JPM].
- The portfolio began with the Fluent acquisition at the 2024 Strategy Update and has expanded to include the SomaLogic acquisition (proteomics) and the new StrataMap Spatial Solution.
- AI is a possible demand driver, since multiomic datasets are used to build and train AI models for target identification and validation (J.P. Morgan cites its own earlier note on AI's impact on life science tools).
- Single-cell, proteomic and spatial research all need additional sequencing: 3-15x (single cell) and 10-20x (spatial) more per sample than WGS (company estimates).
- J.P. Morgan stresses its "core upgrade thesis is centered on the clinical growth opportunity"; multiomics is an add-on.

## How J.P. Morgan builds +HSD organic growth

The primary driver of +HSD organic growth starting in 2027 is a ~+low/mid-teens CAGR (compound annual growth rate) in rest-of-world (ROW, meaning ex-China) clinical sequencing consumables from 2026 to 2030E. Other assumptions [JPM, 2026-06-10]:

- China clinical consumables: declines of -LDD (low double digit).
- ROW research sequencing consumables: +MSD CAGR 2026-2030E.
- China sequencing consumables: no recovery forecast; declines of about -mid-teens.
- Net result: total sequencing consumables CAGR of +LDD from 2026 to 2030E.
- ROW sequencing instruments: +MSD/HSD CAGR 2026-2030E (China about -mid-teens decline), built on ROW research instrument growth of +MSD (helped by a better U.S. A&G backdrop) and ROW clinical instrument growth of +HSD (clinical test volume and new use cases).
- Gross margin: stays in the high 60s because reagent rentals may pressure average selling prices (ASP). Reagent rentals are only LSD% of boxes today.
- Operating expenses: grow +MSD/HSD. Together with +HSD organic growth, this yields +mid/high-teens adjusted EPS growth through 2030E.
- Comparison group: Core Tools average +MSD organic growth and +LDD adjusted EPS growth in 2028E. J.P. Morgan argues Illumina has "scarcity value" as a +HSD organic grower with that EPS profile.

## Valuation

- The $185 target implies 19.7x 2027E EV/EBITDA (enterprise value to EBITDA, a measure of what the whole business is worth relative to operating earnings), versus Illumina's current 17.8x (9 June close).
- Core Tools average is 15.6x on the same measure (9 June close), so the premium is 4.1x. The cover text elsewhere says "~3x" at current prices; the thesis box cites 4.1x, which corresponds to the premium implied at the target multiple (19.7x minus 15.6x).
- The historical average premium was ~15x (2015-2024) on 1-year-forward EV/EBITDA, which J.P. Morgan says makes today's premium look attractive.
- Free cash flow yield of ~4-5% over 2026-2028E, on par with high-quality Core Tools names (DHR, MTD, TMO, WAT).
- J.P. Morgan describes Illumina as "a diversified derivative play" on the structural tailwinds in its Specialty Diagnostics names (therapy selection and MRD).

## Risks to rating and price target

Downside risks listed by J.P. Morgan [JPM, 2026-06-10]:
1. Worse clinical and research customer attrition to Axelios.
2. Slower clinical sequencing consumables growth because of delayed guidelines or approvals in key diagnostics markets such as MRD and MCED.
3. Pricing pressure across sequencing instruments and consumables because of competition.
4. Minimal improvement in the research funding environment.
