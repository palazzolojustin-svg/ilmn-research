# Illumina: Seeking Traction in a Tough Market (Bloomberg Intelligence)

## About this document

This is a Bloomberg Intelligence (BI) equity research package on Illumina (ILMN), the dominant maker of DNA sequencing instruments and the reagent kits (consumables) used with them. It is written by Jonathan Palmer, Senior Industry Analyst on the Health Care BI team, with contributions from analyst Adeline Zandi. It combines several sections written on different dates: the thesis [BI, 2026-02-06], a first-quarter 2026 preview [BI, 2026-04-24], and long-term drivers, key topics, chart book and peer sections [BI, 2026-03-11].

The document gives no buy/sell rating, price target, valuation method or estimate-change table. It does include a Bloomberg Interactive Calculator (a valuation tool, shown only as an unreadable image). It contains no channel checks or survey data. It is an analytical overview, and its views are BI's opinions unless attributed to management.

## Core thesis [BI, 2026-02-06]

- Illumina's flagship NovaSeq X system (a high-throughput sequencer, launched 2022) and its updated chemistry platform should help the company keep its dominance in sequencing.
- The return of Roche to sequencing "adds a wrinkle" to the competitive picture.
- The long transition to NovaSeq X has been "painful." Commercialization is ramping while customer demand is weaker and competition is accelerating in mid-throughput systems (mid-sized sequencers).
- Management has framed 2026 as a year of steady, clinically led growth with cautious assumptions for research. It projects 2-4% organic revenue growth excluding China.
- China's sanctions have cut the market's contribution to Illumina in half. The timing of any resolution is described as ambiguous.

## 1Q26 earnings preview [BI, 2026-04-24, Adeline Zandi]

BI says the 4Q25 results were "a step in the right direction." The question now is whether that momentum can last in a stepped-down 1Q26. Clinical strength needs to keep offsetting softness elsewhere. Research (academic and government-funded labs) remains pressured, and instrument demand is normalizing after a strong 4Q.

BI expects three things to matter more than the quarter's actual numbers:
- Commentary on NovaSeq X utilization (how heavily customers use the instruments, which drives consumable sales).
- The pace of the remaining transition to NovaSeq X.
- How far pricing headwinds in research are beginning to abate.

BI's view is that the 2026 setup looks better than a year earlier, but the story still needs support beyond clinical to feel durable. It says it is likely too early to see any impact from Roche's Axelios (Roche's new sequencing platform).

### Key points for 1Q26

- Consensus core revenue is $1.07 billion, and consensus EPS is $1.05.
- Analysts estimate a 1Q26 core operating margin of 20.5%, up from 20.4% in 4Q25.
- Full-year 2026 guidance: core revenue growth of 1-3% (as written in the preview section; elsewhere in the document it is 2-4%, see the discrepancy note below), adjusted operating margin of 23.3-23.5%, and core EPS of $5.05-$5.20.

## Recovery timeline and guidance detail [BI, 2026-03-11]

Illumina's results depend on demand for sequencing consumables and instruments. 2026 guidance, excluding China, calls for:
- Instrument sales down low single digits to flat.
- Consumables up low to mid single digits.
- Core revenue growth of 2-4%.

China-related geopolitical friction and research-funding uncertainty continue to cloud the outlook. Revenue rose sharply during the pandemic peak in 2021 but has been flat to modestly down since, despite the launch of a new flagship system.

Management's 2027 targets, as cited by BI, are:
- A 26% operating margin.
- High-single-digit core revenue growth.
- EPS growth in the double digits to teens.

The June 2024 spin-off of Grail (the cancer-screening business) let Illumina focus on core operations and removed a large drain on profitability.

## Outlook under pressure [BI, 2026-03-11]

Illumina says it aims to create long-term value through a multiyear systems-upgrade cycle, its partnership model, and momentum in population and clinical genomics. Results in 2023-24 were underwhelming because of the NovaSeq X launch ramp. In 2025, China's "unreliable entity" designation added pressure. 2026 is shaping up as a measured step forward, with low-single-digit growth excluding China.

BI says Illumina must rebuild confidence in its ability to meet longer-term goals after a choppy stretch that followed an outsized 40% gain in 2021:
- Core sales rose just 1% in 2022.
- Sales declined in 2023 and 2024 because NovaSeq X adoption was slower than expected.
- A hoped-for 2025 turnaround proved muted. The year ended with only 2% organic revenue growth excluding China, as tariff headwinds and weak research demand offset clinical strength.

Management expects mitigation actions to increasingly take hold through 2026: pricing, supply-chain shifts and cost cuts. Operating margin guidance of 23.3-23.5% includes a 100 basis point (1 percentage point) headwind from the SomaLogic acquisition (a protein-measurement business Illumina bought). The outlook still assumes a soft research market, with potential upside if NIH (US National Institutes of Health) funding loosens.

## Market position and customers [BI, 2026-03-11]

- Illumina's reach in genomic tools was unparalleled for more than a decade. It sells a broad lineup to research and clinical customers worldwide.
- Competition is rising from new entrants Element Biosciences and Ultima Genomics, and Roche is set to return to the market.
- Customers are diverse by geography and application. Sequencing is increasingly used in commercial markets such as oncology, agriculture and reproductive health, which brings new customers.
- Most sales are still domestic, but overseas is growing as more countries fund genomic research.
- Government funding accounts for almost half of sales. US government action can temporarily cap growth; budget sequestration and a federal shutdown did so in 2012.
- Covid-19 was a wrinkle: academic and research lab timelines were expected to set the recovery pace, but 2021 saw record instrument demand.
- Growth is shifting toward clinical end-markets. Consumables are now 60% of sequencing revenue, driven by NovaSeq X volume and oncology demand.

## Products [BI, 2026-03-11]

- NovaSeq X series launched at Illumina's 2022 Genomics Forum at its lowest cost per gigabase (a unit of sequenced data). BI calls it a critical catalyst for the portfolio, with 2.5x the throughput of existing offerings.
- iSeq and MiSeq use Illumina chemistry in a small footprint at a competitive price, for smaller labs.
- Demand for the mid-throughput NextSeq line is a key driver of the expansion into clinical applications.
- The HiSeq X Ten (first offered 2014) broke the $1,000-per-genome barrier. XLEAP-SBS chemistry upgrades (sequencing by synthesis) are projected to push costs below $200 per genome.

## Market opportunity [BI, 2026-03-11]

- Illumina divides its addressable markets into two categories totaling over $125 billion of potential sales.
- It significantly penetrates only research, its current core, with an estimated 34% captured by 2027 (as of the 2022 investor day).
- Clinical penetration depends on more biomarker profiling and physician use of downstream sequencing applications.
- The next opportunity is liquid biopsy (blood tests that look for circulating tumor cells or DNA), cited as an $800 million market, in cancer screening and monitoring. Illumina hoped to compete there via Grail.
- Competitors in liquid biopsy include Guardant Health, Tempus AI and Exact Sciences, among others.

## M&A and portfolio history [BI, 2026-03-11]

- Illumina's dominance generally precludes large M&A, but the Grail purchase broke the norm and led to a years-long saga ending in its sale. It tends to acquire early-stage technologies that aid or expand its sequencing workflows.
- **Grail:** formed 2016 to commercialize a sequencing-based cancer screening blood test. Illumina closed the acquisition in August 2021 without EU approval, was forced to divest after a dispute, and spun Grail off on June 24, 2024. Illumina sees $35-$50 million of incremental charges related to the spin-off. It kept a 14.5% stake and re-established a royalty agreement, and was fined $476 million for closing without approval. BI says Illumina now needs a new strategy for screening growth, and that the divestiture and a fresh CEO help refocus on core operations.
- **PacBio:** the deal (now terminated) would have added long-read technology.
- **Solexa:** the $480 million acquisition is called by BI possibly "one of the best health-care deals in history." Illumina's market cap peaked at $76 billion in 3Q21, versus $1.8 billion when the Solexa deal was announced. Almost all of Illumina's value stems from sequencing.
- **Helix:** a consumer genomics platform funded with $100 million from two venture investors. Illumina deconsolidated it effective 2019 after years of weighing on profitability, which BI viewed as prudent portfolio tightening. Illumina's 2015 press release said Helix would let individuals acquire "an unprecedented amount of genetic information" via affordable sequencing and database services [Illumina press release, 2015-08-18].

## Capital allocation [BI, 2026-03-11]

- The Grail deal paused buybacks, which resumed modestly in 2H24.
- Cash was $1.6 billion at year-end 2025, and 2025 free cash flow (cash after capital spending) was $931 million. BI expects it will take time to regain pre-Grail flexibility.
- Buybacks historically offset dilution from employee stock options. Illumina spent $115 million on repurchases in 2024, and none in 2022 or 2023. The current authorization is $1.5 billion, with $643 million remaining as of 4Q25.
- BI notes that cash questions grew until the failed 2020 PacBio deal, and the cash was then spent on Grail.

## Chart book and peers (descriptive; chart data mostly unreadable) [BI, 2026-03-11]

- Charts cover product revenue (consumables vs. instruments, 2021-2028E), sequencing vs. microarray revenue, and gross and operating margins. Titles say consumables propel revenue while instruments drag, the NovaSeq X transition is a work in progress, and margins climb despite global headwinds.
- BI says Illumina dominates the genomics landscape against larger rivals such as Thermo Fisher and niche players, helped by innovation and technology leadership in its largest end market, research and applied.
- Operating margins matched peers until 2023, when Grail, the delayed NovaSeq X launch and the Icahn proxy battle hurt performance. The peer margin chart is titled "Margin Setback a 2023 Hiccup, Ambitious Goals Ahead," and the price chart "Amid Choppy Water Post-Pandemic, Stability on the Horizon."

## Discrepancies and risks to note

- The 1Q preview lists 2026 core revenue growth of 1-3%, while other sections say 2-4%. The source does not explain the difference. Possible stale or differing versions.
- Key risks highlighted: China sanctions (market contribution halved, no clear resolution timing), weak research demand and NIH funding, pricing pressure in research, tariffs, rising mid-throughput competition (Element, Ultima, Roche), slow NovaSeq X transition, and SomaLogic margin dilution.
- Possible upside: loosening NIH funding, mitigation actions (pricing, supply chain, cost cuts), and clinical and oncology demand.
