# Barclays (Luke Sergott) on Illumina Q2 2026 results: "Once a Prom King Always a Prom King" – Underweight maintained, price target raised to $160

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/research/20260806_Barclays_ILMN_Illumina_Inc.-_Once_a_Prom_King_Always_a_Prom_King.md` · Date: 2026-07-31 · Barclays Capital Inc. (Barclays Research), U.S. Life Science & Diagnostic Tools team; lead analyst Luke Sergott, with Salem Salem and Anna Kruszenski · sell-side research*

**Summary.** Barclays keeps its Underweight (sell-equivalent) rating on Illumina after a Q2 2026 beat and a raised full-year guide, and lifts its price target 10% to $160 from $145. The analyst credits strong clinical demand and a big quarter of NovaSeq X placements, but argues Illumina is fundamentally a mid-single-digit grower facing competitive price pressure from Element ("Axelios"), Roche and others, and that a 35x P/E (price-to-earnings multiple) is the richest in Tools. The $160 target is 22% below the $205.09 share price.

Barclays Research published this note on 31 July 2026 (completed 04:03 GMT) after Illumina's second-quarter 2026 results. The lead analyst is Luke Sergott; Salem Salem and Anna Kruszenski are also listed. The title is "Once a Prom King Always a Prom King."

## Core thesis (analyst opinion)

**Strong quarter, little to criticize.** The analyst says there is "not much to pick at this Q." Clinical consumables volumes (consumables are the reagent kits customers buy for every sequencing run) were strong. Placements of NSX, the NovaSeq X sequencer (Illumina's high-throughput instrument), continued to drive growth. These gains were partly offset by the research segment, which now makes up only 35% of the business. [Barclays, 2026-07-31]

**Placements.** NSX placements topped 95 in the quarter. Barclays expects placements to moderate in the second half but stay elevated on clinical strength. The key open question is whether high-single-digit (HSD) to double-digit (DD) growth is durable.

**Academic research.** Academic spending "seemed to improve," but management "prudently" did not call it a recovery and kept its academic guidance unchanged.

**2027 consumables.** The many instruments placed in 2026 should drive consumables pull-through (the recurring reagent sales that follow each installed instrument) and growth in 2027. Barclays questions how much of this will be offset by the rest of the business remaining under pressure.

**Multiomics.** Multiomics means combining several types of biological measurement, such as DNA plus proteins. Barclays says this playbook "is beginning to bear fruit." Management feels on track to add 1-2 percentage points of tailwind (TW) to growth next year from it. This is above Barclays' expectations, and without more disclosure "we have no way to confirm these numbers."

**What the market is assuming.** With business momentum and the exit rate, investors are treating a top line above high-single-digit growth as "in the bag." New buyers must believe Illumina can deliver this consistently and may touch double-digit growth.

**The bull math.** The bull case needs clinical to keep "crushing" and the research market to stabilize. The analyst's simple arithmetic: 60% of the business grows more than 20%, and if research stabilizes, the result is double-digit growth. The analyst quips, "Even we can do this math without Excel."

**Why Barclays disagrees.** The bull math ignores competition, which will require "continued price concessions, bundling, and favorable terms" to avoid more material share losses. Barclays therefore does "not envision the market stabilizing anytime soon." The analyst's own view is that Illumina is "a MSD business" (mid-single-digit growth) and that its valuation is "most expensive in core Tools."

**Competition.**
- Element's Axelios sequencer: Barclays thinks it is competitive in the near term (NT) and can take share of the research market because of its pricing advantage in counting applications (uses where sequencing is used to count molecules).
- Roche (covered by Barclays' James Gordon): in the long term (LT), its sequencing stack could challenge Illumina in clinical.
- Illumina's 35B flow cell (a higher-capacity NovaSeq X consumable) would need to be priced to compete with these rivals if Illumina wants to hold share. The analyst adds that "inherent technological barriers" could ultimately prevent this.

**Valuation view.** Barclays "cannot justify the 35x PE multiple it currently trades at, which screens at the top of the Tools industry."

**Management.** The analyst does not want to minimize the changes enacted by CEO Jacob Thaysen and the team, and thinks they are "doing a great job cleaning up and positioning the company favorably within multiomics. It will take time." Meanwhile the analyst says "we continue to take our lumps as bulls win in the NT."

## Valuation and price target

The new $160 target is 28x P/E applied to Barclays' 2027 EPS estimate of $5.73. The prior $145 target was 28x applied to 2026 EPS of $5.21. The target was raised to reflect the Q2 beat and higher estimates for the year, and the base year was rolled forward to 2027. [Barclays, 2026-07-31]

The multiple is called "high." It reflects stronger instrument demand in the first half, which could lead to higher consumables pull-through in the near term. Even so, it leaves Illumina "one of the most expensive names in the Tools space for a company with middle of the road fundamentals."

Scenario values:
- **Upside case, $230:** assumes multiple expansion on the core Illumina business if management delivers on its HSD long-term growth framework.
- **Downside case, $100:** assumes the multiple contracts on core Illumina as competitors eat into its competitive moat.

The stated "Why Underweight?" summary: Illumina leads in next-generation sequencing (NGS), where Barclays projects significant growth driven by large research studies and clinical diagnostics. But with near-term growth issues, emerging competitors and an extended valuation, Barclays sees much upside to current prices as unlikely.

**Risks to the Barclays view (upside risks to its thesis):**
1. More innovation lets Illumina widen its competitive advantages over peers.
2. China fears and uncertainty "don't materialize into anything."
