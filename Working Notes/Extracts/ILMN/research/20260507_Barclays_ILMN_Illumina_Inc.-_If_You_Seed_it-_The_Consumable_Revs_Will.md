This is a sell-side research note (an investment bank's written analysis for institutional investors). The lead analyst is Luke Sergott, who covers U.S. Life Science & Diagnostic Tools, with Salem Salem and Anna Kruszenski. The release timestamp is 1 May 2026, 01:16 GMT. ## Rating, price target and valuation

**Rating.** Barclays rates Illumina **Underweight** (unchanged). Barclays' rating is relative: the stock is expected to underperform the unweighted expected total return of the other stocks in Barclays' coverage group over 12 months. Barclays' view of the industry group, U.S. Life Science & Diagnostic Tools, is **Positive** (unchanged), meaning fundamentals and valuations across the group are improving.

**Price target.** The target is **$122**, raised 11% from $110 [Barclays, 2026-04-30]. The share price was $126.74 on 30 April 2026, so the target implies **-3.7%** downside.

**Valuation method.** The new target is **23.5x** Barclays' 2026 EPS estimate of **$5.17**. The prior target used **21x** on **$5.11**. Barclays says it set the target "to account for the beat and our estimates going up for the year." It says the higher multiple "reflects the increased instrument demand in the 1H of the year that could lead to higher consumables pull-through in the NT" (near term).

**Relative valuation [Barclays].** Illumina trades at the lower end of its group on EV/EBITDA (enterprise value divided by earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization), but at the higher end on P/E (price divided by earnings per share).

**Scenario cases.**
- Upside case: **$150**. It assumes multiple expansion on the "Core ILMN" business if management delivers on its high-single-digit (HSD) long-term growth framework.
- Downside case: **$60**. It assumes multiple contraction on Core ILMN as competitors eat into Illumina's competitive moat.

**Why Underweight (Barclays' standing thesis).** Illumina leads next-generation sequencing (NGS, technology that reads DNA in bulk), where Barclays projects significant growth driven by large research studies and clinical diagnostics. But given near-term growth issues, emerging competitors and an extended valuation, Barclays "struggle[s] to see much upside to current prices."

**Risks to the Barclays view (upside risks to the Underweight call).** (1) More innovation lets Illumina widen its competitive advantages versus peers. (2) China fears and uncertainty do not materialize into anything.

**Stock data.** Market cap was about $19.25 billion on 151.91 million shares outstanding. Free float was 97.09%. The 52-week range was $73.86 to $155.53. Average daily volume was 1.9 million shares. Return on equity (trailing twelve months) was 33.36%, book value per share was $17.80, and there is no dividend.

**Rating history.** Barclays moved Illumina from Equal Weight to Underweight around 10 Feb 2025. Price-target history: $110 (15 Dec 2025, price $136.44), $100 (30 Oct 2025, price $99.01), $95 (2 Oct 2025), $90 (31 Jul 2025), $85 (24 Jun 2025), $77 (10 Apr 2025), $100 (10 Feb 2025), $130 (2 Feb 2025), $145 (5 Nov 2024). ## Core view in the note

### Instruments: the bull case

Barclays expects bulls to point to a "big instrument upside surprise." CEO Jacob Thaysen and team "really put it on the industry from an instrument perspective." The context is that everyone expected a tough instrument quarter given reads from every other tools company, especially around academic and government (A&G) capital-spending demand. Barclays' words: "Apparently ILMN didn't get the memo."

- Illumina beat Barclays' placement estimates (placements are instruments installed at customers) by **more than 50%**.
- Management expects the strength to continue into 2Q26, which Barclays says removes the idea of a pull-forward (customers buying early and borrowing from later quarters).
- The big NovaSeq X (NSX) placement number did not translate into a proportional instrument revenue beat. Barclays reads this as evidence that the **leasing program** is working: leased boxes bring in less revenue upfront. It understands the strategy of "seeding the market with as many boxes as they can."
- Barclays relays management's argument that "a customer is not going to lease a box just to have one because this isn't 2014 and sequencing is not a vanity research project anymore." In Barclays' words, "the consumables should come."

### Consumables: the bear case

Bears will point to in-line consumables revenue (consumables are the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every run) and "what appears to be lower price/Gb on NSX volumes" (price per gigabase, the revenue earned per billion bases of DNA sequenced).

- Barclays expected a beat on sequencing consumables because new instruments now ramp (reach full usage) much faster than in the past.
- Its explanation for the miss is that price per Gb on NovaSeq X may be falling. NSX gigabase output rose as a share of total output, but NSX's share of consumables revenue stayed flat at **55%**.
- Management's response: there is "plenty of noise" around NSX capacity and the types of flow cells used, and the general trend is still moving higher. Barclays gives them "the benefit of the doubt" because it has "imperfect data" and this is one quarter, but says it "warrants watching."
- Barclays' cynical view: Illumina may be discounting ahead of **Roche's** sequencing launch and in response to **Ultima** (a rival sequencing company).

### Net assessment

Because a big instrument beat produced only about a 50 basis point (0.5 percentage point) topline beat, Barclays says it is "hard to declare a clear victory." Still, with few beat-and-raise quarters across the sector and momentum in the business, Barclays expects shares to rise the next day and "the bull/bear debate to rage on." It adds that shares could move higher on continued outperformance. It remains Underweight because it is "not sold on the ability to deliver HSD growth in '27."

### Guidance commentary

- Management passed the quarterly beat through to the full year and now points to the **high end** of the rest-of-world (ex-China) organic growth range of 2-4%.
- The second-half margin ramp embeds a sizable step-up from 2Q to 3Q, because 3Q is typically the higher-margin quarter on seasonal mix. Barclays is comfortable with the topline guide but says the margin guide "is not a layup by any means."

### Guide notes

**2026 revenue notes**
- SomaLogic (a protein-measurement business Illumina acquired): contributes a 1.5%-2% tailwind to growth.
- OMx: 100 bps headwind.
- EPS: 18 cents headwind.
- Organic (ex-China): +2%-4%, "at the higher end of the range." Management does not mean to imply a second-half deceleration.
- Clinical consumables: growth of low-double-digit to mid-teens percent (unchanged). Clinical volume on NovaSeq X moves to 80-85%+ from 77% in 1Q.
- Academic consumables: mid-to-high-single-digit decline (unchanged). Management expects some academic improvement as budgets are released.
- Instruments: now flat to up low-single-digit, versus low-single-digit decline to flat previously. Placement cadence: near 1Q's 80 NovaSeq X placements, then greater than 60 in each of 3Q and 4Q.
- Foreign exchange: ~1% tailwind (unchanged).
- China sales: $210-220M, with roughly a 1% headwind (unchanged).

**2Q26:** rest-of-world organic growth of 4-6%.

## Key takeaways from the call [Q1 2026 call, as summarized by Barclays]

### Clinical is the growth engine

- Clinical sequencing consumables grew about **20% ex-China** for the second straight quarter and made up more than **65%** of sequencing consumables revenue, with its mix still rising.
- **76%** of clinical volume is now on NovaSeq X, expected to reach 80-85%+ by year-end 2026.
- NovaSeq X consumables stayed flat quarter on quarter at 55% of revenue while overall NovaSeq X gigabase share rose 500 bps to 76%.
- Management said trying to read quarter-to-quarter dynamics from revenue share staying flat while gigabases rose 500 bps is "a frivolous exercise" [Barclays' characterization of management]. The reason is mix: for example, if all new volume went exclusively to 25B flow cells on the X while new volume on the older NovaSeq 6000 went to smaller panels that earn more revenue per Gb.

### Research is still pressured

- Research and applied consumables were down about **12% ex-China** in 1Q because of funding caution and slow disbursement of funds.
- Management expects gradual improvement but does not build a recovery into the full-year guidance, though the guide implies a smaller decline through the year.
- About **90%** of research volumes are already on the X, which management says positions the company for cleaner growth when demand returns because pricing comparison effects (the revenue hit from customers moving to cheaper-per-Gb systems) are behind them.

### NovaSeq X placements and backlog

- More than **80** NovaSeq X units were placed in 1Q, with over **60%** going to clinical customers.
- Demand came from incremental capacity additions, multi-unit orders and new clinical trials.
- Backlog is about one quarter of revenue.

## Points of caution and uncertainty

- Barclays acknowledges it has "imperfect data" on NovaSeq X pricing and that this is a single quarter of evidence.
- Management and Barclays disagree on the interpretation of flat NSX consumables share: management attributes it to mix, Barclays flags possible price per Gb pressure from competition (Roche, Ultima).
- Margin guidance depends on a large 2Q-to-3Q step-up.
- The note says nothing new on China beyond unchanged guidance ($210-220M), listing China fears as a risk that could fail to materialize.
