## Q1 2026 results (reported around late April 2026)

**Headline numbers.** Q1'26 revenue was $1.091B, up 4.8% year over year (y/y) and 1.2% on an organic basis (excluding currency and acquisition effects). It beat consensus of $1.073B. Adjusted EPS (earnings per share, excluding certain items) was $1.15, beating both consensus and the company's internal guidance of $1.05. It rose 18.6% y/y from Core Illumina adjusted EPS of $0.97 in Q1'25 ("Core" meaning after the GRAIL divestiture). Adjusted operating margin expanded to 21.9%, up 150 basis points (1.5 percentage points) y/y.

**Drivers.** The analyst says results were driven primarily by better-than-expected NovaSeq X placements (NovaSeq X is Illumina's newest production-scale sequencing instrument) and by clinical consumables demand at the high end of expectations. Consumables are the reagent kits customers must buy for each sequencing run.

## Business segment performance

- **Sequencing consumables:** $726M, up 4.3% y/y. Rest of world (ROW, meaning excluding China) organic growth was 5% y/y.
- **Clinical sequencing consumables:** grew 20% y/y in ROW for the second consecutive quarter (also 20% in Q4'25), which the analyst attributes to expanding use of sequencing-based diagnostics and more sequencing-intensive applications.
- **Sequencing instruments:** $118M, up 8.3% y/y. More than 80 NovaSeq X systems were placed, above the company's prior quarterly target range of 50 to 60 systems.
- **Clinical mix:** Clinical was more than 65% of sequencing consumables revenue in Q1. Management said clinical demand is broad, spanning oncology, rare disease, non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT, a blood test of maternal blood to screen a fetus for genetic conditions) and large centralized labs across regions.
- **Placement mix:** Management indicated more than 60% of recent NovaSeq X placements were in clinical settings, with many representing incremental capacity rather than direct conversions from the older NovaSeq 6000.

## Market dynamics

**Clinical as growth engine.** Management continues to view clinical markets as the primary growth engine, supported by reimbursement progress, new assay launches, and customers shifting to more information-rich testing. Management said clinical is "not showing signs of deceleration" (analyst's paraphrase of management) and that growth above the current mid-teens outlook for clinical could represent upside.

**NovaSeq X transition.**
- NovaSeq X was 55% of high-throughput consumables revenue in Q1, unchanged from Q4'25.
- It represented 82% of high-throughput gigabytes (Gb) shipped.
- Research and applied volume is now about 90% transitioned to NovaSeq X; clinical volume has reached 76%. Management expects clinical to reach around 80-85% by year-end 2026.

**Research and academic weakness.**
- Research and applied sequencing consumables fell 12% y/y in ROW, reflecting continued uncertainty around funding.
- Management said National Institutes of Health (NIH, the main US government funder of academic research) funding commentary appears stable to improving, but customers may stay cautious until grants turn into spending on tools and consumables.
- Analyst's view: "ILMN has not built much improvement into guidance, leaving potential upside if funding improves through the year."

## Strategic developments

- **SomaLogic:** The acquisition closed January 30, 2026 (the note says "in January"). The business is tracking in line with expectations on revenue and profitability. Net cash paid was $363M, plus potential royalties and milestones. After quarter-end Illumina paid the first $25M milestone, tied to achieving certain 2025 targets.
- **TruPath:** Enables whole-genome sequencing with deeper insight while cutting hands-on time to about 10 minutes. Customer interest is notable in rare disease, where it can consolidate several test types into one workflow.
- **NovaSeq X roadmap:** Illumina introduced an 18-month roadmap including 14B and 35B flow cells (the consumable slides on which sequencing occurs, named by number of reads), staggered flow cell runs, and improved Q70 performance (a base-accuracy quality level). Management said these improve flexibility, throughput and workflow efficiency.
- **Spatial transcriptomics** (measuring gene activity while preserving tissue location): on track to launch later in 2026, with early-access users generating data in challenging sample types.

## 2026 outlook and guidance

**Full year 2026.** Illumina raised revenue and EPS guidance:
- Organic revenue growth (excluding China) reaffirmed at 1.0% to 3.0% y/y.
- Revenue range nudged up to $4.52B-$4.62B from $4.5B-$4.6B, versus pre-release consensus of $4.54B.
- Adjusted EPS raised to $5.15-$5.30 from $5.05-$5.20, about 8.0% y/y growth at the midpoint and above pre-release consensus of $5.11.
- Sequencing instrument revenue growth now expected flat to low-single-digit (LSD) y/y on a constant-currency (CC) and ROW basis, a slight improvement, supported by NovaSeq X demand and a placement pipeline above prior expectations.

## Valuation (analyst view)

- Shares trade at 23.7x forward earnings, a discount to the stock's 5-year average forward P/E of 72.2x. The analyst says the large discount reflects in part the divestiture of GRAIL Inc (GRAL, $54.48), completed June 24, 2024, and the return to a normalized "Core Illumina" adjusted EPS.
- Opinion: as the NovaSeq X and X Plus launch continues, ILMN "could generate solid earnings leverage in the near future" as it moves past initial launch expenses and gains complementary disposable (consumables) revenue from instrument sales.
- Opinion: current valuation "may not reflect the opportunity" in next-generation sequencing (NGS) oncology testing markets.
- No explicit valuation method or price target is stated beyond P/E comparison.

## Investment thesis (Ameriprise)

- Illumina offers a vehicle for investing in future genomic medicine, via large diagnostic markets for diagnosing, treating and monitoring diseases with genetic or hereditary origins, including cancer.
- NovaSeq X and X Plus (production-scale systems able to sequence a human genome) could be a multi-year revenue growth tailwind for the instruments franchise.
- The company believes the NGS total addressable market is $125B, with only about $10B serviced today. Single-cell, spatial and liquid biopsy innovation add potential growth.
- For more aggressive investors with multi-year horizons, the analyst views "the recent weakness as an opportunity to accumulate the stock."

## Risks

- Analyst-listed factors that could hurt the thesis: regulatory challenges to acquisitions or integration difficulties, currency fluctuations, market share erosion, increased generic competition, new product or clinical trial disappointments, delayed regulatory approvals, and reimbursement challenges.
- Items from the results: continued research and applied weakness (-12% in ROW), Greater China revenue down 27.8%, and near-term margin pressure from electronic components and freight inflation and higher instrument mix.
