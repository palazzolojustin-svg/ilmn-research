# J.P. Morgan Life Science Tools & Diagnostics Model Updates (ILMN PT raised to $220 from $185; Overweight reiterated)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/research/20260721_JP_Morgan_ILMN_Life_Science_Tools_-_Diagnostics-_Model_Updates.md` · Date: 2026-07-16 · J.P. Morgan North America Equity Research; analyst Casey Woodring (lead, AC), with Marta Nazarovets Zaremba and Jaden Rismay listed on the cover · sell-side research*

**Summary.** J.P. Morgan updated its models for seven life science tools and diagnostics stocks, including Illumina, to reflect the latest balance sheet and cash flow data and revised long-term estimates. For Illumina, the firm kept an Overweight (buy-equivalent) rating and raised its December 2026 price target to $220 from $185, citing better visibility into consumables-led growth and a more constructive long-term demand outlook. The note also lays out the full Illumina thesis (sticky clinical customers, manageable Roche Axelios 1 competition, recovering academic and government demand), the valuation method, the risks, and a financial forecast table through 2029.

This is a short J.P. Morgan sell-side (broker) research note dated 16 July 2026 (completed and disseminated that morning, 05:34–05:35 AM EDT). It is a "model update" covering seven companies. J.P. ## Illumina: what changed

- **Rating:** Overweight, unchanged. J.P. Morgan defines Overweight as expecting the stock to outperform the average total return of the analyst team's coverage universe over the price-target period. [JPM, 2026-07-16]
- **Price target:** $220 (December 2026), up from $185 previously.
- **Stock price and market value:** $187.15 per share (15 July 2026 close), with a market capitalization of about $28,316 million ($28.3 billion). The $220 target is therefore roughly 18% above the share price (this is simple arithmetic from the note's figures, not a figure the note states).
- **What drove the update:** The model reflects balance sheet and cash flow data from the most recent 10-Q (the quarterly report filed with the U.S. SEC). The firm also updated its outer-year revenue assumptions "to reflect improved visibility into consumables-led growth and a more constructive long-term demand backdrop." Consumables are the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every sequencing run, so they are the recurring part of Illumina's revenue. [JPM, 2026-07-16]
- The note does not give old and new revenue or EPS numbers for Illumina, so the size of the estimate change cannot be quantified from this document.

## Illumina: investment thesis (J.P. Morgan view)

All of the following is J.P. Morgan's opinion, not company statements.

1. **Sticky clinical base.** Illumina's clinical franchise is anchored by large CLIA labs (U.S. clinical laboratories certified to run patient tests) that have largely moved to the NovaSeq X, Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer. These labs face "meaningful switching friction," which J.P. Morgan argues makes the user base stickier than the market appreciates. Together with a multi-year runway of test volume growth, this underpins the firm's forecast of "+HSD" organic growth (high-single-digit percentage growth, excluding acquisitions) through 2030E.
2. **Structural tailwinds.** Demand is helped by growth in therapy selection (choosing cancer drugs by genetic profile), MRD (minimal residual disease, testing for tiny amounts of leftover cancer after treatment) and MCED (multi-cancer early detection screening tests). These are amplified by rising "sequencing intensity" as labs adopt whole-genome sequencing (WGS), RNA sequencing and multiomic workflows (measuring several types of biological molecules together), which means more sequencing per patient or sample.
3. **Competition from Roche looks manageable near term.** J.P. Morgan sees competitive risk from Roche's Axelios 1 sequencer as manageable in the near term because of "unresolved concerns around reusable flow-cell contamination, and higher DNA input requirements." A flow cell is the consumable chip on which sequencing occurs; contamination between reuses and a need for more input DNA are the two weaknesses cited.
4. **Incremental upside.** (a) A recovering U.S. academic and government (A&G) funding end market into 2H26/2027; (b) Illumina's expanding multiomics portfolio, named as Fluent, SomaLogic and StrataMap.
5. **Financial profile and valuation argument.** The firm expects mid-to-high-teens adjusted EPS growth and a "leading ~4-5% FCF yield" (free cash flow as a percentage of market value). It argues Illumina offers "scarcity value" because it trades at a 4.1x EV/EBITDA premium to Core Tools (EV/EBITDA is enterprise value divided by earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a common valuation multiple). That premium is "well below its historical ~15x premium," which J.P. Morgan says makes relative valuation attractive "even after the recent run-up" in the stock.
6. **Conclusion:** "we reiterate our Overweight rating with a December 2026 PT of $220."

## Illumina: valuation method

The $220 December 2026 target is derived from a discounted cash flow (DCF) model, meaning future cash flows are estimated and discounted back to today's value. Assumptions: a CAPM-derived WACC (weighted average cost of capital, the discount rate, derived using the capital asset pricing model) of 8.9%, and terminal growth of 4.5% (the assumed growth rate of cash flows after the explicit forecast period). [JPM, 2026-07-16]

## Illumina: risks to rating and price target

J.P. Morgan lists four downside risks:
1. Worse-than-expected clinical and research customer attrition to Roche's Axelios.
2. Slower clinical sequencing consumables growth because of delayed guidelines or approvals in key diagnostics end markets such as MRD and MCED.
3. Pricing pressure on sequencing instruments and consumables from competition.
4. Minimal improvement in the research funding environment.

## Peer company updates (context for Illumina's competitive and sector set)

Each of these is a J.P. Morgan view [JPM, 2026-07-16]. All use DCF-based December 2026 targets.

- **Natera (NTRA), Overweight, PT $315 (from $265):** Update includes balance sheet and cash flow data from the latest 10-Q and modestly revised outer-year revenue and margin assumptions. WACC 7.2%, terminal growth 4.5%. Thesis: leader in cell-free DNA testing (testing DNA fragments floating in blood) across women's health, oncology and organ transplant, with strong competitive position, favorable market trends and reimbursement, and an attractive pipeline; further upside from favorable guideline changes. Risks: competition and price erosion in reproductive health, MRD oncology and transplant; pipeline development and commercialization challenges; regulatory risk. Model: revenue 2,306 (FY25A) to 2,798 (FY26E), 3,407, 4,108 and 4,965 (FY29E); adjusted EBITDA (267) in FY25A, (187) in FY26E, then 98, 401, 947.
- **Guardant Health (GH), Overweight, PT $185 (from $135):** Update includes latest 10-Q data and adjusted revenue and margin assumptions, "partially driven by a more constructive outlook on Shield following Freenome's V2 CRC Data" (Shield is Guardant's blood-based colorectal cancer screening test; CRC is colorectal cancer). WACC 8.4%, terminal growth 5.0%. Thesis: liquid biopsy (blood-based cancer testing) pioneer with use cases in Therapy Selection ($10B TAM, i.e., total addressable market), Recurrence Monitoring and MRD ($20B TAM) and Cancer Screening ($100B TAM); early MRD roll-out and Shield as long-term upside. Risks: reimbursement timing, emerging competition, pipeline hurdles, regulatory risk. Model: revenue 982 (FY25A), 1,318, 1,815, 2,316, 2,936 (FY29E); adjusted EBITDA (219), (179), 131, 438, 765.

WACC 8.5%, terminal growth 5%. Thesis: immune medicine leader with $49B TAM; flagship clonoSEQ MRD test for blood cancers is seeing accelerating volumes from community-setting penetration, expanding guidelines and EMR integrations; Pfizer data licensing agreement announced in December.
