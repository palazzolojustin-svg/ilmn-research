## The news and UBS's bottom line

**What happened:** Roche priced its Axelios 1 consumables (the reagent kits that must be bought for each sequencing run) at **$150 per genome**. [UBS] This was "widely expect[ed] by the market." The headline calls the update "as expected."

**UBS view:** [UBS] The update is "relatively benign" for Neutral-rated Illumina. Two reasons are given:
1. Large Illumina customers are likely already at similar pricing, or better.
2. Illumina's roadmap, announced earlier in the same week, suggests a path to about $150 per genome on the NovaSeq X+ (its higher-throughput configuration of the NovaSeq X sequencer). This assumes the forthcoming **35B flow cell** is priced consistently with the existing **25B flow cell**. A flow cell is the consumable slide on which sequencing happens, and the "25B" and "35B" labels refer to billions of reads.

The headline also says the "competitive overhang could soften," meaning the market worry about Roche as a competitor may ease.

## Where Illumina is more vulnerable: counting applications

- **Definition:** Counting applications (for example RNAseq, which measures how much each gene is being expressed) are priced by the **price per million reads** instead of price per genome.
- **Roche vs Illumina:** [UBS] Roche is quoting **$0.06 per million reads**. Illumina's list price on its 25B flow cell is **$0.68 per million reads**. The source text is garbled here ("$006" and "$068"), so the decimal placement is a reading of the extracted figures. It is consistent with the wording "price / million reads."
- **Size of exposure:** [UBS estimate, "roughly"] Counting applications make up about **40% of Illumina's research usage**, or about **16% of Illumina's total sequencing consumables sales**. UBS describes this as the area where Illumina's pricing structure "seems more vulnerable."

## Early researcher feedback (channel checks)

[UBS] Early feedback after the pricing announcement is "skeptical." This comes from the first couple of researchers UBS has spoken to.

- They are unclear why someone would switch from Illumina to Roche, even after the pricing announcement.
- They continue to see **speed** as the most compelling feature of the Axelios, and speed has limited relevance in research applications.
- On counting, there are questions about the quality needed for **cell barcodes and UMIs** on **10x single-cell RNAseq**. Cell barcodes tag which cell a read came from, and UMIs (unique molecular identifiers) tag individual molecules so they can be counted accurately. The concern is that quality in Roche's **simplex mode** is lower than in its **duplex mode**.
- The **20-times reuse specification** might be a question for clinical applications. UBS notes this question was already raised in its earlier channel checks (a link to that earlier note appears in the original but is not visible in the text).
- UBS says it will "continue to gather more feedback."

## Company description (UBS)

Illumina is described as the largest provider of next-generation sequencing (NGS) instruments and related consumables and services, plus microarrays. It sells to academic and government research labs, clinical labs, pharmaceutical companies, agricultural companies and consumer genomics companies. UBS says it has differentiated itself through rapid product cycles that substantially increased throughput and decreased the cost of DNA/RNA sequencing. It is based in San Diego, California.

## Risks (UBS)

Company-specific risks:
- The NovaSeq ramp slows significantly.
- The sequencing market becomes saturated.
- Pricing pressure drives sequencing prices down faster than expected.
- The clinical diagnostics market fails to open up.
- Academic funding is cut.

Industry-wide risks:
1. Lower pharma spending, caused by large M&A, weaker funding for small companies, or prominent trial failures.
2. Slower global GDP growth, including escalating trade tensions.
3. Pressure on academic and government budgets, from recession-driven cuts or political change.
4. Diagnostics pricing pressure from the Protecting Access to Medicare Act (PAMA).
5. Changes in competitive dynamics, including disruptive new entrants.
6. Inability of companies to compete effectively in China, called a critical growth market.
7. Foreign exchange or tax policy changes.
