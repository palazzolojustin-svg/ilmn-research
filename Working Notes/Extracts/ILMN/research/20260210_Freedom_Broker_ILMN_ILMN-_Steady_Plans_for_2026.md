# Freedom Broker on Illumina (ILMN): "Steady Plans for 2026" (February 10, 2026)

## Document overview

This is a company-update note from Freedom Broker (the research arm of Freedom Finance Global PLC, with the US broker-dealer Freedom Capital Markets), written by Senior Analyst Ilya Zubkov. It was produced and distributed on February 10, 2026, and covers Illumina (ILMN) in the Life Sciences Tools & Services sector. The note follows Illumina's fourth-quarter 2025 results and its 2026 guidance. All views below are the analyst's unless stated as management's.

### Headline points of the note

- Revenue is back on a growth trajectory, and the analyst expects organic growth (growth excluding acquisitions) to continue in 2026.
- Operating margin improved to a "healthy" level and still has room to expand, in line with the company's 2027 targets.
- The Consumables segment (the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run) is driving revenue and signals improving demand.
- A more predictable regulatory environment underpins stabilization in Instruments (the sequencing machines).
- An expanding services portfolio diversifies revenue and opens new ways to monetize Illumina's infrastructure.

## Fourth-quarter 2025 results and revenue trend

- Total revenue in 4Q25 rose 5% year over year (YoY). The analyst says this reinforces a return to growth after declines in 2024 and the first half of 2025.
- Performance was positive across the major regions except China. Regulatory constraints in China drove a 31% YoY decline in China revenue in 4Q25.
- Shipments into China have resumed, so the company keeps a presence in that large market. The analyst says a return to growth there is "not yet in sight."
- The analyst reads the 2026 guidance as implying quarterly revenue averaging roughly the 3Q25–4Q25 level, which they interpret as stabilization of commercial operations in China.
- Renewed growth in China will likely depend on easing regulatory constraints, in particular removal of Illumina from the UEL (Unreliable Entities List, a Chinese government list whose restrictions limit Illumina's instrument sales there) [analyst view].

## Segment commentary

### Consumables: the lead growth engine

- Consumables revenue grew 6.8% YoY in 4Q25 and remains the primary revenue driver, on rising activity among clinical customers (labs that use sequencing for patient care, as opposed to academic research).
- Management highlighted stronger demand for routine sequencing outside China. Clinical consumables grew 20% YoY in 4Q25 and 16% YoY in 2H25 [management, via the analyst].
- The upgrade cycle to NovaSeq X (Illumina's newer high-throughput sequencer line) is nearing a later stage. The share of consumables revenue generated on NovaSeq X rose from 44% at the end of 2Q25 to 55% by year-end 2025.
- The analyst sees additional consumables upside from the upgrade cycle in the clinical business, where NovaSeq X already accounts for 71% of total gigabases (the volume of sequencing data produced). The analyst expects that share to keep rising in 2026.

### Instruments: stabilization, not yet growth

- Instruments revenue fell 1.3% YoY (4Q25). The analyst describes a stabilizing trend that should persist in 2026.
- Management does not expect a return to growth in the coming year despite positive commentary on NovaSeq X shipments.
- Key headwinds are China constraints and subdued demand from academic and research customers.
- The analyst still sees rising consumables shipments as a constructive signal of end-demand that could eventually turn into equipment orders. Management also noted that some customers are considering expanding their fleets.

### NovaSeq X transition through 2026

- Management expects the NovaSeq X transition to be complete in 2026, with an average of 50–60 systems shipped per quarter and a larger share likely in 4Q, which is seasonally stronger for shipments.
- Roughly 60% of systems should go to clinical customers with high utilization. In the analyst's view this will drive consumables growth through 2026–2027.
- Research shipments and utilization are "not strong yet," but management cited stabilizing regulation and funding. The analyst says whether this is a leading indicator will be clearer through 2026.
- On current funding dynamics a positive surprise is possible, but the analyst's base case for research revenue "remains moderately negative for now."

### Service and Other: reaccelerating with new offers

- Service and Other revenue grew 2.4% YoY, its return to growth after four consecutive quarterly declines. The analyst sees continued growth potential in 2026.
- The BioInsight launch can support segment revenue next year. The analyst singles out the Billion Cell Atlas product as an additional way to earn revenue from the still-soft research segment, allowing sales not only of instruments but potentially higher-margin services.

## Margins and profitability

- Adjusted gross margin fell 40 basis points (bps; 0.40 percentage points) YoY to 67%, driven mainly by about 205 bps of tariff headwind.
- Product gross margin stayed healthy at about 70% for the year.
- Service gross margin improved meaningfully in 2H25 to 57%, versus 50% a year earlier.
- Together with lower adjusted operating expenses, this lifted adjusted EBIT (earnings before interest and taxes) margin by 400 bps to 23.7% for 2025.
- The analyst views operating profitability as firmly re-established above 23% for 2025, consistent with levels before Illumina consolidated GRAIL (the cancer-test company it had owned).
- Adjusted EPS (earnings per share) was $0.05 above the top end of company guidance for both the quarter and the full year.

## Capital allocation and balance sheet

- Free cash flow (cash left after operating costs and capital spending) was $931M in 2025.
- Cash and short-term investments were $1.63B, and gross leverage was 1.6x. The analyst says this gives substantial flexibility for M&A and capital returns.
- Share buybacks were $740M in 2025, including $42M in 4Q25. $643M of authorization remains, to be used opportunistically [management, via the analyst].
- Illumina closed its acquisition of SomaLogic in January 2026 for $350M upfront plus potential royalties and milestone payments. The analyst says the SomaLogic platform broadens Illumina's presence in proteomics (the study of proteins) and strengthens its multiomics positioning (measuring several types of biological molecules, not just DNA).
- The analyst's overall assessment is a model balancing organic growth with integration of acquired assets, with excess liquidity returned through buybacks. Heading: "Conservative and Shareholder-Friendly."

## Investment thesis (analyst)

- Illumina remains the standard-bearer in next-generation sequencing (NGS, the high-speed DNA reading technology), supported by a large installed base of systems and a growing mix shift toward clinical applications.
- Growth in consumables sales and expanding free cash flow are underpinned by expansion into new verticals, including multiomics and data/AI platforms. The analyst says Illumina is positioned for a re-acceleration of revenue growth and margin expansion.
- The long-term appeal of the NGS market rests on structural drivers: broader adoption of genomics in oncology, neonatal screening, and wider NGS use across biopharma and population-scale programs.
- Illumina is described as "one of the few pure-play exposures to the long-term expansion of the global genomics market."

### Potential growth drivers

1. **Mid-term revenue growth acceleration.** A shift toward more data-intensive applications (WGS, whole-genome sequencing; MRD, minimal residual disease testing for leftover cancer after treatment; and early cancer detection) is expected to raise utilization of the NovaSeq X installed base, drive consumables demand, and support more system placements.
2. **Completion of the NovaSeq X transition.** Finishing the clinical migration and normalization of research demand should help mitigate the impact of pricing erosion.
3. **Expansion into multiomics and data/AI solutions.** Growth in service-oriented offerings is expected to create new, less cyclical revenue streams and improve differentiation of the portfolio.

### Key risks

1. **Competitive pressure in NGS.** Rising competition in high- and mid-throughput segments could intensify pricing pressure on consumables and instruments, limit margin expansion, and complicate monetization of the installed base.
2. **Macro and budget headwinds.** Persistent weakness in academic funding may constrain instrument and consumables demand in research, raising the risk to sustained mid-term revenue growth.
3. **China geopolitical and regulatory risk.** Inclusion on the UEL continues to restrict instrument sales and creates ongoing downside risk from potential changes to import/export regulations.
