# Bloomberg Intelligence – Illumina: Limited M&A Opportunities (Grail, Solexa, Helix and M&A strategy)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/research/20251113_Illumina_Shifted_to_Target_Grail_From_Traditional_M-A_Strategy.md` · Date: 2025-11-13 · Bloomberg Intelligence; Jonathan Palmer, Senior Industry Analyst, Health Care · independent research*

**Summary.** A short Bloomberg Intelligence note arguing that Illumina's dominant position in sequencing normally rules out large acquisitions, and that the Grail purchase was the exception that led to a years-long regulatory fight and a forced sale (spin-off). The analyst says Illumina tends to buy early-stage technologies that help or extend its sequencing workflow, calls the 2007 Solexa deal possibly one of the best health-care deals in history, and describes the Helix consumer-genomics venture as a platform that was pruned (deconsolidated) in 2019. With Grail gone, Illumina must find a new way to participate in cancer-screening growth.

## Overview and publication details

This is a Bloomberg Intelligence research note by Jonathan Palmer, Senior Industry Analyst on the Health Care team, titled "Illumina: Limited M&A Opportunities" (headline: "Illumina Shifted to Target Grail From Traditional M&A Strategy"). The main item is dated 11/13/25 [BI, 2025-11-13]. Several sections carry a later update stamp of 03/11/26 [BI, 2026-03-11], and the printout is stamped 10/11/2026. The note does not give a rating, price target, estimates, valuation method, survey data or consensus comparison. It is a qualitative view of Illumina's acquisition history.

## Core thesis [BI, 2025-11-13]

- Illumina's dominant position in the genomics markets typically precludes it from large-scale M&A (mergers and acquisitions, meaning buying other companies).
- The purchase of Grail broke that norm. It led to a years-long saga that ended in Grail's sale (described elsewhere in the note as a spin-off).
- Illumina has shown a proclivity to acquire early-stage technologies that aid or expand upon its sequencing workflows. Sequencing is the technology of reading DNA.

## Section 1: Grail and early cancer screening [BI, 2025-11-13; updated 2026-03-11]

- Illumina formed Grail in 2016. The aim was to commercialize a sequencing-based liquid-biopsy screen for cancer, which the analyst says had the potential to unlock a massive market opportunity.
- A liquid biopsy is a non-invasive blood test that searches for circulating tumor cells or DNA. The analyst says that catching early-stage cancers before patients become symptomatic is a "holy grail" for clinicians.
- Illumina closed the Grail acquisition in August 2021 without first gaining EU (European Union) approval. After what the analyst calls an arduous dispute, Illumina was mandated to divest Grail.
- Implication stated by the analyst: without Grail, Illumina must now devise a new strategy to participate in growth in cancer screening.
- Companies impacted: liquid-biopsy tests are being pursued by many public and private genomic-product companies, including Guardant Health, Tempus AI and Exact Sciences, among others [BI, 2026-03-11].

### Key points listed in the note

- Grail was spun off from Illumina on June 24, 2024.
- Illumina sees $35–$50 million of incremental charges related to the spin-off of Grail (the note gives no period or breakdown).
- The Grail divestiture and a fresh CEO should help refocus Illumina on core operations.
- Additional reading pointer: the Illumina Grail Capital Markets Day (a link to a Bloomberg item, not summarized in the note).

## Section 2: Solexa deal and Grail spin-out [BI, 2025-11-13; updated 2026-03-11]

- Acquisitions have not been key to Illumina's strategy, though it does pursue technology deals opportunistically.
- The attempt to acquire Grail was aimed at strategically positioning Illumina in oncology (cancer) screening. With the spin-off complete, the company is now refocusing on its core operations.
- The PacBio deal, now terminated, can still be viewed through an innovation lens, since it would have added long-read technology (sequencing that reads much longer continuous stretches of DNA than standard methods).
- Analyst opinion: the $480 million acquisition of Solexa "may go down as one of the best health-care deals in history, in our view."
- Illumina's current sequencing business is based on the Solexa platform. Its market capitalization (total stock market value) peaked at $76 billion in 3Q21, up from $1.8 billion when the Solexa transaction was announced.
- The analyst states that almost all of Illumina's value stems from sequencing.
- The note includes a chart or table titled "Illumina's M&A Activity (Click)", but its contents are not reproduced in the text provided.

## Section 3: Helix consumer platform pruned [BI, 2025-11-13; updated 2026-03-11]

- Helix was a consumer genomics platform (DNA services aimed at individuals). The analyst says its creation marked an evolution in Illumina's long-term strategy as sequencing became more accessible.
- Illumina worked with two venture investors who funded the startup with $100 million.
- The analyst calls Helix a natural extension as genomic sequencing becomes more accessible to consumers.
- Context given by the analyst: the research market is maturing, and each new instrument generation provides smaller marginal performance gains than prior generations.
- After years of weighing on profitability, Illumina chose to deconsolidate Helix (remove it from its consolidated financial results), effective 2019. The analyst writes: "We viewed this as a prudent step to tighten the portfolio and increase flexibility as other sequencing drivers arise and gain more momentum."

### Company press release quoted in the note

The note quotes Illumina's press release of Aug. 18, 2015: "Helix will enable individuals to acquire an unprecedented amount of genetic information by providing affordable sequencing and database services for consumer samples brought through third-party partners. ... Individuals will be able to manage their data and explore an open marketplace of on-demand applications."
