## The competitive announcement ("Our Take")

The text is partly garbled, but the readable bullets say that an announcement "today" by Element (the page shows "Elements," and refers to a product called VITARI) "keeps the competitive pressure on ILMN." Comparisons are made against Illumina's NovaSeq X Plus (NovaX+), Illumina's high-end DNA sequencer:

- **Instrument price:** about a 40% discount to NovaSeq X Plus, whose list price is $689k [UBS, 2026-02-19]. That implies a price near $410k, although the note does not state the figure in the readable text.
- **Consumables cost per run:** about one-tenth of NovaSeq X Plus's, roughly $3k versus about $32k. Consumables are the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run.
- **Cost per gigabase ($/Gb):** about 50% lower. A gigabase is one billion DNA letters, the standard unit for pricing sequencing output.
- **Output per run:** about one-fifth of NovaSeq X Plus's, 3T versus 16T (presumably terabases, or trillions of DNA letters per run; the unit is not spelled out in the readable text).
- **Timing:** the page shows "Shii 2H 2026" followed by a garbled word ("ppng"). This most likely means shipping begins in the second half of 2026, but that is an inference from corrupted text.

The note's argument, as far as readable, is that a cheaper machine with much cheaper runs, though smaller output, adds pricing and competitive pressure on Illumina. The remaining reasoning is not recoverable from the extract.

## Company description (UBS)

UBS describes Illumina (NASDAQ: ILMN, San Diego) as the largest provider of next-generation sequencing (NGS) instruments and related consumables and services, plus microarrays (chips that read preselected DNA variants). It sells to academic and government labs, clinical labs, pharmaceutical companies, agricultural companies and consumer genomics firms. UBS says it has differentiated itself through innovation and rapid product cycles that have substantially raised throughput and cut the cost of DNA and RNA sequencing.

## Risks listed by UBS

Company-specific: NovaSeq ramp slows significantly; the sequencing market becomes saturated; pricing pressure in sequencing drives prices down faster than expected; the clinical diagnostics market fails to open up; academic funding is cut.

Industry-wide: (1) lower pharma spending due to major M&A, reduced funding for small companies or prominent trial failures; (2) slower global GDP growth, including escalating trade tensions; (3) pressure on academic and government budgets from recessionary cost cutting or political change; (4) diagnostics pricing pressure from the Protecting Access to Medicare Act (PAMA, a US law governing lab test reimbursement); (5) changed competitive dynamics, including disruptive new entrants in certain technologies; (6) inability of companies to compete in China, a critical growth market; (7) foreign exchange or tax-policy changes.
