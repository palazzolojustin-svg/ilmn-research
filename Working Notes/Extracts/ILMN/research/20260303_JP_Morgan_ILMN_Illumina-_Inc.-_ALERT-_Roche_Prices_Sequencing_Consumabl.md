## The main news: Roche's Axelios 1 pricing [J.P. Morgan, 2026-02-26]

At its sequencing solutions workshop and lunch at AGBT, Roche announced a list price for sequencing consumables of $150 per genome for its Axelios 1 instrument. J.P. Morgan notes the following about how that price is defined:

- It includes synthesis reagents and consumables (the chemicals and plastics used in each run).
- It does not include library prep costs (the earlier step of preparing DNA samples for sequencing).
- It assumes users reuse the sensor module 20 times. The sensor module is Axelios' version of a flow cell, the chip on which sequencing happens.

For comparison, J.P. Morgan says Illumina's sequencing consumables are priced at about $200 per genome on the NovaSeq X instrument. That puts Roche's headline price about $50, or 25%, below Illumina's.

### Investor expectations

J.P. Morgan says that, "based on our conversations," most investors expected Roche to price consumables at about $150 per genome, and some estimated as low as $100. The $150 figure was therefore in line with the main expectation rather than a surprise, and above the low-end bets.

### Product specifications cited

- The SBX duplex mode can run 16 genomes per run and 64 genomes per day. J.P. Morgan translates this to a list price of about $2,400 per run (16 genomes at $150 each).
- Roche frames the Axelios 1 around cost per data output, highlighting about $0.06 per million reads in sequencing consumables.
- In SBX simplex mode, the instrument produces more than 40 billion reads per run and about 160 billion reads per day. The note again gives about $2,400 as the list price per run.

## Roche's cfDNA panel pricing

cfDNA is cell-free DNA, fragments of DNA circulating in blood, used in tests such as cancer detection. Roche said pricing will vary by application. As an illustration, assuming a panel of about 1.1 Mb (1.1 million DNA bases), 2,000x coverage (each base read about 2,000 times) and 96 samples per run, the price comes to $25 per sample.

## Sensor module reusability data

Reuse is central to the $150 figure, so Roche presented evidence for it:

- Roche showed data that performance is maintained when the sensor module is reused more than 20 times across one week.
- Roche said the module can be used for longer than one week, although the data presented tracks one week only.
- Roche highlighted that 15 of 16 sensor modules (about 94%) successfully reached 20 runs of reuse.
- Roche said the intended product configuration is designed to support up to 20 uses.

## J.P. Morgan's rating and thesis

- **Thesis:** J.P. Morgan continues to see strong clinical traction for NGS (next-generation sequencing) over the long term, with potentially ramping contributions from Illumina's multiomics pipeline (products that measure several types of biological information, not just DNA). However, "the ongoing competitive concerns and pressured pricing dynamics leave us on the sideline with a Neutral rating."
- **Valuation:** The December 2026 price target of $120 comes from a discounted cash flow (DCF) model, which values the company on its projected future cash flows. It uses a CAPM-derived WACC (cost of capital) of 10.2% and terminal growth (long-run growth rate) of 4.0%.

### Risks to rating and price target

- Upside risks: (1) faster-than-expected recovery in the academic end market; (2) stronger clinical end-market growth from greater adoption of sequencing-based clinical applications; (3) broader uptake of whole genome sequencing; (4) expanding use cases for multiomic offerings.
- Downside risks: (1) ongoing uncertainty or further pressure in academic research funding; (2) intensifying competition and potential pricing pressure in the sequencing market; (3) execution risks in launching and getting adoption of new products.
