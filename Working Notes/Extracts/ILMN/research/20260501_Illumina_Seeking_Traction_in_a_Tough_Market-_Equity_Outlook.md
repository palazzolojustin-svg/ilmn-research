## Core thesis [BI, 2026-05-01]

- Illumina's flagship NovaSeq X system (its newest high-throughput DNA sequencer) and its updated chemistry (the reagent recipe used in sequencing runs) should help the company defend its market position.
- Roche's return to sequencing adds a competitive challenge.
- The transition to NovaSeq X "has been painful." NovaSeq X commercialization is ramping up amid weaker customer demand and intensifying competition in mid-throughput systems (mid-sized sequencers).
- Illumina has framed 2026 as a year of steady, clinically led growth, with cautious assumptions on research. It projects 2-4% organic revenue growth excluding China.
- China sanctions have cut the country's contribution in half, and the timing of any resolution "remains unclear."

## Financial review

### 2Q26 earnings preview: "Charting a Path to 2H Acceleration" [BI, 2026-07-23]

**Demand backdrop.**
- Illumina's high exposure to academic and government end-markets positions it well for an inflection.
- Commentary from peers suggests demand is stable but "has yet to normalize."

**Instrument placements.**
- Management commentary (not dated in the source) suggested 2Q NovaSeq X placements would be similar to the 80 placed in 1Q26.
- Visibility into the second half (2H) of the year would push the company above the guidance given at the start of the year of 200-240 NovaSeq X systems for 2026.

**Consumables.**
- Consumables are the reagent kits customers must buy for every run.
- Sequencing consumable estimates point to muted growth of just 4% in 2Q.
- The analyst expects clinical customers to "dramatically outpace" research customers, as in prior periods.

**Margins.**
- Sequential margin improvement (versus 1Q) is projected to be modest.
- The heavy lifting toward the 23.4-23.6% full-year operating margin target is expected in 2H.
- The drivers are cost efforts and mitigation of the downward pressure from SomaLogic (a business Illumina acquired, which dilutes margin).

**Competition.**
- Competitive dynamics are expected to be a focus because Roche is now in the market with its Axelios system.
- The analyst says it is likely too early to see any real impact.

### Earnings drivers and 2027 targets [BI, 2026-05-08]

- Illumina's financial performance is tied to demand for sequencing consumables and instruments.
- 2026 guidance expects instrument sales to be down low single digits to flat, and consumables up low to mid single digits, both excluding China.
- Guidance calls for 2-4% core revenue growth. China-related geopolitical friction and research-funding uncertainty continue to cloud the outlook.
- Revenue grew significantly during the pandemic peak (2021). Since then it has been flat to modestly down, despite the launch of a new flagship system.
- Management's 2027 targets are:
  - a 26% operating margin
  - high-single-digit core growth
  - EPS growth in the double digits to teens
- The June 2024 spin-off of Grail let Illumina return full attention to core operations. The analyst says it also "removed a massive drain on profitability."

## Key topics

### Outlook under pressure [BI, 2026-05-08]

**Strategy.**
- Illumina aims to create long-term value through a multiyear systems-upgrade cycle, its partnership model, and momentum in population and clinical genomics.
- After underwhelming 2023-24 results tied to the NovaSeq X launch ramp, 2025 added pressure from China's unreliable-entity designation.
- 2026 is shaping up as a "measured step forward" on low-single-digit growth excluding China.

**Track record and confidence.**
- The analyst says Illumina must rebuild confidence in its ability to deliver on longer-term goals after a choppy stretch following an "outsized" 40% gain in 2021.
- Core sales rose just 1% in 2022, then declined in 2023 and 2024 amid a slower-than-expected NovaSeq X adoption curve.
- A hoped-for 2025 turnaround proved muted. The year ended with 2% organic revenue growth excluding China, as tariff headwinds and weak research demand offset clinical strength.

**2026 mitigation and outlook.**
- Management expects mitigation actions to increasingly take hold through 2026. These are pricing, supply-chain shifts and cost cuts.
- Operating margin guidance of 23.4-23.6% includes a 100 basis point (1 percentage point) headwind from the SomaLogic acquisition.
- The outlook still assumes a soft research market, with potential upside if NIH funding (US National Institutes of Health research grants) loosens.

## Long-term drivers

### Sequencing position and competition [BI, 2026-03-11]

- Illumina's reach in genomic tools was "unparalleled" for more than a decade.
- It serves a global market spanning research and clinical customers, with systems aimed at every setting.
- Competition is increasing from new entrants Element Biosciences and Ultima Genomics, with Roche's return to the market "on the horizon" (as of March).

### Customers and geography [BI, 2026-03-11]

- Illumina's customer base is diverse by geography and application.
- Sequencing is increasingly common in commercial markets such as oncology, agriculture and reproductive health, which brings new customers.
- Most sales are still domestic, but overseas is growing as more countries fund genomic research.
- Government funding accounts for almost half of Illumina's sales.
- US government action can temporarily cap growth. Budget sequestration and a federal shutdown did so in 2012.
- Covid-19 added a wrinkle. The timeline for academic and research labs was thought to dictate the pace of recovery, but 2021 saw record demand across instruments.
- Growth is shifting toward clinical end-markets. Consumables are now 60% of sequencing revenue, driven by NovaSeq X volume and oncology demand.
- A chart shows revenue by product (consumables, instruments, service/other) and by geography (including Greater China, Europe and Asia Pacific). The figures are illegible in the source.

### Products [BI, 2026-03-11]

- The NovaSeq X series launched at Illumina's 2022 Genomics Forum at its lowest cost per gigabase (a unit of sequencing output).
- The analyst calls it a critical catalyst for the portfolio, with 2.5x the throughput of existing offerings.
- iSeq and MiSeq are small-footprint, competitively priced systems for smaller labs.
- Demand for the mid-throughput NextSeq line is a key driver of the expansion into clinical applications.
- The HiSeq X Ten, first offered in 2014, broke the $1,000-per-genome barrier.
- The XLEAP-SBS chemistry upgrades (SBS means sequencing by synthesis) are projected to push costs below $200 per genome.

### Market opportunity [BI, 2026-03-11]

- Illumina divides its addressable markets into two categories with over $125 billion in potential sales.
- It significantly penetrates only the research market, its current core business. The company estimated 34% of that market captured by 2027, as of the 2022 investor day.
- Penetrating the clinical market hinges on increased biomarker profiling and physician use of downstream sequencing applications.
- The next opportunity is an $800 million liquid biopsy market. This covers products for cancer screening and monitoring, where Illumina had hoped to compete through Grail.
- The analyst's view is that Illumina has "clearly defined" large-market opportunities as sequencing moves from the lab into the clinic.

## M&A history [BI, 2026-03-11]

**General approach.**
- Illumina's dominant position typically precludes large-scale M&A.
- The Grail purchase broke that norm and led to a years-long saga that ended in its sale.
- Illumina tends to acquire early-stage technologies that aid or expand its sequencing workflows.

**Grail.**
- Illumina formed Grail in 2016 to commercialize a sequencing-based liquid-biopsy cancer screen. Liquid biopsies are non-invasive blood tests that search for circulating tumor cells or DNA.
- Illumina closed the acquisition in August 2021 without first gaining EU approval. After an arduous dispute, it was mandated to divest.
- The analyst notes that catching early-stage cancers before symptoms is a "holy grail" for clinicians. Without Grail, Illumina "must now devise a new strategy" to participate in screening growth.
- Others pursuing liquid-biopsy tests include Guardant Health, Tempus AI and Exact Sciences.
- Grail was spun off on June 24, 2024.
- Illumina sees $35-$50 million of incremental charges related to the spin-off.
- The analyst says the divestiture and a fresh CEO help refocus Illumina on core operations.

**Other deals.**
- The terminated PacBio deal can still be viewed through an innovation lens, since it would have added long-read technology.
- The $480 million Solexa acquisition "may go down as one of the best health-care deals in history," in the analyst's view.
- Illumina's current sequencing business is based on the Solexa platform. Market capitalization peaked at $76 billion in 3Q21, versus $1.8 billion when the Solexa deal was announced.
- The analyst says almost all of Illumina's value stems from sequencing.

**Helix.**
- Helix is a consumer genomics platform. Illumina worked with two venture investors who funded the startup with $100 million.
- The analyst notes the research market is maturing, and each new instrument generation offers smaller marginal performance gains.
- After years of weighing on profitability, Illumina deconsolidated Helix effective 2019. The analyst viewed this as a prudent step to tighten the portfolio.
- Quoted from an Illumina press release of Aug. 18, 2015: "Helix will enable individuals to acquire an unprecedented amount of genetic information by providing affordable sequencing and database services for consumer samples brought through third-party partners."

## Capital allocation [BI, 2026-05-08]

- The Grail acquisition paused share repurchases, which resumed modestly in 2024.
- The divestiture order imposed a $476 million fine for closing without approval, plus the $35-$50 million of incremental charges.
- Illumina keeps exposure to screening by retaining a 14.5% stake in Grail and re-establishing its royalty agreement.
- Before the failed PacBio acquisition in 2020, Illumina drew questions about its rising cash balance. The cash was ultimately spent on Grail.
- The cash balance was $1.6 billion at year-end 2025, and free cash flow for 2025 was $931 million (free cash flow is cash generated after capital spending).
- The analyst says it will likely take time to regain pre-Grail flexibility.
- Buybacks have historically largely offset dilution from employee stock options.
- Illumina spent $115 million on repurchases in 2024, after buying none in 2022 or 2023.
- The latest authorization is a $1.5 billion program, with $400 million remaining as of 1Q26.
