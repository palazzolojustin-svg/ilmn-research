## Key Takeaways (the analyst's summary)

- Q4'25 revenue and adjusted EPS beat consensus. The analyst attributes this to momentum from the NovaSeq X transition (customers moving to Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer) and growing sequencing volumes, particularly in clinical markets.
- Clinical consumables revenue excluding China accelerated to 20% y/y growth in Q4'25. Oncology and genetic-disease testing drove it.
- Research and academic markets stabilized in Q4. The analyst says the recovery is expected to be slow while NIH (National Institutes of Health) budgets and grant funding "get ironed out."
- Illumina launched BioInsight, which combines data, AI and biology. It debuted with the "Billion Cell Atlas" to speed drug discovery for biopharma partners.
- The analyst views the initial FY'26 revenue and adjusted EPS guidance as "solid," and both were ahead of consensus to begin the year.

## Q4'25 Results

Illumina pre-announced Q4'25 revenue ahead of the JPMorgan Healthcare Conference the month before (January 2026). It then reported the full quarter.

- **Revenue:** $1.159B, up 4.0% y/y on a constant-currency (CC, meaning currency swings removed) basis. This compares with consensus of $1.099B.
- **Reported growth:** The product-line and geography tables show total revenue of $1,159M against $1,104M in Q4'24, which is +5.0% y/y as reported (not constant currency).
- **Adjusted EPS:** $1.35. This beat consensus of $1.21 and the company's internal guidance of $1.29. It rose 42.1% y/y from "Core ILMN" adjusted EPS of $0.95 in Q4'24. "Core" means Illumina excluding GRAIL, which was divested.
- **Excluding Greater China:** Revenue grew 7% CC y/y, "driven by a significant acceleration in the clinical business."
- **Adjusted operating margin:** 23.7%, up 400 basis points (4.0 percentage points) y/y. The analyst credits "solid operating leverage" and the continued benefit of cost-optimization initiatives enacted earlier in 2025.

## Business Segment Performance

Q4 was "anchored by robust clinical demand." Clinical consumables revenue (the single-use reagent kits customers buy for each sequencing run) grew 20% y/y excluding China.

Total sequencing consumables revenue was $755M, up 8% y/y. The analyst says this was driven by high-throughput volume growth as customers ramped utilization of NovaSeq X.

Management (per the analyst) noted that adoption of sequencing-based diagnostic tests is expanding. This is especially true for comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP, broad tumor DNA testing) and whole-genome approaches in oncology and genetic disease.

The NovaSeq X transition "continues to progress ahead of schedule." Illumina placed over 100 instruments in the quarter, the second-highest quarterly volume since launch. The installed base reached 890 units.

## Market Dynamics

### Clinical

Management views the clinical market as the primary growth engine heading into 2026. The analyst cites the 20% y/y clinical consumables growth excluding China in Q4'25 as reinforcement. The momentum comes from broader adoption of sequencing-intensive applications, notably whole-genome sequencing and CGP in oncology and genetic disease testing.

Illumina therefore assumes "robust double-digit to mid-teens growth" for the clinical segment in FY'26 as customers keep scaling assays on NovaSeq X.

### Research and academic

The research and academic markets "remain measured." Consumables revenue in this segment stabilized in Q4. However, management assumes no fundamental improvement in demand for FY'26. It projects a mid-to-high single-digit revenue decline for Research and Applied consumables.

On funding, management noted that recent announcements gave improved line of sight into the NIH budget. Uncertainty persists, though, on the timing and mechanics of grant distribution to individual institutes. Illumina has therefore adopted a "prudent outlook." It assumes academic purchasing stays cautious until funding flows fully normalize.

## Strategic Developments

Management highlighted progress across three strategic pillars: core sequencing, multiomics (measuring several molecular layers, such as DNA, RNA and proteins, together) and data services.

- **SomaLogic acquisition:** It closed in January 2026. The deal brings high-plex proteomics (measuring many proteins at once) together with Illumina's NGS (next-generation sequencing) scale. The analyst expects it to accelerate the multiomics roadmap. A spatial transcriptomics solution (mapping gene activity within tissue) is on track for introduction in 1H'26.
- **BioInsight:** This is a new business unit focused on data and AI-driven drug discovery. Its inaugural product, the Billion Cell Atlas, uses single-cell data and CRISPR-based perturbation (deliberately switching genes off to see the effect) to improve target validation for biopharma partners. Initial collaborations are with AstraZeneca, Merck and Eli Lilly. Illumina expects the platform to generate "more meaningful revenue" beginning in 2027.

## FY'26 Outlook (company guidance, as relayed by the analyst)

- **Revenue:** Organic growth excluding China of 1.0% to 3.0% y/y, or sales of $4.5B to $4.6B. This compares with pre-earnings consensus of $4.41B, so guidance is ahead.
- **Adjusted EPS:** $5.05 to $5.20, which is 5.9% growth y/y at the midpoint. Pre-earnings consensus was $5.07, so the range is ahead.
- **Sequencing instruments:** Revenue is expected to be down low-single digits to flat. Management targets 50-60 NovaSeq X placements per quarter.
- **Greater China:** Revenue is expected at $210M to $220M. It remains a headwind as the company works through import license dynamics.
- **Operating margin:** Excluding the SomaLogic acquisition, operating margins are projected to expand about 130 basis points.

## Valuation (analyst view)

ILMN trades at 26.0x forward-year earnings estimates. This is a discount to the stock's 5-year average forward P/E of 72.2x. The analyst says the large discount partly reflects the divestiture of GRAIL Inc. on June 24, 2024 and the return to a normalized "Core Illumina" adjusted EPS. GRAIL (GRAL) closed at $94.06.

In the analyst's opinion, as the NovaSeq X and X Plus launch continues, ILMN "could generate solid earnings leverage in the near future." The reasoning is that the company moves past initial launch expenses and earns complementary disposable (consumables) revenue from each instrument sold. The analyst also says the current valuation "may not reflect the opportunity related to" NGS oncology testing markets the company targets.

## Investment Thesis

- Illumina is a way to invest in "the genomic medicine of the future." It has exposure to large diagnostic markets for diagnosing, treating and monitoring diseases with genetic or hereditary origins, including cancer.
- The NovaSeq X and X Plus are described as Illumina's next-generation production-scale sequencing system, able to sequence a human genome. The analyst believes they can be a multi-year revenue growth tailwind for the Instruments franchise.
- The company believes the NGS total addressable market (TAM) is $125B, and only about $10B of it is being served today.
- Single-cell analysis, spatial analysis and liquid biopsy (cancer or disease detection from blood) are further potential growth opportunities.
- Recommendation: "For more aggressive investors with multi-year time horizons, we view the recent weakness as an opportunity to accumulate the stock."

## Risks

The analyst lists factors that could hurt the thesis:

- Regulatory challenges to acquisitions, or integration difficulties.
- Currency fluctuations.
- Market share erosion.
- Increased generic competition.
- New product or clinical trial disappointments.
- Delayed regulatory approvals.
- Reimbursement challenges.

Operating risks visible in the results and guidance: Greater China fell 31.3% y/y in Q4 and is guided to a further drag, with import license dynamics cited. Research and academic consumables are expected to decline mid-to-high single digits in FY'26. Sequencing instrument revenue is guided down low-single digits to flat.

## Company Profile

ILMN is a global leader in sequencing- and array-based solutions for genetic and genomic analysis. Customers include genomic research centers, academic institutions, government laboratories, hospitals, pharma and biotech firms, commercial molecular diagnostic labs and consumer genomics companies. Its portfolio is integrated sequencing and microarray systems, consumables and analysis tools. It was incorporated in California in April 1998.
