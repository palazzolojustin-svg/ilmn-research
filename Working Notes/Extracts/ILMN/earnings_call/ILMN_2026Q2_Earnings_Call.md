# Illumina Q2 2026 Earnings Call (July 30, 2026): Raised 2026 Guidance, Record Clinical-Driven Growth, 95+ NovaSeq X Placements

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2026Q2_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2026-07-30 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Illumina's Q2 2026 earnings call; speakers Jacob Thaysen (CEO), Ankur Dhingra (CFO), Conor McNamara (VP Investor Relations); analysts from Leerink, Jefferies, Evercore ISI, BofA, RBC, Guggenheim, Canaccord, Stephens, JPM, Operon Research · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q2 2026 revenue of $1.16 billion (up 9.5% reported, 6.5% organic) and non-GAAP EPS of $1.31, both above expectations, driven by clinical sequencing demand and more than 95 NovaSeq X placements. Management raised full-year 2026 revenue and EPS guidance, lifted clinical consumables growth to mid-teens, and reiterated a path to high single-digit revenue growth in 2027. Research and academic demand remains weak but showed early signs of improvement, and memory and freight costs are a headwind that management says it is absorbing. Analysts focused on the Q3 growth step-down, the sustainability of clinical growth, the 2027 outlook, memory costs and the multiomics/BioInsight strategy.

## Context and how to read this document

This is a transcript of Illumina's second-quarter 2026 earnings call, held July 30, 2026 (4:30 PM ET). ### Cash flow, balance sheet and capital allocation

- Operating cash flow was $201 million, below the usual trend because of the timing of tax payments and higher inventory, as Illumina secured supply of critical components for the next few quarters.
- Capital expenditures were $39 million; free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capex) was $162 million.
- Buybacks: 0.9 million shares repurchased for about $122 million, at an average price of $129.07 per share. About $1.8 billion remains under current authorizations, and the company intends to keep repurchasing opportunistically.
- Balance sheet: about $1.17 billion in cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments; $1.99 billion total debt; leverage of about 1.6x gross debt to trailing-twelve-month EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization). [Dhingra, Q2 2026 call]

## Prepared remarks

### Jacob Thaysen, CEO

**Overall message.** Thaysen called the first half of 2026 "great" and said revenue grew at the fastest rate since he joined. Deep relationships with leading U.S. clinical customers and a large installed base "reinforce the durability of our position." The company is raising 2026 guidance for revenue growth and profitability while staying committed to its long-range targets (set in 2024). He organized his remarks around quarter performance and end-market trends, platform expansion through workflows and multiomics (measuring several layers of biology, such as DNA, RNA and protein), and progress on long-term strategy.

**Quarter and demand.**
- NovaSeq X (Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer) demand remained high "more than 3 years after launch," with more than 95 placements in the quarter.
- Clinical markets are about 65% of sequencing consumables revenue and remain the primary growth driver. Clinical growth was broad across regions and applications, with particular strength in U.S./Canada.
- Strong placements over the past three quarters expand customer capacity and "will support consumable growth for many quarters to come." Placements vary quarter to quarter, but demand is elevated.
- Research and academic markets improved from Q1, but customers remain cautious amid funding uncertainty. Some signs of improvement appeared late in the quarter, but "it's too early to call a recovery."

**Innovation.** The strategy is to "deliver the highest quality insights for the lowest end-to-end cost."
- NovaSeq X remains central; the roadmap laid out earlier in 2026 gives customers confidence it will support their workflows for years.
- Whole-genome MRD research workflow launched (MRD is minimal residual disease, detecting tiny traces of cancer after treatment). It is meant to shorten assay development timelines and cut costs, runs on NovaSeq systems, and is in early access with select customers.
- StrataMap Spatial launched this summer: a sequencing-based spatial biology workflow (mapping where molecules sit within tissue).
- Proteomics (protein measurement) momentum continues after closing the SomaLogic acquisition. The newly branded SomaScan and SomaSeq offerings are generating strong interest and linking protein and genomic data.
- BioInsight: Illumina's data and insights business for pharma AI-enabled drug discovery, combining sequencing, perturbation tools (experiments that switch genes on or off), compute and AI. Its first key initiative is the Billion Cell Atlas, a genome-wide perturbation data set. Over 300 million cells have been delivered to date, revenue booking has started, and 3 new partners were added after quarter end, bringing the total to 6. Further BioInsight updates are promised later in 2026.

**Management and board.** Michael Sullivan and Julie Coletti joined the management team (commercial and legal experience). David King and Dan Skovronsky joined the Board (healthcare, diagnostics and R&D experience).

### Ankur Dhingra, CFO

Beyond the figures in the tables above, he added the following details.

**Clinical vs. research consumables.**
- Clinical consumables grew 15% ex China in Q2, with U.S./Canada still above 20%. Europe, Middle East and Latin America grew more slowly, "largely due to ongoing near-term dynamics in the region." First-half clinical growth was about 17%, a slight acceleration from the second half of 2025, reflecting adoption of sequencing-based diagnostics and more sequencing-intensive applications.
- Research and applied consumables declined 7% RoW in Q2. They rose 9% quarter over quarter, which was encouraging, but management still expects mid- to high single-digit declines for 2026 and says it is too early to predict a recovery.

**Transition to NovaSeq X.** As of Q2, about 83% of sequencing volume and 59% of revenue had moved to the X. About 78% of clinical volume is on the X, and management still expects 80% to 85% clinical conversion by end of 2026. Despite transition dynamics (customers move from older, higher-priced-per-unit platforms to a cheaper-per-base one), consumables still grew strongly.

**Activity and instruments.**
- Total gigabase output on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30% year over year again, with clinical growth "well above that."
- Supply investments let Illumina place over 95 NovaSeq X units; several multi-unit capacity expansions came from large clinical customers, including for new clinical trials. It also placed over 10 NovaSeq 6000 units (the previous generation) because some customers plan to stay on it for years.

**Other items.** SomaLogic revenue is tracking toward the high end of deal expectations. Gross margin absorbed higher freight and memory costs. Operating margin benefited from higher volume and "an excellent job in absorbing increased inflationary effects."

**Closing.** The first-half performance and growing clinical installed base set up consumables growth "for years to come." Billion Cell Atlas and multiomics are starting to contribute, and he said Illumina continues "making progress towards our 2027 financial targets."

## Q&A

### Puneet Souda, Leerink Partners

**Question (partly garbled):** About second-half installs, and with clinical growth showing "no clinical cliff" and guidance already above 5% for 2026, why shouldn't investors expect more than the high single-digit growth previously outlined for 2027?

**Answer (Thaysen):** He is convinced clinical momentum "will continue for years to come," as shown by elevated first-half placements. Placed instruments eventually run consumables. About 18 months ago the company framed a logic of 50 to 60 placements per quarter, and recent quarters have run clearly above that. Quarter-to-quarter moderation is possible but elevation should continue in the second half. On the "cliff": "it's not a cliff. It's a wave, and we are surfing it." On 2027, he remains "very, very committed" to high single-digit growth, will keep building customer value, "and then we see where it takes us" (no upgrade offered).

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies

**Question:** Why did clinical consumables slow sequentially, and are consumables being "over-earned" while customers run NovaSeq 6000 and X in parallel, with a headwind when the 6000s are eventually decommissioned?

**Answer (Thaysen):** Volume keeps expanding on the X, and many 6000s remain in use; some customers bought a few 6000s this quarter to continue operations. Customers will eventually move to X, but he sees this as a standard part of the business, noting HiSeq systems were still in the portfolio only a few quarters ago, so he does not view it as a true headwind. On the slowdown from about 20% growth last quarter to 15%: it is "still very strong," the U.S. is still above 20%, and there were "ins and outs" in the Middle East and Latin America, where the Middle East situation is hurting growth short term. Mid-teens remains above where the year started and supports the high single-digit 2027 target.

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI

**Question:** Were there one-offs in Q2 phasing? Why does Q3 RoW organic guidance (4.5%) step down from 8%, and why isn't 8% sustainable? What is assumed for AI-related revenue, and could AI be a bigger theme?

**Answer (Thaysen):** Placements are elevated and "not a one-off": clinical customers are building installed base ahead of volume in their pipelines. The comparison is tougher because instrument placements in early 2025 differed from the second half, when momentum began building, so instrument comparisons change. Consumables should stay strong and keep stepping up, becoming the main growth driver into next year. On AI: more than 300 million single cells delivered in the Cell Atlas, 3 more pharma partners added (6 total). Customers value direct insight for drug discovery programs and, beyond that, the foundation for AI models of biology that are predictive. "It's still early days. We are making money already from day 1 on this, both on top line and bottom line." He sees significant opportunity over time. No specific AI revenue assumption was given.

### Michael Ryskin, BofA Securities

**Question:** Q3 looks like a surprising step down, and Q4 is guided higher than expected. Is there unusual seasonality? Was there stocking or backlog-related purchasing in the quarter? What is the effect of the extra week in Q4 on revenue, margin and EPS?

**Answer (Thaysen):** Q2 was very strong, and Q3 is always a bit lower than Q4, so the phasing is normal and as expected. Clinical customers are building installed base for growth they see.

**Answer (Dhingra):** Think of usual seasonality: Q4 is the largest quarter of the year and will remain so. The extra week in Q4 is "largely a consumables story" and adds maybe 0.5 point of revenue from run-rate days. (He did not separately quantify margin/EPS effects, and did not address stocking.) Consumables growth has been steady all year; variability almost always comes from instrument placements, and elevated placements, which began in the second half of 2025, are expected to continue in the second half of 2026.

### Dan Leonard, RBC Capital Markets

**Question:** How broad versus narrow is the clinical strength (U.S. above 20%, largest customers strong in instruments)? Has application mix shifted?

**Answer (Thaysen):** The opportunity of moving NGS (next-generation sequencing) into healthcare and becoming standard of care is still ahead. Growth is broad-based; the U.S. is the main driver, but all other regions except China are growing nicely. By application, oncology leads. Within oncology, MRD is starting to drive momentum, though therapy selection is still larger in dollars; MCED (multi-cancer early detection) will later become a real contributor but "we don't really see that really in the number today." Rare disease and NIPT (noninvasive prenatal screening) are still growing nicely.

### Subhalaxmi (Subbu) Nambi, Guggenheim Securities

**Question 1:** Memory prices are still rising and allocations are strained. How does this evolve into the second half, with stronger Q4 placements, and into 2027 as Illumina integrates more GPU-based compute for next-generation products?

**Answer (Thaysen):** The team has repeatedly offset headwinds over three years, citing a 200 basis point margin improvement last year in a relatively flat environment. Memory and freight costs were a headwind "we didn't anticipate to the level we have seen," and the costs are real. Illumina is sharing some of the increase with customers and pursuing operational excellence to offset the rest. Costs with good line of sight have been identified and will be managed.

**Answer (Dhingra):** Higher memory cost is already in Q2 results, so the bulk is in gross margin and Q2 is a starting run rate. Additional inventory and supply were secured for the next several quarters to de-risk near-term memory price moves. On GPUs: "our instruments do not use the GPU architecture"; they use a different, relatively cheaper and more cost-efficient architecture, which he called a unique strategic cost advantage.

**Question 2:** After two stronger-than-expected placement quarters in the fourth year since launch, was this due to the market waiting for competitive information, and how much reflects improving funding?

**Answer (Thaysen):** It reflects the value proposition and customer confidence that Illumina helps them succeed; continued innovation on X shows "a lot of leeway" in the platform, so it is "a platform to invest in," in a healthy market. He did not directly address competition or funding.

### Harrison (for Mason Carrico), Stephens

**Question:** Did the clinical/nonclinical split of X placements change? What is heard from research customers on order timing and budget releases, and has the order pattern shifted?

**Answer (Thaysen):** Most placements still go to clinical and are expected to continue to, even with an improved academic environment, though some were placed in academic research.

**Answer (Dhingra):** The placement mix is still about 70% clinical, 30% research (research is global, not only U.S.). Late in the quarter there was increased U.S. funding release activity and more requests and quoting. But "we're not assuming any meaningful improvement in that market or revenue for us for the rest of the year"; improvement was directional only.

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord Genuity

**Question:** How will investors track whether the multiomics strategy (StrataMap, single cell, proteomics) is working, and will it be broken out? How are revenues from these products accounted for, e.g., in Q4?

**Answer (Thaysen):** The 2024 strategy has two legs: convert customers to the X platform (proven, momentum visible) and drive multiomics. He said "the future belongs to multiomics," with genomics as the backbone but multiple layers needed (genome to proteome; bulk, single cell and spatial), delivered as end-to-end workflows with software for cross-modality insight rather than just library preps or sequencing. In 2024 Illumina expected 1 to 2 points of incremental growth from this in 2026-27, and that is "playing out as we're expecting." StrataMap has drawn "significantly more interest" than planned, and TruPath is having a lot of momentum and will be a very strong platform.

**Answer (Dhingra):** Most multiomics products, such as single cell or TruPath, appear in consumables revenue. Billion Cell Atlas and other BioInsight revenue shows up in services. The company will keep commenting on Billion Cell Atlas contributions as it did this quarter. (No commitment to a separate breakout.)

### Jack Meehan, Operon Research

**Question:** Margin steps from 22.4% in Q2 to 24% in Q3. How much is mix versus other factors? Any color on Q3 consumables versus instrument revenue assumptions?

**Answer (Dhingra):** The back-half margin step-up was the thesis from the start of the year, given tariffs and cost actions, and it is playing out. Excluding the EPS-neutral deferred compensation effect, Q2 operating margin was about 23%, so the step to Q3 is about 100 basis points. Part comes from higher consumables mix, and part from several cost actions in gross margin during Q3, with more in Q4. Most of the actions should show up in gross margin. (No Q3 consumables-versus-instrument split was given.)

### "Jen" (for Casey Woodring), JPM

**Question:** How did low- and mid-throughput instruments perform, are mid-throughput customers constrained by the end-market environment, and what is the outlook for the rest of the year?

**Answer (Thaysen):** Illumina has the broadest instrument range. The X addresses high throughput ("production-like" sequencing, which directly drives customers' revenue), while low and mid throughput are more project-based and driven differently. Mid-throughput is particularly sensitive to the macro environment, as it has been for a long time. He still feels good about mid-throughput, sees continued placements, and expects medium-term momentum from investments there. In low throughput, the MiSeq i100, launched a little over a year ago, has many placements and continuing momentum; it is affordable for most labs. Summary: "high end and low throughput is very strong right now. Mid-throughput is more muted because of the macro environment."

## Key themes and risks flagged

- **Clinical durability:** Management frames clinical growth as a multi-year "wave," led by U.S. oncology (therapy selection now, MRD building, MCED later), with the NovaSeq X installed base expanding ahead of consumables pull-through. [Thaysen]
- **Lumpy instruments vs. steady consumables:** The CFO says consumables are steady and variability sits in instrument placements; instrument growth is guided to moderate in H2 on tougher comparisons.
- **Research funding:** Still weak (research and applied consumables -7% in Q2, guided down mid- to high single digits for 2026) with tentative late-quarter improvement not assumed in guidance.
- **Costs:** Memory and freight costs exceeded expectations, partly shared with customers, offset by cost actions and stockpiled supply; Illumina says its instruments avoid costly GPU architecture. Tariffs were cited as part of the early-year margin backdrop.
- **Regional softness:** Europe, Middle East and Latin America slowed (Middle East situation cited); China excluded from RoW organic metrics due to the unreliable entity list.
- **Mid-throughput:** Muted by macro conditions.
- **Notable quote:** "It's not a cliff. It's a wave, and we are surfing it." [Thaysen, Q2 2026 call]
