# Illumina Q3 2026 Earnings Call (reported Oct 29, 2026): beat-and-raise, strong clinical demand, NovaSeq X placements, Billion Cell Atlas and 2027 framing

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2026Q3_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2026-10-29 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Illumina's Q3 2026 earnings call; speakers Conor McNamara (VP Investor Relations), Jacob Thaysen (CEO), Ankur Dhingra (CFO); analysts from Jefferies, Evercore ISI, UBS, Leerink, Guggenheim, Barclays, Stifel, Piper Sandler, Canaccord, RBC, JPMorgan, TD Cowen · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina beat its Q3 2026 guidance on revenue, operating margin and EPS, and raised full-year 2026 guidance, helped by more than 80 NovaSeq X instrument placements and 14% clinical consumables growth. Management said the shift of customers to the NovaSeq X is nearly complete, so price headwinds will shrink in 2027, and it reiterated the long-range target of high-single-digit growth in 2027. Much of the Q&A concerned the 2027 outlook, the Billion Cell Atlas and AI-biology opportunity, China, and clinical customer pricing.

# Illumina Q3 2026 Earnings Call, October 29, 2026

## Reporting basis and headline results

**How the numbers are presented.** Conor McNamara (VP Investor Relations) said Illumina recognized certain tariff refunds in Q3. These raised GAAP and non-GAAP operating income, margins and EPS, and lowered revenue. The refunds were not in prior guidance. All figures discussed on the call are adjusted non-GAAP (non-GAAP means excluding certain accounting items) and exclude the tariff refunds [Q3 2026 call]. "Organic" growth also strips out currency and acquisitions. "Rest of World (RoW) organic" growth additionally excludes Greater China. Reconciliations are in the earnings release and supplementary data.

**Headline figures.** The Seeking Alpha header shows EPS of $1.44 beating consensus by $0.08, and revenue of $1.17 billion (up 7.56% year over year) beating consensus by $13.22 million.

- Revenue: $1.17 billion, up 4.7% year over year on a RoW organic basis. Currency and acquired revenue together added just under 3 percentage points to reported growth [CFO Ankur Dhingra, Q3 2026 call]. Dhingra also described RoW organic growth as 5%.
- Operating margin: 25%, which is 100 basis points (1 percentage point) above guidance, driven by higher revenue and disciplined investment.
- Diluted EPS: $1.44, above the high end of guidance and up 7% from Q3 2025. Year to date, EPS is up 12% versus 2025.
- Gross margin: 69.4%, up 20 basis points year over year.
- CEO Jacob Thaysen: "We had a great third quarter with strong clinical sequencing demand and elevated NovaSeq X placements, driving revenue, margin and EPS above our guidance range." He added, "Our strategy is working," and said the company remains on track for its long-range financial targets.

## Consumables by end market

- Clinical (testing used in patient care): RoW consumables grew 14% in Q3, similar to Q2 [Thaysen and Dhingra]. In the Q&A Thaysen described Q3 as "14%, 15%" growth.
- Research and applied (academic, pharma and other non-clinical use): RoW consumables fell 6%, also similar to Q2. Year-to-date trends are consistent with the outlook set entering the year. Revenue has grown sequentially (quarter over quarter) in each of the last two quarters, and Dhingra said "we are seeing signs research activity is stabilizing."
- Full-year 2026 expectations, unchanged: mid-teens clinical growth and mid- to high-single-digit declines in research and applied RoW consumables.
- Gigabase output (the amount of DNA sequenced) on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30%, which management cited as evidence demand is strong.

## NovaSeq X placements and the transition from the NovaSeq 6000

- Illumina placed more than 80 NovaSeq X instruments in Q3. That is about 360 X placements over the last four quarters, "more than in our first year of launch" [Thaysen]. Management attributed demand to broader adoption of sequencing-based diagnostics and more sequencing-intensive assays (tests). Each placed instrument supports consumables revenue "for years to come."
- Several multi-unit capacity expansion orders came from some of Illumina's largest clinical customers [Dhingra].
- Instrument sales still account for over 90% of X placements, but there is increasing interest in lease-type arrangements from certain research customers.
- Conversion status: about 84% of high-throughput volume and 60% of high-throughput revenue is now on the X. In research and applied, 90% or more of volume has been on the X for each of the last five quarters, so conversion there is "largely complete" and Dhingra expects minimal transition pricing headwinds from these customers. In clinical, 78% of volume is on the X, and the pace of conversion has slowed. Some clinical customers keep existing on-market assays on the older NovaSeq 6000 while adding X capacity for new tests. Dhingra expects clinical conversion to reach 80% to 85% in coming quarters, so year-over-year pricing headwinds from the transition should be lower in 2027 than in 2026.
- Disclosure change: because conversion is nearly complete, Illumina will stop showing its X-transition price and volume slide in earnings presentations after Q4 2026.

## Cash flow, balance sheet and capital return

- Operating cash flow: $419 million. Free cash flow (cash from operations less capital spending): $378 million.
- Share repurchases: $9 million in Q3. About $1.8 billion remains under current authorizations; Illumina intends to keep repurchasing shares opportunistically.
- Cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments: about $1.34 billion. Total debt: $1.79 billion. Leverage: about 1.4x gross debt to trailing-twelve-month EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization).

## Prepared remarks: Jacob Thaysen (CEO)

**Billion Cell Atlas.** Introduced early in 2026, it targets a bottleneck in applying AI to biology: the lack of accurate, standardized, reproducible functional data sets. Thaysen: "There is no Internet of biology." The Atlas is "over halfway complete" and is building an AI-ready "perturbation" data set (data on how cells respond when genes are changed) to help biopharma partners link genetic signals to cellular mechanisms, validate drug targets and speed drug discovery. Illumina integrates partner input and keeps the data set proprietary. GSK is the latest company to join the Billion Cell Atlas Consortium, and other companies are showing growing interest in access to the finished Atlas. Thaysen called it a large long-term opportunity and said more on strategy will be shared in coming quarters.

**Illumina Insight ecosystem at ASHG (American Society of Human Genetics, held earlier in October).** Illumina combined assays, sequencing platforms and software, with customers showing work that combined genomics, transcriptomics (RNA activity), methylation (chemical marks on DNA) and proteomics (proteins) across bulk, single-cell and spatial approaches. Thaysen said customers value that Illumina looks at their work end to end, making it easier to get all components while lowering total workflow cost.

**BioInsight and SpliceAI2.** Illumina launched the BioInsight platform, which puts its analysis application, AI models and curated biological data in one place. It includes SpliceAI2, a new AI model predicting how genetic changes affect RNA splicing; in rare-disease research data sets it identified 17% more disease-relevant splice variants than other splicing models. Thaysen said the next era of life sciences will be powered by software, AI and differentiated data as well as sequencing, and described a path from predicting biology to more proactive health care.

**Closing.** He said the team managed investments through a challenging environment and that the strategy set in 2024 is delivering.

## Prepared remarks: Ankur Dhingra (CFO)

Beyond the figures above, Dhingra said the broader picture is growing clinical sequencing adoption, a rapidly growing X installed base that supports a strong consumables stream, expanding revenue and interest in the Billion Cell Atlas, and a strong balance sheet that gives flexibility to invest in strategic growth areas.

## Q&A

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies (first question)

**Question:** Were there any stocking orders (customers buying ahead of need), as there were last quarter? And does Q4 imply low-double-digit clinical growth, and is that the right jumping-off point for next year?

**Answer:** Thaysen said Illumina is pleased with clinical performance and expects mid-teens for the full year. Q3 looked much like Q2 (14% to 15%), he expects that to continue, there are always puts and takes in a quarter, and Q4 is "coming along" well. He did not call out stocking orders. Dhingra said Q4 consumables guidance is being raised on strong placements. Q4 2025 was an unusually strong comparison, with 11% consumables growth, so he still expects "a pretty good 5%, 6%" consumables growth in Q4 2026 on top of it.

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI

**Question:** The Q4 jumping-off point implies close to 6% RoW growth. What is the framework for 2027, are the long-range plan (LRP) targets intact, and could strong instrument placements this year become an instrument headwind next year, and how is it offset?

**Answer:** Thaysen (joking that it took until the second question to ask about 2027) declined to give details before the Q4 call. Points made:
- The market is healthy; clinical should be a growth driver "for the foreseeable future."
- Multi-omics capabilities position Illumina to benefit as research recovers, and AI demand for large training data sets is only beginning.
- Consumables have grown above 5% in each quarter this year, which he called "a good starting point."
- The 2024 strategy was to return to growth by completing the X transition and lapping its price impact; Illumina is "on the latter end of that," and elevated X placements will keep driving growth.
- He did not directly answer the instrument-headwind part of the question.

### Doug Schenkel, UBS

**Question:** Does recent X strength reflect pull-forward of future demand? And what is the mix between capacity expansion tied to clinical volume and transitions or replacements?

**Answer:** Thaysen said there was no pull-forward; "this is a normal course of business." The last four quarters of placements outdid the first year after launch. Drivers: a healthy clinical market and new, very sequencing-intensive assays, with intensity expected to rise further ("a lot more coming"). Innovations showcased at AGBT (a genomics conference) earlier in 2026 are convincing customers the X is the platform to bet on, with more coming on sequencing quality, turnaround time, capacity and multi-omics assays. He did not give a capacity-versus-replacement split.

### Puneet Souda, Leerink

**Question:** Is lower cost per gigabase from newly placed instruments driving elasticity (more volume as price falls) in consumables? And is the high-single-digit 2027 framework intact?

**Answer:** Thaysen said the move from the NovaSeq 6000 to the X was a price step down but generated substantially more volume, and the volume continues; it is not just a six-month gain under a fixed budget. In clinical, customers are building bigger assays, gaining competitive position and advancing oncology testing. He added that, as with many companies, customers willing to sign up for large volumes can negotiate price: "that's a conversation I like to have." On 2027, he said the strategy set in 2024 was to move into growth (mid-single digits in 2025), and Illumina "still [is] on the track to deliver on the '27 of high single digit."

### Subhalaxmi (Subbu) Nambi, Guggenheim

**Question:** Do new products still have the potential to add 1 to 2 points of growth in 2027? Is the exit-2026 operating margin of 25% the right launch point for further margin improvement?

**Answer:** Thaysen said Illumina must deliver Q4 before detailing 2027, but the 2024 plan to add growth drivers through new assays and data "is playing out well." Over 18 months Illumina launched a "very significant" multi-omics portfolio, and with SomaLogic it is well positioned; momentum is from a low base but will show in 2026 and especially 2027 and beyond. On margins, they have improved about 400 basis points in recent years despite unanticipated headwinds this year (electronic component pricing, shipping costs and some tariffs). "I think there is more to come in the years to come and also in '27."

### Luke Sergott, Barclays

**Question:** How should we think about the clinical exit rate next year, given liquid-biopsy (blood-based cancer test) players growing sample volumes and converting to the X, including initial reads and concordance studies (validation work comparing results across instruments)?

**Answer:** Thaysen said it is playing out as expected. Illumina has a "razor-razor blade" model (instruments drive recurring consumables): "nobody buys an instrument to put in the corner." Whether clinical grows 14%, 15% or 16% matters less than the fact that the market is strong with many applications coming online. U.S./Canada clinical has grown very strongly for four quarters and he does not expect that to stop. Dhingra added that the number of recently launched tests, volume growth of on-market tests and the number of clinical trials expected to read out in the next year point to a "very rich pipeline."

### Dan Arias, Stifel

**Question:** For the AI and virtual-cell opportunity, how does genome pricing factor in? Some genomes are being discussed at around $50. Does Illumina need to revisit its price-elasticity approach to win large projects, or will the new 35B flow cell lower genome cost enough?

**Answer:** Thaysen said AI models are "way beyond" whole-genome pricing. The value lies in creating large data sets across genome, transcriptome (RNA), methylome and proteome, in bulk, single-cell and spatial contexts, and "no other company in this industry" has the portfolio and ability to do this. The Billion Cell Atlas is a functional data set that shows what happens to a cell when a gene is up- or down-regulated. He said "We love elasticity" and Illumina can address any opportunity. He described a flywheel in which Illumina and customers build data sets and customers add to them. On competitors' cheap genomes: "I'm not super concerned about what others are doing right now." He did not address the 35B flow cell directly.

### David Westenberg, Piper Sandler (first question)

**Question:** Clinical is about 70% of consumables. How fast can it grow to support the long-term high-single-digit target, and what net pricing or discounts should be expected from large customers (e.g., makers of Signatera and Shield tests) in 2027 to 2029, given 25% to 30% volume growth for those customers?

**Answer:** Thaysen said it was a very specific pricing question he would not answer. Customers are building bigger assays for therapy selection, MRD (minimal residual disease, detecting leftover cancer) and now MCED (multi-cancer early detection). Growth rates may differ at times, but "when our customers are growing, we were also growing very healthy." Eighteen months ago analysts questioned whether Illumina could grow at all; now it has mid-single-digit consumables growth. Dhingra added that in Q3, U.S./Canada consumables growth (a proxy for those large customers) and U.S. clinical growth were "well above" 14%. Large customers are a meaningful but not total part of a global business.

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord Genuity

**Question:** Is there upside to high-single-digit growth beyond 2027 given AI, multi-omics and a possible research inflection, perhaps approaching double digits with operating margin in the high 20s? What would it take?

**Answer:** Thaysen said it is "absolutely not impossible" and "there's definitely upside to a high single-digit growth," but in 2024 Illumina was in negative growth, has moved to mid-single digits, and wants to prove high-single digits first. He cited a large clinical opportunity, research recovery with grants shifting toward multi-omics, and AI data-set demand. Customers increasingly want data sets and model performance rather than just sequencing.

### Dan Leonard, RBC

**Question:** When will BioInsight become visible in the P&L, as soon as next year?

**Answer:** Thaysen said BioInsight was established about a year ago, combining software (doing "extremely well") and data. The 2024 strategy said data and multi-omics would generate 1% to 2% of growth in the latter part of the strategic period, i.e., 2027, and that is on track; AI could make it "substantially bigger." Dhingra said that sometime in 2027 there should be visible color, with the Billion Cell Atlas and its second generation (expanding to 5 billion cells) among the first drivers, and a broad range of customers in discussion. New products and BioInsight adding 1 to 2 points of growth in 2027 remains the expectation.

### Casey Woodring, JPMorgan

**Question:** (1) What is the extra selling week in Q4 worth, and is a budget flush (customers spending leftover annual budgets) expected? (2) Given news this week of US trade restrictions on some Chinese competitors, and Illumina's continued problem with China's unreliable entity list, has Illumina's position with Chinese authorities changed, and what are customers saying now that China returned to year-over-year growth in Q3?

**Answer:** On China, Thaysen said Illumina is pleased to be back to growth, remains very committed to China, and has worked closely with customers and regulators on a path forward. It learned of the US action "as many of you did yesterday morning" and is still assessing the impact. No specific budget flush is anticipated beyond what is normal for Q4. Dhingra said nothing meaningful is assumed for a flush, so a stronger one would be upside. Illumina reports on a fixed number of weeks per quarter, so about every four years there is an extra week to return to a 13-week cycle. The extra week was in guidance all year, falls over the holidays and early January, and has minimal revenue impact; it adds one week of people cost. The Q4 consumables raise comes "entirely" from strong placements and customer demand.

### Dan Brennan, TD Cowen

**Question:** Over the next 3-plus years, is the AI opportunity mostly services through BioInsight or instruments and flow cells? Is Billion Cell Atlas pipeline weighted to single-cell and multi-omics versus whole genome? And what was NovaSeq X pricing in the quarter?

**Answer:** Thaysen said small data sets built by individual labs are noisy, and models often predict where data came from rather than the biology. So there is big demand for large, reproducible, standardized data sets, which is a major service opportunity. Customers will also want to augment Illumina data with their own, so instruments will still sell: "it will be a blend." Illumina has become expert at perturbation across cell lines. Dhingra said the multi-omics portfolio enables combinations such as whole genome plus proteomics, single-cell plus transcriptomics, and spatial plus transcriptomics, depending on organ or disease pathway. The Billion Cell Atlas is a starting point that is already helping prove causality. Neither speaker gave X pricing detail in the quarter beyond the transition commentary above.

### David Westenberg, Piper Sandler (second question)

**Question:** On China, growth in the quarter (reported and organic, with a figure noted as 7% and double digits); what happened, and how to think about China going forward given the unreliable entity list?

**Answer:** Thaysen did not quantify China. He said Illumina is committed to finding a path forward, has good relationships, focuses on what it controls (supporting customers, driving innovation), sees strong interest in its innovations, believes the opportunity is big, and that Chinese customers want Illumina in the market.

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies (final question)

**Question:** Dhingra mentioned more operating leases, in the research context. Can that spill into clinical?

**Answer:** Dhingra said operating leases and reagent rental models (instrument provided in exchange for committed consumables purchases) have long been offered, but demand is growing in both research and clinical. It has not been large among the biggest clinical customers, who are in extensive capacity expansion, but it has picked up among mid-sized clinical labs worldwide, including in the U.S. and Europe. The offering is available globally.

## Items worth flagging

- Sources of ambiguity: Dhingra described Q3 growth as both 4.7% and 5% RoW organic (rounding). Kumar's "close to 6%" Q4 RoW figure differs from management's stated Q4 RoW organic growth guidance of about 4.5%; management did not reconcile this on the call.
- Management repeatedly avoided 2027 specifics, deferring to the Q4 call, but reaffirmed the 2024-set target of high-single-digit growth in 2027 and 1 to 2 points of growth from new products, data and BioInsight.
- The "35B flow cell" and "$50 genome" references came from analyst Dan Arias; management did not confirm or discuss them.
- The transcript contains a few garbled passages (e.g., an unclear figure in Westenberg's second question), which are noted rather than interpreted.
