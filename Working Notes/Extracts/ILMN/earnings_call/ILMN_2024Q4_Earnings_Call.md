# Illumina Q4 2024 Earnings Call (core Illumina, ex-GRAIL) with 2025 guidance and China "unreliable entity" discussion

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2024Q4_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2025-02-06 · Seeking Alpha transcript; speakers Salli Schwartz (VP Investor Relations), Jacob Thaysen (CEO), Ankur Dhingra (CFO); analysts from Evercore ISI, TD Cowen, Leerink, Wolfe, RBC, Stifel, JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, Jefferies, Piper Sandler, Guggenheim, Scotiabank, Citi · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q4 2024 revenue of $1.1B (+1% y/y, constant currency), beating its own expectations on NovaSeq X placements, with non-GAAP EPS of $0.95 (the Seeking Alpha header shows $0.86 and a miss, which conflicts with the CFO). It reaffirmed 2025 guidance given in mid-January (low single-digit revenue growth, ~23% operating margin, EPS $4.50-$4.65) but explicitly excluded any effect from China's just-announced Ministry of Commerce action. Most analyst questions probed China (about 7% of revenue), the NovaSeq X transition and pricing, competition from emerging rivals, and cost savings.

## Results and guidance at a glance

**Scope.** All figures are for "core Illumina," meaning Illumina excluding GRAIL (the cancer-detection business Illumina divested in June 2024). Revenue growth rates are in constant currency (foreign-exchange effects removed). "Year over year" compares with Q4 2023; "sequential" compares with Q3 2024. Results are non-GAAP (adjusted, and here still including stock-based compensation). [Q4 2024 call, Salli Schwartz]

**A discrepancy to note.** The Seeking Alpha page header says "EPS of $0.86 misses by $0.06 | Revenue of $1.10B (-1.60% Y/Y) beats by $554.68K." That is Seeking Alpha's own data summary, not what management said. The CFO stated non-GAAP EPS was $0.95 per diluted share, above the company's expectations. The header's -1.6% revenue figure also differs from management's constant-currency +1%, which is probably a reported (not constant-currency) number or a different basis. The header figures are unexplained in the document.

### 2025 guidance (core Illumina, constant currency)

Management said its views "are aligned with the commentary we provided in mid-January," so this is a reaffirmation rather than a change, except for the tax rate. [Jacob Thaysen; Ankur Dhingra]

- **Revenue:** low single-digit percentage growth, weighted toward the second half because of the NovaSeq X transition.
- **Sequencing consumables:** low single-digit growth, driven by strong sequencing activity, especially from clinical customers.
- **Sequencing instruments:** a low single-digit decline, as customers manage capital spending closely and placements moderate.
- **Total sequencing revenue** growth expected in line with total Illumina revenue growth.
- **Non-GAAP operating margin:** about 23%, up 170 basis points from 2024. Drivers are gross margin improvement and expense reduction, partly offset by inflation and market-based merit pay increases.
- **Non-GAAP tax rate:** now about 22.5%. The CFO described this as "lowering" it; the 2024 rate was 23.6%.
- **Non-GAAP diluted EPS:** $4.50 to $4.65, about 10% growth at the midpoint.
- **Transition target:** roughly 75% of high-throughput gigabases shipped on the X series by the second half of 2025 (the CEO also said "by midyear").
- **Assumptions:** guidance does not attempt to reflect any impact of the China announcement, and assumes the current macroeconomic and political environment continues. Management is also watching Washington developments on research funding. Pull-through assumptions (the consumables revenue a customer generates per instrument) are in the slides.

### Q1 2025 guidance [Ankur Dhingra]
- Revenue flat to down 1% y/y, driven mainly by a decline in sequencing instruments and the timing of certain strategic partnership revenues.
- Non-GAAP operating margin about 20.5%.
- Non-GAAP EPS $0.93 to $0.98.
- In Q&A: consumables expected to rise sequentially from Q4 and to be positive y/y; instruments likely negative y/y.

### Long-term targets (reiterated) [Jacob Thaysen]
- Return to revenue growth in 2025 and reach high single-digit growth by 2027.
- More than 500 basis points of operating margin expansion; the CEO later cited a 26% operating margin goal.
- Double-digit to teens EPS growth.
- Three growth drivers: (1) the core sequencing business as the X transition progresses; (2) scaling the entry into multiomics (measuring several biological layers, such as DNA, RNA and proteins) in 2025 and 2026; (3) expanding data and services.

## Prepared remarks

### Jacob Thaysen, CEO

**China (opening statement).** Thaysen addressed "the recent announcement from the Chinese Ministry of Commerce" first. He said Illumina is "in dialogue with the relevant parties and promptly seeking additional information to reach a resolution," has a long-standing presence in China serving clinical and research customers, and complies with all applicable laws wherever it operates. China is approximately 7% of global revenue, and he sees "significant opportunity" there. (The Q&A shows the announcement involved an "unreliable entity list" designation, per analyst phrasing; the call itself gives no further detail.)

**2024 review.** He called 2024 "transformative." Achievements he listed:
- Launched a new corporate strategy aiming for revenue growth in 2025 and high single-digit growth by 2027.
- Deepened focus on customers and partners to expand genomics into multiomics.
- Introduced innovations across the sequencing workflow, aimed at "the highest quality insight for the lowest end-to-end cost."
- Progressed the NovaSeq X transition, with research and clinical customers using instruments for deeper and broader sequencing.
- Built a new leadership team and refined the organization.
- Rolled out operational excellence initiatives to lift margins and drive EPS growth.

**NovaSeq X progress.** NovaSeq X is Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer. Q4 growth came mainly from consumables as customers raised instrument utilization. Average pull-through in 2024 was $1.3 million per system. Sequential results beat expectations because of higher-than-anticipated X shipments. Illumina placed 91 instruments in Q4, bringing the installed base to 630.

**Three priorities.** (1) Deeper customer and partner collaboration; (2) continuous innovation; (3) commercial and operational excellence.

**Partnerships.**
- NVIDIA: combines Illumina software with NVIDIA's AI tools to improve analysis and interpretation of multiomic data.
- Truveta Genome Project, with Truveta, Regeneron and several US health systems: building one of the largest genetic databases linked to phenotype (clinical) data, sequencing 10 million exomes (the protein-coding parts of the genome) "exclusively using Illumina technology."

**Product launches.**
- December 2024: began shipping a single flow cell NovaSeq X, with the same performance and speed as NovaSeq X Plus at a more accessible price.
- Began shipping new 25B flow cell 100- and 200-cycle kits for NovaSeq X, key for multiomic applications; they also support the new single-cell solution (formerly PIPseq, from Fluent Biosciences).
- MiSeq i100, an automated sample-to-answer benchtop system for oncology, microbiology and other uses: uptake since its Q4 launch has been strong, with more than 70 instruments placed with early-access customers before year-end.
- Proteomics (study of proteins) solution developed with Standard BioTools: a fully integrated end-to-end workflow with more automation and ease of use than other products on the market. As part of the rollout, Illumina announced a pilot program to analyze 50,000 UK Biobank samples, generating a new NGS-based proteomics dataset.
- Constellation mapped-read technology: the early-access program is being extended. Enrolled customers are encouraged by its ability to detect structural variants (large DNA rearrangements) that short-read sequencing struggles to find.
- More roadmap news promised at the AGBT conference later in February.

**Operational excellence.** Manufacturing and logistics efficiencies in Q4 contributed to more than $100 million in cost savings across 2024. Thaysen said improving margins in a tough market "showcases the essence of the new Illumina operating model."

**Closing.** He thanked employees and said the refreshed strategy is driving progress.

### Ankur Dhingra, CFO

**Revenue drivers.** Q4 growth came mainly from high-throughput consumables; above-expectation performance came from NovaSeq X placements, including "encouraging uptake from clinical customers." Consumables also grew in mid-throughput (smaller instruments) against a modest prior-year comparison.

**X transition status.** At the end of Q4, more than 65% of high-throughput gigabases shipped, and about 40% of high-throughput consumables revenue, was on the NovaSeq X series.

**Volume.** Total sequencing output (gigabases) on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30% y/y, with robust growth from both clinical and research customers.

**Instruments.** The 3% decline was driven mainly by lower mid-throughput shipments because of capital purchase and cash-flow constraints on customers, partly offset by the MiSeq i100 launch.

**Services and other.** The 1% decline was mainly due to the timing of certain strategic partnership revenues last year. Instrument service contracts, companion diagnostic (CDx) agreement revenue, and informatics revenue grew as anticipated.

**Margins.** Q4 gross margin reflected typical Q4 seasonality, with a high instrument mix. The y/y gain came from operational excellence (productivity and cost savings) and consumables growth.

**Balance sheet.** Cash generation is strong and the balance sheet robust, "positions us well for the future."

## Question-and-Answer Session

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI
**Question:** What has Illumina heard on China, what is the China clinical versus research mix and margin profile, and with Q1 flat to down, what is the implied exit rate for the year?
**Answer (Thaysen):** The news is only days old. China is a significant market with an aging population and a government prioritizing healthcare and sequencing, so competitors also see opportunity. Illumina believes its NGS technology and pipeline serve Chinese customers well, and is working promptly with relevant parties. He would not give detail beyond the ~7% revenue share.
**Answer (Dhingra):** Cadence is unchanged from the January discussion: growth is skewed to the second half as the high-throughput transition completes. As the transition occurs, pricing comes down a little, which makes top-line growth more profitable in the second half. Cost-out and operational excellence will show throughout the year.

### Dan Brennan, TD Cowen
**Question:** Q4 volume growth of more than 30% looked like a deceleration from the prior quarter. What is the volume and elasticity (how volume responds to lower price) outlook, and how ratable is the 2025 move from NovaSeq 6000 to X?
**Answer (Thaysen):** Customers moving to X drive "significantly more volume" than those who stayed on the 6000, through larger assays and deeper sequencing. The volume figure is indicative, not precise: whether 30% or 40% "is not the main message." Longer term, volume growth should be around the mid-20% range; the current rate is above that and should stay above for a while. He still expects more than 75% of volume on X by midyear.
**Answer (Dhingra):** 2024 ended with very good pull-through; he is pleased with the X ramp and sees potential for continued growth; assumptions are on the slide.

### Puneet Souda, Leerink Partners
**Question:** Why expect the China action to be reversed, given it looked pinpointed (another large software company was involved)? And where will the clinical X transition (50% of gigabases) land by end of 2025?
**Answer (Thaysen):** He would not comment on timelines or whether it could be reversed; Illumina is in dialogue with the parties and wants to keep that dialogue between them for now.
**Answer (Dhingra):** About 65% of total volume had moved to X at year-end; split out, about 50% of clinical volume has moved. For 2025 he still guides 75% in aggregate; because about 80% of research volume has already transitioned, most of the remainder must come from clinical.

### Doug Schenkel, Wolfe Research
**Question:** (1) Not ring-fencing China creates another stock overhang; what margin and buyback levers protect at least the low end of EPS guidance if China is not reversed? (2) Management inherited problems, and every quarter brings something new (pricing, mid-throughput, now China, and Roche news in a couple of weeks). Are they committed to getting back to mid-single-digit growth in 1-2 years and better-than-peer EPS?
**Answer (Thaysen):** After about 1.5 years in the job he feels full ownership. The plan set out in summer 2024: high single-digit growth by 2027, double-digit to teens EPS growth, and a 26% operating margin. "That is our commitment."
**Answer (Dhingra):** Illumina is looking at all opportunities regardless of China's outcome, and will keep driving margin, tax and operating leverage. The base case already has 170 basis points of margin expansion, and he sees additional opportunities; he did not detail specific levers or buybacks.

### Conor McNamara, RBC Capital Markets
**Question:** Was Q4 sequencing consumables growth slowed by higher placements or customers waiting for single-cell X? With conversion already at 65%, ahead of the 75% target, how should early-2025 pricing impact compare with the analyst-day outline?
**Answer (Dhingra, as transcribed):** Pleased with conversion and on track for midyear. Volume growth remains very high; mix can move quarterly results. Illumina had anticipated a slight Q4 slowdown (fewer working days). As the company laps the main part of the shift of revenue to X, it will be growing "on a different price base" and expects consumables growth to improve through the year. He did not directly quantify the pricing impact.

### Dan Arias, Stifel
**Question:** How much of the production and efficiency opportunity was captured in 2024 versus what remains?
**Answer (Dhingra):** Significant opportunities remain. 2024 captured manufacturing strategy tweaks and lower-hanging fruit in team optimization. Now underway:
- Consolidating manufacturing and R&D in Singapore, for cost arbitrage, R&D/manufacturing synergies, lower R&D and manufacturing costs, and a beneficial long-run tax structure (some benefit is already in the 2025 guide).
- An India office set up about 4 months earlier, now with 150 people and a runway to expand.
- Further plays in sales and marketing and R&D.
He sees opportunities through 2025 and 2026 to optimize the cost structure.

### Rachel Vatnsdal, JPMorgan
**Question:** What was assumed for China in 2025 guidance (she cited a roughly $300 million business last year), and what are Q1 consumables assumptions, including the sequential change?
**Answer (Thaysen):** A new head of China joined about a year earlier, assessed the opportunity, added structure and strategy, and changed the organization; this is starting to show in Q4 growth and a good sequential trend. Guidance assumed approximately flat China revenue in 2025.
**Answer (Dhingra):** Consistent with strategy day, no meaningful growth contribution from China was expected near term; stable was the goal, and Q4's +1% was good. Illumina is assessing the situation while still supporting customers on the ground. (The Q1 consumables question was answered later, under Patrick Donnelly.)

### Tejas Savant, Morgan Stanley
**Question:** Would Illumina support a deal in which BGI's US exclusion (military entity list, Biosecure) is undone, with data safeguards, in exchange for unfettered China access? Also, what works in the competitive RFPs where an emerging competitor won UK Biobank proteomics and another single-cell deal?
**Answer (Thaysen):** "I do like competition. I think that keeps us on the toes." Illumina will compete with anyone, believes it has the best technology, and the Washington issues are for lawmakers on Capitol Hill, outside Illumina's influence. On the emerging vendors: competition exists, but Illumina does not issue a press release on every deal it wins.

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies
**Questions:** (1) Was there any Q4 placement pull-forward ahead of the new administration and NIH noise? (2) Is the view that about 20% of tests will never move to X still right, and what is assumed for 25B uptake? (3) Where are application-specific pricing talks? (4) How does Illumina deal with a rational competitor giving away instruments, as seemingly happened at UK Biobank?
**Answer (Thaysen):** Management did not call out any pull-forward. Historically most customers move to new technology, but a fraction stays on older platforms because their assay works and is profitable or they do not want to invest. The rule of thumb is about 20% stay and shrink over time; the last portion takes much longer to transition than the first 75-80%. On UK Biobank, the 50,000-sample pilot is the first step, and the other proteomics assay provider followed the same path at 50,000 before its full project, so Illumina is on the same trajectory with a strong solution. Everyone must eventually show they can make money: "Otherwise, eventually, the bank will run dry." No 25B uptake figure was given.
**Answer (Dhingra) on pricing:** Illumina has had active talks with quite a few clinical customers on separate pricing for emerging tests, enabling much higher volume as a distinct line rather than cutting prices across the whole portfolio. Some customers are on board and plan test launches over coming quarters; initial talks are "quite encouraging." A second and third set this year will come with proteomics and single cell, where placements may be tied to the full end-to-end workflow, a set of studies, or programs rather than per-gigabase pricing.

### David Westenberg, Piper Sandler
**Question:** What do customers get from NVIDIA, could it grow, and how does it affect the network effect? Are there other private-sector genome projects after Truveta? How close is sequencing at birth?
**Answer (Dhingra):** NVIDIA: DRAGEN (Illumina's analysis software), which runs on FPGA chips, will also be made available on GPUs for customers already invested in GPU infrastructure, giving a network effect and supporting large multiomic data volumes. NVIDIA also provides infrastructure for foundational AI models of biology that need a lot of sequencing; the collaboration enables those models on Illumina's platforms. Truveta: a private-sector genome project drawing interest in the US, Middle East, UK and Germany, both from sovereign-country sequencing and from pharma seeking more diverse datasets; more pharma activity is coming. The sequencing-at-birth question was not answered.

### Subbu Nambi, Guggenheim
**Question:** R&D is just under $1 billion a year, far above peers as a share of sales. Will that continue, how have priorities changed, and how to judge success?
**Answer (Dhingra, as transcribed):** The genomics and multiomics opportunity is large and requires continued R&D, tied to the commitment to return to growth, with high single-digit growth over three years. Illumina has a 10-year roadmap. R&D was made more efficient in 2024 and some costs removed, and it will keep assessing, but the intent is to "grow ourselves into a different percentage point rather than cut ourselves into that." No R&D dollar target was given.

### Patrick Donnelly, Citi
**Question:** Consumables came in about $20 million below what he expected even after the preannouncement; what happened as the quarter progressed? What is the Q1 consumables versus instruments guide? Can Illumina currently sell into China?
**Answer (Thaysen):** Yes, Illumina continues to serve customers and patients in China and will do its best to keep doing so; the opportunity is vast and it will work through the challenges quickly.
**Answer (Dhingra):** The company had guided at the start of the quarter that Q4 consumables would step down from Q3 given holidays, and results followed that path. X consumables were in line, with the research and clinical uptick as expected; the 6000 (6K) was slightly lower. Overall roughly in line, with nothing worrying in 6K-to-X conversion or activity. He is comfortable with reaching 75% conversion in the second half. For Q1, consumables rise sequentially from Q4 and are up y/y, while instruments are likely negative y/y.

## Key themes and risks flagged
- **China:** about 7% of revenue (about $300 million per an analyst); 2025 guidance assumed roughly flat China revenue and excludes any impact from the Ministry of Commerce action. Management declined to give timelines, clinical/research mix or margin detail, and did not say it would be reversed.
- **Competition:** emerging rivals won UK Biobank proteomics and single-cell deals (per analysts); management declined to discuss specific deals.
- **Funding and policy:** US research funding developments are being watched; management said it does not influence Capitol Hill.
- **Pricing:** price per gigabase falls with the X transition; clinical application-specific pricing is the first step in a more tailored pricing approach.
- **Customer capital constraints:** weigh on mid-throughput instruments and 2025 placements.
