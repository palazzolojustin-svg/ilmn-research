# Illumina Q2 2025 Earnings Call (July 31, 2025): Results, Raised FY25 Guidance, SomaLogic Acquisition and Analyst Q&A

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2025Q2_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2025-07-31 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Illumina's Q2 2025 earnings call; speakers Jacob Thaysen (CEO), Ankur Dhingra (CFO), Brian Blanchett (VP Finance & Treasurer, Interim Head of IR); analysts from Evercore ISI, Guggenheim, Leerink, Wolfe Research, Bernstein, Piper Sandler, Jefferies, Canaccord Genuity, Stifel, TD Cowen · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q2 2025 revenue of about $1.06 billion (down ~3% year over year, high end of guidance) and non-GAAP EPS of $1.19 (well above guidance), helped by strong clinical demand, high-throughput consumables and cost cuts. It raised full-year 2025 guidance for revenue (via China and FX), operating margin and EPS, while cutting its instrument outlook because US research customers remain cautious on NIH funding. It also announced the $350 million (plus up to $75 million contingent) purchase of SomaLogic, adding proteomics to its multiomics strategy. Analysts focused on the NovaSeq X transition pace, China, competition (Roche), 2026 growth framework, pricing and mid-throughput weakness.

# Illumina Q2 2025 Earnings Call (July 31, 2025)

All figures are "Core Illumina" (excluding the separated GRAIL business) unless noted. Revenue growth rates in prepared remarks are on a constant currency basis (exchange-rate effects removed) unless stated. Non-GAAP means adjusted to exclude certain items such as acquisition-related costs and stock compensation. [Q2 2025 call]

## Headline results and guidance

### Headline results

- **Summary (Seeking Alpha header):** non-GAAP EPS of $1.19 beat consensus by $0.18; revenue of $1.06 billion (down 4.77% year over year per Seeking Alpha) beat consensus by $7.13 million.
- **Revenue:** about $1.06 billion, down approximately 3% year over year on both constant currency and reported bases, at the high end of Illumina's guidance range [Dhingra, Q2 2025 call]. Management attributed the beat to strength in high-throughput consumables.
- **Profitability:** non-GAAP operating margin 23.8% (up 160 basis points, or 1.6 percentage points, year over year); non-GAAP EPS $1.19 (up 9% year over year), "well above" the top of guidance.
- **Clinical share:** the clinical market is now roughly 60% of total sequencing consumables (consumables are the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for each sequencing run) [Thaysen].
- **Research:** US research demand remained soft because of NIH funding uncertainty. Management said Q2 trends were generally in line with the latter part of Q1.

### Raised full-year 2025 guidance

Management raised guidance for total revenue, operating margin and EPS. The table below summarizes the new guidance and the change from the prior (May 2025) guidance where stated.

| Item (FY 2025) | New guidance | Change from prior |
|---|---|---|
| Greater China revenue | ~$200 million | +$25 million at midpoint |
| Rest of world (ex-China) revenue growth, constant currency | 0% to 2% | Reiterated |
| Total Illumina revenue growth, constant currency | -0.5% to -2.5% | Raised (revenue guidance raised) |
| Total reported revenue | $4.23 billion to $4.31 billion (includes FX) | Midpoint up ~$50 million |
| Rest of world sequencing consumables growth | +1% to +3% | Up from flat to +2%; aligned with reported revenue including pricing actions |
| Rest of world sequencing instruments | Decline of 4% to 6% year over year (including pricing actions) | Lowered |
| Non-GAAP operating margin | 22% to 22.5% | Up ~50 basis points |
| Non-GAAP tax rate | ~20% | Lowered because of new US legislation |
| Weighted average diluted shares (WASO) | ~157 million | Lowered on higher buybacks |
| Non-GAAP EPS | $4.45 to $4.55 | Up $0.25 at midpoint |

- **EPS bridge [Dhingra]:** of the $0.25 midpoint increase, about $0.10 is from tax changes, about $0.10 from China, and the remainder from FX and operating margin improvement.
- **China guidance logic:** the company remains restricted from exporting instruments into China, but has seen resilient consumables purchases and strong customer support, which it believes will extend "at least in part through Q3" [Dhingra].
- **Instrument cut:** the lower rest-of-world instrument outlook is "largely due to conservatism from our research customers that we saw towards the end of Q2." Management still expects NovaSeq X instrument demand to increase slightly and low-throughput growth from MiSeq i100 placements, but saw weakness in the throughput (mid/high) category [Dhingra].
- **Margin raise drivers:** cost discipline, higher revenue from China outperformance and favorable FX [Dhingra].
- **Tax:** legislation passed earlier in July 2025 makes US-based R&D spending tax deductible; given Illumina's large US R&D base, management expects a positive effect on its tax rate for 2025 and beyond. It had no effect on the Q2 tax rate because it was enacted after quarter end.

### Q3 2025 guidance

- Revenue outside Greater China: growth of 1% to 2% year over year (constant currency).
- Greater China revenue: $35 million to $45 million.
- Total Illumina constant currency revenue: decline of 1.5% to 2.5%.
- Non-GAAP operating margin approximately 22%; non-GAAP tax rate approximately 16%; WASO approximately 155 million shares; non-GAAP EPS $1.15 to $1.19.
- **Q4 implication [Dhingra]:** revenue ticks up in Q4, with roughly half from usual seasonality in instrument purchases (customers tend to buy instruments late in the year as budgets are used). The rest is expected to come from data service offerings such as AGD (see below) and new products such as the proteomics solution.

## Prepared remarks

### Jacob Thaysen, CEO

**Quarter overview.** Revenue was at the high end of guidance (~$1.06 billion) despite the year-over-year decline, and profitability beat expectations (non-GAAP operating margin 23.8%, EPS $1.19). Management said the results "reinforce our view that the underlying fundamentals remain robust."

Highlights cited:
- More than 50 NovaSeq X placements (the NovaSeq X is Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer).
- Increased high-throughput consumable sales, especially among NovaSeq X users, as the transition from the older NovaSeq 6000 continues.
- Progress on the innovation roadmap and multiomic strategy (multiomics means measuring several kinds of biological molecules, such as DNA, RNA and proteins).

**Clinical strength.** Clinical "has proven more resilient and in some areas, is exceeding expectations." By application:
- *Oncology:* growing adoption of comprehensive genomic profiling (sequencing many cancer-related genes in a tumor to guide therapy) and rising interest in sequencing-intensive minimal residual disease (MRD, detecting tiny amounts of remaining cancer after treatment) testing.
- *Genetic disease testing:* growth driven by expanding national genome programs and broader use of whole genome and whole exome sequencing for rare diseases.
- *Reproductive health:* growth in NIPT (non-invasive prenatal testing from maternal blood) sample volumes, particularly in the US as more customers complete validation and ramp.
- Long-term, management believes NGS (next-generation sequencing) adoption rises as genomics becomes standard of care for therapy selection, early detection and monitoring, and expands to other diseases.

**Research and China.** US research remains constrained by NIH funding uncertainty; some labs delay projects or hiring because of concerns about future grant availability. In China, instrument exports remain restricted; Illumina continues to engage regulators for a solution that supports a "long-term sustainable presence" and will update on material developments.

**Long-term targets.** Reaffirmed: high-single-digit revenue growth and non-GAAP operating margin expanding to 26% by 2027. Three drivers: (1) growing the core sequencing business including the NovaSeq X transition; (2) scalable entry into multiomics; (3) expanding services, data and software. The vision is shifting from a cost-per-gigabase model to "delivering the highest-quality biological insight at the lowest end-to-end cost."

**Multiomics and the SomaLogic acquisition.**
- Already announced capabilities: single cell, CRISPR-based Perturb-Seq and spatial analysis. In June 2025 Illumina announced it will acquire SomaLogic from Standard BioTools, expanding into affinity-based proteomics (measuring proteins using binding reagents), "a small but fast-growing segment of the broader proteomics market." It builds on an exclusive commercial relationship with SomaLogic since 2021.
- The SomaScan Assay can analyze over 9,500 unique human proteins from small samples, for drug discovery, diagnostics and health monitoring. Its proprietary SOMAmer binding reagents bind specific proteins with "unmatched sensitivity, scalability and reproducibility," giving broader coverage than other methods while reducing time and cost.
- Rationale: conviction deepened during the partnership; Illumina's product development skills and global reach can scale the technology faster and add operational efficiencies. It enhances the value proposition of NovaSeq X and could extend to single cell and spatial applications. Portfolio now spans DNA, RNA, methylation and proteomics.
- Expected close: first half of 2026, subject to regulatory approvals.

**MiSeq i100 Plus.** The latest benchtop (small, lab-bench) sequencer launched late 2024; more than 500 instruments placed, with customers ordering additional units after a few months. Customers call it a "game changer": faster turnaround, ease of use, and room-temperature shipping and storage of reagents. It supports 18 proven workflows, 9 of them fully integrated from library prep through analysis. Management said it appeals to first-time NGS labs and emerging markets, reduces reliance on centralized labs and shortens diagnosis time. It "sets a new standard for the future of Illumina innovation," with end-to-end workflows.

**Outlook stance.** Encouraged by Q2 momentum but "cautious" on H2 2025 given US research funding uncertainty; nevertheless guidance for total revenue growth, reported revenue, operating margin and EPS was raised.

### Ankur Dhingra, CFO

**Framing.** Q2 exceeded expectations. Research customers, especially US academic and government, are managing budgets tightly; clinical customers keep investing in new tests and scaling existing ones. Greater China was slightly better than guide; Chinese customers remain engaged, consumables sales "held up well," and talks with relevant authorities continue.

**Revenue by region and product**

| Line | Q2 2025 | Comparison / note |
|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | ~$1.06B | Down ~3% year over year (constant currency and reported) |
| Greater China revenue | $63M | $12M lower than Q2 2024; slightly ahead of expectations |
| Revenue excluding Greater China | n/a | Down ~2% constant currency |
| Sequencing consumables | $740M | ~Flat year over year; up ~6% sequentially (reported) |
| Sequencing instruments | $96M | Down ~18% year over year |
| Sequencing services and other | $136M | Down ~5% year over year, in line with expectations |

- **Consumables:** high-throughput consumables grew both sequentially and year over year, including greater than 10% sequential growth in NovaSeq X consumables revenue. Clinical (~60% of consumables) growth was driven by broader comprehensive genomic profiling adoption and momentum in MRD.
- **NovaSeq X transition:** over 80% of research customers' sequencing volumes have already moved to X (these customers face budget constraints). Clinical customers are validating and balancing investment between on-market tests and new test development; clinical transition is roughly 55% in the quarter. In Q2, ~69% of high-throughput gigabases shipped and ~44% of high-throughput consumables revenue were on the NovaSeq X series. At the current pace, management expects towards the end of 2025 about 50% of high-throughput revenue and about 75% of gigabases shipped on X, driven by clinical.
- **Sequencing activity:** total Gb (gigabase, a billion DNA letters) output on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30% year over year, driven by clinical, with more muted research growth. Not a predictor of near-term revenue but a directional gauge of demand and utilization.
- **Instruments:** decline reflects constrained budgets among high- and mid-throughput research customers. Funnel pipelines held but decision times extended. About 60% of X instruments placed in Q2 went to clinical customers. MiSeq i100 Plus is progressing well; the base-model MiSeq i100 launches "next month" (August 2025). In Greater China, instruments were down ~40% because of export restrictions.
- **Services:** the decline was mainly timing of certain strategic partnership revenues last year related to the AGD consortium (Alliance for Genomic Discovery, a data and sequencing program). Excluding those, the core services and informatics business grew high single digits.

**Profit and loss**
- Non-GAAP gross margin 69.4%: up 200 basis points from Q1, stable year over year. Drivers: favorable mix from high consumables share, and operational excellence initiatives. Tariffs had a partial impact, about 110 basis points net on Q2 gross margin.
- Non-GAAP operating expenses $484M, down ~6% ($32M) year over year, reflecting cost actions while prioritizing growth investments; no typical post-Q1 seasonal rise in OpEx.
- Non-GAAP operating margin 23.8%, up 160 basis points year over year; operating profit grew ~4% on lower revenue.
- Non-GAAP other expense (mostly net interest) $10M; non-GAAP tax rate 22.2%.
- Average diluted shares ~157M, ~2M lower than Q1 due to buybacks net of employee equity dilution. Non-GAAP EPS $1.19, up 9% year over year.

**Cash flow, balance sheet and capital allocation**
- Operating cash flow $234M; capital expenditures $30M; free cash flow $204M.
- Repurchased ~4.5M shares for $380M at an average of ~$85 per share; ~$800M authorization remained at quarter end, and management intends to keep repurchasing over the year.
- SomaLogic: definitive agreement with Standard BioTools to acquire SomaLogic and other specified assets for $350M cash at closing, plus up to $75M in near-term performance-based milestones and royalties; expected close H1 2026 subject to regulatory approvals.
- Cash, equivalents and short-term investments about $1.16B; gross leverage about 1.7x (gross debt to last-twelve-months EBITDA).

## Q&A

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI
**Question:** Asked for a bridge of the guidance change (instruments down ~$25M, China up ~$25M, maybe some FX; any changes in consumables or microarrays?), and asked Thaysen how big the product pipeline is, saying the Street has not paid enough attention and it could be more meaningful next year.

**Thaysen:** Illumina has a lot in the pipeline. He saw recent feedback from early-access customers on Constellation, 5-base and spatial solutions and said feedback was "very, very strong" on all three. Engaging customers early lets them get excited, plan publications and write grants, which management believes will begin to have an impact in 2026: "an addition of growth starting from '26 and onwards."

**Dhingra:** Guidance is up because of FX and the China increase. For the rest of the world, instrumentation expectations are reduced based on Q2 and the pipeline, primarily from research, while consumables expectations rise (Q2 came in well and the pipeline supports it). On services: AGD consortium business was about $80M to $90M for full-year 2024 and was weighted to H1 2024, so the comparison is favorable in H2 2025 (as flagged on the prior call). Management anticipates some AGD business and potentially new services in H2; 2026 expectations will be discussed with 2026 guidance.

### Subbu Nambi, Guggenheim
**Question:** Has customer behavior changed in anticipation of competition, how is Illumina preparing, and since turnaround time looks hard to match versus the Roche platform, is there something in the pipeline to compete on turnaround for high throughput?

**Thaysen:** Management spends time on competition but more on customers; he thinks Illumina can engage customers better (early access, roadmap sharing). Illumina has the largest R&D focus and budget in the industry. It is openly discussing multiomics but not everything on instrumentation, which targets the main growth markets: clinical and decentralized. Some customers want fast turnaround, but that is "a niche market in the NICU" (neonatal intensive care) where users may compromise ease of use or quality. For the vast majority an overnight run is "more than reasonable." The focus is "the highest-quality insight for the lowest end-to-end" cost regardless of customer.

### Puneet Souda, Leerink
**Question:** Was there pull-forward (customers stocking ahead of tariffs in April/May)? What are large clinical customers with big NovaSeq 6000 fleets saying about moving to X, and could the transition accelerate in H2?

**Thaysen:** No pull-forward; a normal quarter. Backlog is higher: early on, customers ordered "hand to mouth" while validating X; now many run X in production mode and plan better, which shows up in backlog. Each customer has its own validation plan and many clinical customers are growing rapidly and do not want to compromise that growth. "There's no cliff happening"; customers will move an assay or several assays rapidly when ready. Some quarters will be a little higher or lower, but no cliff soon.

### Doug Schenkel, Wolfe Research
**Question:** Does guidance imply about 2 points of Q4 revenue growth excluding China, the highest in at least two years, and what does it signify for pricing issues? Framework offered: global research under 40% of sales exiting 2025 (possible 2 to 3 point headwind in 2026 on a weighted basis, even with good Senate news on NIH); China about 2% of sales; clinical over 60% of sales growing double digits (volume up, partly offset by price), contributing ~6 points. Implies core revenue growth of low to maybe mid single digits ex-China next year. Is that reasonable, and what else to consider (pipeline upside, competition caution)?

**Thaysen:** Called it a long way of asking for 2026 guidance, which is too early. Reiterated that the X transition is the main growth driver, so "2026 to look better than 2025 and 2027 to look better than 2026." Clinical is the main growth driver. Multiomics should add "at least 1% point of growth" coming into 2026 and 2027, "probably more '27 level." Markets are large; management focuses on the more than 95% of business outside China that it controls.

### Eve Burstein, Bernstein
**Question:** Was China consumables demand pulled forward by customers worried about future supply, and is the China guidance increase specific to 2025 rather than a sign of an improving 2026 outlook?

**Thaysen:** Praised the China team under General Manager Jenny. Customers want Illumina to stay for its quality, innovation and ecosystem, but "under the current circumstances, the situation is unsustainable," so the company is working with regulators on a resolution. Some pull-forward was seen in Q1, not now; business is at a natural run rate.

**Dhingra:** Outside China there was little pull-forward: good orders for the largest business, high-throughput X consumables, which have limited shelf life, so pull-forward cannot be significant; orders were for future shipping, not Q2 shipments. On China, the guidance raise is for the current quarter and not a long-term view; discussions continue toward a constructive long-run resolution.

### David Westenberg, Piper Sandler
**Question:** Asked about spending patterns of clinical customers, including those on X since the beginning, and whether they transition entire fleets or part. Also: X went from only 43% to 44% of high-throughput revenue; if there is a big jump in H2 driven by clinical, why not a revenue drop?

**Thaysen:** Clinical is strong and will remain so "for the foreseeable future, which is quite far out there." Opportunities: oncology (therapy selection, MRD "just opening up," eventually screening); genetic disease (rare disease "not as rare as you would think", expanding to broader populations, newborn screening). Customers moving from 6000 to X mostly are not just cutting test cost but expanding tests to stay competitive. Others will remain on the 6000 until an assay's end of life (for example about two more years) because switching does not make financial sense.

**Dhingra:** Agreed; transition is step by step, validation-driven and "lumpy," not linear. He is not reading much into one quarter or expecting a big material change in the next couple of quarters. The goal of 75% of volume on X in H2 might land "more towards latter part of Q4."

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies
**Question:** Trajectory of X placements for the rest of the year given X share of high-throughput revenue only rose from 43% to 44%; how to be comfortable with 50% by year end? What share of customers get 30B reads (software release 1.3)? And was the operating margin beat tariff unwind, cost-out, or both?

**Thaysen:** Q4 2024 had more than 90 placements and Q1 2025 had 60; those customers, mostly clinical, are still bringing instruments into production, which will have a meaningful H2 impact, though not a cliff. Placements average 50 to 60 per quarter as guided, with potential for more in Q4 depending on budgets. On flow cell yield, the 1.3 software improved some yield on the 25B flow cell, "but we are not committing to a 30B at this point."

**Dhingra:** The margin improvement came mostly from cost actions (some from last year, some early this year) reaching the P&L. On tariffs, work has started and a pathway to lower impact is visible, but little benefit was in Q2; expected in H2 and early 2026. OpEx at the lower Q2 level reflects "almost over $100 million annualized improvement," a structural change that should give better operating leverage as revenue returns to growth.

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord Genuity
**Question:** On the growth framework (volume growth of 20% to 30%, price declines in the mid-teens), how much volume growth is clinical versus research, and is there cushion to cut price if new competition (for example Roche) unveils pricing?

**Thaysen:** Volume growth is strong; clinical was stronger than research in Q2, as anticipated due to NIH and funding issues, and the gap was a bit larger this time. Clinical should grow faster long-term because the opportunity is larger; multiomics taking off should help research later in the strategic period. Pricing is "very stable" and within the expected decline range. Illumina discusses differentiated pricing to open new applications with customers. Others may price lower, but "in most circumstances, Illumina have the best cost structure," so it can compete on price if it chooses.

### Dan Arias, Stifel
**Question:** Can high-throughput Gb growth stay around 30% in Q3 and Q4, and does that keep the 2026 framework (mid-20s Gb growth, mid-teens pricing headwind) intact?

**Dhingra:** Connected high-throughput instrument runs grew above 30%; clinical strong, US research slower as expected. He thinks Gb growth can stay above 30%, particularly for clinical and rest of world ex-China (China instruments are not connected). Structural supports: MRD, and reimbursement progress in genetic disease testing that helps whole genome adoption. On research, proteomics (via the SomaLogic deal or the IPP launch) should be net new sequencing demand, bringing proteomics, historically mass-spec heavy, into the sequencing ecosystem.

**Thaysen:** Opportunity is "tremendous"; no reason growth would not stay very strong though it will shift quarter to quarter. Next areas include cardiovascular and neurological disease.

### Dan Brennan, TD Cowen
**Question:** Why is mid-throughput weaker (market, competition)? Is the R&D tax-expensing benefit forward-looking or retroactive, and fully baked in? What is assumed for US academic revenue this year and has it changed?

**Thaysen:** High-throughput X buyers run it in production-like, high-utilization mode. Mid-throughput (about 75% of that business is NovaSeq 1000/2000, associated with research) is project-based; institutions are delaying purchase decisions, with longer decision times. Some work is outsourced to service providers, so volume shows up in high throughput; he expects some to insource again when the macro environment changes. MiSeq i100 (500+ placed since late 2024) is pushing into the low end of mid-throughput. Competition is greatest in this segment but dynamics are unchanged over the past quarter, so he would not attribute the softness to competition.

**Dhingra:** Academic expectations are unchanged; Q2 resembled late Q1, and the assumption remains a 15% decline for the year for that part of the business, as said on the last call. (The tax-timing part of the question was not directly answered beyond the guidance statement that the ~20% FY25 rate reflects the new law.)
