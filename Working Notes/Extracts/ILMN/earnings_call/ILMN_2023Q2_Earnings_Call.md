# Illumina Q2 2023 Earnings Call (Aug 9, 2023): guidance cut, NovaSeq X rollout problems, interim CEO

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2023Q2_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2023-08-09 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Illumina's Q2 2023 earnings call. Speakers: Salli Schwartz (VP Investor Relations), Charles "Chuck" Dadswell (Interim CEO and General Counsel), Joydeep Goswami (CFO and Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer). Analysts: Puneet Souda (SVB Leerink), Dan Brennan (TD Cowen), Dan Arias (Stifel), Vijay Kumar (Evercore ISI), Tejas Savant (Morgan Stanley), Sung Ji Nam (Scotiabank), Rachel Vatnsdal (J.P. Morgan), John Sourbeer (UBS) · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina beat its own Q2 2023 guidance (revenue about $1.18B, non-GAAP EPS $0.32) but cut full-year 2023 guidance, now expecting core Illumina revenue about flat versus 2022. Management blamed a bigger-than-expected temporary drop in NovaSeq 6000 consumables as customers move to the NovaSeq X, cautious customer spending, and a weak, more competitive China. The NovaSeq X rollout was harder than planned (field issues, slower ramp), though shipments (109 in Q2) and supply (now more than 390 for the year) beat expectations. The call also covered interim CEO Chuck Dadswell, executive departures, cost cuts, the EU fine on the GRAIL deal, and long-term targets that management says are unchanged.

## Results and guidance summary

### Q2 2023 results [Q2 2023 call, Aug 9, 2023]

Illumina reported its second-quarter 2023 results after the market closed on August 9, 2023. Both revenue and earnings beat the guidance management gave on the Q1 call. Non-GAAP figures (adjusted numbers that exclude certain items, though here they include stock-based compensation) are used throughout unless stated otherwise. ### Revised 2023 guidance [Q2 2023 call]

Management lowered its full-year outlook. The table shows the new guidance. Where the call gave the prior figure, it is noted.

| Item | New 2023 guidance | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidated revenue growth | about +1% | Rachel Vatnsdal (J.P. Morgan) described the cut as about 750 basis points at the midpoint |
| Core Illumina revenue | about flat vs 2022 | Described as "reduced" guidance. Includes about 200 bp COVID-surveillance headwind, about 100 bp from Russia sanctions, and negative foreign exchange |
| GRAIL revenue | $90M–$110M | Unchanged |
| Core Illumina sequencing instrument revenue | about +3% | Reflects higher NovaSeq X shipments, partly offset by customer capital constraints, including in China, where local competition mainly hits mid-throughput |
| Core Illumina sequencing consumables revenue | about −3% | See drivers below |
| Core Illumina sequencing revenue | about flat | Includes about $30M of intercompany sales to GRAIL, eliminated in consolidation |
| Consolidated non-GAAP operating margin | about 5% | Lowered |
| Core Illumina non-GAAP operating margin | about 20% | Lowered |
| Non-GAAP tax rate | about 41% | Still includes about $75M tax expense from R&D capitalization; the dollar amount is unchanged but the rate rises as earnings fall |
| Non-GAAP EPS | $0.75–$0.90 | Includes GRAIL non-GAAP operating loss of about $670M |
| NovaSeq X supply capacity | more than 390 instruments | Up from 330 previously expected |

The four drivers Goswami gave for the consumables decline are: (1) a more persistent impact of funding issues and cautious purchasing causing project delays; (2) a more meaningful fall in NovaSeq 6000 sequencing ahead of planned moves to NovaSeq X, partly because Illumina is shipping more X units than planned; (3) a delay in the ramp of X consumables; and (4) a slower second-half recovery in China. The lower operating margins reflect lower revenue and lower gross margin (less fixed-cost leverage on lower manufacturing volume), partly offset by the accelerated $100M-plus annual run-rate cost reduction program.

### Pull-through guidance

Pull-through is the annual consumables revenue generated per installed instrument. The new 2023 ranges are:

- NovaSeq 6000: about $800,000–$900,000 per instrument (lowered; the reduction is attributed to the transition to the X).
- NextSeq 550: $80,000–$130,000 (changed).
- MiSeq: $30,000–$40,000 (changed).
- NextSeq 1000/2000: unchanged at the historical $120,000–$170,000.
- MiniSeq: unchanged at the historical $20,000–$25,000.
- NovaSeq X: no pull-through figure has been given; Goswami said it will only be provided once ramp-up has stabilized.

### Q3 2023 guidance [Q2 2023 call]

- Consolidated revenue: about $1.14B, up about 2% year over year. That is a sequential decrease of about 320 basis points from Q2, driven mainly by lower NovaSeq 6000 consumables as customers move to the X.
- Non-GAAP diluted EPS: about $0.10–$0.15.
- Consolidated non-GAAP operating margin: about 4%.
- Core Illumina non-GAAP operating margin: about 19%.

## Prepared remarks

### Salli Schwartz, VP Investor Relations

Schwartz noted that the results include GRAIL, which must be held and operated separately and independently from Illumina under interim measures ordered by the European Commission, which prohibited the GRAIL acquisition under the EU Merger Regulation. (Standard forward-looking-statement language omitted.)

### Charles Dadswell, Interim CEO and General Counsel

**Leadership and governance.** Dadswell said he became interim CEO two months earlier, the CEO search is underway, and the Board is "pleased with the outstanding candidates they are seeing." He thanked former CEO Francis deSouza and former Chairman John Thompson. New Board members are Stephen MacMillan (now Chairman), Andrew Teno and Scott Ullem. Chief Technology Officer Alex Aravanis and Chief Medical Officer Phil Febbo have departed. Steve Barnard, a 25-year Illumina employee, becomes CTO; a search will be run for a new CMO.

**Headline message.** Q2 revenue (about $1.18B) and EPS ($0.32) were both ahead of Q1 guidance. Illumina shipped 109 NovaSeq X instruments, but cut guidance. Dadswell cited three factors:

1. A larger-than-expected temporary decline in high-throughput consumables as more customers than expected move to the NovaSeq X.
2. Many customers remaining more cautious in purchasing.
3. In China, a more protracted economic recovery and an "increasingly challenged competitive landscape." By contrast, the Americas and Europe are still expected to grow year over year in 2023.

**NovaSeq X rollout.** Dadswell said it "has been a more challenging process than we anticipated." He called the X "the most sophisticated platform we have ever launched" with the most comprehensive end-to-end software Illumina has released, rolled out at "unprecedented magnitude and pace," and said issues typical of new product releases were found in the field. Actions taken: a planned software update released in June (first customer shipment was in March), and technical teams deployed worldwide to bring customer systems online. He said the rollout will take longer than originally expected, but demand remains encouraging:

- 20% of customers who bought a NovaSeq X have ordered more than one.
- Customers say larger single-cell and spatial experiments (analysis of cells individually, or of where molecules sit in tissue) become practical on the X, and are requesting funding for them.
- Large population genomics customers plan additional multiomic programs (studies combining several types of molecular data) for population-specific variants.
- Several of the largest customers are using the X to move from targeted panels and exomes (sequencing only selected genes or the protein-coding portion of the genome) to whole genome sequencing, with projects expected to scale "in the near future."

**Cost reduction.** Illumina is accelerating the $100M-plus annual run-rate expense reduction program announced on the Q1 call. A late-June 8-K reported reduced global headcount and a smaller global real estate footprint; vendor spend is being optimized and travel and other costs cut. These steps mitigate the margin impact of lower revenue in 2023 and create flexibility for investment in high-growth areas.

**Platforms.**
- NovaSeq X: more than 260 orders since launch. Q2 shipments of 109 were above the expected 80, bringing the installed base to 176. Expected 2023 shipments rose to more than 390 from 330.
- NextSeq 1000/2000 (mid-throughput): about 160 units shipped in Q2, up 8% year over year, as customers expanded fleets or migrated from MiSeq or NextSeq 550. More than 20% went to new-to-Illumina customers. Sales cycles are lengthening as customers raise funding, prioritize spending or run longer procurement. Mid-throughput win rate outside China rose from Q1. Dadswell sees a long runway of differentiation, notably XLEAP-SBS chemistry coming to these instruments next year.

**Clinical markets.** Clinical was about 53% of total sequencing consumables revenue in Q2.
- Oncology reimbursement: Anthem (second-largest US commercial payer) and Blue Cross Blue Shield of Michigan added coverage for comprehensive genomic profiling (broad tumor gene testing) for advanced cancer, adding more than 30 million covered lives. Switzerland now reimburses large NGS panels, including comprehensive genomic profiling.
- TruSight Oncology 500 (TSO 500) is on track to exceed $100M of 2023 revenue, driven by greater utilization and broader adoption. In Q2 Illumina completed its TSO Comprehensive submission for US IVD (in vitro diagnostic) registration.
- GRAIL's Galleri (a multi-cancer early detection blood test): 100,000th commercial test reached; prescribed by more than 7,500 US providers; ordered in more than 80 health systems.
- NHS-Galleri study: second-year follow-up visits completed, with 130,000 participants returning (91.3% retention). Third and final-year invitations have begun; first appointments expected this fall.
- At ASCO, GRAIL presented University of Oxford-sponsored SYMPLIFY study results showing high specificity, positive predictive value and accuracy for cancer signal detection and origin prediction, and feasibility of an MCED test assisting primary-care referral decisions.
- At AACR, GRAIL and AstraZeneca presented data supporting GRAIL's methylation platform for minimal residual disease (leftover cancer after treatment): a 92% cancer detection rate in relapsed or refractory patients across six hematological malignancies.
- Reproductive health: five US state Medicaid programs (Louisiana, Michigan, North Carolina, Rhode Island, Tennessee) now cover non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT) for all pregnancies. NIPT is now available for all pregnancies in the Netherlands and approved for broader coverage in Italy.

**Research and applied markets.** The Alliance for Genomic Discovery (launched 2022 with Nashville Biosciences) now has five founding members: AbbVie, Amgen, AstraZeneca, Bayer and Merck. Members will co-fund whole-genome sequencing of 250,000 samples and share the data for drug discovery. Phase one, announced in January, covers 35,000 samples, mostly from individuals of African ancestry.

**Innovation.** PrimateAI-3D (an AI that predicts disease-causing mutations) was the cover story of Science's June issue. DRAGEN 4.2 (the latest version of the data analysis software) launched in July. Upcoming launches: the 25B flow cell for the NovaSeq X (a higher-capacity consumable; "highly anticipated"), the Illumina Complete Long Reads enrichment assay, XLEAP-SBS chemistry on NextSeq 1000/2000, and future offerings in proteomics, multiomics and spatial.

Closing line: "While the year hasn't progressed the way we expected, we remain focused on execution, innovation, and supporting our customers."

### Joydeep Goswami, CFO

**Consumables.** Core sequencing consumables of $739M fell 1% year over year. Mid-teens growth in clinical (led by oncology and genetic disease testing) was offset by research headwinds: about 260 bp from COVID surveillance, Russia sanctions, reduced NovaSeq 6000 consumables as customers begin moving to the X but before ramping on it, and constrained funding.

**Sequencing activity** (on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments, a reference indicator directionally correlated with revenue): total up 3% from Q1 and 9% year over year. Research and applied was flat from Q1 and down 1% year over year. Clinical rose 6% from Q1 and 23% year over year. Going forward Illumina will report activity in gigabases sequenced rather than run counts, to reflect higher output per run on the NovaSeq X and NextSeq 1000/2000.

**Instruments.** Revenue of $193M grew 2%: stronger NovaSeq X shipments more than offset lower NovaSeq 6000 shipments, lower NextSeq 550 placements in China, and fewer MiSeq shipments as customers move to NextSeq 1000/2000. NextSeq 1000/2000 shipments grew 8% with strong demand from new-to-Illumina customers.

**Services and other.** $134M, up 7%, on higher instrument service contract revenue from a larger installed base, partly offset by lower codevelopment partnership contributions.

**Regions.**

| Region | Q2 2023 revenue | Year-over-year change |
|---|---|---|
| Americas | $623M | −2% |
| Europe | $303M | +11% (+14% constant currency); helped by Germany's NIPT reimbursement decision last year and the Dutch national NIPT program |
| AMEA (Asia, Middle East, Africa) | $118M | −10% (−6% constant currency); includes a 14-point hit from Russia sanctions |
| Greater China | $115M | −3% (+1% constant currency); hurt by macro and geopolitical challenges and local competition, especially in mid-throughput |

All regions faced tighter funding and the X transition: customers cut NovaSeq 6000 consumables purchases before fully ramping on the X. Goswami said this effect, though anticipated, was magnified by more X deliveries and a slower ramp in Q2. Clinical consumables shipments in the Americas, Europe and AMEA grew just under 20% year over year.

**Margins.** Gross margin fell 280 bp year over year to 67.0% because of lower instrument margins from the NovaSeq X launch (typical in a launch year), less fixed-cost leverage on lower manufacturing volumes, and higher field service and installation costs. Operating expenses were lower than expected because of accelerated cost cuts and lower performance-based compensation; the $12M year-over-year rise reflects the full-year effect of 2022 hiring. GRAIL's revenue growth was attributed to accelerating Galleri adoption, and its expense growth to scaling its commercial organization.

**Balance sheet and legal.** Illumina has $750M of convertible debt maturing in August 2023. On July 12, 2023, the European Commission imposed a €432M fine for closing the GRAIL acquisition while the EC review was pending. Illumina plans to issue a guarantee and defer payment pending its appeal of the EU General Court's ruling that the EC has jurisdiction to review the deal.

## Question-and-answer session

### Puneet Souda, SVB Leerink

**Question.** What are the NovaSeq field challenges, how soon can they be fixed, and what does it mean for Q3, Q4 and 2024 installs? Does the market freeze until problems are resolved? On elasticity of demand: the consumables dip this year is larger than after the NovaSeq 6000 launch, so how much is due to field challenges? And what is the CEO search status?

**Answer.**
- Dadswell: Positives are that more instruments were placed than expected, with some of the biggest software placements ever, and a platform that is "revolutionary from the ground up." Illumina "underestimated the amount of time it would ultimately take to bring these instruments online," and technical teams are working to bring customers up to speed.
- Goswami: Bugs are normal in launches of this size, and Q2 was "nothing out of the ordinary." Fixes already exist and are being deployed, causing some delay to when instruments are fully up to speed but not changing Illumina's or customers' expectations. Not all customers see the issues.
- Elasticity: "we're not seeing any fundamental change to elasticity." The problem is the transition. Because more Xs were installed (supply was moved up), NovaSeq 6000 consumables fell faster and by more than expected, which is "the flip side of people really being excited about the X." The decline is temporary, with X consumables expected to return the volume; there is a timing gap.
- Market stall: no. Demand for the X and the late-stage pipeline keep ramping, and the X enables previously impossible experiments.
- CEO search (Dadswell): The Board is actively searching, including internal and external candidates, and is encouraged by candidate quality. Details are confidential; an update will come when possible.

### Dan Brennan, TD Cowen

**Question.** Is the 25B flow cell still on track and when does it ship? What is baked into the second half for China, which grew in the quarter? Where are the customer challenges, given academic and government demand outside China looks robust? How can we be comfortable the faster customer switch to the X is not due to competition or weaker utilization, and what was different from last cycle's forecasting?

**Answer.**
- Dadswell: Illumina keeps prioritizing proprietary investments and has "probably … the biggest and the best proprietary pipeline in the industry." The 25B flow cell remains on track for launch in the second half of 2023. The Complete Long Reads enrichment assay is progressing and is particularly compelling with the 25B flow cell. XLEAP-SBS for NextSeq 1000/2000 and future proteomics, multiomics and spatial offerings continue.
- Goswami on China: like other companies, Illumina sees a slower than expected recovery. It had expected the first half to be soft and the second half to accelerate, but does not see that happening, because of broader economic challenges and increased competitive intensity in mid- and low-throughput.
- Ex-China: "I don't think the situation is dire as it was in last year," but there is conservatism and lengthening purchase cycles for mid- and low-throughput instruments and some consumables. Activity remains strong; new projects and instruments are coming in slower. Win rates in mid- and high-throughput are high and rose slightly versus Q1, so this is lengthening cycles, not competition.
- On the X: "the issue is not competition at all." Interest is strong from existing customers and newer ones moving up from mid-throughput. The draw goes beyond lower price per gigabase: onboard DRAGEN, faster runs, simpler workflow and ambient-shipped reagents.

### Dan Arias, Stifel

**Question.** Given the cost-reduction effort looks multiyear, how do you think about core Illumina operating margin next year relative to the 25% target discussed last quarter?

**Answer.** Goswami: After the Q1 call Illumina began a cost reset and has taken out more than $100M of annual run-rate cost. In the second half it will keep a close eye on expenses and direct spending to the highest-return areas, including innovation. "Given the challenges we are having this year, obviously, we acknowledge that getting to margins next year is going to seem like a stretch, but we remain very committed to planning for and delivering on those margins."

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI

**Question.** Q3 is guided up 3% and Q4 comps look easy; historically revenue has ramped sequentially from Q2, and Q2 saw instruments, consumables, pull-through and China all up. Are there incremental headwinds to the second half? Also, will NovaSeq X pull-through be lower because of lower sample price points, and could there be a rebound since customer inventories are probably low?

**Answer.** Goswami:
- Composition of the guidance cut: about 25% from China (slower economic recovery including liquidity challenges for mainly clinical customers, plus higher competitive intensity in mid- and low-throughput). The other 75% splits roughly equally: about half from the high-throughput consumables gap (NovaSeq 6000 consumables falling rapidly ahead of the X transition, worsened by more Xs delivered, plus a slower full ramp of the X), and about half from customer conservatism (longer sales cycles and slower recovery outside China).
- Q1 to Q2 ramp: more Xs shipped lifted instruments; NovaSeq 6000 consumables rose healthily, mainly from clinical; China jumped from a depressed Q1, as expected.
- Q3 versus Q2: two reasons for the sequential decline. NovaSeq 6000 consumable reduction hits a "local maxima" as more Xs ship, while X consumables are not yet fully ramped (expected in Q4); and China is expected slightly down from Q2 because recovery is slower than hoped.
- Q4: expected to be "up significantly," as it is the first quarter X consumables really appear, plus the traditional year-end spending ramp, both built into guidance.
- Pull-through: none has been given for the X; the initial assumptions are still considered justified given the cohort studies and experiments customers plan. The NovaSeq 6000 number was called down purely because of the transition.

### Tejas Savant, Morgan Stanley

**Question.** (1) Does the "stretch but committed" framing for the 25% margin also apply to the mid-teens long-term growth for core Illumina from analyst day? (2) What is the thinking behind the CTO and CMO changes? (3) X placements beat by about 28–30 units but sequencing instrument revenue beat by only about 5–6%; is there more aggressive discounting, and what are the instrument pricing dynamics, with BGI becoming competitive in China?

**Answer.**
- Dadswell on executives: Thanked Aravanis and Febbo. Aravanis is moving on to become CEO of another company; Dadswell said Illumina should be proud of the leaders it grows (Aravanis started at Illumina, went to GRAIL, returned, and now will be a CEO). Barnard, "employee number four" and the first scientist Illumina hired, knows the technology and team and "will be able to seamlessly pick up where Alex left off." For the CMO, Illumina will take time to evaluate its clinical and medical organization before opening a search; commitment to clinical customers, markets and programs continues.
- Goswami on long-term growth: expectations for mid-teens growth "have not changed." It rests on a large, under-penetrated NGS market and Illumina taking growing share, showing up as sequencing volume growth along three dimensions: more samples (cohorts, spatial, single cell), more analyses per sample (multiomics), and higher sequencing intensity (larger panels, liquid biopsy, MRD). The open question for the next year or so is how quickly the X ramp occurs.
- Instrument revenue versus X units: the higher X supply was a good outcome, offset by lower-than-expected mid- and low-throughput instruments, both in China and, because of lengthening purchase cycles, worldwide. Outside China win rates did not fall and rose slightly in Q2.
- Pricing: in China Illumina is "being very surgical about when to use price" but saw an overall price decline there. In the rest of the world price is on track with expectations, and Illumina is using price as a means to encourage faster technology adoption.

### Sung Ji Nam, Scotiabank

**Question.** Is cautious purchasing mainly academic or also clinical? What share of consumables purchase delays is due to customers waiting for the 25B flow cell's better economics?

**Answer.** Goswami: Outside China, caution spans both academic and clinical customers, for different reasons. Many clinical customers are not profitable and are managing cash; they keep sequencing at their core, but their purchasing intensity has come off less quickly than expected. On the 25B: "I don't think there's a delay in 25B." Of the guidance reset, about 25% is China and about 50% of the other 75% is the high-throughput consumables impact from the X transition (faster NovaSeq 6000 decline plus some delay from the Q2 issues, with solutions already being deployed), which flows through 2023.

### Rachel Vatnsdal, J.P. Morgan

**Question.** Revenue growth was cut by 750 bp at the midpoint; how does that break down across the three areas? On China stimulus (a possible 4Q tranche), what is being heard, was any seen in Q2, and how is it treated in second-half guidance?

**Answer.** Goswami: Same breakdown as above: about 25% China; of the remaining 75%, about half the high-throughput consumables impact from the X transition and half cash conservatism outside China. On stimulus: none is built into second-half expectations "and there never was" in guidance. Stimulus in the first half benefited industrial segments Illumina does not serve, and none was seen in Q2.

### John Sourbeer, UBS

**Question.** Does the long-term outlook for China change, since some headwinds may be structural from rising competition? And what is the demand for Complete Long Reads?

**Answer.** Goswami:
- China: remains an important market; Illumina's brand there is "actually very positive" and it is committed to serving customers. It will keep refining go-to-market strategy and partner choices to reach the highest-growth segments more efficiently, while observing how China evolves, as most peers are. None of that has changed.
- Complete Long Reads: interest is "very high," with many customers working with the current whole-genome product. Illumina always expected the real jump to come from the enrichment product, which is on track but, because of its economics, is coupled with the 25B flow cell launch, arriving toward the end of 2023 and picking up in 2024.

## Points of note and tensions in the call

- Management repeatedly framed the guidance cut as a timing issue (transition from NovaSeq 6000 to X, slow X ramp) rather than a demand or competition problem, except in China, where it explicitly cited competition and price declines.
- Management said NovaSeq X field issues were "nothing out of the ordinary" (Goswami) while Dadswell said the rollout was "more challenging" than anticipated and that Illumina "underestimated" onboarding time.
- Management said 2024 margin targets (25% for core Illumina) are a "stretch" but remain committed; long-term mid-teens growth is described as unchanged.
- Management expects X consumables to begin appearing meaningfully in Q4 2023 and Q4 revenue to be up significantly; Q3 is the guided low point.
