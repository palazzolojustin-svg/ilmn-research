# Illumina Q1 2026 Earnings Call (April 30, 2026): beat, raised 2026 guidance, strong NovaSeq X and clinical demand

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2026Q1_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2026-04-30 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Illumina's Q1 2026 earnings call; speakers Jacob Thaysen (CEO and interim Chief Commercial Officer), Ankur Dhingra (CFO), Conor McNamara (VP Investor Relations); analysts from Evercore ISI, Leerink, Jefferies, Wolfe, TD Cowen, Citi, Guggenheim, Canaccord, Stifel, Baird, JPMorgan, Nephron, BofA, Stephens · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q1 2026 revenue of $1.09B (up 4.8%, 1.2% organic) and non-GAAP EPS of $1.15, beating guidance and consensus, helped by over 80 NovaSeq X placements (vs a 50-60 per quarter target) and clinical sequencing consumables growing 20% ex-China. Management raised 2026 revenue guidance by $20M to $4.52-4.62B, raised the instrument outlook, and lifted EPS guidance by $0.10 to $5.15-5.30, while leaving the consumables outlook unchanged. Analysts focused on why organic growth was not raised, the competitive launches (a $150 genome from a rival), NovaSeq X supply constraints and discounting, Q2 margin pressure from freight and electronic component costs, and weak research and academic demand.

# Illumina Q1 2026 Earnings Call, April 30, 2026

Illumina is a maker of DNA sequencing instruments and the consumables (the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run). This call covered the first quarter of 2026. All claims below are from the call [Q1 2026 call] unless noted.

## Results and guidance

### Headline results

The Seeking Alpha summary header reports that non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS, profit per share excluding certain items) of $1.15 beat consensus by $0.10, and that revenue of $1.09 billion (up 4.80% year over year) beat consensus by $17.61 million [Seeking Alpha header, Q1 2026 call]. Management said revenue came in $20 million above the midpoint of its own guidance, driven mainly by better-than-expected instrument sales, and that clinical consumables came in at the high end of expectations [Dhingra, Q1 2026 call]. Revenue, margin and EPS all exceeded the guidance range [Thaysen].

The company also changed its geographic reporting segments in January 2026 to match its commercial organization. Historical results under the new segments are in the supplementary file on its website [McNamara].

Definitions used on the call: "organic" growth strips out currency and acquisitions. "Rest of World organic" growth also excludes Greater China. Growth rates are year over year and reported unless stated otherwise [McNamara].

### Cash flow, balance sheet and capital allocation

- Cash from operations was $289M, capital expenditures $38M, and free cash flow $251M [Dhingra].
- In Q1 Illumina repurchased 2 million shares for about $242M, at an average of $120.85 per share. About $400M remained on the existing authorization at quarter end [Dhingra].
- Announced the same day: the Board authorized an additional $1.5B of share repurchases. Management intends to keep buying shares opportunistically [Dhingra].
- SomaLogic (a protein-measurement, or proteomics, business) closed January 30 for a net cash payment of $363M plus potential royalties and milestones. After quarter end, Illumina paid a first $25M milestone for hitting certain 2025 targets [Dhingra].
- Quarter-end cash, equivalents and short-term investments were about $1.16B. Gross leverage was about 1.5x gross debt to trailing 12-month EBITDA (a profit measure) [Dhingra].

Excluding acquisitions, guidance implies about 350 bps of margin expansion by end of 2026, progress toward a 500 bps target by 2027 [Dhingra]. On NovaSeq X placements, Dhingra said the full-year total would come in "reasonably above the range that we had provided at the start of the year."

### Guidance, Q2 2026

- Rest of World organic revenue growth of 4% to 6%.
- Reported revenue of $1.12B to $1.14B.
- Non-GAAP EPS of $1.20 to $1.25.
- Operating margin of about 22%. Management attributed this to a higher instrument mix and related investments, near-term inflation in freight and electronic component costs, and a full quarter of SomaLogic. Mitigating actions began in Q2 and are intended to fully offset the inflation during the year. Their effect should show from Q3 into Q4, producing an implied margin improvement in the second half [Dhingra].
- The company is investing to scale NovaSeq X supply, with progress expected through Q2 and into Q3 [Dhingra].
- Q2 NovaSeq X placements are expected to be close to Q1 levels [Dhingra].

### Long-range targets

Management says it remains on track for its 2027 targets: high single-digit revenue growth, continued margin expansion (500 bps target by 2027) and double-digit to teens EPS growth for years to come [Thaysen, Dhingra].

## Prepared remarks by speaker

### Jacob Thaysen (CEO and interim Chief Commercial Officer)

**Overall.** Thaysen described a "great start," with performance driven by disciplined execution, clinical strength and growth in all regions except China. He thanked staff for managing a "higher cost environment." He said the end-market dynamics match expectations: clinical leads, research and applied are more cautious. He thanked three outgoing Board members and noted he joined Illumina in 2023.

**Quarter highlights.**
- Rest of World organic growth of 3.5%, above the high end of guidance.
- Rest of World sequencing consumables grew about 7%, including about 20% growth in clinical.
- Over 80 NovaSeq X placements, about 20 more than Q1 2025, with year-over-year growth in both clinical and nonclinical markets. NovaSeq X is Illumina's current high-throughput sequencer. New high-volume clinical applications are being built on it.
- SomaLogic closed and is performing in line with expectations on revenue and profit.
- Margins were about 150 bps above guidance.

**Clinical.** Clinical was more than 65% of sequencing consumables revenue. Drivers: expansion of sequencing-based diagnostics, more data-intensive applications such as comprehensive genomic profiling (broad tumor gene testing) and whole genome sequencing, new assays launching, and progress in reimbursement. Thaysen sees a "long runway."

**Research and academic.** Demand remains cautious amid funding uncertainty. Illumina continues to invest in proteomics (protein measurement) and single cell, with spatial offerings coming. These markets are an entry point for new technologies that later drive clinical adoption. Management expects a return to growth as funding uncertainty eases.

**Innovation (AGBT conference).**
- TruPath: whole genome sequencing with deeper insight and no traditional library prep, cutting hands-on time to about 10 minutes. Rare disease customers are using it to consolidate several tests into one workflow. Several customers are in clinical studies, and demand should rise in coming quarters.
- Spatial transcriptomics (mapping gene activity within tissue): very high engagement. Early access users generated data from hard samples such as lymphatic tissue. On track to launch later in 2026.
- NovaSeq X 18-month road map: new 14B and 35B flow cells (the consumable chip a run is performed on, sized in billions of reads), staggered flow cell runs, and Q70 data quality performance. These add flexibility, throughput and efficiency, and are driving placements even 3 years after the first X shipped.
- The company exited the quarter with a solid backlog, supporting the raised instrument outlook.
- BioInsight (data and AI business introduced late 2025): the Billion Cell Atlas, introduced earlier in 2026, uses Perturb-seq (gene-disruption experiments read out by sequencing) to study how genes drive disease. Hundreds of millions of cells are already generated, more companies want to join, and insights could feed AI models for drug discovery.

### Ankur Dhingra (CFO)

Dhingra covered the numbers in the results section above. Additional operating detail:

- **Supply:** Illumina placed more than 80 NovaSeq X units against a targeted range of 50 to 60 per quarter. It was supply constrained in Q1 because demand was very robust. Several multiunit clinical orders came in. High-throughput research placements also grew year over year.
- **Consumables:** High-throughput volume drove most growth as the X installed base expands and utilization rises.
- **Clinical consumables** grew 20% ex-China for the second straight quarter, on rising clinical volumes and adoption of information-rich tests in new trials. Products such as 5-base (an assay reading extra DNA information) and TruPath broaden access to richer data.
- **Research and applied consumables** fell 12% ex-China on funding uncertainty. Macro commentary seems "stable to improving," but year-to-date trends match 2026 expectations.
- **NovaSeq X transition (as of Q1):** about 82% of volumes and 55% of revenue are on X. In research and applied, 90% of volume is on X. In clinical, 76% of volume is on X, and management expects over 80% to 85% by end of 2026.
- **Sequencing output:** Total gigabase output on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew over 30% year over year, with clinical growth well above that.
- **Service and other:** BioInsight partnership and data deal timing can be lumpy.
- **Microarrays:** Down 20% Rest of World organic, largely because of specific large direct-to-consumer customers.

## Q&A

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI

**Question:** Given the Q1 beat, strong instruments and clinical, and a raised instrument guide, why was organic growth for the year not raised? Is cautiousness built in, and why can't clinical sustain 20%?

**Answer:** Thaysen said clinical momentum should continue this year and beyond. Instrument placements were strong in Q4 and Q1 with a strong pipeline, and instruments begin generating consumables roughly 6 months after install, which supports optimism for the second half. He noted it is only one quarter into the year and Illumina is "leaning in" by raising top and bottom line early. Dhingra said revenue rose $20M, about 0.5 point, so the guide now sits toward the higher end of the stated range; "decimal points move," and there is nothing more to it.

### Puneet Souda, Leerink

**Question:** How much of the instrument strength is clinical versus research? What about the pricing effect when labs move from NovaSeq 6000 to NovaSeq X consumables, and is it in the guide?

**Answer:** Thaysen said both clinical and research placements grew. Clinical is more than 60% of placements and is mostly incremental placements rather than conversions of 6000s, so it drives top-line growth immediately. Research shows large projects and single-cell work but no substantial mix change. Spatial should add upside late 2026 into 2027. Because most research customers have already converted to X, Illumina no longer faces the price headwind when that market recovers. Dhingra said there is no change in pricing-transition assumptions; clinical transition continues and the price effects are already in the guide.

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies

**Question:** Can you quantify the backlog and how much could not ship? And how can you reassure that the 18-month road map does not "freeze the market" (customers waiting for new products)?

**Answer:** Thaysen said placements show no market freezing, the funnel is strong, and the road map (new flow cells, spatial, TruPath, 5-base) supports both clinical and research. He sees no substantial change in the competitive environment and said "We know there's a lot of noise out there. We are looking forward to compete when they finally get to the market." Dhingra did not give a backlog figure but said performance obligations (contracted future revenue, in the 10-Q) are up more than 20% year over year, across instruments and consumables. He expects Q2 placements close to Q1 levels with a robust pipeline for the rest of the year, and much of the demand comes from new trials and tests.

### Madeleine (on behalf of Doug Schenkel), Wolfe Research

**Question:** Q2 operating margin guidance looks lower than the Street, with supply investment continuing into Q3. How should margins progress through the year, and what is the ramp to the exit rate?

**Answer:** Thaysen noted the multi-year margin improvement and the plan laid out in 2024 to rebuild high single-digit growth by 2027: flattish growth last year, mid-single-ish now, high single digit next year. There are quarterly puts and takes and inflation to offset. Dhingra listed Q2 factors: a much higher instrument mix year over year, component and freight inflation, and a full quarter of SomaLogic. Mitigating actions started in Q2 and should become visible in Q3 and Q4, so he feels good about a second-half versus first-half margin ramp.

### Daniel Brennan, TD Cowen

**Question:** How should the guide be read on clinical given where X conversion stands, and could conversion accelerate late in the cycle?

**Answer:** Thaysen said there is still a mix of customers. Some clinical customers with regulated or FDA-approved assays on the NovaSeq 6000 will stay longer ("laggards"). Many have already transitioned. By end of 2026 the transitioning business should have fundamentally moved, leaving a long tail. He sees no deceleration in clinical for now.

### Patrick Donnelly, Citi

**Question:** What are customers saying about research and applied (down 12% against an easy comparison)? Do they expect improvement? Also, where does SomaLogic revenue sit?

**Answer:** Thaysen said there seems to be alignment in Washington on committing to NIH funding, and he expects funding to pick up during the year. But released grants must still become spending on tools and consumables, and customers are cautious until they see who receives grants. Little improvement is built into the guide, so it is potential upside. Dhingra added that more than 90% of volume in this market has moved to X, so pricing comparisons should keep improving through the year. SomaLogic is reported mostly in microarrays.

### Subhalaxmi (Subbu) Nambi, Guggenheim

**Question:** Given two quarters of 20% clinical growth, is continued momentum fair to assume? The guide implies clinical low double digits to teens against research down mid to low single digits; could it go well beyond the high end?

**Answer:** Thaysen said clinical has been strong for two quarters and through 2025, and the price headwind is receding as more consumables move to X. Mid-teens is strong growth, and anything higher would be upside to the guide. Dhingra said there are no signs of deceleration.

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord Genuity (first question)

**Question:** Did the modest guide raise account for competition launching this summer, now that a rival has disclosed a $150 genome and attractive pricing for counting applications?

**Answer:** Thaysen said he is pleased to raise after one quarter. He repeated confidence in Illumina's competitive position and said the first-half competitive dynamic should not change much in the second half; expectations are built into the guide.

### Daniel Arias, Stifel

**Question:** What cost per gigabase or per genome will Illumina quote customers for the 35B flow cell versus the $150 benchmark?

**Answer:** Thaysen said opportunity remains large. Moving rare disease from exome to genome needs about 15x more sequencing intensity. Conversations with exome-heavy customers center on elasticity: aggressive pricing brings aggressive volume growth, and cost per gigabase keeps falling for customers. He said "we are not ready at this point to go out and talk about pricing" for the 35B; it will come closer to launch.

### Catherine Ramsey (Schulte), Baird

**Question:** What are you seeing in low- and mid-throughput placements?

**Answer:** Thaysen said the MiSeq i100 (low throughput, launched around end of 2024 or early 2025) had more than 1,000 placements in 2025 and momentum continues in 2026. Mid-throughput has been harder for longer: MiSeq i100 takes some of the low end, some customers move up to high throughput, and the middle layer is most sensitive to the macro environment, with many not in production mode and wanting flexibility. He expects it to grow better when research markets turn.

### Casey Woodring, JPMorgan

**Question:** Did you quantify the inflation headwind (memory chips, freight)? And how should NovaSeq X placements look in Q2 and the second half?

**Answer:** Thaysen spoke generally about operational discipline and a track record of offsetting cost challenges. Dhingra did not give a figure but said the headwind is within a range Illumina can act on. He confirmed Q2 placements similar to Q1 and a fairly robust second-half pipeline, so full-year placements should be reasonably above the initial range.

### Jack Meehan, Nephron Research

**Question:** Which clinical customers drive NovaSeq X demand (multicancer, MRD, therapy selection, women's health)?

**Answer:** Thaysen said "all of the above": broad interest, including large US centralized labs that report oncology momentum, plus rare disease and NIPT (noninvasive prenatal testing), across regions.

### Michael Ryskin, BofA

**Question:** Placements of 80 in Q1 and Q2 are well above the 50-60 per quarter norm, yet instrument revenue of $118M is below the roughly $125M-$130M expected. Is there discounting, and what is driving demand?

**Answer:** Thaysen said customers buy X to drive consumables, and at JPMorgan (January 2026) Illumina showed pull-through (consumables revenue per instrument) above $1.3 million per X, which he expects to hold or rise. Customers ordering 5, 10 or more get a volume discount, which he calls reasonable, but the focus is getting units out. Dhingra added that some of the roughly 83 units were placed under reagent-rental or lease models, which have different revenue recognition.

### Mason Carrico, Stephens

**Question:** Where will Illumina win in spatial given recent competitor announcements: rising tide, or distinct pockets?

**Answer:** Thaysen said spatial drew strong interest at AGBT. He sees it as a rising tide and praised 10x Genomics' launch ("I'm actually quite impressed of what [Serge] and the team is putting out there"), saying it raises interest overall. He said technologies differ and address somewhat different customer segments, with room for several players. Illumina developed its spatial approach with customers, and "I'm pretty sure we have a winner" at launch later in 2026.

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord Genuity (second question)

**Question:** The X share of consumables revenue was stable at 55% versus Q4, while X share of gigabase volume keeps rising toward 80-90%. What moderated in Q1, and should revenue share stay in the high 50s?

**Answer:** Thaysen said it is "a little bit dangerous" to read this figure precisely; it is a directional trend that varies by quarter. The key point is that clinical customers continue to transition well, with over 85% expected by end of 2026, at which point the transition is behind them.

## Items worth flagging

- Management gave no backlog dollar figure and no cost-per-genome or 35B pricing.
- The $150 genome and counting-application pricing came from an analyst's reference to a competitor, not from Illumina. The competitor is not named in the transcript.
- The Q2 operating margin of about 22% was lower than the Street expected, per the Wolfe analyst. The inflation headwind was not quantified.
- Slight inconsistency: Dhingra says "more than 80 placements" while his answer to BofA cites "83 units"; Thaysen separately cites 80+ and about 20 more than Q1 2025.
- Consumables share of Rest of World organic growth is 5% in the CFO's remarks and about 7% in the CEO's remarks; the CEO's figure appears to be a different basis (ex-China), and the transcript does not explain the gap.
