# Illumina Q3 2023 Earnings Call (first call under new CEO Jacob Thaysen): guidance cut, GRAIL special committee, flat 2024 outlook

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2023Q3_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2023-11-09 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Illumina's Q3 2023 earnings call; speakers Salli Schwartz (VP Investor Relations), Jacob Thaysen (CEO), Joydeep Goswami (CFO and Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer); analysts from Evercore ISI, TD Cowen, Leerink, Stifel, Bank of America, Morgan Stanley, UBS, Scotiabank, Canaccord, Baird, RBC, J.P. Morgan · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q3 2023 revenue of about $1.12 billion, flat year over year, and non-GAAP EPS of $0.33, and cut its full-year 2023 guidance because customers bought fewer NovaSeq X instruments and slowed NovaSeq 6000 consumables purchases. New CEO Jacob Thaysen, about six weeks into the job, set up a board special committee to decide how to divest GRAIL and said 2024 results will look "very similar" to 2023, with formal 2024 guidance only in February. Analysts focused on GRAIL's divestiture funding, whether the slowdown is macro weakness or a platform transition, China competition, and margins.

# Illumina Q3 2023 Earnings Call (November 9, 2023)

## Context and headline takeaways

This was Illumina's third-quarter 2023 earnings call, held on November 9, 2023. It was the first call led by Jacob Thaysen, who became CEO a little over six weeks earlier. The Seeking Alpha header shows consensus comparisons: non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS) of $0.33 beat by $0.19, and revenue of $1.12 billion (up 0.36% year over year) missed by $14.02 million. [Seeking Alpha summary, Q3 2023 call]

Management called the quarter "a disappointing result." Revenue was flat, the company cut its 2023 guidance on revenue and instruments and consumables, and it said 2024 would look much like 2023. It also described a new push to resolve the GRAIL situation. GRAIL is the cancer-screening company Illumina acquired, and the European Commission (EC) ordered Illumina to divest it.

Terms used throughout:
- **Non-GAAP**: results adjusted to exclude certain accounting items. Illumina's non-GAAP figures still include stock-based compensation.
- **Core Illumina**: the business excluding GRAIL.
- **Consumables**: the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every sequencing run.
- **NovaSeq X**: Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer, launched in 2023. It is replacing the older NovaSeq 6000 ("6K").
- **Basis points**: hundredths of a percentage point.

## Results

### Consolidated results (Illumina including GRAIL)

Joydeep Goswami (CFO) reviewed the numbers [Q3 2023 call]:

- **Revenue**: about $1.12 billion, flat year over year and up 1% in constant currency (excluding exchange-rate effects). It was down 5% from Q2 2023.
- **Cause of the sequential decline**: management correctly expected a sequential drop in high-throughput consumables revenue because of the NovaSeq X transition. However, Illumina "placed fewer NovaSeq X instruments than we expected in the quarter, as customers' purchase constraints led to lengthened sales cycles."
- **Non-GAAP net income**: $52 million, or $0.33 per diluted share, roughly flat year over year (Thaysen). This includes dilution from GRAIL's non-GAAP operating loss of $155 million for the quarter.
- **Why EPS beat internal expectations**: continued expense reduction and a higher gross margin than forecast, despite lower revenue.
- **GAAP net loss**: $754 million, or $4.77 per diluted share. It included $821 million of goodwill and intangible impairments (write-downs of acquisition-related asset values) tied to the GRAIL segment. Goswami attributed them to a decrease in Illumina's consolidated market capitalization and a higher discount rate used in the fair-value calculation for GRAIL.
- **Non-GAAP tax rate**: 39.7%, versus 43.2% in Q3 2022. Both quarters reflect the effect of R&D capitalization requirements (tax rules that force R&D costs to be deducted over several years). The decrease was mainly due to increased amortization of capitalized R&D.
- **Share count**: about 158 million non-GAAP weighted average diluted shares.

### Core Illumina results

Core Illumina revenue was $1.11 billion, flat year over year on both a reported and constant-currency basis. It absorbed about seven percentage points of anticipated drag from two sources, each about 3.5 points:
1. Lower COVID surveillance revenue plus the effect of sanctions on Russia.
2. A year-over-year reduction in China revenue.

COVID surveillance contributed about $4 million in Q3 2023, compared with $28 million in Q3 2022.

The table below summarizes the Core Illumina revenue lines, all for Q3 2023 versus Q3 2022.

| Line | Q3 2023 | Year-over-year change |
|---|---|---|
| Sequencing consumables | $695M | down 4% |
| Sequencing instruments | $179M | up 10% |
| Sequencing service and other | $142M | up 15% |

**Consumables.** The 4% decline was mainly driven by a 12% decline in sales to research customers. These customers are cutting NovaSeq 6000 consumables purchases before they have fully ramped activity on NovaSeq X. COVID, Russia, China and macroeconomic pressure on customers' purchasing power and project planning also hurt. Strength in clinical customers partly offset this: clinical sequencing consumables grew 10%, led by oncology and genetic disease testing.

**Sequencing activity.** Total gigabase output (the volume of DNA sequenced) on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew 5% from Q2 2023 and 29% year over year. Customers with a NovaSeq X installed, in both research and clinical, showed higher output growth than high-throughput customers without one, both quarter over quarter and year over year. Goswami called this data "directionally correlated with revenue over time."

**Instruments.** Revenue growth was driven mainly by NovaSeq X, which more than offset lower NovaSeq 6000 shipments. Growth in high-throughput instruments was partly offset by an expected decline in mid-throughput instruments. Causes cited were capital purchase and cash-flow constraints at customers globally, and local competition in China.

**NovaSeq X counts.** Illumina exited Q3 with more than 310 orders since launch. It shipped 97 NovaSeq X instruments in Q3, bringing the installed base to 273.

**Services and other.** The 15% growth was driven mainly by higher instrument service contract revenue on a growing installed base, plus more lab services revenue.

### Regional results (Core Illumina)

Goswami said all regions were affected by two issues: tighter funding and budget pressure, and the transition of high-throughput customers to NovaSeq X. The table shows each region's Q3 2023 revenue and year-over-year change.

| Region | Revenue | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Americas | $650M | up 10% |
| Europe | $260M | flat (up 1% constant currency) |
| Asia-Pacific excluding China (called "India" in the transcript; likely transcription error) | $98M | down 22% (down 19% constant currency) |
| Greater China | $98M | down 26% (down 25% constant currency) |

- **Americas**: growth came from NovaSeq X placements and clinical testing volume. Clinical sequencing consumables shipments grew more than 20%.
- **Europe**: consumables growth was driven by mid-teens growth in clinical and strength in mid-throughput consumables, offset by lower COVID surveillance and negative exchange rates. In instruments, high-throughput growth from NovaSeq X was more than offset by a mid-throughput decline.
- **The "India" line**: the 22% decline included an 11-percentage-point impact from Russia sanctions. It was also driven by softness in Japan from macroeconomic factors and the expected slowdown in COVID surveillance.
- **Greater China**: reflects continued macroeconomic and geopolitical challenges and local competition in mid-throughput.

### Core Illumina profitability

- **Gross margin**: 66% non-GAAP, down 290 basis points year over year. Causes: product mix; less fixed-cost leverage on lower manufacturing volumes; lower instrument margins; and higher field service and installation costs from the NovaSeq X launch, which Goswami called "typical in a launch year."
- **Operating expenses**: $481 million non-GAAP, down $33 million year over year and lower than expected thanks to continued expense reduction.
- **Operating margin**: 22.5%, versus 22.6% in Q3 2022. It was roughly flat despite lower gross margin because of cost management.

### GRAIL results

- **Revenue**: $21 million, up 110% year over year, driven mainly by adoption of Galleri (GRAIL's multi-cancer blood test).
- **Non-GAAP operating expenses**: $161 million, up $12 million year over year, from scaling commercial and R&D organizations.
- **Operating loss**: $155 million non-GAAP, as noted above.

### Cash flow and balance sheet

- Operating cash flow was $139 million, capital expenditures $45 million, and free cash flow $94 million.
- No stock was repurchased in the quarter.
- The quarter ended with about $933 million in cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments.
- In Q3, Illumina used $750 million in cash to repay the principal of convertible notes that matured in August 2023.

## Guidance for full-year 2023

Goswami presented a revised outlook. The table gives the new figures, with the prior-guidance comparison where the call gave one. The call did not state prior numeric ranges beyond those noted.

| Item | New 2023 guidance | Change from prior |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidated revenue | down 2% to 3% vs 2022 | Lowered (prior figure not stated) |
| Core Illumina revenue | down 3% to 4% | Lowered |
| GRAIL revenue | low end of $90M-$110M range | Narrowed to low end |
| Core sequencing instrument revenue | down 5% to 6% | Lowered, mainly on fewer NovaSeq X shipments |
| NovaSeq X shipments | 330 to 340 instruments | Lowered |
| Core sequencing consumables revenue | down 5% to 6% | Lowered |
| Core total sequencing revenue | down 3% to 4% | Lowered |
| Consolidated non-GAAP operating margin | 4% to 4.5% | Lowered |
| Core non-GAAP operating margin | 19% to 19.5% | Lowered |
| Non-GAAP tax rate | about 39% | Changed (discrete Q3 benefit from prior-year return adjustments) |
| Non-GAAP EPS | $0.60 to $0.70 | Updated |
| Diluted shares | about 159 million | |
| GRAIL operating loss dilution | about $660 million | Updated; GRAIL managing expenses to its revenue outlook |

Further detail:
- The revenue ranges include anticipated reductions of about 200 basis points from COVID surveillance, about 100 basis points from Russia sanctions, reductions in China, and a negative foreign-exchange effect.
- Instrument guidance reflects customers' capital and cash-flow constraints globally, plus China's decline. The cut was "primarily driven by our lower NovaSeq X shipment expectations."
- Consumables guidance reflects lower NovaSeq 6000 consumables as customers move to X, macro pressure on project planning and budgets, Russia, the COVID slowdown and China. The cut "primarily reflects a slower ramp in NovaSeq X consumables, in part due to our lower placement expectations, as well as the increasing impacts of macroeconomic constraints."
- Total sequencing revenue includes about $30 million of intercompany sales to GRAIL that are eliminated in consolidation.
- The operating margin cuts reflect lower revenue, partly offset by continued expense reduction.

## Prepared remarks by speaker

### Salli Schwartz (VP Investor Relations)

Schwartz reminded listeners that GRAIL "must be held and operated separately and independently from Illumina" under transitional measures ordered by the European Commission, which prohibited the GRAIL acquisition under the EU merger regulation. The results discussed include GRAIL.

### Jacob Thaysen (CEO)

**Background and view of the company.** Thaysen spent more than a decade at Agilent, running the Diagnostic and Genomics Group and then Agilent's largest business, the Life Science and Applied Markets Group. He spent his first weeks meeting employees and customers and found a capable, passionate team. He is especially passionate about genomics in healthcare and personalized medicine, particularly oncology. He said "Illumina will remain the key player, even as others enter the market," citing its infrastructure built over two and a half decades, its offerings that "set the global standard," and its commitment to innovation.

**Q3 assessment.** Revenue was about $1.12 billion, flat year over year and up 1% in constant currency. The macro environment remains challenging, with customers "increasingly cautious and constrained in their purchasing decisions." Tight expense management let Illumina deliver $0.33 non-GAAP EPS despite lower gross margin.

**Strategic review.** He is reexamining strategic initiatives and long-term targets for revenue growth and operating margin. New targets will be laid out "later next year."

**GRAIL.** A key priority is getting clarity on GRAIL.
- At his request, the Board established a special committee to expedite GRAIL decisions.
- Advisers have been retained and the company is preparing for sale and capital markets transactions.
- It expects to file a Form 10 (a registration filing used for spin-offs) confidentially with the SEC.
- Afterward, it will contact third parties as investment capital sources or potential purchasers, while appeals continue.
- The appeals are "not just about GRAIL." They provide flexibility for any GRAIL divestiture and for future transactions, and "will not impact our ability to move swiftly."

**Closing remarks.**
- Illumina will support customers through the difficult environment and position itself "for a return to accelerated growth on the other side."
- **2024 view.** With the caveat that 2023 is unfinished and the 2024 budget is not complete, "Our initial views is that 2024 results will look very similar to 2023." Management does not expect near-term macro improvement, and geopolitical issues have been persistent. Thaysen is encouraged by early NovaSeq X utilization signs and says the continued rollout positions Illumina for a consumables ramp when conditions improve. No 2024 guidance will be given before the Q4 call in February.
- **Priorities:**
  1. Drive the top line through continuous placements of NovaSeq X and all instruments, laying groundwork for consumables demand.
  2. Keep innovating on customer priorities, including automation and sample-to-answer solutions, while managing R&D with discipline. He cited the recent launch of the 25B flow cell kit, "highly anticipated by our customers," which "will unleash the full power of the NovaSeq X."
  3. Pursue operational excellence across geographies, functions and processes. Earlier in 2023 Illumina announced a plan to cut annualized run-rate expenses. It has reduced them by about $175 million, ahead of the original projection of more than $100 million. The savings support flexibility to invest in high-growth areas and support margins.

## Question-and-answer session

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI

**Question.** The EC's divestiture order asked for 2.5 years of cash support for GRAIL. Given GRAIL's current spending, does that mean about $1.75 billion? Who is on the new special committee, what is its focus, and what has changed?

**Answer (Thaysen).** He wanted clarity on GRAIL and board support for swift decisions. The committee has three board members plus himself, who chairs it. It works with management to walk through all elements of the divestiture order. Key decisions concern the path: "a trade sale, the spin, the split, without a sponsor," with many considerations.

**Answer (Goswami).** The number behind "2.5 years of cash support" is still being worked out with the EC, so he could not disclose it. Illumina does not have to fund it alone. The divestiture order gives flexibility: even in a spin or split, a sponsor could support all or part of the requirement, or Illumina could raise the money in the markets. "Stay tuned."

### Dan Brennan, TD Cowen

**Question.** (a) What is Thaysen's view of the NGS (next-generation sequencing) market's growth characteristics, given price cuts and questions about demand elasticity? (b) Any color on NovaSeq X orders or backlog? (c) Illumina has had a series of guidance reductions, longer than peers. What can change to better set guidance?

**Answer (Thaysen).** The core business and NGS market have tremendous opportunity, and the industry, including Illumina, is in a tough period. He does not yet have a final view on appropriate growth rates or commitments and will return "later in '24." He believes the overall market is very healthy and is excited by Illumina's innovation engine and roadmaps. On guidance, he wants to be prudent given no short-term change in the economic environment, which is why he shared an early 2024 view.

**Answer (Goswami).** Illumina committed to giving its read on macroeconomics and kept that commitment; it may have been "a little bit canary in the coal mine" in signaling the slowdown earlier than others. The longer period of weak growth was worsened by the platform transition: customers moving from 6000 to X run down inventory and then wait to fully ramp X, which "will balance out over time." Demand for X remains strong. He pointed to interest in the 25B at the ASHG conference (the transcript says "ASHE"), data from the 25B and software improvements. The backlog question was not answered directly in the response.

### Puneet Souda, Leerink Partners

**Question.** (a) With the 25B launch, how will the transition play out over the next couple of months? The 25B brings a price uptick, but not all customers can fill the flow cell. (b) How does Thaysen see Illumina's long-term position in China against a larger local competitor? (c) A diagnostic customer in NIPT (prenatal testing) and MRD (minimal residual disease cancer monitoring) said it is validating different platforms. What is the view on competition?

**Answer (Thaysen).** He has experience running businesses in China. Illumina is unusual among life-science tools companies in having a Chinese competitor with "at least good enough" sequencing. China remains extremely important; Illumina is working on an "in China for China" strategy and is committed to the market. Many Chinese customers prefer Illumina for quality and as the number one brand. On competition generally, Illumina's strong brand, installed base (almost all papers rely on its technology) and product roadmap keep it competitive. "I'm here, I'm ready to fight for it."

**Answer (Goswami).** Outside China, competition and share trends have been "very much according to expectations," with a little share decline from new entrants. On the 25B, customers with an X are growing sequencing output faster than those without, which he called an encouraging sign that X is spurring more activity, though it is early. The 25B should spur more capacity and experiments; customers wanting very large single-cell experiments are excited. Running full versus partial flow cells involves a complex interaction that leads to higher prices per gigabase until flow cells are fully loaded. He expects dynamics similar to the NovaSeq 6000 and S4 flow cells.

**Addition (Thaysen).** Clinical customers he has met are excited that the new flow cell opens new assays and products, and many are "rushing to be first to market," though validation takes time.

### Dan Arias, Stifel

**Question.** What is the special committee's timeline for a decision, and when will the NHS-Galleri study read out?

**Answer (Thaysen).** The team is working as quickly as possible. "I'm as frustrated as all of you." He would not commit to a timeline. He reported that morning's news: the ECGA (likely the EU General Court or Court of Justice; transcript text) set an oral hearing for mid-December. The appeals matter for GRAIL and equally for flexibility on future transactions.

**Answer (Goswami).** As GRAIL has stated, the final NHS readout is expected at some point in 2024, with a preliminary readout before that.

### Michael Ryskin, Bank of America

**Question.** Illumina cites macro headwinds (China, funding, budgets) and says X is hurting consumables, but orders only rose from about 260 to 310 between Q2 and Q3 (up 50) while 97 units were placed. If 6000 usage were falling due to waiting for X, orders should be growing more. How can management be confident this is a transition and not weaker underlying demand?

**Answer (Thaysen).** He sees demand: gigabase growth is up, activity is high, and the long-term outlook is healthy. Everyone expected the second half to improve when guiding last year, and it did not, so Illumina is now more prudent.

**Answer (Goswami).** There is a macro element, but the transition adds to it. Comparing high-throughput customers who bought an X with those who did not shows the 6K consumables slowdown "is much more pronounced" at X buyers. That serves as a control separating transition effects from macro. Early adopters tend to be higher-output users; X consumables should ramp, especially after 25B launch, but it will take a few months after workflow validation.

### Tejas Savant, Morgan Stanley

**Question.** (a) With an ECJ (European Court of Justice) decision expected in mid-2024, what can the committee do before then? (b) Is monetizing GRAIL's data being discussed at board level? (c) Is the Core Illumina leadership team settled, or could significant changes come?

**Answer (Thaysen).** The committee exists because of the European divestiture order; appeals run in parallel and nothing prevents moving quickly within the framework. After the Form 10 is filed, Illumina will talk to potential acquirers and explore a spin. On leadership, he will conduct a comprehensive business review over the coming period, has been in the role 40 days, and is impressed by the team. He will update in 2024.

**Answer (Goswami).** Monetizing GRAIL data is one option the committee and team will consider, depending on the chosen divestiture option and discussions with the EC.

### Dan Leonard, UBS

**Question.** With little X backlog left, demand looks more hand-to-mouth. What does the pipeline look like, and how much has the sales cycle lengthened?

**Answer (Thaysen).** The sales cycle has "extended quite a lot," not only for the decision but also because of more scrutiny at every level, an industry-wide pattern.

**Answer (Goswami).** Interest in X remains strong and the 25B perked it up further. The pipeline "continues to have hundreds of opportunities"; it is not declining and keeps growing. The challenge is converting the pipeline to orders as quickly as imagined, which will continue for a while until macro conditions return. Demand is not moving to another technology.

**Evidence of momentum.** Thaysen said Illumina received a 10-instrument NovaSeq X order from one of its biggest customers in the last few days. Goswami called it a fleet-expansion order from a customer with X experience that is "doubling down."

### Sung Ji Nam, Scotiabank

**Question.** Why is GRAIL revenue guided to the low end of its range?

**Answer (Thaysen).** Two factors: challenges with PDX (pharma services) revenue this year, and Galleri sales growing nicely but below initial expectations. Goswami added growth is still healthy; Thaysen said GRAIL growth is "100%-ish."

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord

**Question.** (a) If GRAIL is divested through a capital markets deal needing 2.5 years of cash, what are the options for providing that capital other than equity or debt? (b) Does an initial 2024 view similar to 2023 mean flat revenue for a third year? Could placements grow in 2024?

**Answer (Thaysen).** Full guidance will come on the Q4 2023 call in February. If conditions change during 2024, momentum would improve. It is too early for details because 2023 and the 2024 budget are unfinished. The pipeline for X is strong, sales cycles are longer than expected, and "you should actually see that the consumables are starting to pick up in 2024."

**Answer (Goswami).** Several options exist for the 2.5-year funding:
- A sponsored spin, where a sponsor could put in some or all of the required money.
- A split via a capital markets transaction, including raising money through a GRAIL IPO.
These depend on market conditions and interest in GRAIL from private placement.

### Catherine Schulte, Baird

**Question.** If 2024 results look like 2023, will Core Illumina operating margins also be similar, or is there room for improvement given cost reductions?

**Answer (Thaysen).** Illumina has "a wonderful model" with strong gross margins; growth fuels the bottom line, but in a flat environment it is harder. Currently Illumina expects to be "flat both on top line and on the bottom line."

**Answer (Goswami).** Gross margin should improve in 2024, with more consumables in the mix. But a substantial portion of the 2023 cost reductions is already recognized this year, so it will not be incremental. Offsetting those positives: variable compensation will return (executives were not paid stock-based or variable compensation in 2023 because performance was "not up to par"), plus merit increases and inflation.

**Addition (Thaysen).** He sees nothing changing the thesis of Illumina returning to historical margins. Historically the logic was that margin comes from growth; growth is needed, but operational excellence will also be a focus.

### Conor McNamara, RBC Capital Markets

**Question.** Flat 2024 implies roughly 5% annual Core growth from 2019 to 2024, in line with the life science tools market, despite Illumina spending about 20% of sales on R&D. Should investors see this as an EBIT margin expansion story with R&D brought down toward peers, or can Illumina grow above historic tools-market rates?

**Answer (Thaysen).** He wants to be careful 40 days in. He believes Illumina has better growth opportunity than many other life science tools companies, and will give more insight later in 2024. He said Illumina is not currently at the growth level of many peers. It is too early for clear guidance.

### Rachel Vatnsdal, J.P. Morgan

**Question.** Implied Q4 Core Illumina margin steps down, even though lower placements should skew mix toward consumables. Are there one-time items or other puts and takes?

**Answer (Goswami).** Operating margin falls because revenue steps down from Q3 to Q4. Gross margin is hit by three things:
1. Lower volume means less absorption of fixed costs.
2. Although mix shifts from instruments toward consumables, the transition reduces NovaSeq 6000 consumables, which are highly profitable.
3. Some strategic deal revenue arrives in Q4 versus Q3, which pulls down gross margin.

On operating expense, cost actions are helping, but certain R&D investments moved from Q3 into Q4, raising Q4 operating expense.

## Closing remarks

Thaysen closed by saying Illumina has a "great foundation" in its infrastructure and markets, and a "tremendous opportunity to create value for our customers and our partners worldwide, and of course for our shareholders."

## Items to note for later cross-checking

- The transcript header labels the call "Q4 2023" in one place, but the event is Q3 2023.
- The transcript contains apparent errors: "India" for Asia-Pacific, "ASHE" for the ASHG conference, "ECGA" for the EU court, "25D" for 25B, "zeroables" (likely "consumables"), and "Sally" for Salli.
- Prior numeric 2023 guidance ranges were not stated on the call, only that the new figures are lower. GRAIL's prior range ($90M-$110M) was given.
- Management claims, not independent facts: the attribution of the 6K slowdown to the X transition, the view that competition outside China is on expectation, and the 2024 "similar to 2023" outlook.
