# Illumina Q3 2024 Earnings Call (Core Illumina results, lowered revenue guidance, raised margin and EPS guidance)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2024Q3_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2024-11-04 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Illumina's Q3 2024 earnings call; speakers Jacob Thaysen (CEO), Ankur Dhingra (CFO), Salli Schwartz (VP Investor Relations); analysts from Wolfe, Leerink, Evercore ISI, TD Cowen, Jefferies, Piper Sandler, RBC, Stifel, Citi, Guggenheim, Scotiabank, Stephens, UBS · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q3 2024 revenue of $1.08 billion (down 2% year over year), with consumables up 7% and instruments down 42%, while margins and earnings beat expectations (non-GAAP operating margin 22.6%, EPS $1.14). Management cut 2024 revenue guidance to roughly -3% (Q4 about $1.07 billion) because instrument deal closings were more variable, but raised operating margin guidance to 21%-21.5% and EPS guidance to $4.05-$4.15. Most analyst questions probed the "clinical cliff" worry (clinical customers moving to the cheaper NovaSeq X), the 2025 outlook, mid-throughput competition, and the cost-savings plan.

# Illumina Q3 2024 Earnings Call: Detailed Notes

All remarks are from the Q3 2024 earnings call held on 2024-11-04 [Q3 2024 call]. Unless noted, all figures and guidance are for "Core Illumina," meaning Illumina excluding GRAIL, the cancer-screening business Illumina divested in June 2024. Year-over-year (Y/Y) compares with Q3 2023; sequential compares with Q2 2024. "Non-GAAP" figures are adjusted results that exclude certain items (they do include stock-based compensation, per the IR introduction).

The Seeking Alpha header summarizes the quarter as: EPS of $1.14, beating consensus by $0.26, and revenue of $1.08 billion, down 3.49% Y/Y (the header's revenue-beat figure appears garbled and is not used here).

Other guidance points from Ankur Dhingra:
- Management said it was "tempering our revenue expectations for year-end business." The macro environment "remains constrained and does not support any uptick in purchasing behavior through the end of the year," though utilization and pull-through (consumables use on installed instruments) remain strong.
- MiSeq i100 (the new low-throughput sequencer) is in early access and will add minimal Q4 revenue. The existing low-throughput business will likely decline in Q4 as customers wait for the new instrument.
- Second-half 2024 NovaSeq X shipments are still expected to exceed first-half 2024 shipments.
- Ankur said he was "disappointed in our lower revenue guidance."
- For 2025, Jacob Thaysen said Illumina is "looking forward to returning to growth," but he wants to finish Q4 before giving specific guidance. Guidance would come around the JPMorgan healthcare conference in January, with Illumina's usual full-year placement update then.

### Long-term targets (from the August 2024 Strategy Update, reaffirmed)

- High single-digit revenue growth by 2027.
- 500 basis points (5 percentage points) of operating margin expansion by 2027, which Ankur said he views as working off a base of 21%.
- Double-digit to teens annual EPS growth over three years.
- Revenue growth is expected to step from negative in 2024 to positive in 2025 [Thaysen, in response to Leerink].

## 2. Prepared remarks

### Jacob Thaysen, CEO

**Overall message.** Thaysen described "another quarter of strong financial performance": margins and earnings are expanding, NovaSeq X utilization is growing, and new sequencing innovations are coming. He said the near-term macro environment "has been persistently constrained," even though funding for customers "remain[s] healthy."

**Installed base.** Illumina placed 58 NovaSeq X Plus instruments in the quarter, bringing the total installed base to 527. About 40% of that base has shipped to clinical customers. The X-Series (the NovaSeq X family, Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencers) passed $1 billion in cumulative revenue.

**Capital allocation.** The company began executing the capital allocation plan from its August Strategy Update (detailed below under the CFO's remarks).

**Priority 1: deeper customer collaboration.** Thaysen said he met academic core lab directors (shared university sequencing labs) with long histories using Illumina. He said "my observation was that we needed to do better" in how Illumina interacts with customers, and he is now hearing that customers see Illumina "present itself in a new way." Illumina is also giving customers a clear view of its innovation pipeline and collecting their early input.

**Priority 2: continuous innovation.**
- *MiSeq i100*: A newly launched low-throughput benchtop sequencer aimed at smaller projects with faster turnaround. Features cited: room-temperature shipping and storage of reagents (sequencing on demand with no freezer storage), shorter run times, and 18 proven end-to-end workflows. Customers called it "a game changer." Early-access units begin shipping in late Q4, driven by strong demand in the days after the announcement. Illumina intends to use these technologies across its portfolio over time.
- *Constellation mapped read technology and comprehensive whole genome solution*: Announced at the Strategy Update, to launch over the next 12 to 18 months. It adds extra layers of genomic information beyond what standard SBS chemistry (sequencing by synthesis, Illumina's core method) yields. Library prep (preparing DNA for sequencing) is performed directly on the flow cell (the consumable chip where sequencing happens), cutting hands-on time from multiple hours to a few minutes.
- *5-base genome* (the transcript renders this as "five-phase genome"): Delivers variant and epigenetic (methylation) information from a single library prep, so customers get methylation data in every run. Methylation matters for understanding cancer, obesity and infectious diseases. Both technologies are still in development, with customer feedback shaping them. More detail and early-access results were to be shown at the ASHG genetics conference later that week.
- *NovaSeq X Series near-term*: On track to begin shipping a single-flow-cell NovaSeq X by year-end, upgradable to the NovaSeq X Plus. In Q4, Illumina will introduce 100-cycle and 200-cycle 25B flow cells designed for high-output counting applications such as single-cell and proteomics.

**Priority 3: operational excellence.** The goal is a foundation for long-term success "regardless of the top line," through productivity, optimized investment spending and smart capital allocation. He said the company has built a culture in which every employee is driving efficiencies.

**Closing remarks.** Reflecting on his first year, Thaysen said Illumina refocused on its core business, launched a new strategy, reset the leadership team, and made structural changes for a customer-first orientation. Operational excellence results are "beginning to show in our financials," and this is "a good start to our multi-year transformation journey." He is "encouraged and feel[s] confident" about returning to high single-digit growth by 2027.

### Ankur Dhingra, CFO

**Consumables and the NovaSeq X transition.**
- Sequencing consumables grew 7% Y/Y to $741M, driven by continued strong uptake of X consumables.
- The transition moved faster than forecast. At the end of Q3, more than 55% of high-throughput gigabases (GB, the unit of sequencing data) sequenced and more than 35% of high-throughput consumables revenue were on the NovaSeq X Series.
- The shift from the older NovaSeq 6000 ("6K") to the X accelerated, including in clinical: about 40% of high-throughput clinical gigabases were on the X.
- As legacy assays (tests) move to the X, Illumina sees higher clinical volumes and greater clinical adoption of the 25B flow cell.
- Quarterly pace will vary with customer choices, but he still believes almost half of high-throughput consumables revenue could be on the X by mid-2025.
- Total GB output on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments is growing more than 40% Y/Y, with robust growth from both clinical and research customers.

**Instruments.** Instrument revenue of $104M fell 42% Y/Y, slightly behind expectations, for two reasons: (1) fewer NovaSeq X placements than the significant pre-order launch shipments of Q3 2023, and (2) lower mid-throughput shipments as capital and cash-flow constraints moderate purchasing globally.

**Services and other.** Revenue of $150M rose 6% Y/Y on higher revenue from strategic partnerships and higher service-contract revenue on a growing installed base.

**Gross margin.** Non-GAAP gross margin of 70.5% rose 450 basis points Y/Y, driven mainly by operational-excellence initiatives that improved productivity and cut costs. A more favorable mix toward consumables (higher-margin) accounted for roughly half of the improvement. Mix will vary quarter to quarter, but he said the productivity gains are "sustainable."

**Operating expenses and margin.** Non-GAAP opex of $517M was roughly flat to Q2 and includes added headcount and costs from the Fluent BioSciences acquisition. Operating margin of 22.6% was well above the roughly 20% guided, helped by gross margin and expense discipline. Several actions are in play to reprioritize and reduce expenses.

**Debt.** In the quarter Illumina issued $500M of debt at a 4.65% coupon and, with cash on hand, fully redeemed its high-cost $750M delayed-draw term loan, reducing debt and lowering the interest rate.

**Cash flow and capital deployment.**
- Acquired Fluent BioSciences, adding instrument-free single-cell technology (single-cell analysis examines individual cells rather than bulk samples). He is excited about enabling very large single-cell experiments.
- After board authorization earlier in the quarter, Illumina repurchased 770,000 shares for $98M at an average price of $127.71.
- Deleveraged as described above.

## 3. Question-and-answer session

### Doug Schenkel, Wolfe Research

*Question:* Revenue is guided down slightly sequentially; what drops, and does sequencing consumable revenue grow sequentially? On clinical customers, how much visibility does Illumina have into when existing assays move from legacy instruments to the X? He wanted help with concerns about a "clinical cliff" for consumables revenue in 2025.

*Answer, Thaysen:* "I've heard this clinical cliff a few times, and I don't think that's the way we look at the business right now." Momentum toward the X is strong and consistent with the Strategy Day plan. The Q4 tweak reflects that, despite a healthy instrument funnel, Illumina is not seeing the normal end-of-year buying activity, while consumables revenue rose nicely. Illumina spends a lot of time with large academic and clinical customers on their projects, its funnel and its R&D pipeline, so it has a good understanding of their plans. Customers rarely move all of their 6K work to the X in a few months; it goes assay by assay and usually takes a year to 18 months. Clinical accounts are already above 40% of volume on the X.

*Answer, Dhingra:* The Q4 guide assumes a typical seasonal decline in consumables from Q3 to Q4 because of holidays, consistent with the last few years' pattern. It reflects "nothing underlying from a transition perspective or a clinical transition... simple seasonality."

### Puneet Souda, Leerink Partners

*Question:* Did the mid-throughput decline come mostly from China, or from the U.S. and Europe with more competition from a U.S.-based mid-throughput competitor? What are the considerations for 2025, given clinical customers buy new instruments (revenue) but also get lower costs (less revenue)?

*Answer, Thaysen:* Competition is intense worldwide, especially in mid-throughput. He takes it seriously but welcomes it: it "keeps us on the toes" and makes Illumina "more agile." On China, he is encouraged. A new China leader (referred to as Jenny) arrived almost six months earlier and has reset and reconfigured the organization, reset relationships with partners and revisited pricing strategy. China revenue is now flat sequentially, "a change in pattern," though "too early to call it up." On mid-throughput, he said macroeconomic pressure remains the majority of the issue: small biotech companies that used to buy mid-throughput instruments are outsourcing sequencing because of financial constraints, a trend core lab directors confirmed. For 2025 he wants to see the end of the year first, but Illumina remains committed to high single-digit growth by 2027 and expects to step from negative growth in 2024 to positive growth in 2025.

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI

*Question:* Was price per GB in line with the -20% to -25% expected at the Analyst Day? Is the 500 basis points of margin expansion off the updated 21%-21.5% base, or should it be readjusted?

*Answer, Thaysen:* Pleased that Illumina is delivering on gross margin, operating margin and EPS even in tough times; this is a "very strong proof point" of commitment to the 500 basis points.

*Answer, Dhingra:* Price impact was within the model range but toward the higher end, "closer towards 25 rather than 20," because the 6K-to-X transition accelerated. On margin, he thinks of 21% as the base for the 500 basis points. The expansion rests on lifting gross margin into the "high 60s to 70% range"; there is still opportunity to take cost out of cost of goods sold (COGS) to get structurally closer to 70%. This quarter's richer mix helped, and several operating-expense levers remain.

### Dan Brennan, TD Cowen

*Question 1:* How did NGS consumable growth split between research and clinical customers, and versus expectations?
*Answer, Dhingra:* Consumables grew overall (he first said 6%, then corrected to 7%); both research and clinical "grew nicely," with no dramatic shift between them.

*Question 2:* What is assumed for mid-throughput in the full-year guide, since placements were light?
*Answer, Thaysen:* Illumina is not providing mid-throughput placement numbers now; it will update on the full year at JPMorgan, as usual.

*Question 3:* Is it fair to expect X placements in 2025 to start down Y/Y versus 2024?
*Answer, Thaysen:* Illumina will guide when ready, at the beginning of the year.

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies

*Question:* How should we think about the pricing headwind as clinical customers move to the X (lower cost per GB), and do multiyear contracts that oncology customers signed around GRAIL break with the transition?

*Answer, Thaysen:* The X offers customers a lower price point and better workflows, combined with Dragen compute (the transcript renders this as "Dragon") and interpretation power. Clinical customers must do significant up-front validation, and sometimes the validation cost outweighs the savings of moving an existing assay, so they may keep it on the old platform. Encouragingly, many assays moving over are bigger panels or deeper sequencing, "so the elasticity is still working out" (lower prices drive more volume). More than 40% of clinical volume is on the X and he expects that to grow. He rejected the cliff framing: "That's not how we see it. That's not how we speak to our customers." It is a staged transition following the trajectory of past quarters. On contracts: the multiyear arrangements are what Illumina calls the open offer letter; they are not instrument specific and continue with the X.

*Answer, Dhingra:* In his two quarters at the company, elasticity and higher throughput have played out each time a customer transitions. This quarter saw a little acceleration, solid GB growth and a higher price impact, yet consumables still grew at a high single-digit rate because over half of volume is already on the X, so GB growth increasingly converts to revenue.

### David Westenberg, Piper Sandler

*Question:* Can management conceptualize new assays that show higher GB usage and flow cells translating into net positive revenue? On Q4 seasonality (Q4 has been lower than Q3 in only one past year), what budget-flush dynamics are missing, given low-throughput is a small share of capital sales?

*Answer, Thaysen:* Customers are making bigger assays: oncology customers adding markers, including methylation markers, which need substantially deeper sequencing. Volume is rising strongly in both clinical and academic segments, and most volume is on the X, so even at a lower price the elasticity works for both. Few customers simply shift while keeping volume flat.

*Answer, Dhingra:* Sequentially, instruments in the guide are still up versus Q3; the Q3-to-Q4 decline he referred to is for consumables, because fewer working days mean potentially less testing and more inventory management. X consumables still have a relatively shorter shelf life than 6K consumables, so customers may manage inventory accordingly at year-end.

### Conor McNamara, RBC Capital Markets

*Question:* The guidance change is about $20M-$25M, mostly in Q4. Is it all instruments, and where (low or mid throughput)?

*Answer, Dhingra:* The size is about right relative to the midpoint. Consumables were very good, but instrument deal-closing pace has been "more variable of late," so the year-end outcome is hard to pin down. Illumina now sees landing closer to the lower end of its prior range rather than the midpoint, approximately the $10.70 billion-ish mark (as transcribed; this appears to refer to roughly $4.3B-scale full-year revenue guidance being mis-transcribed, and is flagged as unclear). For modeling, he suggested taking the cut roughly equally (about a third each) from instruments, consumables and services, since the cause is deal-closure velocity rather than weakness in one area. Services is included partly because certain companion diagnostic (CDx) deals are affected: the TSO Comprehensive assay was recently FDA-approved, and milestones tied to several CDx agreements are being pushed into 2025.

### Dan Arias, Stifel

*Question:* Management has cited macro as a placement headwind all year, and nothing obviously changes in 2025. What makes it comfortable that the headwind abates, and what should we watch?

*Answer, Thaysen:* He "can't wait for the day that none of us have to talk about macro headwinds." Consumables growth is the early indicator of a turnaround. Looking a few years out is easier than calling any quarter or beyond. He sees "green shoots": better consumables, and MiSeq i100 momentum. He added that the U.S. election the next day "is going to change everything again," so Illumina is taking it quarter by quarter.

*Answer, Dhingra:* Consumables are the right leading indicator, and the thesis that they return to growth and lay a foundation is playing out in the second half. Instruments still have to play out.

### Patrick Donnelly, Citi

*Question:* On the cost-savings programs (the $100M this year and the incremental $200M going forward), how is pacing, what is the 2025 impact, and what offsets or headwinds exist?

*Answer, Thaysen:* The results reflect an intentional company-wide focus on operational excellence since he arrived, covering commercial, R&D and operations. He said this "is what good companies do" and won't change when the top line improves.

*Answer, Dhingra:* Teams are now generating productivity ideas bottom-up. He feels good about the $100M of 2024 savings. Even at the guided operating margin, 2024 shows a 3% revenue decline with substantial margin expansion, now weighted more toward gross margin, and Illumina has offset a good part of inflationary pressures. For the $200M and the 500 basis points, cost removal will target both COGS and opex through global capability centers, shipping-line changes, consolidating some manufacturing lines into specific sites, and reviewing R&D programs. Illumina will likely reinvest somewhat more in sales and marketing. Headwinds beyond usual annual merit and inflation pressure: none that he sees.

### Subha Nambi, Guggenheim

*Question:* Was anything abnormal in the nearly $300M of free cash flow, are there enough levers to sustain it, and would Illumina consider a more aggressive buyback?

*Answer, Thaysen:* Pleased with cash generation after refocusing on the core. Capital allocation priorities are stock repurchases and M&A, and both began this quarter (Fluent acquisition finalized; buybacks started).

*Answer, Dhingra:* Nothing material unusual. Net-income-to-FCF conversion was close to 150% this quarter and is expected to stay well above 100% near term. Over three to four years conversion should narrow a bit as expense controls, lower capex, and lower depreciation and stock-based compensation reduce its drivers, but it should remain well above 100%. Per the Strategy Day plan, the focus is revenue growth; buy back shares at least enough to offset dilution and keep share count constant, then be opportunistic. M&A will stay on tuck-ins that are revenue-accretive. The balance sheet will be managed conservatively; no bonds come due in the short term.

### Sung Ji Nam, Scotiabank

*Question:* What explains Europe's steady growth versus the Americas?

*Answer, Thaysen:* Pleased with Europe's consistent results under tough conditions and expects that to continue. In the U.S., mid-throughput competitive pressure is higher and Illumina placed substantially more X instruments last year, so the compare is tougher. Compares should ease for the U.S. going forward.

### Ben Mee (on behalf of Mason Carrico), Stephens

*Question:* How does owning Fluent change the single-cell opportunity: expanding the market or taking share?

*Answer, Thaysen:* Excited; it enables a strong workflow. With no capital outlay needed up front, starting single-cell experiments is easy, and low cost per cell at very high volume allows huge experiments, so Fluent helps "expand the market opportunity for all of us." Illumina remains committed to its current single-cell partners. He is "very bullish" and sees single cell as a cornerstone of research; whether customers choose Fluent or a partner, Illumina will ensure their success.

### Dan Leonard, UBS

*Question:* What is the growth trend in low throughput and the potential MiSeq i100 impact? Has the XLEAP launch on the NextSeq affected mid-throughput consumables?

*Answer, Thaysen:* The original MiSeq launched over 10 years ago; the i100 should be the best solution in its segment, easy to use with no freezer storage. Some customers who might have bought a mid-throughput instrument may choose the i100. Low-throughput performance had weakened because the portfolio needed an update; he expects a pickup over the next 12 to 18 months, little effect in Q4, with impact starting in 2025. On XLEAP chemistry (the new chemistry for NextSeq 1000/2000), more than 60% of NextSeq 1K/2K users have downloaded the software and started using it. Illumina will stay committed to being competitive in mid-throughput.

*Answer, Dhingra:* Low-throughput instruments are less than or about 1% of Illumina's revenue, so while interest is substantial and creates additional customer call points, it will not move total revenue much.

## 4. Key themes and risks

- **Guidance split:** Revenue guidance was lowered by management because of variable instrument deal closings and macro constraints, while margin and EPS guidance rose on cost discipline [Q3 2024 call].
- **Clinical cliff debate:** Several analysts (Wolfe, Jefferies, Piper) pressed this. Management consistently denied a cliff, citing assay-by-assay migrations taking 12-18 months, elasticity from bigger or deeper assays, and contracts that carry over to the X.
- **Price versus volume:** Price per GB fell near the high end of the -20% to -25% model range, but GB output grew over 40% and consumables still rose 7%.
- **Competition and macro:** Mid-throughput faces competition (especially in the U.S.) and customer outsourcing; China was down 23% Y/Y but flat sequentially.
- **Items management did not give:** 2025 guidance, mid-throughput placement counts, 2025 X placement direction.
