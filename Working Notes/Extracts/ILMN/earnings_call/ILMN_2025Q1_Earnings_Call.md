# Illumina Q1 2025 Earnings Call (May 8, 2025): guidance cut on China export restrictions, US research funding pressure and tariffs

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2025Q1_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2025-05-08 · Seeking Alpha transcript; speakers Brian Blanchett (Interim Head of IR), Jacob Thaysen (CEO), Ankur Dhingra (CFO); analysts from Wolfe, Piper Sandler, Nephron, Evercore, Jefferies, RBC, Bank of America, Canaccord and JPMorgan · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q1 2025 revenue of $1.04 billion (roughly flat in constant currency) and non-GAAP EPS of $0.97, at the high end of its guidance range. It cut full-year 2025 guidance to revenue of $4.18-4.26 billion and EPS of $4.20-4.30, citing China instrument export restrictions, weaker US academic and government research spending, and about $85 million of gross tariff costs. It began guiding Greater China separately from the rest of the world. Management stressed strong clinical demand, continued NovaSeq X adoption, a $100 million cost reduction program, and a competitor technology preview that has not yet changed customer behavior.

# Illumina Q1 2025 Earnings Call, May 8, 2025

## Overview and key takeaways

This call covered Illumina's first-quarter 2025 results and a revision of its full-year 2025 outlook. Illumina makes DNA sequencing instruments and sells the consumables (the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run) that run on them. Management said it delivered Q1 revenue and earnings per share (EPS) at the upper end of its guidance range, but lowered the full-year outlook because of three outside pressures: China's restriction on Illumina's instrument exports, uncertainty over US research funding, and new US import tariffs. [Q1 2025 call, Thaysen]

Management framed these as "transitory challenges" that do not change its long-term strategy. It reiterated its goal of high single-digit revenue growth and 500 basis points (5 percentage points) of operating margin expansion by 2027, now stated excluding Greater China. [Q1 2025 call, Thaysen]

Reported results versus the market (from the Seeking Alpha header, not from management): EPS of $0.97 beat consensus by $0.03, and revenue of $1.04 billion (down 3.25% year over year per that header) beat by $4.10 million. Management's own as-reported figure was a 1.4% decline (see below).

## Results and guidance

Why gross margin was lower than expected [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]:
- A higher mix of instrument sales, which carry lower margins than consumables.
- A rollout of software upgrades for high-throughput instruments, which improves their performance. As part of the upgrade, Illumina is pulling forward routine instrument servicing, which cost more than assumed. The vast majority of upgrades should be done by Q2.
- Manufacturing cost actions continue to make good progress.

### Consumables, research caution and ordering

In the second half of Q1, as uncertainty grew around National Institutes of Health (NIH) and other research funding, customers (especially in research and academia) became "slightly more conservative" in consumable purchases. Illumina estimates this cut consumables growth by about 1 point year over year. Sequencing activity on connected instruments stayed strong. [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]

Q1 is seasonally strong for ordering, including long-range purchase commitments, which build backlog (reported as "performance obligations" in the 10-Q). This year's ordering was stronger than in the last couple of years, which Dhingra called an encouraging sign. In Q&A he added that performance obligations were up double digits year over year. [Q1 2025 call, Dhingra]

### NovaSeq X transition (the NovaSeq X is Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer)

- Over 60 NovaSeq X placements in Q1, following more than 90 in Q4 2024; management said this exceeded expectations. [Thaysen]
- About 60% of X units placed in Q1 went to clinical customers. [Dhingra]
- About 68% of high-throughput gigabases (units of sequencing output) shipped and about 43% of high-throughput consumables revenue were on the X series. [Dhingra]
- More than 80% of gigabases shipped to research customers were on the X, and over 50% of clinical volumes were now on X. [Dhingra]
- Reaffirmed framework: in the second half of 2025, about 50% of high-throughput revenue and about 75% of gigabases shipped will be on the X. [Dhingra]
- Thaysen said X and overall high-throughput consumables rose sequentially, showing the driver is gaining traction and "demonstrate[s] elasticity" (lower cost per unit driving more volume).
- Dhingra explained that as the mix of older 6K (NovaSeq 6000) consumables shrinks, the price effect of the lower-priced X fades, and a larger part of gigabase growth turns into revenue growth.
- Total sequencing output on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30% year over year, with robust growth from both clinical and research customers. [Dhingra]

### Cost actions

- In March 2025, Illumina launched a global incremental $100 million cost reduction program to address China and the research environment. Thaysen said as of the call the actions needed to realize full 2025 savings had been implemented. [Thaysen]
- The $100 million includes certain stock-based compensation changes and equals over $225 million in total run-rate reductions when fully annualized over the next 4 years. [Dhingra] (In Q&A Thaysen referred to a fully annualized value of "more than $200 million.")
- Because of this, Dhingra said the typical seasonal rise in operating expenses after Q1 will not repeat; OpEx is expected to be flat to slightly down for the rest of 2025.

### Capital return

- Q1 buyback: about 1.73 million shares for $200 million, at an average of $115.74 per share, completed in February. [Dhingra]
- $1.2 billion remains on the existing repurchase authorization. Illumina plans to keep buying back shares, which should give a small in-year EPS benefit. [Dhingra]

### Full-year 2025 guidance (updated)

Management explained that its March revision had held EPS at about $4.50 while absorbing the loss of Greater China revenue, the tighter funding environment and new cost actions. The new EPS guidance additionally reflects later developments, chiefly the tariff environment. [Dhingra]

| Item | Updated 2025 guidance | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $4.18 billion to $4.26 billion | A decline of 3% to 1% |
| Revenue excluding Greater China | Flat to +2% growth | Rest-of-world growth of about 1% at the midpoint |
| Greater China revenue | $165 million to $185 million | Cut by $125 million at the midpoint; $72 million recognized in Q1, $60 million projected for Q2, and only $43 million at the midpoint for the second half |
| Non-GAAP operating margin | about 21.5% to 22% | Expansion of about 45 basis points at the midpoint versus 2024; tariff items reduce it by 125 basis points |
| Non-GAAP tax rate | about 22% | Lowered |
| Non-GAAP EPS | $4.20 to $4.30 | Prior guide was about $4.50 (March); midpoint cut by $0.25 mainly because of tariffs |
| Sequencing consumables ex-Greater China | Flat to +2% growth | Driven by strong sequencing activity, especially clinical |
| Sequencing instruments ex-Greater China | Roughly flat | NovaSeq X demand assumed relatively constant; low-throughput growth from the MiSeq i100 |

Details of the rest-of-world revenue revision [Dhingra]:
- Research-weighted weakness from the constrained funding environment reduces quarterly revenue by 2% to 4% for the next three quarters.
- This is partly offset by about 1% of additional quarterly growth from clinical customers, who have placed many instruments in recent quarters.
- The net of these two is about $60 million over the next three quarters.
- Pricing actions add incremental revenue, mainly in the second half. Pricing applies only to new orders, so the benefit in Q2 is minimal.
- Favorable foreign exchange versus the prior guidance adds about $25 million to reported revenue.
- Management is taking a "pragmatic view" on China. Any positive resolution would be additional upside to the guide.

Greater China breakdown: the company said Greater China contributes about $0.35 of FY25 EPS, versus an estimated $0.76 in FY24. Rest-of-world EPS at the guidance midpoint would be $3.90, growing about 15% over the comparable prior year. [Dhingra]

Tariffs [Dhingra]:
- Estimated gross cost of tariffs for 2025 is about $85 million. The largest part is goods shipped from the Singapore manufacturing facility to the US; the rest is parts and subassemblies imported into US manufacturing and Illumina products sold into China.
- Because of inventory effects, Q2 gets a partial-period impact, with a $30 million to $35 million impact per quarter after that.
- Guidance assumes no incremental tariffs, including any counter-tariffs from the EU or others.
- Mitigation (supply chain optimization, pricing, other expense measures) takes time. Illumina expects to offset roughly half in 2025 and, at current levels, more fully mitigate in 2026. [Thaysen and Dhingra]

## Prepared remarks by speaker

### Brian Blanchett, Interim Head of Investor Relations

He noted that all financial information relates to "core Illumina" (the company after the GRAIL divestiture) and that growth rates in prepared remarks are in constant currency. Safe-harbor language is omitted here.

### Jacob Thaysen, CEO

**Governance.** He thanked former board chair Steve MacMillan, welcomed new chair Scott Gottlieb, and welcomed Keith Meister to the board, citing Meister's investor experience in genomics and record of driving shareholder value.

**Business tone.** Despite macroeconomic challenges, "we have a good start to the year." Illumina is "a resilient franchise with multiple drivers of near and long-term growth." The clinical transition to the X "reaffirm[s] both the value we provide and the resilience of the clinical markets."

**Three pressures and responses:**
1. **China.** The ability to export sequencing instruments is restricted; lower 2025 China revenue is expected on minimal instrument placements. Illumina will issue separate guidance for Greater China and the rest of the world until the situation is resolved, and continues to engage Chinese regulators on solutions for a sustainable long-term presence.
2. **US research funding.** Uncertainty is weighing on customers and delaying purchases. Illumina is offering flexible solutions to sustain research projects.
3. **Tariffs.** The US enacted a baseline 10% import tariff with significantly higher rates for some countries. Illumina is working to minimize the impact via supply chain optimization, cost measures and pricing actions, partially offsetting in 2025 and aiming to more fully mitigate in 2026.

**Innovation pipeline.**
- **Spatial offering** (technology that maps gene activity within tissue): announced in February with a significantly larger capture area, higher resolution and greater sensitivity than existing technologies; it can analyze millions of cells per experiment, helping to find rare cell populations. It integrates with end-to-end Illumina workflows and with Illumina Connected Multi-Omics, a new tertiary analysis (data interpretation) solution powered by Partek's [transcribed as "Partex"] visualization technology. Early access has begun with positive feedback, citing accessibility without special equipment purchases and a simpler analysis process. Release planned for 2026.
- **Perturb-Seq single-cell CRISPR offering:** lets researchers study how genetic changes affect single cells at scale, for oncology, immunology and drug target discovery. It captures both CRISPR guide RNA and messenger RNA from the same cell, enabling genome-wide CRISPR screens at "market leading costs." It builds on technology acquired last year (name indiscernible in the transcript). Launch expected later in 2025.
- **Proteomics solution** (measuring proteins), developed with Standard BioTools: in early access, commercial launch targeted for the first half of 2025.
- **Constellation mapped reads and 5-base genome technologies:** in early access, full commercial launches on track for 2026.
- Thaysen said "our innovation pipeline remains unmatched" and "the core of Illumina is strong, and our growth projections ex-China [are] on track."

### Ankur Dhingra, CFO

His remarks are captured in the results and guidance sections above. He closed by thanking employees and expressing confidence in near- and long-term financial targets.

## Question-and-answer session

### Doug Schenkel, Wolfe

**Question.** Investing in Illumina requires believing revenue growth rebounds soon (hopefully 2026) and margins expand above 25%. He asked: (1) Q1 clinical and research growth outside China; (2) what the guide assumes by end market (he guessed a double-digit research decline and mid-single-digit-plus clinical growth); and (3) China falling to about 4% of sales, from about 10% two years ago, could still be a headwind in 2026, so what else is being done to manage the risk beyond 2025.

**Answer (Thaysen).** The business is resilient with a lot of sequencing activity, which underlies the return to high single-digit growth over the next few years. Outside China, the strategy told to investors last summer is unchanged: the X transition lets market volume translate more deeply into revenue growth, with at least 50% of high-throughput revenue on X by the second half of 2025, and revenue acceleration after. He agreed China would be about 5% of the business in 2025 and would decline further in 2026 absent change. But the cost actions ($100 million this year, more than $200 million fully annualized) are designed to fully compensate for China. He called the $4.25 EPS (the guidance midpoint) "the new base" from which to grow at the strategic framework's double-digit to teens EPS growth, and reiterated that the focus is ex-China, which is 95% of the business.

**Answer (Dhingra).**
- Clinical consumables grew mid-single digits in Q1, helped by strong X placements carried over from Q4. Expectations for clinical were raised for the rest of the year, with good demand for existing tests and newer tests expected later in the year.
- Research consumables were down in the mid-to-high single digits in Q1 (partially garbled in the transcript). The research environment changed around mid-quarter, so Q1 had only part of the effect.
- Academia and government face two factors: constrained funding and tariff-driven inflation, which will persist through the year.
- The guide assumes a research decline "close to 15-ish percent" for the rest of the year, particularly in the academic and government space, worse than Q1. Stronger clinical, weaker research.

### Dave Westenberg, Piper Sandler

**Question.** On the weakness in mid- and low-throughput consumables, which of three causes is at work: customers switching to X directly or via a service provider (elasticity absorption), competitive headwinds, or funding-driven shutdowns? Can he quantify? Also, did Dhingra say customers had made more contractual commitments, which would add comfort in the guide?

**Answer (Thaysen).**
- He is "super excited" about the MiSeq i100 (launched very late last year, in low throughput): well received, strong order book, a lot of momentum.
- Mid throughput has been more challenged than high throughput "since I came in." It is less of a production environment; it serves research segments or small clinical accounts without the volume to fill a high-throughput machine. In constrained times customers may push out an additional instrument purchase or move to a service provider (Illumina still receives consumables flow in that case).
- Illumina introduced new chemistry (name indiscernible, "[X]" chemistry per the transcript) about a year ago; many customers have shifted to it because it offers a better price point, high quality and higher capacity per sequencer. Illumina is also going through price rebalancing in this space. These are the two main drivers of mid-throughput weakness.
- There is also more competition here, especially in China, where Illumina's instrument placements are currently weak. But research that has come out indicates Illumina has "performed very well in China versus our closest competitor there." Overall, he said it is "much more macro than it is a competitive environment." He gave no percentage quantification.

**Answer (Dhingra).** Confirmed: Q1 is seasonally when long-range contract negotiations and renewals occur, reported as performance obligations in the 10-Q. This year long-range bookings grew significantly; performance obligations were up double digits year over year, much better than in the last couple of years.

### Jack Meehan, Nephron

**Question.** Did tariffs influence Q1 demand, and since "Liberation Day" (early April tariff announcement) have announced tariffs changed customer purchasing?

**Answer (Thaysen).** No specific change in customer behavior related to tariffs in Q1, and he does not think so in Q2 either. There has been "a little bit of pull-in in China," from concern about what would happen there. For the rest of the world nothing material; tariffs did not affect the quarterly progression.

**Addition (Dhingra).** For the rest of the year, Illumina does expect some impact as tariff effects become known and show up in costs, and has cut its guide accordingly.

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore

**Question.** Prior revenue guidance was up low single digits and the new guide is down low single digits, a change of 300-400 basis points that seems almost entirely China. Where is the research cut (about 15%) baked in, and where does the offset come from? Did the clinical assumption change?

**Answer (Thaysen).** The March forecast had already anticipated both China and NIH funding impacts, with various outcomes flagged; the new call provides the full detail. Academic and government is expected to continue at roughly a 15% decrease and is the main driver. Clinical customers showed up stronger in Q1 and the clinical business is "more robust and stronger."

**Answer (Dhingra).** The biggest change is China, now separated out. For the rest of the world, the puts and takes versus the prior guide, for the remaining three quarters, are:
- Research and tariff-related impact: about 3 percentage points lower at the midpoint, roughly $90 million.
- Stronger clinical (continued X placements and units coming live): about 1 point offset.
- Pricing actions tied to tariff mitigation: about 1 point contribution.
- Foreign exchange, less unfavorable than in the last guide: about 0.5 point added to reported (not constant currency) growth.

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies

**Question.** Stress-testing the pricing assumption: where in the portfolio can Illumina raise prices, given competitors, including one "giving away free sequencing"? How confident is it?

**Answer (Thaysen).** Illumina has a long history of driving prices down and will keep ensuring competitive pricing. But in this environment customers want to work with companies that will be around in years, so Illumina takes a "prudent approach" to pricing and to offering innovative solutions. With tariffs, it wants to mitigate as much as possible with as little customer impact as it can; the company did "a lot of analysis" on how much it could put in. "Nobody likes a price increase," but he expects customers to understand that Illumina is "for the long term." He added: "If you want to give your sequencing wafer free, I can guarantee you don't have a business in a few quarters." (No specific products or price levels were named.)

### Conor McNamara, RBC Capital Markets

**Question.** Are customers delaying or deferring equipment purchases in anticipation of current or future competitor products?

**Answer (Thaysen).** Illumina is in a competitive space and thinks it is "doing really well against our competition." A competitor made an announcement a few weeks earlier, which he called "a technology overview. It was not a product launch." Of such overviews: "it's easy to impress. It's very difficult to satisfy our customers." There is some excitement, but "every day, you have to deliver again and again and again," and in tough times customers favor suppliers with certainty of delivery. Illumina has not seen disruption or delayed decisions from customers over this technology, which customers themselves do not yet fully know. It is a brand-new technology that must be verified through academic research, with papers and analysis, and "it will take time." This "is not a mature market." Right now, "there's no impact." (The competitor is not named in the transcript.)

### Mike Ryskin, Bank of America

**Question.** What are China and academic/government (the "[ANG]" shorthand in the transcript) trajectories for 2026 and beyond? If China is $15-25 million per quarter in Q3 and Q4, does it go to zero in 2026 or is it a sustainable base to grow from? And with NIH changes, does the US academic headwind look temporary?

**Answer (Thaysen).** He agreed too much time was being spent on China (about 5% of the business) and NIH headwinds, when other markets are strong, especially clinical and Europe ("a very, very strong performance for us"). Illumina is "a very resilient business" generating "fantastic cash flow."
- **China.** Illumina is working with Chinese authorities toward a solution. If it cannot keep a long-term sustainable business there, the cost measures can compensate. The guide has China revenue decreasing further in the second half, and if nothing changes in regulator talks it will keep falling in 2026. But he remains hopeful: China is an important market, he spent time with Chinese colleagues the prior week, and "the Chinese customers really want us to continue in this market." "We don't know at this point, but we are doing everything we can to rectify the situation. But if not, we have taken the cost structure."
- **Academia and government.** He believes the headwind is "short-term or temporary": many politicians in the US and elsewhere see academic research as pivotal (as a funnel into pharma and medical science). Calling it "a hiccup" (with part of a sentence about the 21st century indiscernible), he expects strong performance afterward.

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord

**Question.** With the SEC investigation into GRAIL reportedly set to close, would that give Illumina freedom for larger acquisitions or clinical lab purchases with big markets such as MRD (minimal residual disease, detecting leftover cancer)? And almost a year after the GRAIL divestiture, has it accelerated core Illumina?

**Answer (Thaysen).** Illumina is "very pleased that the SEC investigation is done and there were no findings, which we also expected." It continues to look at value-added bolt-on acquisitions, with discipline, and its annual cash generation gives flexibility. "MRD is likely not the space we will be looking into for the time being." He later clarified: "there's a huge opportunity in MRD, just not from an M&A perspective from Illumina."

**Answer (Dhingra).** The balance sheet is very good: in the three quarters since the GRAIL divestiture Illumina has generated over $1 billion of cash, and in Q1, despite weak research markets and the annual bonus payout, it generated over $200 million. It also repurchased about $200 million of shares. The primary focus remains growth and bolt-on M&A in technologies that can use Illumina's size, scale and large installed base, but it does not intend to keep accumulating cash and will keep buying back shares opportunistically for the rest of the year. (Neither executive directly said whether the divestiture accelerated core Illumina.)

### Unidentified analyst ("Martha," on behalf of Rachel Vatnsdal, JPMorgan)

**Question.** Which tariff mitigation actions are already implemented and which are still to come, and how will that affect EPS pacing through the year? Also, is a Q4 budget flush (customers spending leftover annual budgets) assumed?

**Answer (Thaysen).** Tariff clarity is only a few weeks old and "it's still a movie that is playing," so it is a moving target. The current view is a roughly $85 million 2025 impact with about half offset. Illumina is changing some supply chains, working with suppliers and considering where it manufactures. Most manufacturing is in Singapore and the US; the footprint can be optimized but no substantial change is planned now. Pricing actions have already been taken, and it will continue to optimize footprint and supply chain. (The EPS pacing is addressed only by the earlier second-half-weighted guide commentary, and the Q4 budget flush question was not answered.)

## Points to watch and source caveats

- Management's as-reported Q1 revenue change (down 1.4%) differs from the Seeking Alpha header (down 3.25% year over year); the header is third-party and its basis is not explained.
- The transcript has indiscernible or garbled passages (research consumables decline wording, Perturb-Seq acquired technology name, the new chemistry name, and the "[ANG]" abbreviation, which appears to mean academia and government).
