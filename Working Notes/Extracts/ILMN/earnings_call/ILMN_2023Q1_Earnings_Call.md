# Illumina Q1 2023 Earnings Call (April 25, 2023): NovaSeq X launch, Russia sanctions, cost cuts and 2024-25 margin targets

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2023Q1_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2023-04-25 · Seeking Alpha transcript of the Illumina Q1 2023 earnings call; speakers Salli Schwartz (IR), Francis deSouza (CEO), Joydeep Goswami (CFO); analysts from SVB Securities, TD Cowen, Stifel, Evercore ISI, Morgan Stanley, J.P. Morgan, Piper Sandler, Canaccord · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q1 2023 revenue of about $1.09 billion (down 11% year over year) and non-GAAP EPS of $0.08, both above guidance, helped by 67 NovaSeq X shipments versus an expected 40-50. Management raised its 2023 NovaSeq X shipment target to more than 330 instruments, held total 2023 revenue and EPS guidance, and absorbed about $60 million of lost Russia revenue. It also announced more than $100 million of annualized cost cuts and committed to Core Illumina operating margins of 25% in 2024 and 27% in 2025. Analysts focused on X demand and manufacturing pace, the second-half ramp, gross margins, GRAIL regulatory outcomes, China and NextSeq pricing.

# Illumina Q1 2023 Earnings Call (April 25, 2023)

Participants: Salli Schwartz (Vice President of Investor Relations), Francis deSouza (President and CEO), Joydeep Goswami (CFO and Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer). Analysts: Puneet Souda (SVB Securities), Dan Brennan (TD Cowen), Dan Arias (Stifel), Vijay Kumar (Evercore ISI), Tejas Savant (Morgan Stanley), Julia Qin (J.P. Morgan), David Westenberg (Piper Sandler), Kyle Mikson (Canaccord).

A note on reporting basis: Illumina's results here include GRAIL, its cancer-detection subsidiary. Schwartz reminded listeners that GRAIL must be held and operated separately and independently from Illumina under interim measures ordered by the European Commission, which prohibited the GRAIL acquisition under the EU Merger Regulation [Q1 2023 call]. "Core Illumina" means Illumina excluding GRAIL. Figures are non-GAAP (adjusted, and here including stock-based compensation) unless stated.

## Results and guidance

### Headline results

The Seeking Alpha header showed EPS of $0.08 (beating consensus by $0.05) and revenue of $1.09 billion (down 11.12% year over year, beating consensus by $20.68 million).

deSouza said revenue of about $1.09 billion and diluted non-GAAP EPS of $0.08 were both ahead of the guidance given at the start of the year but down year over year, "as expected" [Q1 2023 call]. Goswami said consolidated revenue was up 1% from Q4 2022 and exceeded the high end of the guidance range because of stronger-than-expected NovaSeq X shipments. Year over year, revenue fell 11%, or 9% in constant currency (net of hedging effects).

Management attributed the year-over-year decline to four factors:
- An expected slowdown in COVID surveillance (sequencing used to track the virus).
- COVID-related disruptions in China.
- Some high-throughput customers moving to NovaSeq X (a transition effect).
- Customers managing constrained capital markets globally.

Tax rate: Goswami said both quarters reflect R&D capitalization requirements (a tax rule that spreads R&D deductions over several years). The dollar impact was the same in both periods, but the effect on the rate was larger in Q1 2023 because earnings were lower.

COVID surveillance: it contributed about $11 million of revenue in Q1 2023 versus $60 million in Q1 2022, a $49 million headwind.

Consumables: the year-over-year drop reflected lower NovaSeq 6000 consumables pull-through (reagents used per instrument), China disruptions, the move to NovaSeq X, and constrained capital markets, in addition to COVID.

Instruments: the decline was mainly lower NovaSeq 6000 shipments against a record Q1 2022, and fewer mid-throughput shipments, mostly fewer NextSeq 550 placements in China. Stronger NovaSeq X shipments partly offset this, though Illumina "remain[s] supply constrained in the first year of launch." Demand for NextSeq 1k/2k from new-to-Illumina customers stayed strong.

Service and other: growth came mainly from higher instrument service contract revenue on a growing installed base.

Gross margin: the 500 bp year-over-year decline came from less fixed-cost leverage on lower manufacturing volumes and lower instrument margins from the NovaSeq X launch, "which is typical with a new platform introduction." Goswami expects gross margin to improve sequentially through the year as NovaSeq X manufacturing scales.

Operating expenses: the $9 million increase was mainly the full-year effect of 2022 headcount growth. Expenses were lower than expected thanks to cost-containment initiatives.

Balance sheet: no shares were repurchased in the quarter. Illumina used $500 million to repay the principal of its 2023 term notes that matured in March.

### Sequencing activity (installed-base usage trends)

Goswami cited usage data from connected high- and mid-throughput instruments, which he described as directionally correlated with revenue over time:
- Total activity: +6% vs Q4 2022 and +3% y/y.
- Research and Applied: +7% vs Q4 2022 and -4% y/y.
- Clinical: +7% vs Q4 2022 and +15% y/y ("robust").

### Regional results (Core Illumina)

Illumina has a new global commercial structure that merges Asia Pacific with emerging markets in the Middle East, Africa, Turkey and CIS. It will now report four regions: Americas, Europe, Greater China, and AMEA (Asia Pacific, Middle East and Africa) [Goswami, Q1 2023 call].

| Region | Q1 2023 revenue | Change | Drivers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Americas | $605M | +5% sequentially; -6% y/y | Beat expectations on stronger NovaSeq X shipments. Y/y decline from lower Research (COVID surveillance slowdown, delayed recruitment for some large research projects). NextSeq 1k/2k shipments up over 20% y/y. |
| Europe | $261M | -9% y/y (-4% constant currency) | Clinical growth, led by oncology, was more than offset by lower COVID surveillance and lower high-throughput instrument shipments due to NovaSeq X supply constraints. |
| Greater China | $91M | -28% y/y (-23% constant currency) | Persistent COVID disruptions and customer liquidity and funding constraints; lower consumables and fewer mid-throughput instruments. Illumina will keep monitoring and mitigating. |
| AMEA | $119M | -27% y/y (-23% constant currency) | Completion of a large research project in Japan, lower COVID surveillance, fewer NovaSeq 6000 instruments (only partly offset by NovaSeq X, where demand outpaced supply), and Russia sanctions. |

Russia: Goswami said sanctions will persist through 2023, and the 2023 outlook reflects about $60 million of lower sequencing consumables revenue in AMEA.

### GRAIL results

GRAIL's Q1 revenue was $20 million, up 100% y/y and ahead of plan, driven by accelerating adoption of its Galleri multi-cancer early detection blood test. Revenue fell sequentially because Q4 2022 included a milestone payment from MRD (minimal residual disease) pharma partnerships. Non-GAAP operating expenses were $173 million, up $34 million y/y, from clinical trial investment and building the commercial organization. The non-GAAP operating loss was $164 million.

### 2023 guidance

Unchanged items [Goswami]:
- Consolidated revenue growth of 7% to 10%, including Core Illumina growth of 6% to 9%. These ranges include about 200 bp of COVID surveillance headwind and an FX headwind.
- GRAIL revenue of $90 million to $110 million.
- Non-GAAP EPS of $1.25 to $1.50, with consolidated non-GAAP operating margin of about 8% and Core Illumina operating margin of about 22%.
- Core Illumina sequencing revenue growth of about 8% at the midpoint.
- All other guidance from February 7, 2023 is reaffirmed.

Three offsetting factors now sit inside the unchanged revenue range: (1) lower AMEA revenue from Russia sanctions, (2) NovaSeq X shipment expectations raised to more than 330 instruments (from 300), and (3) higher contributions from strategic partnerships related to drug discovery.

Changes at the midpoint of the revenue range:
- Sequencing instrument revenue growth: now about 13% y/y, reflecting higher NovaSeq X shipments.
- Sequencing consumables revenue growth: now about 5.5% y/y, mainly due to the Russia-related AMEA reduction.
- Sequencing service and other revenue: higher, primarily from strategic pharma partnerships, which keeps total sequencing revenue growth at about 8%.

Timing: quarterly Core Illumina revenue should ramp sequentially, with a pattern like 2017 when the NovaSeq 6000 launched, when about 54% of revenue came in the second half, including about 26% in Q3 and the rest in Q4. The ramp assumes (1) quarterly increases in NovaSeq X shipments as manufacturing capacity improves, (2) rising consumables as X customers start projects in the second half, and (3) easing macro headwinds in the second half, including a China recovery from COVID disruption.

### Q2 2023 guidance

- Consolidated revenue of $1.15 billion to $1.17 billion, up about 670 bp (about $70 million) sequentially at the midpoint.
- Drivers: more NovaSeq X shipments as manufacturing ramps; continued sequential growth in consumables and service as new instruments come online; higher GRAIL revenue on Galleri adoption. Partly offset by a seasonal sequential decline in microarrays revenue.
- At the midpoint: non-GAAP EPS of about $0.01, consolidated operating margin of about 1%, and Core Illumina operating margin of about 17%.
- The Q2 margin reflects a sequential rise in Core operating expenses from the annual equity grant and merit adjustment in March. Operating margins are still expected to improve in the second half as revenue ramps and NovaSeq X production scales.

### Long-term margin commitments and cost reduction

Announced earlier on the day of the call:
- Core Illumina non-GAAP operating margin of 25% in 2024 and 27% in 2025, while maintaining investment in the key elements of the innovation roadmap.
- New steps, building on cost actions announced in November 2022, to cut annualized run-rate expenses by more than $100 million beginning later in 2023. The savings accelerate margin progress and free capital for high-growth areas.

Goswami's listed actions:
- Using the recent modularization of R&D (reusable building blocks such as XLEAP-SBS chemistry and the new flow cell technology from NovaSeq X development) to lower cost and speed future platforms.
- Using the global footprint to do work in more cost-effective hubs.
- Streamlining the organization and processes, including rationalizing the global real estate portfolio and third-party vendor spend, and accelerating IT optimization.
- Continuing to prioritize highly differentiated products customers value.

## Prepared remarks

### Francis deSouza (CEO): business update

Priorities for the rest of 2023: deliver sequential growth mainly by scaling NovaSeq X production and distribution, driving demand elasticity (more sequencing as cost per sample falls) from its added capability, and improving margins.

Product milestones in Q1: first NovaSeq X shipments; launch of Illumina Complete Long Read technology; launch of Illumina Connected Insights, a cloud-based tertiary analysis tool that addresses a key adoption barrier and lets more labs perform comprehensive genomic profiling for advanced tumors.

**High-throughput (NovaSeq X and NovaSeq 6000)**
- The company exited Q1 with over 200 NovaSeq X orders from more than 30 countries.
- Clinical demand was stronger than expected, about 40% of orders. Some customers plan new clinical offerings on the X. About 15% of orders came from new-to-high-throughput customers, such as a clinical genomics lab using the X's ease of use and cost for high-throughput whole genome, single cell and RNA sequencing.
- Shipments: 67 NovaSeq X in Q1 versus initial expectations of 40 to 50. The full-year expectation rose to more than 330 from 300.
- Early runs: some customers report output above 7 terabases and more than 95% of bases above Q30 (a standard accuracy score), "well ahead of published specifications."
- Elasticity examples: a leading European life science institute will use the X for "atlas-scale" multi-omics, spatial and single cell projects. Clinical customers plan to move from targeted panels to exomes and genomes for higher diagnostic yield. An oncology customer plans liquid biopsy testing, which needs 12 to 15 times more sequencing than solid tumor testing. Some customers will enter deeper applications such as MRD to build datasets at scale.
- NovaSeq 6000: 17 shipped in Q1, mainly fleet expansions of existing workflows. More than 80% went to clinical customers and about 25% to oncology testing.

**Mid-throughput:** NextSeq 1k/2k unit shipments rose 6% y/y as customers expanded fleets or shifted projects from MiSeq and NextSeq 550. More than 20% of Q1 units went to new-to-Illumina customers. Win rate remains strong, but "we are seeing some sales cycles lengthen."

**Low-throughput:** about 30% of Q1 shipments were to new-to-Illumina customers, expanding the installed base.

**Clinical markets**
- Milestone: clinical sequencing consumables were 50% of total sequencing consumables revenue for the first time.
- Oncology testing consumables fell 3% y/y but rose 21% sequentially, driven by clinical testing volumes and product development in liquid biopsy and MRD. deSouza warned that some customers face profitability pressure that may slow test development and scaling.
- TruSight Oncology 500 (TSO 500) revenue exceeded $25 million, up 26% y/y, across more than 540 accounts, driven by utilization and pull-through, particularly in Europe.
- In March Illumina expanded its partnership with Myriad Genetics to broaden homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) testing, which identifies tumors with a specific DNA damage type (ovarian, breast, prostate, pancreatic cancers). TSO 500 HRD, a research-use-only test, is now available in the US, with early customer Florida Cancer Specialists & Research Institute. The partnership also creates an alliance to develop HRD as a companion diagnostic for novel agents.
- European reimbursement progress: seven Italian regions committed €10 million for next-generation sequencing in lung cancer; German insurers began reimbursing whole genome sequencing in relapsed pediatric cancer; the Czech Republic added comprehensive genomic profiling to its national fee schedule effective January 1.
- GRAIL: revenue $20 million (+100% y/y), opex $173 million; more than 85,000 Galleri orders since launch, about 20,000 in Q1 alone. The 140,000-participant NHS-Galleri trial finished enrollment in July 2022 in just over ten months; more than half of participants have returned for the second annual blood draw, the trial just hit its halfway point and is on track for retention targets. John Hancock (a Manulife unit) will expand Galleri access to eligible life insurance customers after a pilot; Providence expanded its partnership to offer Galleri across 52 hospitals and 900 clinics in seven states.
- Reproductive health: consumables shipments fell 4% y/y (mainly FX) but rose 14% sequentially on coverage progress. Virginia and Michigan began state Medicaid coverage of NIPT (noninvasive prenatal testing) for all pregnancies, adding about 4.3 million covered lives and more than 70,000 pregnancies a year.
- Genetic Disease Testing (GDT) had a record quarter: consumables shipments +7% y/y and +6% sequentially. One of the largest US insurers updated its policy to cover whole genome sequencing for rare and undiagnosed disease in Managed Medicaid in 16 states (about 3.8 million lives). Illumina also announced a partnership with Henry Ford Health to assess whole genome sequencing in cardiovascular disease management, especially in diverse and underserved populations.

**Research and Applied:** sequencing consumables shipments fell 19% y/y, primarily from the COVID surveillance slowdown.

**Upcoming releases:** the ICLR enrichment assay (affordable, high-throughput targeted long-read) ships in Q3 2023; XLEAP-SBS chemistry arrives on NextSeq 1k/2k in the first half of 2024, with step-change cost, speed and sustainability gains.

deSouza said Illumina is "on track to deliver on our financial commitments for 2023."

## Q&A

### Puneet Souda (SVB Securities)

**Questions:** (1) Could the 330 NovaSeq X number keep rising, and what quarterly production cadence should be assumed? (2) Do the legal fronts (European Commission, FTC, and a new activist campaign) distract from product execution? (3) Is biotech funding weakness affecting emerging biotechs, and is it in the guide?

**Answers (deSouza):**
- 330 is "going to be more manufacturing bound than demand bound." Interest is huge across the customer base. Illumina expected Research to be the most aggressive early adopter and Clinical to lag, "but we're not seeing that this time"; Clinical demand is strong, along with new-to-Illumina and new-to-high-throughput buyers.
- Q1 shipments of 67 beat the 40 to 50 guide. Q2 is planned at 80 X instruments, with gradual steps through the year toward 330; Illumina will update if manufacturing scales better.
- On distractions: "the story of this year is the X," the regulatory process involves a very small part of the company, and "the vast, vast, vast majority" of time and attention goes to products, mostly the X.
- Biotech funding: direct exposure to small and mid-cap biotechs is very small, so no material revenue impact is expected this year.

### Dan Brennan (TD Cowen)

**Questions:** (1) How is the replacement ratio (X for 6000) shaping up from the order book? (2) Can customers fill the X's greater capacity; is that a gating factor? (3) What is the likely GRAIL regulatory outcome and when is clarity expected?

**Answers (deSouza):**
- Illumina has modeled how many X units the market can absorb. The best method is "customer out" rather than capacity-based ratios: about 1,000 high-throughput customers, roughly 750 of which own a single NovaSeq 6000. Those 750 cannot buy less than one X, giving about 750 X units "right off the bat" even with no demand growth. The other 250 with multiple 6000s will apply their own conversion ratios. Elasticity comes on top of this base case.
- Most X buyers are buying to tap elasticity from lower price per sequence: clinical customers launching new applications (a solid tumor lab adding liquid biopsy, needing 12 to 15 times more sequencing, at a price within reimbursement limits) and researchers running larger single cell or spatial experiments. He did not describe filling capacity as a gating factor.
- GRAIL: the process "comes to a head" late 2023 or early 2024. Decisions are expected on the two most important appeals: the jurisdiction appeal in Europe, and the US appeal against the FTC, for which the US Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals granted expedited review in the past week, so a decision may come slightly earlier than initially anticipated. A divestiture work stream continues in parallel, awaiting the European Commission's divestiture order, which Illumina expects "shortly."

**Addition (Goswami):** clinical elasticity involves new, complex signatures needing high sequencing levels, and because Illumina supplies tools and reagents it "starts making money as those clinical trials kick off even before the tests are available in the public market," so elasticity begins before a test reaches market.

### Dan Arias (Stifel)

**Question:** Will elasticity materialize meaningfully in the back half to contribute to consumables growth (it was an explicit driver of the roughly 8% consumables growth outlook given in January)? How do installation timing and usage ramp fit?

**Answer (Goswami):** Yes. Installations and initial workflow bring-up occur in Q2 and Q3; in Q4 customers start ramping projects, building inventory and moving workloads to the X in earnest.

### Vijay Kumar (Evercore ISI)

**Questions:** (1) Quantify the Russia headwind. (2) Why did gross margin fall sequentially (sub-65% versus about 66% in Q4)? (3) With Q2 Core operating margin about 17%, what drives the step-up to roughly 26-27% in the second half to hit the annual guide?

**Answers (Goswami):**
- Russia: about $60 million for the full year and about $18 million year-over-year headwind in Q2.
- Gross margin: signaled in February. Two reasons: the X launch (low initial margins, and more Xs sold than anticipated) and the typical Q4-to-Q1 drop in factory absorption (fixed costs spread over fewer units).
- Q2 operating margin: a one-time step-up in operating expense from annual salary, promotion and equity adjustments (which then stabilizes), plus a higher instrument mix because of the Russia consumables loss, which weighs on gross margin. Together these offset operating leverage and hold margin at 17%.
- Second half: improving X margins and rising consumables give sequential step-ups each quarter, improving factory absorption and gross margin. He said Illumina expects to exit the year with gross margins in the high 60%s and operating margins "close to the high 20% numbers" Kumar cited.

### Tejas Savant (Morgan Stanley)

**Questions:** (1) X shipments beat, yet instrument revenue was in line with forecasts; was the offset EMEA weakness, NextSeq 550 in China, or discounting? (2) Where must NextSeq per-GB pricing land once XLEAP arrives (competitors around $2.00 per gigabase; is $4-5 fair)? (3) How committed is Illumina to divesting GRAIL if it wins the jurisdictional appeal and recovers the EC fine, and under what scenario would it keep it?

**Answers:**
- Goswami: the primary offset was the mid-throughput cycle, mostly reduced NextSeq 550 shipments in China. On pricing: "We're not seeing any differential with the expected prices nor a decrease in the prices that we track, both for instruments and our consumables."
- deSouza on NextSeq/XLEAP: it brings lower price points, better chemistry performance and sustainability benefits (no cold chain needed for shipping consumables). Not announcing price now, but the aim is to price to "maximize the market opportunity," so prices will come down, while recognizing unique value: onboard compute via FPGAs (programmable chips) built into NextSeq and onboard storage optimization, which no other mid-throughput instrument offers. Final pricing will be set around what opens the mid-throughput market the most.
- deSouza on GRAIL: the divestiture order will lay out process and structure; a divestiture team is already engaged with the European Commission. In parallel two appeals run. If Illumina loses either, it will "divest promptly in the best interest of our shareholders," expected late 2023 or early 2024. It would keep GRAIL only if it wins both appeals and a fresh look confirms the keep-and-integrate business case assumptions still hold.

### Julia Qin (J.P. Morgan)

**Questions:** (1) Upside/downside scenarios on margin targets, and does in-house X manufacturing reach full efficiency by end of 2024 or 2025? (2) How large is the R&D efficiency gain from the new structure? (3) Any government stimulus offset or recovery signs in China?

**Answers (Goswami):**
- 2023 Core margin guidance of 22% stands (the transcript says "2022," apparently a slip for 2023). The extra instruments from the Russia offset imply a slight gross margin hit, offset by manufacturing productivity and the new cost reductions.
- X manufacturing: efficiencies initially outlined should be achieved by the end of 2023, and further efficiencies will continue in years two and three, as with past instruments.
- R&D: modularized innovations (new flow cell, XLEAP-SBS) radically reduce spend per platform and speed time to market. "You won't see this in 2023, but you'll see this in '24 and beyond." Also moving R&D and other functions to cost-effective hubs, rationalizing real estate, spend and IT; savings begin toward the end of 2023 and take hold in 2024 and beyond.
- China: "we are not seeing the benefits of any stimulus in China, at least for our business"; Illumina is monitoring customer funding challenges and taking mitigating steps.

### David Westenberg (Piper Sandler)

**Question:** Have any of the 67 X customers begun decommissioning NovaSeq 6000s, or placed subsequent 6000 orders?

**Answer (Goswami):** Most customers will transition projects over time and, especially in clinical, do not intend to decommission their 6000s. For the initial customers tracked from Q1 into Q2, Illumina expects just 10 to 12 decommissions; decommissions and replacements will be a smaller fraction, with more updates on the installed base later in the year.

### Kyle Mikson (Canaccord)

**Question:** Historically growth was elevated in the year or two after signature launches (2017-18 with the NovaSeq 6000, 2014-15 with HiSeq X) but weaker at times (2016 single digit, 2019 a setback). Will the NovaSeq X differ, or is Illumina in a perennial product-cycle dependence, now with more headwinds?

**Answers:**
- deSouza: product cycles matter because they catalyze upgrade cycles, historically quick in research, and the market was primarily research. Now 50% of consumables come from clinical, so a big growth driver is clinical adoption itself: more genetic disease testing, more oncology therapy selection coverage, more NIPT coverage. Growth will come "more so necessary than just a straight upgrade cycle."
- Goswami: some transition dip in consumables as customers cut over between systems is unavoidable, but it is seen much more with research than clinical customers, which mitigates it.

## Closing remarks (deSouza)

April 25 is DNA Day, marking the 70th anniversary of the discovery of the DNA double helix, and the month is Illumina's 25th anniversary. deSouza said only 4 million genomes have ever been sequenced, "representing only 0.07% of the population," so "we are still at the beginning of the genome era."

## Items to watch / caveats

- The Q&A names three pending regulatory matters (EU, FTC, and the divestiture order). deSouza expects the two key appeal decisions (EU jurisdiction; US Fifth Circuit on the FTC, expedited) by late 2023 or early 2024. All timing statements are management expectations as of April 2023.
- Puneet Souda referred to an activist campaign, which management did not address directly.
- Some analyst phrases were marked inaudible or uncertain in the transcript (e.g., "replacement ratio," "world-class platform"); the substance recorded above reflects what was clearly stated.
