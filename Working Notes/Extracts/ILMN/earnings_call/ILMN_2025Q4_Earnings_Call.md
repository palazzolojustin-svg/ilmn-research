# Illumina Q4 2025 earnings call (Feb 5, 2026): clinical-led return to growth and 2026 guidance

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2025Q4_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2026-02-05 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Illumina's Q4 2025 earnings call. Speakers: Jacob Thaysen (CEO and interim Chief Commercial Officer), Ankur Dhingra (CFO), Conor McNamara (VP Investor Relations). Analysts: Wolfe, Evercore ISI, Leerink, Jefferies, BofA, Canaccord, Stifel, Nephron, JPMorgan, Piper Sandler, Guggenheim. · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q4 2025 revenue of $1.16B (+5% reported, +4% constant currency, +7% excluding China) and non-GAAP EPS of $1.35 (+~42%), both above guidance. Clinical consumables outside China grew 20%, NovaSeq X placements were strong, and research stayed weak. For 2026 management guides to $4.5B-$4.6B revenue (+4% to 6% reported; 2% to 4% organic ex-China), 23.3%-23.5% operating margin and EPS of $5.05-$5.20 including $0.18 of dilution from the just-closed SomaLogic acquisition. Analysts probed the gap between the Q4 exit rate and the 2026 guide, instrument placements, competition (including $100 genome headlines), China, BioInsight and the 2027 targets.

# Illumina Q4 2025 Earnings Call: Detailed Notes

Illumina (ILMN) makes DNA sequencing instruments and the consumables (the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run) that run on them. This call took place on February 5, 2026. Management said Q4 2025 beat expectations and that the company is on track for its 2027 financial targets. Unless stated otherwise, everything below is a management statement from the call [Q4 2025 call].

Conventions from the opening remarks (Conor McNamara):
- Revenue growth rates in the prepared remarks are year-over-year and in constant currency (excluding foreign-exchange effects), unless noted.
- "China" means the Greater China region, which includes Taiwan and Hong Kong.
- Seeking Alpha's summary banner (not management) says EPS of $1.35 beat by $0.12 and revenue of $1.16B (+4.98% year-over-year) beat by $47.92M. It does not say whether these are consensus comparisons.

- **Drivers of the beat.** Dhingra cited strength in clinical consumables, better-than-expected NovaSeq X placements, outperformance in China, and a small benefit from year-end budget purchasing. The higher revenue pushed margins and EPS ahead of guidance, and also reflected earlier cost actions.
- **Q4 gross margin versus Q3.** Q4 margins reflect the usual instrument-heavy fourth quarter (instruments carry lower margin than consumables). This is Dhingra's framing.
- **Tax.** Management continues to assess longer-term tax structure optimization to balance U.S. R&D benefits against efficient use of credits across jurisdictions.

### Consumables detail and the NovaSeq X transition

- **Why consumables grew.** High-throughput volume growth drove strength as customers in research and clinical ramped use of their NovaSeq X instruments (Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer, launched 2023).
- **Clinical.** The clinical market grew 20% outside China. Drivers were broader adoption of NGS-based testing and customers converting assays to ones needing far more data, such as moving from whole exome to whole genome sequencing in oncology and genetic disease.
- **Research and applied consumables.** These were roughly flat year-over-year in Q4. That was a notable improvement versus Q3 but still below historical levels, because of funding uncertainty and year-over-year pricing effects from converting customers to the X.
- **Conversion progress as of Q4.** About 80% of volumes and 55% of revenue have moved to NovaSeq X. In research, about 90% of high-throughput volume has transitioned, so pricing headwinds from the transition should "continue to dissipate," especially in H2 2026. Clinical volume is more than two-thirds converted, and future clinical pricing dynamics should depend more on new applications like whole-genome sequencing, which raises volumes. Management expects the conversion to be substantially complete by the end of 2026.
- **Sequencing output.** Total sequencing gigabases (Gb, the unit of data) on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30% year-over-year. Growth was robust in clinical and more muted in research.
- **Instrument placements.** Illumina placed over 100 NovaSeq X systems, about five of them on rental or lease contracts. Over 60% went to clinical customers, mirroring the consumables mix. Strong placements of the NovaSeq X and the MiSeq i100 (a smaller benchtop sequencer) drove the instrument result. The active installed base is 890 NovaSeq X instruments. Jacob Thaysen said Q4 was the second-highest quarterly placement count since the X launched in 2023.
- **Services.** Strategic partnerships and the timing of data deals were "lumpy" in 2025, and management was pleased with the return to growth in Q4.

### Full-year 2025 recap

- Illumina returned to growth outside China in Q3 and grew about 4% in H2 (Dhingra). Thaysen said ex-China revenue grew 2% for the year and 7% in Q4.
- Clinical consumables growth accelerated through the year: mid-teens in H2, 20% ex-China in Q4. Dhingra and Thaysen both put H2 clinical consumables growth at about 16% ex-China (Thaysen's guidance remarks).
- Non-GAAP operating margin expanded 180 bps (Thaysen) or "nearly 200 basis points" (Dhingra), despite about 200 bps of macro-related headwinds (tariffs and other items).
- Non-GAAP EPS grew 16% to $4.84, above the original guidance given at the start of 2025.
- About $740M was returned to shareholders through buybacks.
- Thaysen said Illumina met its high-throughput transition milestones and extended its portfolio into multiomics (measuring several biological layers such as DNA, RNA and protein) and data, software and AI.

### 2026 guidance

This table sets out the full-year 2026 outlook as presented. Growth rates are versus 2025.

| Item | 2026 guidance |
|---|---|
| Total revenue | $4.5B-$4.6B, up 4%-6% reported |
| Organic growth ex-China | 2%-4% |
| Currency | Adds roughly 1 point to reported growth |
| SomaLogic | Adds 1.5-2 points of revenue growth |
| China | About a 1-point headwind to total growth; sales of $210M-$220M |
| Operating margin | 23.3%-23.5% (non-GAAP) |
| EPS | $5.05-$5.20, including $0.18 dilution from SomaLogic at the midpoint |

- **Organic growth.** "Organic" excludes currency and SomaLogic revenue. It assumes overall demand similar to H2 2025.
- **Consumables (rest of world, organic).** Low-to-mid single-digit growth. Clinical is expected to grow double-digit to mid-teens on volumes from clinical customers' on-market tests and new whole-genome approaches. Research and applied consumables are expected to decline mid-to-high single digits, assuming no fundamental change in academic end markets.
- **NIH upside.** Dhingra called recent NIH budget announcements "a welcome development" that could, as funds flow, provide a better environment than the guidance assumes.
- **Instruments.** Expected down low-single-digit to flat. The goal of 50-60 NovaSeq X placements per quarter on average continues through 2026 (about 200-240 for the year, per Dhingra in Q&A). Pull-through assumptions by platform are in the earnings presentation.
- **China.** Little or no step-up in instrument sales in H1. Management will revisit assumptions as it works with the government on importing instruments.
- **Operating margin.** Excluding SomaLogic, margins are guided to expand 130 bps at the midpoint. SomaLogic costs about 100 bps, giving 23.3%-23.5% all-in.
- **EPS.** Excluding SomaLogic, EPS grows 10% at the midpoint. SomaLogic is dilutive by $0.18 at the midpoint, giving $5.05-$5.20.
- **Q1 2026.** Rest-of-world organic revenue growth of 1%-3%, equal to $1.06B-$1.08B. EPS of $1.02-$1.07, including $0.04 of deal dilution.
- **Reporting change.** From January 2026 Illumina changed its geographic reporting segments to align with its commercial organization. The new segments start in Q1 2026, with historical reconciliations provided.
- **Long-term targets.** Thaysen said Illumina remains on track for its 2027 financial targets.

## 2. Prepared Remarks by Speaker

### Jacob Thaysen (CEO and interim Chief Commercial Officer)

**Overall message.** Q4 "exceeded expectations." Thaysen said the momentum going into 2026 gives "high confidence that the strategy we put in place in 2024 to return to long-term growth is working."

**Clinical drivers.** Thaysen named two main drivers of clinical strength:
1. Rising adoption of sequencing-based diagnostic tests as customers launch new assays and win reimbursement coverage, especially in minimal residual disease (MRD, tests that detect leftover cancer after treatment) and early cancer detection.
2. Broader demand for comprehensive genomic profiling and whole-genome approaches in oncology and genetic disease, which need more sequencing and benefit from the NovaSeq X's power and consistency.

The "elasticity" dynamic (cheaper or more powerful sequencing leading customers to run far more) continues. It is driving instrument sales and consumables demand. Thaysen said next-generation sequencing "remains vastly underutilized."

**New Chief Medical Officer.** Dr. Eric Green joined as CMO. Green was most recently Director of the National Human Genome Research Institute at the NIH and brings genomics and health-policy experience. Thaysen expects Green to help drive adoption of genomics and multiomics toward standard of care.

**Pillar 1: core sequencing (NovaSeq X).**
- Clinical is the primary growth driver.
- In research, conditions remain "measured." Customers are cautious, but there are signs of stabilization, including more clarity on U.S. policy and funding. Management assumes 2026 end-market dynamics similar to 2025, while believing research can return to healthy growth in the longer term.

**Pillar 2: multiomics.**
- Illumina completed the SomaLogic acquisition. SomaLogic has an aptamer-based affinity proteomics platform (it measures thousands of proteins in one experiment with high sensitivity and throughput). This builds on a longstanding partnership.
- Thaysen called proteomics "the frontline in multiomics." Illumina plans to apply the scale of NGS to proteomics to cut the time and cost of protein analysis.
- Illumina recently launched Illumina Connected Multiomics, software that integrates and interprets data across genomics, proteomics, single-cell and epigenomics.
- Illumina's spatial transcriptomics solution (mapping gene activity within tissue) and its constellation mapped-read technology remain on track for H1 2026.

**Pillar 3: services, data and software.**
- In Q4 Illumina launched BioInsight, which combines sequencing at scale, genome-wide CRISPR perturbation tools, dedicated compute and AI for predictive biological models. The pitch is that pharma and biotech can build and test biological models digitally rather than through years of wet-lab iteration.
- In January 2026 it introduced BioInsight's first data product, the Billion Cell Atlas, billed as the most comprehensive map of human biology for drug discovery. It is built with single-cell methods, CRISPR perturbation and AI. Initial collaborations were announced with AstraZeneca, Merck and Eli Lilly, and management says more partners are showing interest.
- Thaysen said BioInsight strengthens Illumina's position in biopharma, a growing part of the research end market.

**AGBT and the pitch to customers.**
- Illumina is Gold sponsor of the AGBT conference later in February 2026, with customers and key opinion leaders presenting.
- Thaysen said customer conversations have shifted "from cost per gigabase to the total cost of workflow," from sample prep through analysis and interpretation.

**2026 outlook (Thaysen's version).** Thaysen reiterated organic ex-China growth of 2%-4%, double-digit to mid-teens clinical growth, mid-to-high single-digit declines in research and applied consumables, instruments roughly flat to slightly down, and the revenue, margin and EPS ranges above. Excluding SomaLogic dilution, EPS growth is 10%.

### Ankur Dhingra (CFO)

Dhingra's remarks are the source of the quantitative results and guidance in Section 1. Three additional points:
- Year-end budget purchasing gave only a "small benefit" to Q4.
- The 2025 margin gain of nearly 200 bps came despite about 200 bps of macro headwinds.
- Dhingra described the cost reduction programs as multi-year, with prioritization of key growth investments.

## 3. Q&A

### Douglas Schenkel, Wolfe Research

**Question.** Does the 2026 guide embed an exit-rate operating margin of 26%-27% by year-end? Is the 2027 margin target changed by SomaLogic? How is management thinking about capital deployment, given a clean balance sheet and about $1B of free cash flow, and what are the M&A criteria and priorities?

**Thaysen's answer on targets.**
- In 2024 Illumina said it would return to high-single-digit growth by 2027 and add 500 bps of operating margin, which gets to about 26%. Management still feels "great" about these targets.
- The company has moved into growth, and Thaysen believes it is on the right path to high-single-digit growth in 2027 in the core business.
- Margins rose about 200 bps in 2025 despite about 200 bps of tariff and other headwinds, and 130 bps more is committed for 2026.
- SomaLogic is dilutive as a starting point. Management is working on synergies and "will get as close to the 26% operating margin as possible in '27."

**Dhingra's answer on exit rate.**
- Dhingra said Q4 exit-rate margins are usually higher than the full-year average because of normal seasonality.
- The full-year guide excluding SomaLogic is getting close to 24.5%, and the Q4 exit rate "generally tends to be higher than that." Dhingra did not confirm the 26%-27% figure.

**Capital deployment and M&A.** The transcript shows no answer to this part of the question.

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI

**Question.** Q4 organic ex-China growth was about 6.5% (the analyst's figure) and the 2026 guide is about 3%. What explains the step down? Was anything in Q4 one-off or pulled forward?

**Thaysen's answer.**
- Momentum from H2 is expected to continue into 2026. Clinical guidance is mid-teens.
- The research and academic environment remains challenging. Visibility on the NIH budget is a step forward, but uncertainty remains about how institutes distribute grants and who receives them, so the environment is still "muted."
- Improvement during the year could sustain or improve performance. Thaysen sees no substantial step down. Q4 is always a strong quarter, and management feels good about the guide.

**Dhingra's answer.**
- Dhingra said clinical momentum is unchanged, with customers doing well on both on-market tests and new tests.
- The 2%-4% ex-China range was built from H2 2025, which Dhingra described as an average of Q3 (+2% ex-China) and Q4 (+7%).
- Q3 is the low end of the range, with clinical growing double-digit and research down high single digits. Research improvement would be upside.
- Management did not want to use a single quarter (Q4) as the base for the full-year guide, though "momentum is pretty good."

### Puneet Souda, Leerink Partners

**Question.** What was the research versus clinical split of instrument placements? What is baked in for research in both mid- and high-throughput, and for competition beyond academic pressure?

**Thaysen's answer.**
- More than 100 X placements in Q4, with over 60% clinical, as expected. Clinical should be the majority of placements through 2026.
- Customers keep building assays on the X, with no slowdown.
- The mid-throughput business has been more challenged by macro trends, with research and academic customers more conservative about spending. The market is still muted but not changed from 2025.

**Dhingra's answer.**
- Little competition was seen in Q4, with strong placements across clinical and research.
- Interest in new launches is very good. The 5-base product has launched (it reads methylation along with the DNA sequence) and spatial is in early access.
- The 2026 research view rests on the funding environment rather than competition.

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies

**Question.** How much clinical growth is mix (larger tests) versus volume? What is the thinking on acquiring PacBio's "Onso" technology and how quickly could it be incorporated? What is baked in for spatial, constellation and 5-base?

**Thaysen's answer.**
- Clinical customers are seeing significant volume uptake. More physicians use genetic profiling for cancer and for monitoring disease. Customers are also building larger panels for therapy selection and expanding panels for MRD, and some genetic customers are moving from exome to whole genome.
- Illumina is not splitting growth into mix and volume. Over time Thaysen expects intensity (larger panels) to be the main growth driver.
- On the PacBio IP acquisition: it was a "great opportunity to strengthen our portfolio" and a way to keep optionality. Thaysen is "very bullish" about Illumina's own SBS (sequencing by synthesis) technology and sees many options left in it. No timeline for incorporating the technology was given.
- Adjacencies are early. Interest has been "very, very strong," but they will not be meaningful in 2026. They should become meaningful in 2027 and beyond, when 1%-2% of growth is expected to come from new multiomics assays and the data business.

### Michael Ryskin, BofA

**Question.** The consumables and instruments guides look closer than expected: consumables might be better as price-transition headwinds fade, and instruments worse. Are 2026 NovaSeq X placements similar to 2025 (the analyst said mid-to-high 200s)? What about NextSeq, other mid-throughput platforms and the NovaSeq 6000?

**Thaysen's answer.**
- High-throughput clinical consumables are very strong and could have upside if the market stays as strong as late 2025.
- Blended consumables, including research and mid-throughput, still face headwinds. The low end of the consumables guide is roughly Q3's level, and the high end reflects the momentum since then.
- On instruments, guidance of 50-60 placements per quarter is in place, but many clinical customers keep expanding, so there is potential strength.

**Dhingra's answer.**
- For X units, Dhingra confirmed about 200-240 for the year, with 50-60 a quarter as "still quite good."
- Q4 placements were "phenomenal," with several multipack orders. Some customers are considering "tens of Xs" fleet expansions.
- A major software upgrade to the X in early 2025 means performance is running above spec for a large part of customers. Clinical customers see extremely high performance, and research customers are excited about the new multiomic technologies enabled on the X.
- The NextSeq and other mid-throughput platforms were not separately addressed.

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord

**Question.** Analyst estimates oncology at just under $1B in 2025 and genetic disease at just under $0.5B. Which grows faster in 2026? Is BioInsight a needle mover this year?

**Thaysen's answer.**
- Clinical is growing across applications, regions and customer types.
- Oncology is the main growth driver and will remain so over the next year. Rare disease is also growing at a healthy pace.
- BioInsight was not directly answered here. Elsewhere on the call, management said new multiomics and data offerings should add 1%-2% to growth from 2027.

### Daniel Arias, Stifel

**Question.** Illumina argues that labs should evaluate the full workflow. When and where will it show customers the economics of Fluent plus NovaSeq plus DRAGEN? Can customers see the math?

**Thaysen's answer.**
- This is the conversation already under way with customers, and AGBT, with Gold sponsorship and key opinion leaders, will be a platform for it.
- The goal is to show a more cost-efficient route to the highest-quality insight at the lowest end-to-end cost.
- Constellation mapped reads is an example. It needs essentially no upfront hands-on workflow, eliminating library prep cost, so pricing is transparent because it is "really just the cost of sequencing," whereas others must also pay for library prep.
- For SomaLogic, Illumina will present cost per sample and cost per experiment. More will come at AGBT.

### Jack Meehan, Nephron Research

**Question.** At least one competitor has talked about a $100 genome. Given Illumina lists prices but discounts, what would such headlines mean for Illumina?

**Thaysen's answer.**
- AGBT is where all competitors will put their best foot forward, and Illumina will too.
- Customers are "more sophisticated than just looking at one element," and Illumina will highlight whole-workflow strengths.
- Illumina has "the portfolio, … the pipeline … the capability to compete on all parameters."
- If there are segments with significant elasticity where a different price point makes sense, "we are all for it," and these conversations with customers happen regularly.

### Casey Woodring, JPMorgan

**Question.** How are China import conversations going and what is the range of outcomes? How will BioInsight be monetized, how enthusiastic is pharma, and can the revenue opportunity be quantified?

**Thaysen on China.**
- China is still less than 5% of the business. Thaysen praised the China general manager (named as "Jenny") and the team for serving customers.
- Illumina has had "great conversations and collaboration" with Chinese regulators to keep operating. It remains on the UEL (unreliable entity list, the Chinese government list behind the import restrictions), but Thaysen feels good about the relationship and the path to importing instruments.
- After a long period without instrument sales, rebuilding the funnel takes time, which is what the guide reflects.
- Getting off the list or other improvements would mean upside. Management has good line of sight to 2026.

**Thaysen on BioInsight.**
- It is early days. The Billion Cell Atlas was presented at JPMorgan's healthcare conference in January 2026 and was well received, with many pharma conversations about access. It helps pharma pick the right targets.
- With multiomics, BioInsight should create at least 1%-2% growth in 2027, with accelerating momentum.

**Dhingra on BioInsight.**
- Illumina will work directly with pharma customers, effectively adding a third customer base. Over a five-year view it is a "very meaningful opportunity." Interest since the conference has been "tremendous."
- Monetization will come in two or three ways: specialized data and AI tool construction first, then, over time, significant subscription models for pharma on both discovery and development. It is a multiyear opportunity, and no revenue figure was given.

### David Westenberg, Piper Sandler

**Question.**
1. What are conversations with academic core labs about instrument investment, and what does the guide assume for academic consumables and instruments? Can Eric Green help academic confidence, given NIH is only slightly up?
2. A follow-up on competitor pricing: better specs mean more output, which cuts customers' cost per gigabase, so even at a headline price (the analyst used "25p at $200") customers effectively pay less. Is that right?

**Thaysen's answer.**
- Over about 18 months Illumina has built out relationships with academic core labs and supported them through a tough environment hit by NIH and other funding. When labs have opportunities to acquire instruments, Illumina has made sure it can place access in them. Thaysen said feedback from labs is good.
- With Green aboard there are more ways to help labs navigate. There is better line of sight to NIH funding, but details still need to fall into place before institutes and labs move ahead.
- The specific academic guidance assumptions were not quantified beyond the mid-to-high single-digit research consumables decline.
- On specs: Illumina has a tradition of delivering on par with or better than specifications, and customers know it does not commit to what it cannot keep. Thaysen said "many could learn from" that, and that customers are enjoying the benefits. This broadly agrees with the analyst's point but does not confirm the cost-per-gigabase math.

### Subhalaxmi (Subbu) Nambi, Guggenheim

**Question.** NovaSeq 6000 pull-through (consumables use per installed instrument) is holding up better than expected. Has it leveled off, or is it still a material headwind? How will Europe and Asia look versus the strong U.S. clinical market?

**Thaysen's answer.**
- Customers moving to the X often build new assays there and keep the 6000 for validated, traditional assays, so pull-through stays up. Others have shifted fully to the X and see substantial growth.
- There will be fewer 6000 customers over time.
- Europe has done "tremendously well" with nice growth in recent years, and that is expected to continue in 2026. APAC and EMEA are expected to "rebound somewhat."

**Dhingra's answer.**
- On the research side the 6000-to-X transition is substantially complete, and clinical is in the latter part of its transition.
- By the end of 2026 the transition should be mostly done by volume, in line with recent trends.

## 4. Points to Note

- **Terminology.** Management uses "ex-China" and "rest of world" interchangeably for the base on which organic growth is measured.
- **Possible transcription errors.** Thaysen said Q4 was 20% clinical consumables growth "in '27 -- excuse me, in Q4 in '25," and referred to the "$1 billion -- excuse me, 1 Billion Cell Atlas."
- **Discrepancy.** The "6.5%" Q4 organic ex-China rate and "3%" guide midpoint come from Vijay Kumar's question and were not confirmed as such by management. Management's own Q4 figure is +7% ex-China.
- **Truncated answer.** An "Operator: Your next question will come..." line appears mid-answer in the Schenkel exchange, so the M&A and capital deployment answer appears to be missing from the transcript.
- **Not covered.** No analyst ratings, price targets or estimate changes appear in this document.
