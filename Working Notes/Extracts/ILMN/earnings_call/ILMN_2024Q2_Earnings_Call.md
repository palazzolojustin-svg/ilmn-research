# Illumina Q2 2024 Earnings Call (Aug 6, 2024): Core Illumina results, NovaSeq X transition disclosures, cut to 2024 revenue guidance, raised margin guidance, new EPS guidance

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2024Q2_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2024-08-06 · Seeking Alpha transcript of the Illumina Q2 2024 earnings call; speakers Salli Schwartz (VP Investor Relations), Jacob Thaysen (CEO), Ankur Dhingra (CFO); analysts from Evercore ISI, RBC, Citi, Wolfe, Stifel, Jefferies, Guggenheim, Leerink, Scotiabank, Bernstein, Piper Sandler, TD Cowen · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Core Illumina (the business excluding the divested GRAIL cancer-test unit) Q2 2024 revenue of $1.1 billion, down 6% year over year but ahead of guidance, with non-GAAP operating margin of 22.2% versus a guided 18%. Management cut full-year 2024 revenue guidance to down 2% to 3% (instruments weaker, consumables slightly stronger, China weaker), raised operating margin guidance to 20.5%-21%, and introduced non-GAAP EPS guidance of $3.80-$3.95. The call added new disclosures on the shift of high-throughput customers from NovaSeq 6000 to NovaSeq X and its pricing effects, and set up the August 13, 2024 strategy update.

## Results and guidance

### Context
This is the Q2 2024 earnings call for Illumina, which makes DNA sequencing instruments and the consumables (the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every sequencing run) that run on them. The call was held August 6, 2024. Illumina divested GRAIL (its cancer-detection test business) in June 2024, so management discussed only "Core Illumina," meaning Illumina without GRAIL. Illumina kept a minority 14.5% stake in GRAIL. Consolidated results including GRAIL are in the earnings release and SEC filings, not on the call. [Q2 2024 call]

The Seeking Alpha header summarizes the quarter versus analyst consensus (the average analyst forecast): EPS of $0.36 missed by $0.54, and revenue of $1.11 billion (down 5.44% year over year) beat by $26.63 million. These are the transcript site's figures, and they are on a consolidated basis, which differs from the Core Illumina non-GAAP figures management discussed. [Seeking Alpha header]

### Drivers of the quarter
- **Revenue beat.** Dhingra said the above-expectations result came mainly from rising high-throughput sequencing consumables revenue as customers ramped activity on the NovaSeq X Plus (Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer). This was partly offset by fewer-than-expected mid-throughput instrument shipments, because capital and cash-flow constraints affect customer purchasing. [Q2 2024 call]
- **Consumables.** High-throughput consumable shipments grew both year over year and sequentially, which Dhingra called a "particularly bright spot." [Q2 2024 call]
- **Instruments.** The 40% year-over-year drop had two causes: fewer NovaSeq X placements compared with large pre-order launch shipments in Q2 2023, and an expected decline in mid-throughput shipments. [Q2 2024 call]
- **Services and other.** Growth came from strategic partnerships and higher instrument service contract revenue on a growing installed base. [Q2 2024 call]
- **Gross margin.** It rose on a more favorable mix of consumables and on operational excellence initiatives that improved productivity and cut costs. [Q2 2024 call]
- **Operating margin.** It beat the 18% guide because of higher revenue, better gross margin and lower operating expenses (from headcount reductions and other cost containment). [Q2 2024 call]
- **Sequencing activity.** Total gigabases (Gb, a unit of sequencing data output) on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 40% year over year and about 10% from the prior quarter. Growth was healthy in both research and applied/clinical customers. Dhingra said Gb output is not a predictor of near-term revenue but shows underlying demand and instrument utilization. [Q2 2024 call]
- **Regional revenue (Thaysen).** Americas was relatively flat year over year; Europe down 5% (against a large backlog worked through early 2023 after the X launch); AMEA (Asia, Middle East and Africa) down 8%; Greater China down 35%. [Q2 2024 call]

### NovaSeq X transition disclosures (new in this call)
Dhingra said Illumina is "increasing our disclosures around the NovaSeq X to give you a clearer picture of the progress." The transition matters because customers moving from the older NovaSeq 6000 to the NovaSeq X pay much less per gigabase, so volume growth does not translate one-for-one into revenue. [Q2 2024 call]

- As of Q2, about 45% of high-throughput gigabases sequenced, and a little over 25% of high-throughput consumables revenue, were on the NovaSeq X Plus.
- On average, roughly 5 percentage points of high-throughput consumables revenue have moved from NovaSeq 6000 to X Plus each quarter.
- If that trajectory holds, almost half of high-throughput consumables revenue should be on the NovaSeq X by mid-2025. Dhingra said this progressively reduces the price effect and converts volume growth into higher revenue growth afterward.
- Thaysen said the NovaSeq X Plus average annual pull-through (consumables revenue per installed instrument per year) has topped $1 million, a milestone.
- Illumina placed 62 NovaSeq X Plus instruments in Q2, bringing the X Plus installed base to 469.

### 2024 guidance (full year, Core Illumina)
Management described "puts and takes": constrained customer capital spending on one side, solid high-throughput consumables from the X transition on the other. [Q2 2024 call]

| Item | New guidance | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Down 2% to 3% from 2023 (down 1.5% to 2.5% constant currency) | Reduced. About half of the cut is lower expectations for China and broader Asia; the other half is reduced mid-throughput and NovaSeq X shipment expectations |
| Sequencing instruments revenue | Down in the mid-30s percent versus 2023 (at midpoint of revenue range) | Lowered |
| Sequencing consumables revenue | Growth toward the upper end of the low single-digit range versus 2023 (at midpoint) | Slightly stronger view |
| Non-GAAP operating margin | 20.5% to 21% | Raised; reflects Q2 outperformance plus lower second-half operating expenses |
| Non-GAAP tax rate | About 25% | |

Dhingra added that new initiatives under the internal continuous improvement program are expected to deliver an additional $200 million in expense savings "over the next few years," to be detailed at the strategy update. [Q2 2024 call]

### Q3 2024 guidance
- Core Illumina revenue of $1.075 billion to $1.085 billion. The year-over-year decline is mainly from lower NovaSeq X instrument shipments, given the large backlog worked through in 2023 after launch.
- Non-GAAP operating margin of about 20%. The sequential decline from Q2 is mainly lower revenue and timing of project spend delayed from Q2.
- Non-GAAP tax rate of about 25%.
- Non-GAAP diluted EPS of $0.80 to $0.90, including the term-loan interest. [Q2 2024 call]

## Prepared remarks

### Salli Schwartz (VP Investor Relations)
She opened the call, noted that GRAIL was divested in June, and pointed listeners to the earnings release and SEC filings for GRAIL and consolidated results. [Q2 2024 call]

### Jacob Thaysen (CEO): business update
**Customer environment.** Thaysen said he meets customers and partners worldwide, who are excited about launching next-generation products. Science (multiomics, meaning measuring several biological layers such as DNA, RNA and proteins, and whole genome sequencing) and the integration of genomics into health care create opportunity. But he said the global economy has "many moving elements," Illumina is "taking a more measured view," customers "feel constrained to make significant capital outlays," and this shows up in sales-cycle delays and pushed-out orders. [Q2 2024 call]

**Two jobs.** Job one is to stabilize the base through the NovaSeq X rollout and operational excellence. Job two is accelerating growth, which will be covered at the strategy update (virtual, Tuesday August 13, 2024), including vision, technology from the "R&D vault," and a multi-year financial outlook. [Q2 2024 call]

**Guidance posture.** Thaysen said Illumina is "adding caution to our guide": a more prudent view on instruments because of extended sales cycles, and a slightly stronger view on consumables. [Q2 2024 call]

**Priority 1: driving the top line.**
- NovaSeq X Plus: 62 placed in Q2, 469 total, pull-through above $1 million, and a corresponding rise in sequencing activity as projects move over.
- Mid-throughput (NextSeq 1000/2000): XLEAP-SBS chemistry (a newer sequencing chemistry with improved quality, more usable reads, faster turnaround and attractive pricing) was added in Q2 to the P1, P2 and P3 flow cells, complementing the P4 flow cell launched earlier in 2024. Thaysen said the launch "has exceeded our expectations," with more than 60% of the 1000/2000 installed base having upgraded software, a leading indicator of adoption.
- However, translation of XLEAP interest into extra instrument sales "has been less than we expected." Mid-throughput is "the most sensitive to the macroeconomic environment," sales cycles keep lengthening because of constrained capital, but win rates are stable and the pipeline has grown. [Q2 2024 call]

**Priority 2: operational excellence.**
- Everett Cunningham joined as Chief Commercial Officer.
- Marketing and commercial functions were combined into a new commercial organization to serve customers as "partners for innovation," with go-to-market expertise for multiomics and for selling software across research and clinical customers.
- A portfolio optimization strategy aims at supply-chain productivity; a number of products will be rationalized to focus on more profitable offerings, with customer transition support.
- Progress continues toward greater operating leverage in 2024. [Q2 2024 call]

**Priority 3: resolving GRAIL.** The spin-off completed in June; GRAIL is an independent public company and Illumina kept 14.5%. Illumina will keep supporting GRAIL with sequencing technology and services. [Q2 2024 call]

### Ankur Dhingra (CFO)
Dhingra said that in his first three months he has spent time with teams across functions, found the team mission-driven and transformation "taking hold," and has been on an investor listening tour. His financial remarks are summarized in the results and guidance sections above. [Q2 2024 call]

### Thaysen: innovation and closing remarks
- **DRAGEN** (Illumina's genomic data-analysis software): a new version launched with its most accurate multi-genome mapping, machine-learning advances, and the ability to genotype difficult genes. Thaysen said bioinformatics has become a key driver of customer decisions.
- **NovaSeq X Series:** announced late 2022 with two systems. The X Plus has shipped globally since March 2023. The single-flow-cell NovaSeq X, a lower-cost entry point, will begin shipping in Q4 2024 and be upgradable to the X Plus.
- **Q4 2024 launches:** 100-cycle and 200-cycle 25B flow cells for high-output counting applications such as single cell and proteomics, and a software update for the X and X Plus enabling increased yield.
- **Fluent BioSciences acquisition:** single-cell technology that sorts and labels complex cell mixtures for sequencing. Thaysen said it will make single-cell analysis available to a broader set of customers and, together with the Partek multiomics software acquisition, integrates customer workflows. Illumina will "remain an open NGS platform" and is committed to existing single-cell partnerships. [Q2 2024 call]

## Question-and-answer session

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI
**Question:** Tying the guidance to the X commentary, is the gap between 40% data growth and the revenue contribution the pricing impact, and is that how to model the transition? How much of the guidance change is high-throughput versus mid-throughput instruments (no X orders number was given)?

**Answer:** Thaysen said the new disclosures are meant to help modeling, and he highlighted the $1 million pull-through milestone. Dhingra said about 45% of high-throughput consumable volume is on X, so half of volume should move in the second half of 2024 if the trajectory holds, with roughly 5 points of mix shifting each quarter and about half of revenue on X by next year. Overall Gb sequenced grew about 40% (across high- and mid-throughput); high-throughput revenue grew about 1%, and the difference is roughly pricing and mix. He did not give an X orders number or a high- versus mid-throughput split of the guide cut in this answer. [Q2 2024 call]

### Conor McNamara, RBC Capital Markets
**Question:** If volume was up 40% and consumables flat, is a roughly 40% pricing headwind the right way to think about it as the 5-point shift continues? Also, are equipment prices changing?

**Answer (pricing):** Dhingra said the headwind is higher early on and shrinks as more volume moves to X. Looking at the whole transition from 6000 to X, the all-in price decline would easily be more than 60% to 70% on a Gb-to-Gb basis, but the baseline keeps coming down as mix improves. He said the volume midpoint comes in the back half of 2024 and the revenue midpoint by mid-2025. [Q2 2024 call]

**Answer (instrument pricing):** Thaysen said NovaSeq X Plus pricing has been "pretty much stable." The single-flow-cell NovaSeq X offers a lower price point for capital-constrained customers, who can later upgrade to the X Plus to use both flow cells; he expects interest when it launches in Q4. [Q2 2024 call]

### Patrick Donnelly, Citi
**Question:** On the $200 million cost savings initiative: pacing, where the opportunities are, and whether R&D (over 20% of revenue) is a lever; are any areas off limits?

**Answer:** Thaysen said "there's certainly no off-limits area," all elements of the P&L are being examined, and he would not give pacing until the next week. On R&D, he said he values each project internally using measures such as net present value (NPV) and return on invested capital (ROIC) rather than a top-down budget, and is not "just driving by an overall number" but focusing on programs that drive growth and shareholder value. Dhingra said this is a step in a multi-year margin expansion strategy aimed at a structurally better, sustainable cost structure across all lines. [Q2 2024 call]

### Doug Schenkel, Wolfe Research
**Question:** Consumables bottomed at $687 million in Q4 2023, rose to just under $700 million in Q1 and just under $740 million in Q2, while instruments look stable at about $110-120 million a quarter. Doesn't that math imply a return to mid-single-digit-plus revenue growth in 2025?

**Answer:** Thaysen agreed that consumables growth is the right indicator for a turn to better performance, but said it is too early to discuss 2025 given moving parts: the U.S. election, Middle East concerns and interest rates. He said Illumina is doing everything to return to a better growth trajectory than in recent years. Dhingra said Schenkel was pointing to the right metrics and that the X transition, high-throughput consumables and shrinking price impact are positive. He noted Gb output growth was over 35% in Q1 and over 40% in Q2, versus a five-year average in the 20s to mid-20s percent, and said fundamentals seem to be moving in the right direction but it is too early to call 2025. [Q2 2024 call]

### Dan Arias, Stifel
**Question:** How is the clinical community adopting the X, what to expect from clinical labs adopting the 25B kit, and does later clinical adoption mean an added pricing headwind in the second half or 2025, or can elasticity (more volume at lower prices) help?

**Answer:** Thaysen said academia has moved faster than clinical because clinical labs must validate assays on the X before production. Many clinical customers keep currently validated assays on the 6000 and focus new assays on the X, and a few new-assay releases have appeared in recent weeks. Nearly all big clinical customers already have multiple X-series systems in their laboratories, so he does not foresee material swings in the transition and is "not too concerned about certain change in trajectory." [Q2 2024 call]

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies
**Question 1:** Why is the mid-throughput weakness not a competitive issue?

**Answer:** Thaysen said Illumina takes competition seriously but has always had competitors, and he has taken a "very different approach" and proactive stance since arriving. Mid-throughput is the most competitive area, and China remains an issue with competitive pressure that "hasn't really changed over the past quarters." Illumina tracks each deal closely and sees no change in win rates, which have been range-bound for several quarters, so it attributes most of the weakness to the economic environment. [Q2 2024 call]

**Question 2:** With half of high-throughput consumables on X by mid-2025, how much more must transition, and should 2025 be below the coming long-range plan?

**Answer:** Dhingra clarified that volume crosses the halfway mark in the second half of 2024 (45% in Q2), and half of revenue transitions in 2025. With each 5 to 7 points of mix, the price impact falls and volume contributes more to revenue. He said 2025 has several moving parts and will be addressed with guidance. [Q2 2024 call]

**Question 3:** Is the $200 million program the cost-out to be discussed at the Analyst Day, over three years?

**Answer:** Thaysen said more will come the next week, with a comprehensive view of growth, margin expansion and earnings power over a three-year period. [Q2 2024 call]

### Subhu Nambi, Guggenheim
**Question:** What is the mix of clinical versus research for NovaSeq X placements, new versus replacement among clinical customers, and are existing customers moving existing assays to X or using it for new, deeper-sequencing assays?

**Answer:** Dhingra said Illumina has not provided that split on an ongoing basis; it has generally said roughly half and half. Clinical customers' general approach is to develop newer tests on X at launch, where high volumes then appear. Thaysen agreed and said new tests are more sequencing-intensive, with customers wanting larger panels, whole genome sequencing with new insights and deeper sequencing. In academia, single cell is strong and he sees an uptick in spatial (mapping gene activity within tissue), which requires much higher sequencing intensity. [Q2 2024 call]

### Puneet Souda, Leerink Partners
**Question 1:** Can cost per gigabase be a lever in mid-throughput, or will the single-flow-cell NovaSeq X pricing address a competitive gap?

**Answer:** Thaysen said both. The single-flow-cell X can run all applications, including the 25B flow cell at X Plus-level pricing. In mid-throughput, Illumina could use pricing if needed but has already priced "to a very, very competitive" level. He said the issue is more complicated, that competitors sometimes get "caught up in very too narrow definitions of claims," looking at interim workflow steps, limited applications or limited data. He argued that with DRAGEN's full computational chain, quality and total workflow cost, Illumina stays "highly differentiated" even at a premium cost per gigabase. He acknowledged Illumina could communicate this better and promised examples from the R&D vault at the strategy update. [Q2 2024 call]

**Question 2:** Why change the commercial organization now?

**Answer:** Thaysen said he wanted to bring marketing and sales together before Everett arrived, creating one commercial team with aligned leadership. They also looked at putting more quota-carrying people in the field and strengthening application capabilities as Illumina moves into multiomics and informatics. He said it is less about changing regions and more about "adding more feet on the ground." [Q2 2024 call]

**Question 3:** Where might pull-through eventually land?

**Answer:** Thaysen said $1 million is not the exit point or equilibrium, some customers are already much higher, and Illumina is not ready to share a detailed view. [Q2 2024 call]

### Sung Ji Nam, Scotiabank
**Question:** How has the outlook for China, Greater China and AMEA changed since last quarter, and do you expect significant deterioration?

**Answer (Thaysen):** It is too early to call where China is in the cycle; the economy is weak and customers constrained, with no material change in competitive intensity, but it is a tough market. A new head of the China region (hired and in place a little more than a quarter) is optimizing the commercial structure by moving more people from the back office to the field, resetting partnerships, considering targeted pricing for specific product groups, and bringing more IVD (regulated diagnostic) products to China. He expects it to turn eventually but would not give deeper insight now. [Q2 2024 call]

**Answer (Dhingra):** Prior estimates did not assume a material China recovery, though they implied some sequential build even while down year over year. China has been weak for 6 to 8 quarters and shows no signs of recovery, so Illumina removed any assumed volume increase for the rest of the year to de-risk. The full-year midpoint is about 1.5 points lower (about 2 points on a constant currency basis, as stated), and almost two-thirds of that is China. [Q2 2024 call] (Note: this differs from his prepared remark that about half the cut was China and broader Asia; the "two-thirds" refers to China alone in the Q&A framing.)

### Eve Burstein, Bernstein
**Question:** Quest and LabCorp recently announced or expanded collaborations with a competitor. What share of revenue do they represent, and why shouldn't this be read as a sign of clinical share loss?

**Answer:** Thaysen did not give the revenue share and suggested asking those companies about their decisions. He said there is "a little bit over rotation" on price for a very limited application space that does not address today's patients or whole genomes. He argued that customers also need confidence that a vendor can provide and support the product, and that Illumina focuses on total workflow cost and quality of answers, not one element such as sequencing cost. [Q2 2024 call]

### David Westenberg, Piper Sandler
**Question 1:** What is the dollar impact of the XLEAP pricing transition in this year's guidance, and what validation is needed for clinical customers?

**Answer:** Thaysen said 60% of the installed base has downloaded the software, indicating interest, with notable demand for the P4 flow cell and now P1-P3, at a different price point with higher quality and capacity. On validation, he said it depends on the assay; normal validation applies as with any software or chemistry change, and Illumina requires no specific extra steps. Dhingra said there will be a pricing transition from prior consumables to XLEAP, but it is significantly smaller and likely more proactive than on the X side, because mid-throughput consumables are a much smaller portion of revenue than X-related consumables. He did not give a dollar figure. [Q2 2024 call]

**Question 2:** On Fluent, what about pricing, bundling and intellectual property (IP) given it uses an emulsion step rather than microfluidics?

**Answer:** Thaysen said Fluent allows broader single-cell adoption, especially in mid-throughput and high-volume applications where per-cell cost is competitive. It is too early to discuss commercialization, but Illumina will keep working with all single-cell providers. He did not directly address IP or bundling. [Q2 2024 call]

### Dan Brennan, TD Cowen
**Question 1:** Is there really little margin drag as mid-throughput customers adopt XLEAP, given a $9 per Gb figure versus on-label NextSeq prices in the teens or low 20s?

**Answer:** Dhingra said there will be a margin drag, but list prices differ from on-market prices, and mid-throughput consumables are a relatively small part of the P&L, so the drag on total Illumina should be "generally manageable." Thaysen added that R&D and manufacturing lowered the cost of XLEAP chemistry, so it carries a lower cost of goods sold (COGS) and less margin drag. [Q2 2024 call]

**Question 2:** Any breakdown of research versus clinical growth, and what is assumed in the 2024 guide?

**Answer:** Thaysen said Illumina does not normally split it out but is "growing healthy on both sides" of academic and clinical. [Q2 2024 call]
