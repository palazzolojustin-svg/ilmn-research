# Illumina (ILMN) Q1 2024 Earnings Call: Core Illumina results, reiterated 2024 guidance, Q2 outlook, GRAIL divestment progress and analyst Q&A

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2024Q1_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2024-05-02 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Illumina's Q1 2024 earnings call; speakers Salli Schwartz (VP Investor Relations), Jacob Thaysen (CEO), Ankur Dhingra (CFO), Joydeep Goswami (advisor, former CFO); analysts from Wolfe, Leerink, Evercore ISI, TD Cowen, Stifel, Piper Sandler, Guggenheim, Scotiabank, Morgan Stanley, Stephens, JPMorgan, Citi, Bernstein and RBC · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q1 2024 Core Illumina revenue of about $1.06 billion (down 2% year over year), ahead of its own expectations, helped by high-throughput consumables, partnership revenue timing and a few early NovaSeq X deliveries. It reiterated full-year 2024 Core guidance (revenue about flat, non-GAAP operating margin about 20%) and guided Q2 Core revenue down 6.5 to 7.5 points year over year with an operating margin of about 18%. Management said the macro and China remain weak, the European Commission approved the GRAIL divestment plan, terms are due by end of Q2, and a full strategy update will come in the fall of 2024.

## Summary of the call

This was Illumina's first-quarter 2024 earnings call, held on May 2, 2024. It was the first call with new CFO Ankur Dhingra, who had joined about two and a half weeks earlier. Management focused on "Core Illumina," meaning Illumina excluding GRAIL (the cancer-detection-test company that Illumina is being forced to divest). Core Illumina beat the company's own expectations. Management nonetheless stayed cautious, citing a difficult global economy in which customers are constrained in their purchasing, no meaningful improvement in China, and a strengthening US dollar.

The main points:
- Core revenue was about $1.06 billion, down 2% year over year. Management said this beat its expectations [Q1 2024 call].
- Full-year 2024 Core guidance was reiterated, not raised: revenue about flat versus 2023 and non-GAAP operating margin about 20%.
- Q2 Core revenue was guided to $1.072–1.084 billion, with an operating margin of about 18%.
- The European Commission approved Illumina's GRAIL divestment plan in April. Management committed to finalizing terms by the end of Q2.
- Illumina will hold a virtual strategy-update event in the fall of 2024. The CEO declined to give long-term growth or margin targets before then.

## Results and guidance

### Headline reported figures

The Seeking Alpha header gave the market-facing summary. It reported non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS) of $0.09, which missed by $0.02. Consolidated revenue was $1.08 billion, down 1.01% year over year, which beat by $25.23 million. This header is from the transcript publisher, not from management. "Non-GAAP" means the company's adjusted results, which exclude certain items. Illumina's version includes stock-based compensation.

Management said the EPS figure exceeded the company's own expectations, "driven primarily by higher core revenue and lower core operating expenses in the quarter" (CFO Dhingra) [Q1 2024 call]. The header's "miss" therefore reflects a comparison with outside analyst expectations, not management's internal plan.

### Core Illumina Q1 2024 results (all non-GAAP, per CFO Dhingra)

The table below shows each Core Illumina line, its Q1 2024 value and its change versus Q1 2023.

| Item | Q1 2024 | Change / comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Core revenue | ~$1.06 billion | Down 2% year over year, both reported and in constant currency (i.e., excluding exchange-rate effects) |
| Sequencing consumables revenue | $698 million | Up 1% year over year, mainly high-throughput growth |
| Sequencing instruments revenue | $110 million | Down 29% year over year |
| Sequencing services and other revenue | $151 million | Up 27% year over year |
| Gross margin | 67.1% | Up 190 basis points (1.9 percentage points) year over year |
| Operating expenses | $491 million | Down $23 million year over year |
| Operating margin | 20.6% | Versus 17.4% in Q1 2023 |

**Drivers of the revenue beat.** Dhingra said the beat versus expectations came from three things:
1. Strong performance in high-throughput consumables (the reagent kits and flow cells used on the largest instruments).
2. Timing of revenue from certain strategic partnerships.
3. Some customers pulling forward delivery of a few NovaSeq X instruments from Q2 into Q1.

**Consumables.** Growth came primarily from high throughput. Sequentially (versus Q4 2023), NovaSeq X consumables grew in the double digits after the successful launch of the 25B flow cell (the largest-capacity consumable for the NovaSeq X). Total sequencing output, measured in gigabases (GB) on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments, grew over 35% year over year and at a high-single-digit rate versus the prior quarter. Management called this activity data "a useful reference" that is "directionally correlated with revenue over time." Research and applied activity benefited as the transition to the NovaSeq X ramps and 25B adoption grows at large core labs (shared sequencing facilities). Clinical activity was still driven mainly by the older NovaSeq 6000.

**Instruments.** The 29% decline had two causes:
- An expected drop in mid-throughput shipments, as capital and cash-flow constraints at customers moderate placements.
- Fewer NovaSeq X placements than in Q1 2023, which had significant launch-related shipments to fill a strong pre-order book.

**Services and other.** The 27% growth came from higher revenue from strategic partnerships and higher instrument service-contract revenue on a growing installed base. In Q&A, Dhingra gave more detail (see Patrick Donnelly below).

**Gross margin.** The 190-basis-point rise came from a more favorable mix toward sequencing consumables and from operational-excellence cost savings, including freight and productivity. It was partly offset by lower-margin strategic partnership revenue and higher warranty and field-service costs.

**Operating expenses.** The $23 million decline came mainly from reduced labor expense after headcount cuts and continued cost-containment savings. Expenses were lower than expected for two reasons: project spending shifted from Q1 into Q2, and stock-based compensation was lower because of one-time reversals.

### GRAIL Q1 2024 results

GRAIL revenue was $27 million, up 35% year over year, driven primarily by adoption of Galleri (GRAIL's multi-cancer blood test). GRAIL non-GAAP operating expenses were $197 million, up $24 million year over year, driven by higher headcount for commercial expansion and R&D. GRAIL's non-GAAP operating loss was $185 million for the quarter.

### Consolidated Q1 2024 results (Core plus GRAIL)

- **Revenue:** $1.08 billion, down 1% year over year, both reported and in constant currency.
- **Net income and EPS:** non-GAAP net income was $14 million, or $0.09 per diluted share. This includes the dilution from GRAIL's $185 million operating loss.
- **Tax rate:** the non-GAAP tax rate was 46.4%, versus 27.3% in Q1 2023. Both periods were distorted by consolidating GRAIL's operating loss, which grew by $21 million year over year. Excluding GRAIL, the rate was in the mid-20s in both years.
- **Share count:** weighted average diluted shares were about 159 million.
- **Cash flow:** cash from operations was $77 million. Capital expenditures were $36 million, so free cash flow (cash from operations minus capital spending) was $41 million. No common stock was repurchased.
- **Cash position:** about $1.12 billion in cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments at quarter end.

### Regional revenue (Core Illumina, year over year, per CEO Thaysen)

Thaysen said three regions declined. The Americas fell 4%, EMEA (Europe, Middle East and Africa) fell 3%, and Greater China fell 14%. He also said "Europe revenue was up 7%" year over year, but warned of a decline in Q2 because of strong NovaSeq X shipments to Europe in Q2 2023. The transcript lists both EMEA down 3% and Europe up 7% without reconciling them, so the two figures should be treated with caution.

### Guidance

Guidance covers Core Illumina only, because of uncertainty over the timing and impact of the GRAIL divestment. It assumes no impact from a GRAIL divestment in 2024. Non-GAAP EPS guidance will be provided only after the divestment is complete.

| Item | Guidance | Change from prior |
|---|---|---|
| FY2024 Core revenue | About flat versus 2023 | Reiterated, unchanged |
| FY2024 Core non-GAAP operating margin | About 20% | Reiterated, unchanged |
| Q2 2024 Core revenue | $1.072–1.084 billion, a decline of 6.5 to 7.5 percentage points year over year | New |
| Q2 2024 Core non-GAAP operating margin | About 18% | New |

**Reasoning (Dhingra).**
- The full-year operating-margin view reflects gross margin improvement and disciplined expense management, including reduced headcount. These are offset by normalization of performance-based compensation and by inflation.
- The Q2 revenue decline is "predominantly because of the lower NovaSeq X instrument shipments, given the significant backlog we worked through early last year following the launch." At the midpoint, Q2 is a $22 million sequential increase from Q1 2024.
- The Q2 margin dip comes from a seasonal step-up in operating expenses, mainly higher stock-based compensation from the timing of equity grants. Some project spending also shifts from Q1 into Q2.

**Backdrop cited for caution (Dhingra).** Management saw encouraging signs, such as total performance obligations (backlog) rising more than 20% from the end of Q4 as part of the usual seasonal rebuild. It also saw "no significant improvement" in the macro environment or in China, and was monitoring a stronger US dollar. "While we are seeing early strength in consumables to start the year, it's being offset by capital constraints that are continuing to weigh on instrument purchases."

## Prepared remarks

### Salli Schwartz (VP Investor Relations)

Schwartz introduced the speakers and noted that Goswami would serve as an advisor through June 30. She reminded listeners that GRAIL "must be held and operated separately and independently from Illumina," under the transitional measures ordered by the European Commission, which prohibited Illumina's acquisition of GRAIL under EU merger regulation.

### Jacob Thaysen (CEO)

**Leadership changes.** Thaysen thanked Joydeep Goswami, the former CFO and Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer, for over four years of service. Goswami stays in an advisory role through June. He welcomed Ankur Dhingra as the new CFO. He also congratulated Jakob Wedel, who joined Illumina in November, on being named Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer.

**Customer themes.** Thaysen met with customers worldwide and hosted several at the San Diego headquarters. Customers described:
- Scaling projects on NovaSeq X for greater discovery power.
- Whole-genome work in minimal residual disease (MRD, i.e., detecting small amounts of remaining cancer after treatment).
- Sequencing deeper and adding multiomics layers (combining DNA with other molecular data such as proteins).
- Using epigenomic biomarkers and methylation (chemical tags on DNA) to diagnose and characterize disease.

**Multiomics.** He called multiomics "a significant opportunity." The recent acquisition of Partek, a specialized multiomics software company, is "a building block." Illumina intends to offer more "sample to answer" solutions (covering the whole workflow from sample preparation to final result). "Illumina is at the heart of the ecosystem."

**Q1 commentary.** Core revenue of about $1.06 billion was "ahead of our expectations." He called it "a decent start to the year" but said management remains cautious because customers are "still constrained in their purchasing decisions." Lower NovaSeq X placements than in Q1 2023 were expected, since Q1 2023 included the first X shipments against a strong pre-order book.

**Priority 1: driving the top line.**
- Illumina shipped 55 NovaSeq X instruments in Q1, bringing the total X installed base to more than 400. He called it "a solid start of the year" and expects momentum to build.
- Consumables demand was promising. The 25B reagent kit launched in Q4 2023 and the 1.5B in Q1 2024, alongside the well-received upgrade of the 10B flow cell. Customers now have the full suite of NovaSeq X products.
- In Q1 Illumina made XLEAP-SBS chemistry (its newer sequencing chemistry) available to mid-throughput customers via the new P4 flow cell on existing NextSeq 1000/2000 instruments. Early feedback was "exceptional," with reads exceeding specifications for quantity and quality. Customers liked the simple migration, which let them run spatial and single-cell projects, and were impressed with accuracy from the on-board DRAGEN software (Illumina's genomic analysis engine).
- Partnerships: Pillar Biosciences announced FDA approval of its pan-cancer IVD (in vitro diagnostic) test for general tumor profiling on Illumina's MiSeq Dx system. Illumina has partnered with Pillar since 2017. Bristol Myers Squibb joined the previously announced collaboration with Johnson & Johnson Innovative Medicine to develop Illumina's multi-cancer, whole-genome-sequencing-based MRD assay. "You should expect to see more of these types of activities going forward."

**Priority 2: operational excellence and margin improvement.**
- In March, Illumina merged its marketing and commercial teams into one "customer first" global commercial organization.
- It is improving the end-to-end supply chain and taking a disciplined approach to its cost structure.
- In Q1 it implemented initiatives to adjust pricing across the portfolio "to better cover our global operational cost."
- It exited select facilities in the Bay Area and San Diego to streamline its real estate footprint.
- China: Jenny Zheng joined as Head of Region in Q1. She is moving the business toward "in China for China," including better local manufacturing and local partnerships.

**Priority 3: resolving GRAIL.**
- In April, the European Commission approved Illumina's GRAIL divestment plan. The plan contemplates both a sale and capital-markets options (such as a public listing), and Illumina has made progress on both paths.
- If it takes the capital-markets route, Illumina must capitalize GRAIL with about $1 billion, equal to two and a half years of funding under GRAIL's long-range plan. The amount includes cash already on GRAIL's balance sheet.
- Illumina is "on track to finalize the terms of the transactions by the end of the second quarter."

**Closing: strategy update.** The leadership team has been re-examining strategy, the roadmap and the profitable growth it can produce. The strategy will keep playing to Illumina's strength in building the genomics ecosystem with a tight customer focus "in an increasingly competitive space," with the goal of "making customers the heroes in their labs." Illumina will:
- Develop more sample-to-answer solutions for genomics and multiomics.
- Keep its open-platform approach.
- Keep innovating, on the strength of its hands-on support, expertise across research and clinical markets, and "decade-long technology roadmap."

A comprehensive strategy update will be shared in a virtual event "in the fall of this year," with more information in the coming month.

### Ankur Dhingra (CFO)

Dhingra opened with a personal introduction. He said he was passionate about healthcare and familiar with Illumina's role over the last 25 years. He thanked Goswami for the transition support. His segment, consolidated and guidance commentary is covered above under "Results and guidance."

## Question-and-answer session

### Doug Schenkel, Wolfe Research

**Question.** About seven to eight months into Thaysen's tenure as CEO, Schenkel noted the leadership changes and progress on GRAIL. He asked how far management had moved from assessment toward "putting some stakes in the ground" on the medium- and long-term outlook, covering:
- Long-term growth, given uncertainty about competition and market elasticity (whether lower prices expand sequencing volume).
- Reasonable operating targets.
- Whether the company is positioned to "play offense."

He also asked whether high-throughput sequencing consumables revenue grew low-to-mid teens, and what that says about competition and elasticity.

**Answer (Thaysen).** He joked that he was "a little bit over seven months" into the job. He is spending much time with customers and employees and is looking closely at Illumina's direction.
- **Elasticity:** There is "a lot of elasticity" already, as customers use NovaSeq X for single-cell, spatial, bigger programs and deeper analysis. "This is not something that is in the future. It's already happening now." Illumina is working through a price-point change and expects elasticity to help over the coming period.
- **Growth:** He was "not ready to share" long-range growth targets but guaranteed the company is "optimistic about the growth of Illumina going forward."
- **Margins:** Management is "highly dedicated" to expanding margins and pointed to Q1 gross margin expansion as evidence. "I don't think there's anything that holds us back to drive up margins over the next period of time to where we have been in historic levels."
- **Timing:** He wants to wait until the strategy, growth algorithm and cost-structure algorithm are lined up before sharing details.
- **Positioning:** Illumina can keep leading genomics and sit in the middle of the ecosystem. Customers want end-to-end workflows and new multiomics innovation.

**Answer (Goswami, on consumables).** As expected, X consumable growth offset the expected decline in NovaSeq 6000 consumables. Sequencing activity is growing strongly, driven by X customers. This underpins new or expanded applications in clinical and research, and should continue as 25B adoption grows. He did not confirm or dispute the low-to-mid-teens figure.

### Puneet Souda, Leerink Partners

**Question.** Souda asked for mid-throughput growth in consumables and, especially, instruments. He said core-lab customers without an X or a 6K are increasingly taking on a competitor system because of lower price, with cost per gigabase "almost half of what XLEAP is in the next week." He asked what is happening and how Illumina responds.

**Answer (Thaysen).** Illumina does not give mid-throughput growth details. He said:
- The company is "very committed" to mid-throughput.
- XLEAP on the NextSeq 1000/2000 needs no new instrument, only a software upgrade and new flow cells. It gives higher quality scores, more capacity and a lower price point, which "makes us highly competitive."
- Competition is real and takes the company's attention. Mid-throughput has more competitive activity than other segments, mostly in China but also elsewhere. "We expect to fight for every market share points out there."

**Answer (Goswami).** Consumables continue to grow, showing elasticity in mid-throughput. Illumina had already anticipated a certain level of competition when setting the year's budget, and what is being seen is in line. The mid-throughput forecasts also already accounted for macro headwinds, which "have come to bear."

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI

**Question.** Kumar welcomed Dhingra. He noted Core revenue guided up about $30 million sequentially and asked whether that is all consumables, given improving X consumable pull-through during the quarter. He asked about the exit rate and where the increase comes from.

**Answer (Thaysen).** Strength was seen throughout the quarter, not just in the last few weeks. Customers are adopting X consumables, and with the 25B, 10B and 1.5B all available, more applications are coming on. The improvement was driven by the X. Besides the consumables beat, business development (BD) revenue grew and some partnerships accelerated, which he called a big part of the strategy.

**Answer (Dhingra).** The sequential increase built into guidance comes from both instruments and consumables, and he expects both positive trends to continue.

### Dan Brennan, TD Cowen

**Question.** Brennan asked about multiomics and new products. Is the SomaLogic partnership the source of the multiomic excitement? What about sample-to-answer and other new areas, and when will an update come (e.g., at an investor day)?

**Answer (Thaysen).** He is excited about sequencing's role in multiomics and about helping partners and customers stimulate that market.
- **SomaLogic (proteomics, i.e., protein measurement):** Short term, Illumina is "very excited" about the relationship. It is in early access, and 2024 will be the year of going out to "a few handfuls of customers" to understand the power. Early feedback has been "very positive."
- Illumina will keep looking at ways to play more strongly in multiomics, organically and with partners.
- End-to-end workflows are a great opportunity. Customers want the best technology plus ease of use, including automation and simplification of sample prep and informatics. More will come in the strategy update.

### Unidentified analyst ("Paul on for Dan"), Stifel

**Question.** The analyst asked about population genomics (large national sequencing programs). They noted budget pressure and recent cuts and asked whether that creates risk to the 2024 consumables forecast. They also noted chatter that Genome of Europe is picking up and asked whether it could be meaningful in 2025. (The transcript renders the program name as "POPSEQ.")

**Answer (Thaysen).** He is very excited about population genomics over the long run, as a way to move big genomic studies into healthcare systems. Illumina has "more than 30 programs ongoing" and at least as many in the funnel, but "we're not banking on any one individual program." Because sovereign governments must set up systems and budgets, there are "puts and takes." Budgets are not built on individual programs, and he does not foresee any single program making a big impact on 2024 or 2025.

### David Westenberg, Piper Sandler

**Question.** Following on Souda, he asked how XLEAP helps mid-throughput, and where elasticity shows up. He said it is clearer in high throughput (whole genome versus exome, single cell). He asked how to keep mid-throughput from becoming a race to the bottom given a price-focused competitor. On high throughput, he asked whether the expected build in order momentum is based on actual orders, late-cycle orders, or anticipated budgets.

**Answer (Thaysen, mid-throughput).** XLEAP offers high sequencing quality and higher capacity at lower cost. Customers who felt they couldn't afford single-cell or spatial work on prior capacity and cost can now do it. An "elasticity game" will play out. Customers want to work with innovators, and the macro environment has impacted the segment. He is excited about XLEAP's effect and new mid-throughput applications.

**Answer (Goswami).** XLEAP lets customers use the same field instruments with a simple software change for more output, accuracy and performance. Elasticity appears immediately in research, where multiomics, single-cell and spatial work tends to be done first on mid-throughput. Because of "innovation and technical superiority," Illumina maintains a price premium over what others offer. Macro pressures will end at some point, and customers' cash constraints should lift then.

**Answer (Thaysen, high-throughput orders).** X orders were up year over year. He expects momentum to improve through the year. Many customers who bought one or two Xs have finished validation work, and multiple orders of up to 10 instruments at a time are now arriving.

**Answer (Goswami).** The expectation is "not based on some magic new budget thing." Illumina expects the macro conditions to continue as they are.

### Subha Nambi, Guggenheim

**Question.** Does the mix of consumables versus instruments in the reiterated guidance remain the same, or has the trajectory changed?

**Answer (Dhingra).** The full-year revenue and operating-margin guidance were reiterated, and the Q1 performance "derisks" it a bit. The construct is unchanged. Instrument growth rates will vary between the first and second halves because of how X shipments fell last year, whereas consumables, "as they're starting now," should have a steadier profile. Pricing and pull-through were good in the quarter.

### Sung Ji Nam, Scotiabank

**Question.** What is the status of the European jurisdictional appeal (at the European Court of Justice, ECJ)? If it is not resolved before the end of Q2, will Illumina still provide final divestiture terms?

**Answer (Thaysen).** Illumina is moving as fast as it can and has committed to give insight by the end of Q2. "There's nothing, no information, nothing that holds us back from that." Illumina does not know when the ECJ ruling will come and "we're not waiting for it." If it comes before the end of Q2, "it really doesn't change the timeline."

### Tejas Savant, Morgan Stanley

**Question.** Three questions: (1) the 25B flow cell and the prior "40%" figure for X customers adopting 25B; (2) where high-throughput clinical customers are in X validation, and whether production ramp is mainly a 2025 dynamic; (3) whether excess high-throughput capacity is weighing on purchase decisions, given Ultima's launch, and how to separate macro headwinds from digestion of capacity.

**Answer (Thaysen).**
- **25B adoption:** He is "very pleased." "Approximately half of our customers" are now using the 25B flow cell, up from the roughly 40% the analyst referenced.
- **Clinical validation:** Many clinical customers still run most assays on the 6K. Illumina tracks each closely but is not ready to share specifics. Many are still validating and will bring new assays onto the X one by one, likely with greater depth or more insight than on the 6K.
- The transcript does not show a direct answer on the excess-capacity or Ultima part of the question.

### Mason Carrico, Stephens

**Question.** Have win rates outside China, which Illumina said had stabilized in Q3 and Q4 2023, stayed stable so far in 2024? What is the mix of incoming orders (6K upgrades, fleet expansions, new customers, new-to-high-throughput customers)?

**Answer (Thaysen).** Illumina watches win rates closely, and they have stabilized. He is "actually pleased with the performance." He said, "we take competition very seriously." The long-run winning formula is high customer support and success. On order mix: more research customers have moved to the X, and customers with multiple 6Ks are starting to move over, but "the vast majority still are in the transition phase." He was unsure whether such mix is normally shared.

### Rachel Vatnsdal, JPMorgan

**Question.** How many of the 55 X placements were pulled forward from Q2 into Q1? Given the difficult macro, what are the latest full-year placement assumptions?

**Answer (Thaysen).** "We didn't pull anything forward." A few customers asked for earlier delivery because programs were running faster than anticipated, which was not a big change in expectations. He liked it as a sign of interest from customers who already bought one and want to accelerate. Illumina does not give a placement number. 2023 was a very strong high-throughput placement year because of the large entering backlog, so 2024 is expected to be lower, as in the original guidance. Expectations for the year are not changing.

**Answer (Dhingra).** Only about one-fourth of the top-line overachievement came from customers asking for earlier instrument delivery.

### Patrick Donnelly, Citi

**Question.** Explain the Q2 margin step-down: what spending picks up and how should margin evolve through the year? Also, why did service revenue come in well above expectations?

**Answer (Dhingra, operating expenses).** Q2 spend rises for two reasons:
- Timing of merit increases and the annual stock grant cycle, with the full cost landing in Q2. This is seasonal and about half of the sequential OpEx increase.
- The other half is project-related spend pushed out of Q1 or milestones occurring in Q2.

He expects operating margin to improve sequentially every quarter: "Q3 better than Q2 and then Q4 better than Q3."

**Answer (Dhingra, services).** Services grew about 27% year over year. A little less than half of the growth came from specific pharma customer services whose timing fell in the quarter. Roughly half came from the core instrument services business.

**Answer (Goswami).** Strong instrument service growth is expected given the number of instruments placed. Gross margin should keep performing well, and revenue steps up. Headcount is down about 12% versus last year. Both support operating margin through the year.

### Eve Burstein, Bernstein Research

**Question.** The FDA published a final rule that regulates laboratory-developed tests (LDTs, tests designed and run within a single lab) earlier that week. Illumina has IVD-compliant machines that many challengers lack. But research-use-only machines are used quite a bit for FDA-approved tests, though with more difficulty. Is the right view that this does not affect Illumina much or give a big advantage, or is there more upside?

**Answer (Thaysen).** Illumina is pleased to have more clarity on the direction of the FDA ruling, though "a lot of detail" still needs to be clarified. He said that with its Dx portfolio, its investment in quality, and its help with the documentation customers need to get PMAs (premarket approvals, the FDA's most stringent device approval) approved, Illumina is "very well positioned." He added that LDTs can be used for FDA approval "really only if you go into a single site PMA setup," which takes a lot of work from vendors like Illumina on documentation. So the rule "plays to our strength," and Illumina will support customers whether through a full PMA, single-site or other pathways.

### Conor McNamara, RBC Capital Markets

**Question.** Of the roughly 400 X instruments placed, how many customers are at scale and ordering at expected demand versus still validating or not ordering as expected due to economic conditions? Has there been any stocking of consumables?

**Answer (Thaysen).** Most customers are "still in the ramp." Some research customers have scaled fast and run bigger programs, with single-cell a notable interest area. Some are at scale on at least one instrument and may be moving on to the next. Across the broader 400, "most of the general is still that this is still in a ramp positioning."

**Answer (Goswami, stocking).** There has not been much stocking on the 25B, because its initial shelf life is shorter, so customers do not stock up much. On the 10B it is "very early in that stock up phase." He expects a little more stocking as the year goes on, "but not yet."

- The EMEA (down 3%) and Europe (up 7%) regional figures appear side by side and are not reconciled in the transcript.
