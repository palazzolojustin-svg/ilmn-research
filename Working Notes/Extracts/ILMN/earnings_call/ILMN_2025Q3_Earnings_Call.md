# Illumina Q3 2025 Earnings Call (Oct 30, 2025): return to growth ex-China, NovaSeq X transition milestones, raised 2025 guidance

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2025Q3_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2025-10-30 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Illumina's Q3 2025 earnings call; speakers Jacob Thaysen (CEO), Ankur Dhingra (CFO), Conor McNamara (VP Investor Relations); analysts from Leerink, Wolfe, Evercore ISI, Jefferies, UBS, Citi, Stephens, Baird, Canaccord, Nephron, TD Cowen and Guggenheim · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q3 2025 revenue of $1.08 billion (flat year over year, but up about 2% excluding China), non-GAAP operating margin of 24.5% and non-GAAP EPS of $1.34, all above guidance. Clinical sequencing demand (double-digit consumables growth outside China) drove the beat as the NovaSeq X transition passed its year-end targets early, while research and applied demand stayed soft. Management raised 2025 guidance (revenue $4.27-4.31 billion, operating margin 22.75-23%, EPS $4.65-4.75), declined to give 2026 guidance, but said it expects clinical strength, muted research, and continued earnings growth in 2026.

## Results and guidance summary

### Q3 2025 headline results [Q3 2025 call, 2025-10-30]

Illumina (the dominant maker of DNA sequencing instruments and the reagent kits, called consumables, that customers must buy for every run) reported third-quarter 2025 results above its own guidance on revenue, non-GAAP operating margin and non-GAAP diluted EPS. "Non-GAAP" means adjusted figures that exclude certain items such as one-time charges. Seeking Alpha's summary header says EPS of $1.34 beat consensus by $0.17 and revenue of $1.08 billion (up 0.37% year over year) beat consensus by $18.70 million. Unless stated otherwise, growth rates on the call were in constant currency (excluding the effect of exchange rates), and "China" means Greater China, including Taiwan and Hong Kong.

### Balance sheet and capital allocation [Ankur Dhingra, Q3 2025 call]

- Illumina repurchased about 1.24 million shares at an average $97.10, totaling $120 million, in Q3. $684 million remained on the repurchase authorization at quarter end, and Ankur said the company intends to keep buying opportunistically.
- Cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments were roughly $1.28 billion. Gross leverage was about 1.6x (gross debt to trailing-12-month EBITDA, a measure of operating earnings).
- The SomaLogic acquisition (from Standard BioTools, for SomaLogic and other specified assets) is under regulatory review. Ankur said the company still expects it to close in the first half of 2026; Jacob said "in 2026."

### 2026 and long-term framing [Q3 2025 call]

- No 2026 guidance was given; detailed guidance will come with Q4 results. Management said it wants to see how the next quarter, the market and China regulation evolve.
- Ankur's three 2026 trends: (1) clinical should look like 2025, with strong volume growth and continued transition to the NovaSeq X; (2) research and applied should stay muted, like the second half of 2025, with pricing headwinds easing now that 91% of high-throughput volume is on the X; (3) planned 2026 multiomics launches will contribute "as and when research end markets recover." Overall, end markets in 2026 should resemble the second half of 2025.
- Jacob reaffirmed confidence in high-single-digit revenue growth and 20% non-GAAP operating margins by 2027, excluding Greater China. In Q&A he also referred to a goal of 26% operating margin in 2027 and above (this appears to be the operating margin target from the 2024 investor day; the transcript uses both figures).

## Prepared remarks

### Jacob Thaysen, CEO

**Overall performance.** Jacob said Q3 was "another strong performance," with revenue, operating margin and EPS all above guidance and a return to growth outside China (about 2%). He credited momentum from the NovaSeq X transition (the shift from the older NovaSeq 6000 instruments to the newer, higher-throughput NovaSeq X), especially in clinical markets, where sequencing consumables revenue grew at a high-single-digit rate year over year. He said the company is raising full-year outlook while "staying disciplined as we monitor macro and funding dynamics through year-end."

**End markets.**
- Clinical (diagnostic and patient-testing customers) "continues to accelerate." Customers are advancing new assays that need more sequencing capacity and intensity, and the X delivers higher throughput with the same accuracy and workflow as the NovaSeq 6000. Growing volumes are "more than offsetting transitional pricing effects" (lower per-unit prices as customers move to the X), producing both sequential and year-over-year consumables growth.
- Research saw stabilization in demand, though many labs are still managing spending carefully amid regulatory and funding uncertainty.
- China was resilient, with revenue ahead of guidance despite export restrictions.

**China and OEM partners.** A fraction of the business is served by OEM partners (companies that resell Illumina instruments and consumables into specific customer segments). Illumina has received approval to serve those partners by manufacturing select instruments locally in China. Jacob called it "a measured step forward" but said no long-term resolution of China operations has been reached, and the company remains in dialogue with the relevant agencies.

**Strategy: three pillars.** These underpin the long-term financial targets, and shift the conversation from cost per gigabase (a unit of sequencing output) to "the highest quality biological insights at the lowest end-to-end cost."

1. *Core sequencing.* More than 55 NovaSeq X instruments were placed in Q3, in line with the goal of 50 to 60 per quarter. Illumina's year-end goals were about 75% of high-throughput gigabases shipped and 50% of high-throughput revenue on the X; both were exceeded in Q3. NovaSeq X consumables revenue growth accelerated even as conversion rose. Clinical is the main driver, helped by new assay approvals, positive reimbursement decisions and more sequencing-intensive tests. He said that as pricing headwinds ease and research stabilizes, these dynamics should put the company back on track toward long-term revenue growth targets.
2. *Multiomics* (measuring more than DNA, e.g., proteins and methylation).
   - The SomaLogic acquisition (announced in Q2) is still expected to close in 2026.
   - Illumina Protein Prep launched in Q3: a co-developed proteomics assay bringing next-generation sequencing (NGS) to protein analysis. It measures up to 9,500 proteins per sample, with consistent results in about 2.5 days, minimal hands-on time and lower cost per insight. It integrates with DRAGEN (Illumina's analysis software) and the upcoming Illumina Complete multiomics software suite.
   - At ASHG (the American Society of Human Genetics meeting) earlier in October, Illumina launched its 5-base solution: an integrated library-prep and software offering that reads genetic variants and DNA methylation simultaneously, using proprietary chemistry and a new DRAGEN algorithm, with single-base resolution and lower complexity and cost.
3. *Services, data and software.* BioInsight is a new business, introduced in October, that brings together population sequencing programs, data partnerships and software/AI capabilities to work with governments, biopharma and research institutions on drug discovery. Near term it will focus on large-scale data-generation partnerships; longer term it plans to monetize data, software and AI-enabled services.

**Customer feedback.** Jacob met many customers during the quarter and at ASHG; they were enthusiastic about recent launches and upcoming products such as "Constellation with CLIA" (transcribed as "Constellations with CLIA"). Conversations reaffirmed Illumina's leadership, built on end-to-end solutions, data quality and consistency, and reliability and service.

**Outlook statement.** Clinical remains the primary near-term growth driver as X volume more than offsets conversion pricing headwinds, and management anticipates "a gradual return to growth in our research business" as pricing headwinds abate and end markets stabilize.

### Ankur Dhingra, CFO

**Revenue by area.**
- Sequencing consumables: Rest-of-world clinical consumables grew at double digits outside China, driven by broader use of comprehensive genomic profiling (tests that look at many cancer genes at once) and sequencing-intensive applications like MRD (minimal residual disease, detecting tiny traces of cancer after treatment). Research and applied consumables declined high single digits outside China, reflecting funding uncertainty and pricing dynamics tied to the X transition.
- New disclosure: Illumina added revenue-outside-China detail (instruments and consumables revenue, and consumables growth by clinical and research segments) on Slide 10.
- Instruments: $107 million, up about 3% (6% ex-China), driven by broad adoption of the MiSeq 100 in low-throughput sequencing. In Greater China, instrument sales fell about 54% because of restrictions on exporting instruments into China.
- Services: down about 3%, below expectations, mainly due to timing of certain strategic partnership revenues.
- Sequencing output: total gigabase (GB) output on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30% year over year, driven by clinical, with more muted growth from research.

**NovaSeq X transition metrics (Q3).**
- About 78% of volumes and 51% of revenue were sequenced on X (up from 44% of high-throughput revenue on the X previously, per Jacob in Q&A).
- 91% of research volumes were on X; clinical was about 64% of volumes.
- Over 55 X placements, with over 50% going to clinical customers, in line with recent trends.
- Customers are using the X to add content, expand to new indications and adopt whole-genome approaches, evidenced by launches and approvals across therapy selection, MRD and genetic disease.

**Clinical elasticity evidence (Slide 12).** Looking at the 40 customers that had fully transitioned to X as of Q3, "volume offset price in year 1 and then revenue and volume both accelerated in year 2." Ankur said continuing dialogue with clinical customers about growth and 6K-to-X plans supports continued revenue growth in 2026 and beyond, and that business with the largest customers is projected to grow faster than the company average, at least over the strategic plan period.

**Costs and margin.** Tariffs reduced gross margin by about 220 bp year over year. Operating expense decline reflects multiyear cost programs while prioritizing growth investments. Ankur said the company continues to assess long-term tax structure optimization, balancing U.S. R&D benefits with efficient credit use across jurisdictions.

**Closing.** Ankur said the quarter was "extremely encouraging" as Illumina returned to growth and progressed toward short- and long-term goals.

## Q&A

### Puneet Souda, Leerink Partners

*Question:* How to think about China in 2026, given the $33 million Q4 implied; whether competitive announcements at ASHG are "freezing" research and applied purchasing; and how to think about the 12% clinical growth for the full year and 2026.

*Answer (Jacob):* He is "very pleased" with China performance. The unresolved situation improved by now being able to serve OEM partners so they can serve China customers. He praised the China general manager (named Jenny) and team for resilience, and said customers strongly want to keep working with Illumina, valuing its quality and innovation. Illumina is taking China quarter by quarter; it is too early to give a 2026 view, and a resolution with Chinese regulators is still needed. (The competition and clinical-growth parts of the question were not directly addressed in his reply; competition was answered in Patrick Donnelly's question below.)

### Douglas Schenkel, Wolfe Research

*Question:* Framework for 2026: research revenue flat to down low single digits; clinical up mid- to high single digits; China a 50-100 bp headwind; together implying low-single-digit total growth. Also, operating margin is up about 50 bp year to date despite revenue down a couple of points and roughly 25% lower high-margin China revenue; what does that imply for margins as the environment normalizes?

*Answer (Jacob):* No guidance yet; he wants to see the next quarter, the market and China regulation. He said the framework is sensible: clinical drives growth, research is more muted ("a second-half environment... stabilizing, but still soft"), and new multiomics launches contribute some. He would not factor China into the framework yet. He feels good about further margin expansion and moving toward the goal of 26% in 2027 and above.

*Answer (Ankur):* He is pleased with the 190 bp expansion and said several actions have not yet fully reached the P&L, especially in gross margin, where Illumina is optimizing its manufacturing footprint. On operating expenses, many structural moves are in place, some still to play out, while growth investments continue. He expects additional margin expansion from cost actions and "much stronger operating leverage."

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI

*Question:* Consensus expected consumables of roughly $720-725 million; was there pull-forward in academic and government? How to split the ~3% consumables growth between macro and transition effects? And is the "500 basis points of margin expansion" goal still relevant?

*Answer (Jacob):* Consumables were a highlight. There was no pull-forward from China or from NIH. Grants are starting to flow again, but there was no catch-up spending from grant recipients; he is hopeful but says stabilization will take time.

*Answer (Ankur):* Most of the rest-of-world outperformance was clinical consumables. The 500 bp goal was set at last year's Analyst Day from a base near 21% operating margin, and the company is marching toward it over time. He noted gross margin of roughly 69% and a long-term opportunity above that, to be discussed after the first milestone is achieved (transcript garbled: "75%, 70% gross margin").

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies

*Question:* With research muted, how does Illumina think about multiyear grants, labs tapping indirect funds for capital equipment, and pent-up demand? Can earnings grow in 2026 given China and Roche headwinds? How much of the consumables beat was China and tariff surcharges? Why did gross margin fall 130 bp year over year, and what levers offset it?

*Answer (Jacob):* Grants have been awarded but many researchers have not begun spending. Academics typically hold several grants, so it is predictability of future grants that drives decisions; the market is waiting to see that grants will keep flowing in 2026. Illumina will be ready, and its multiomics lineup will be relevant when funding returns. He is "very confident" it can grow earnings in 2026, citing 2025 as proof despite many headwinds.

*Answer (Ankur):* Of the 130 bp gross margin decline, almost 220 bp was tariffs; excluding that, base gross margin rose about 90 bp. Illumina is working to mitigate tariffs over time. The consumables beat was "roughly half and half" China and outside China, and most of the outside-China part was clinical. The tariff surcharge came in exactly as forecast.

### Daniel Leonard, UBS

*Question:* Did double-digit clinical consumables growth include positive lumpiness, or is it run rate?

*Answer (Jacob):* No specific lumpiness. He pointed to X revenue share rising from 44% to 51% of high-throughput revenue while volume moved strongly, showing growth despite fast transition. He would not count on that rate as the run rate going forward, but expects continued strong clinical growth.

### Patrick Donnelly, Citi

*Question:* Perspective on the competitive landscape after ASHG, its role in 2026, and any customer "freezing" in clinical.

*Answer (Jacob):* Illumina has always had competition, and he "love[s] it" as it pushes the company to be a better partner. He said most competitors differentiate on a single dimension, while sophisticated customers weigh multiple dimensions: highest-quality data, best workflow and lowest end-to-end cost. In his view Illumina is "really the only one that's delivering across all these dimensions" while continuing to innovate. (No competitors were named in his answer.)

### Mason Carrico, Stephens

*Question:* How sustainable is 50-60 X placements per quarter, what does the pipeline look like, and could placements hold with a flattish NIH budget?

*Answer (Jacob):* The 50-60 per quarter algorithm was set at the start of 2025, before China and NIH challenges, and Illumina still delivered. He expects Q4 to be stronger than the yearly average, as usual, and sees no reason the trend would not continue next year, though it is too early for specifics.

*Answer (Ankur):* Many sequencing-intensive applications in clinical and research are gaining traction, implying several more years of ecosystem expansion that should translate into placements.

### Catherine Ramsey (Schulte), Robert W. Baird

*Question:* With the research and applied X transition nearly complete, how has GB output trended in that group, and is a government shutdown impact baked into Q4?

*Answer (Jacob):* Faster research transition was expected: researchers can start a new project directly on the X (e.g., a larger single-cell project), whereas clinical customers must validate on the new platform first. With the transition done, pricing headwinds are reducing. NIH funding concerns remain and will keep research muted for now. (The shutdown part was not explicitly answered.)

*Answer (Ankur):* GB grew in both clinical and research in the quarter; clinical was much stronger. Research GB growth slowed from historical rates because of the funding environment, but was still positive, though below the 30% overall figure.

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord Genuity

*Question:* R&D expense fell sharply quarter over quarter, below $230 million for the first time since 2021; is this a new run rate, and how does it relate to launches and competitiveness? Also what is driving the clinical acceleration and are there more catalysts?

*Answer (Jacob):* He praised the CTO (named Steve) and R&D team for disciplined execution and improved R&D productivity. He said much of the pipeline has not been shared yet: "much more to come." (He did not state whether the R&D level is a run rate.)

*Answer (Ankur):* Clinical acceleration reflects X validation taking longer in clinical (so benefits arrive later), and large customers launching and winning approval for several new tests across early detection, therapy selection, MRD and genetic disease. Many of these sequencing-intensive tests are enabled on the X. He said the market is building momentum with "legs," though eventual growth rates will "pan out the way they would pan out."

*Addition (Jacob):* Many X placements happened in Q4 2024 and Q1 2025, largely into clinical, and volume from them is now showing up.

### Jack Meehan, Nephron Research

*Question:* Confidence in delivering the previously stated goal of double-digit EPS growth in 2026.

*Answer (Jacob):* At last summer's investor event the company focused on operating margin (500 bp expansion to 26%), which implies strong EPS growth. He is confident EPS can keep growing in that range, having expanded EPS in tough environments in 2024 and 2025, but it is too early for specific 2026 guidance.

*Answer (Ankur):* Levers remain: operating expense discipline (cost structure is close to delivering stronger operating leverage), centers of excellence in Singapore and India, instrument gross margin improvement, with some results not yet in the P&L, plus operating leverage as growth returns. Specifics will come with the 2026 guide.

### Daniel Brennan, TD Cowen

*Question:* Could the 900 bp sequential increase in X volume share (55% to 64% in clinical) repeat in Q4? How long to reach research-like levels (~90%)? What is Illumina seeing from U.S. academic and government customers, and would a flat budget or continuing resolution (CR) be enough to lift spending?

*Answer (Jacob):* Last quarter's debate was why the transition slowed; now it is why it accelerated. What matters is that revenue accelerated along with conversion. He cautioned against over-interpreting one quarter; the trend matters. On academia: grants are flowing and NIH is spending, which is a "great checkmark." Next steps are budget approval (at least a CR) and regular, predictable grant flow, which will stretch into 2026 "for sure."

### Subhalaxmi Nambi, Guggenheim

*Question:* Is Q4 ex-China instrument revenue implied at about $125 million (down year over year but a notable sequential step-up), and what is assumed for budget flush? Also, is it right that research customers, being mostly through the X transition, can now grow faster?

*Answer (Jacob):* A strong Q4 for instruments is normal for life science tools generally, not an Illumina quirk; he does not consider it a budget flush and has built none in.

*Answer (Ankur):* The forecast assumes a pickup but not the 91 placements seen in Q4 2024; repeating that would be upside. He agreed the vast majority of research volume has transitioned to X, so as research returns to growth, or even stabilizes, pricing headwinds should dissipate and improve revenue growth; new multiomics launches add to positioning.
