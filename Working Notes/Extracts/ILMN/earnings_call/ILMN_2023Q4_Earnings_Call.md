# Illumina Q4 2023 Earnings Call (Feb 8, 2024): Results, 2024 Core Illumina Guidance, GRAIL Divestiture Update and Analyst Q&A

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2023Q4_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2024-02-08 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Illumina's Q4 2023 earnings call; speakers Salli Schwartz (VP Investor Relations), Jacob Thaysen (CEO), Joydeep Goswami (CFO and Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer); analysts from Wolfe, Leerink, Evercore ISI, Stifel, Guggenheim, Stephens, Piper Sandler, RBC, Scotiabank, Citi, Bernstein, TD Cowen, UBS, Canaccord and JPMorgan · earnings call*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q4 2023 consolidated revenue of about $1.12 billion (up 4% year over year) and non-GAAP EPS of $0.14, both ahead of expectations, helped by stronger NovaSeq X placements and consumables. For 2024, management guided Core Illumina (Illumina excluding GRAIL) to roughly flat revenue and about 20% non-GAAP operating margin, with instruments falling in the high-teens percent and consumables growing in the low single digits. Management also said it is on track to finalize a GRAIL divestiture by the end of June 2024, and discussed China weakness, NovaSeq X pull-through, pricing, competition and FDA regulation of lab-developed tests.

# Illumina Q4 2023 Earnings Call, February 8, 2024

## Context and structure of the call

This was Illumina's fourth-quarter and full-year 2023 earnings call, held February 8, 2024 at 5:00 PM ET. Speakers were Salli Schwartz (Vice President, Investor Relations), Jacob Thaysen (Chief Executive Officer, who joined "a little over four months ago" per his remarks) and Joydeep Goswami (Chief Financial Officer and Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer).

The Seeking Alpha header summarized the quarter as: EPS of $0.14, beating by $0.02, and revenue of $1.12 billion (up 3.6% year over year), beating by $15.4 million. [Seeking Alpha summary, Q4 2023 call]

Two structural points shape everything below:

- **Consolidated results include GRAIL.** GRAIL is Illumina's cancer early-detection blood-test business. Schwartz reminded listeners that GRAIL "must be held and operated separately and independently from Illumina" under transitional measures ordered by the European Commission, which prohibited Illumina's acquisition of GRAIL under EU merger regulation. [Schwartz, Q4 2023 call]
- **"Core Illumina" means Illumina excluding GRAIL.** Management reports both consolidated and Core Illumina figures, and 2024 guidance is given for Core Illumina only.

All financial figures are non-GAAP (adjusted, excluding certain items, but including stock-based compensation, per Goswami). A reconciliation to GAAP is in the earnings release.

## Results and guidance

### Q4 2023 consolidated results

Consolidated revenue was about $1.12 billion, up 4% year over year on both a reported and constant-currency basis (which strips out exchange-rate effects). It was flat versus Q3 2023, and higher than management had expected. [Goswami, Q4 2023 call]

- Non-GAAP net income was $22 million, or $0.14 per diluted share. This included dilution from GRAIL's losses. Non-GAAP EPS exceeded management's expectations, driven by higher revenue, higher gross margin and lower operating expense.
- Non-GAAP operating loss was $152 million for the quarter (consolidated, which includes GRAIL's large losses).
- The weighted-average diluted share count was approximately 159 million (the transcript says "$159 million," which is evidently a transcription error).

### Tax rate

The non-GAAP tax rate was unusually high: 55.4% in Q4 2023 versus 29.3% in Q4 2022, and 41.8% for full-year 2023 versus 26% for full-year 2022. [Goswami]

- Both periods reflect the effect of R&D capitalization requirements (a U.S. tax rule forcing companies to deduct research spending over several years rather than immediately). The effect was larger in 2023 because earnings were lower.
- Both years also include a meaningful effect from consolidating GRAIL's losses.
- Excluding GRAIL, Goswami said the full-year 2023 "core" tax rate was in the mid-20s percent.

### Regional revenue (Q4 2023, year over year)

Thaysen gave the regional picture:

- **Americas** (more than half of the business): flat.
- **Europe:** up 17%, on a relatively easy prior-year comparison.
- **EMEA** (Europe, Middle East and Africa): down 1%, which included a 10 percentage point drag from sanctions in Russia.
- **Greater China:** down 13%, reflecting "continued geopolitical challenges and local competition" in mid-throughput sequencers. Thaysen said Illumina has "already taken certain pricing and other strategic actions that are beginning to yield results" and will share more specifics over time.

### Core Illumina Q4 2023 results

Core Illumina revenue was $1.1 billion, up 3% year over year on both a reported and constant-currency basis. It absorbed an anticipated drag of about 5 percentage points from two sources: [Goswami]

1. Falling COVID surveillance sequencing plus the effect of Russia sanctions, together about 3 points.
2. The year-over-year decline in China revenue, about 2 points.

Despite this, revenue beat expectations because of stronger-than-projected NovaSeq X placements and uptake of X consumables, with strong early demand for the 25B flow cell (a high-capacity consumable for the NovaSeq X) that launched in Q4.

COVID surveillance contributed only about $4 million of revenue in Q4 2023 versus $20 million in Q4 2022.

The table below summarizes Core Illumina revenue lines in Q4 2023, as described by Goswami.

| Line | Q4 2023 | Year-over-year change | Drivers cited |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sequencing consumables (reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for each run) | $687 million | Flat | Stronger X consumables largely offset by expected decline in NovaSeq 6000 consumables and "pricing transitions" as customers adopt the X; also hit by COVID, Russia, China and macro conditions |
| Sequencing instruments | $161 million | Up 10% | NovaSeq X more than offset fewer NovaSeq 6000 shipments; partly offset by lower mid- and low-throughput shipments from customer capital and cash-flow constraints and local competition in China |
| Sequencing services and other | $152 million | Up 16% | Strategic partnership revenue and higher instrument service-contract revenue on a growing installed base |

### Sequencing activity

Total activity on Illumina's connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew 46% year over year in Q4, after 29% growth in Q3. Sequentially, Q4 activity was up 13% over Q3. Goswami said this data is a useful guide to general activity trends and is "directionally correlated with revenue over time."

### Instrument installed base

- NovaSeq X: 390 orders since launch at the end of Q4. Q4 shipments were 79, bringing the total installed base to 352 instruments. Thaysen noted this was above the 330 to 340 management had expected, and called the NovaSeq X "the most successful high-throughput product launch in our history."
- Across NovaSeq X and NovaSeq 6000 combined, more than 400 high-throughput instruments were placed in 2023 (Thaysen).
- NovaSeq 6000: net installed base of about 1,770 instruments at end of 2023, after roughly 110 instruments were deactivated between 2017 and 2023, mostly in 2023 as customers moved to the X. Goswami expects deactivations to rise in 2024.
- Reporting change: Illumina will now report annual installed base figures on a net basis (subtracting decommissioned or inactivated instruments). A presentation with modeling detail was to be posted to the Investor Relations site.

### Core Illumina margins and expenses (Q4 2023)

- Non-GAAP gross margin was 64.7%, down 260 basis points (2.6 percentage points) year over year. Causes: mix of lower-margin strategic partnership revenue, lower instrument margins from the NovaSeq X launch ("typical with a new platform introduction"), and higher field service and installation costs; partly offset by lower freight costs.
- Non-GAAP operating expenses were $507 million, down $21 million year over year, due to continued expense reduction and lower performance-based stock compensation.
- Non-GAAP operating margin was 18.5% versus 17.8% in Q4 2022.

### GRAIL (Q4 2023)

GRAIL revenue was $30 million, up 30% year over year, driven mainly by adoption of Galleri (its multi-cancer early detection blood test). GRAIL non-GAAP operating expenses were $167 million, up $1 million year over year.

### Cash flow and balance sheet (consolidated, Q4 2023)

- Cash from operations: $224 million.
- Capital expenditures: $51 million.
- Free cash flow: $173 million.
- No share repurchases.
- Cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments: about $1.05 billion at quarter end.

### 2024 guidance (Core Illumina only)

Because of uncertainty about the timing and impact of the GRAIL divestiture, the outlook covers Core Illumina only and assumes no GRAIL divestment impact in 2024. Once the divestment completes, Illumina will provide full-year 2024 non-GAAP EPS guidance. The outlook assumes the current challenging macro environment persists, with tighter funding and budgets continuing to weigh on customer purchases. [Goswami]

| Item | 2024 guidance |
|---|---|
| Core revenue | Approximately flat versus 2023 |
| Sequencing instrument revenue | Decline in the high-teens percent, mainly fewer NovaSeq X placements |
| Sequencing consumables revenue | Growth in the low single digits percent |
| Total sequencing revenue | Approximately flat; includes about $30 million of intercompany sales to GRAIL, eliminated in consolidation |
| Core non-GAAP operating margin | About 20% |

Reasoning given:

- **Instruments:** The decline reflects the adoption curve shifting from early adopters to the early majority, a lower backlog entering 2024 than 2023, and continuing capital and cash-flow constraints.
- **Consumables:** Modest growth in high-throughput consumables, as higher NovaSeq X purchases and sequencing volume outpace the expected NovaSeq 6000 decline and pricing transitions.
- **Operating margin:** Benefits from gross margin improvement and expense reductions, offset by normalization of performance-based compensation, inflation and market-based merit increases.

Annual pull-through (consumables revenue per installed instrument per year) is now calculated on the net installed base. Goswami stressed this "does not change reported revenue in any way." 2024 expectations:

| Instrument | Expected 2024 annual pull-through per system |
|---|---|
| NovaSeq 6000 | About $700,000 to $800,000 (as customers shift volume to the X) |
| NextSeq 1k/2k | $80,000 to $130,000 (reflecting customers moving to XLEAP chemistry as it becomes available on these instruments) |
| MiSeq | $30,000 to $40,000 |
| Other instruments | In line with historical guidance |

### Q1 2024 guidance (Core Illumina)

- Revenue of $1.03 billion to $1.04 billion (the transcript says "million," evidently an error), down 3.5% to 4.5% year over year.
- Drivers: lower NovaSeq X instrument shipments because Illumina enters 2024 with a more modest backlog than the large pre-order book it had after the NovaSeq X launch; higher X consumables again largely offset by lower NovaSeq 6000 consumables and pricing transitions (same as Q4); higher sequencing service and other revenue from strategic partnerships and service contracts.
- Non-GAAP operating margin of about 18%.
- Non-GAAP net other expense of about $12 million, with the year-over-year increase driven mainly by lower interest income on a lower cash balance after convertible notes were repaid in mid-2023.

## Prepared remarks by speaker

### Salli Schwartz (opening)

Schwartz introduced the participants, said the call would review results, then commercial and regulatory activity, and noted that Jacob would give a business update and Joydeep would review the financials including GRAIL. She restated the requirement that GRAIL be held separate under the European Commission order.

### Jacob Thaysen (CEO)

**Customer and employee engagement.** Over his first four-plus months, Thaysen met customers to understand priorities. He said customers think highly of Illumina and want strategic partnerships to move the ecosystem forward. The decision to divest GRAIL has made it easier for customers to plan their future with Illumina. On a trip to Europe he saw Illumina working with customers and governments to speed whole-genome sequencing adoption; he cited Germany's "modern project" (as transcribed) to integrate comprehensive genomic testing into the healthcare system starting in Q2 2024. He also met employees and investors, and said these conversations "reinforced my confidence in Illumina's core business."

**2023 and outlook.** He called 2023 a "dynamic year" in which many customers stayed constrained in purchasing amid a tough macro backdrop. He expects customers to stay cautious globally and "for now, we continue to expect 2024 results to look very similar to 2023." Encouraging macro headlines "haven't yet" translated into increased industry investment, so they are not in guidance.

**Priority 1: Drive the top line.** The plan is to grow the installed base and help customers raise utilization in new and existing applications.
- Management anticipates ongoing high-throughput instrument orders through 2024, mainly from further conversion of NovaSeq 6000 customers to the X, from fleet expansion, and longer term from new-to-high-throughput customers. Given the large number of 2023 placements, "we will ultimately ship fewer high-throughput instruments in 2024."
- In 2024 the installed base will support customers launching large projects and sequencing-intensive applications such as multiomics (combining DNA with other biological measurements), liquid biopsy and minimal residual disease (MRD, detecting traces of cancer remaining after treatment).
- **XLEAP-SBS** (the new sequencing chemistry that powers the X platform, improving quality, turnaround time and cost) will be made available this quarter to mid-throughput customers on existing NextSeq 1k/2k instruments without needing hardware upgrades. Thaysen said this strengthens Illumina's leadership and helps in single cell, spatial and proteomics.
- Q4 saw higher-than-expected growth in X consumables after the late-October launch of the 25B reagent kit. In January 2024 Illumina launched a 1.5B kit, which together with the 25B and 10B flow cells gives NovaSeq X customers a full product suite.
- Customers will keep drawing down NovaSeq 6000 consumables inventories as they transition, but pull-through on the NovaSeq X will drive overall high-throughput consumables growth in 2024.

**Priority 2: Operational excellence.** Illumina has launched a multi-year effort to improve margins through productivity and further cost savings. In January 2024 it took another action to optimize its global workforce; cumulative role reductions over the past year total about 12%. The latest actions were aligned with late-2023 portfolio optimization. Thaysen said Illumina will manage near-term costs while building agility for sustained growth and margins.

**Priority 3: Resolve GRAIL quickly.** In December 2023 Illumina announced it will divest GRAIL, aiming to finalize terms by the end of Q2 2024. It is pursuing parallel paths, a third-party sale or a capital markets transaction (such as an offering to the public), consistent with the European Commission order. Progress so far: GRAIL's Form 10 (a registration filing for spinning off or listing a company) has been confidentially filed with the SEC; advisors are advancing both paths; and a special committee of the Board, set up in Q3 2023, helps keep the process moving.

**Closing remarks.** Thaysen said success means deepening customer relationships and collaboration to drive NGS (next-generation sequencing) adoption, with the aim of making "our customers the heroes in their labs." He highlighted evolving the sequencing platform and delivering "greater sample to answer solutions," integrating customers' workflows to build Illumina's share of wallet. A "comprehensive strategy work is well underway," with more to be shared later in 2024.

### Joydeep Goswami (CFO)

Goswami delivered the financial results and guidance detailed in the "Results and guidance" section above.

## Question-and-answer session

### Doug Schenkel, Wolfe Research

**Question:** Asked what Illumina is seeing in the field with the 25B flow cell, which cuts cost per genome to about $200 but requires enough samples to fill the flow cell. He asked about X consumables spending versus Q3 and whether sample access is a problem.

**Answer:** Thaysen said management has been "very pleased" with 25B pickup since the late-November launch (as he phrased it here), which met expectations and was well received. It has "completely now unleashed the full performance of the instrument," and he expects it to draw more customers to the X. Many customers are still doing validation work, but they have projects big enough to fill the flow cell. Goswami added that about 40% of X customers have adopted the 25B so far.

### Puneet Souda, Leerink Partners

**Question:** How does Illumina get to low-single-digit consumables growth given the 25B, lower cost per genome, China and the macro backdrop? He also asked for GRAIL progress, level of interest, and expected 2024 spend.

**Answer (GRAIL, Thaysen):** He is "very pleased" with progress; advisors are in conversations with interested parties, running dual tracks (capital markets or a transaction). Illumina remains on track to finalize all terms by the end of June. Because management expects to finish with GRAIL this year, it is not providing guidance on GRAIL expenditure.

**Answer (consumables, Goswami):** Management was encouraged by Q4 uptake of the 25B and 10B flow cells and by the elasticity (more sequencing as cost falls) seen in underlying sequencing activity, which rose for a second consecutive quarter. The growth is broad-based across research and clinical customers, both quarter over quarter and year over year. What offsets it is the price transition as customers move from NovaSeq 6000 to X, "very similar to the price transition" seen moving from HiSeq to NovaSeq 6000, plus the expected drop in NovaSeq 6000 consumables. That is the main reason growth is only low single digits.

### Vijay Kumar, Evercore ISI

**Question:** When will GRAIL's confidential S-1 (as he called it) become public? He noted GRAIL's operating losses were about $700 million last year and said a capital markets deal at that spend level is hard to foresee. He also asked whether NovaSeq 6000 pull-through of $700,000 to $800,000 starts the year at the low end and exits at the high end.

**Answer (GRAIL, Thaysen):** The filing is confidential; he does not expect it to be public "before very late in Q1 maybe dipping into Q2," and Illumina will say when it is available.

**Answer (pull-through, Goswami):** No. Pull-through is a calculated figure, total consumables sold in a quarter divided by the active installed base, and management has no assumption about quarterly spread.

### Dan Arias, Stifel

**Question:** Clinical customers, many of them commercial labs watching margins, could be incentivized to move from NovaSeq 6000 to X faster to save costs. Is that being seen, given clinical validation moves slowly?

**Answer (Thaysen):** Most clinical customers are using the X's lower cost to expand sequencing depth and run bigger panels. Most current panels stay on the NovaSeq 6000, continuing to drive volume there, while customers validate new assays (some not possible on the 6000) on the X. Several specialty lab customers have ordered "tens" of NovaSeq X, so he expects a lot of volume.

### Subha Nambi, Guggenheim Securities

**Question:** R&D was about $1 billion in 2022; since 2019 revenue has grown at a 6% compound annual rate but R&D at 12%. Why can't dollar R&D fall over the next one to two years, and where is it allocated?

**Answer (Thaysen):** Substantial innovation investment is still required, with a strong pipeline and early-stage opportunities in NGS and multiomics. He agreed R&D has risen and said Illumina wants to change that trajectory: high investment is still needed, but R&D as a percentage of revenue will start to come down over the next years.

**Answer (Goswami):** R&D has already been cut over the past year, strategically, in areas with much longer or riskier payoffs. R&D also included areas like medical affairs supporting customers; as customers matured, Illumina could pull back "without affecting innovation."

### Mason Carrico, Stephens

**Question:** For high-throughput customers who already bought the X, what is the fleet-expansion conversation, how much has already come through in orders or shipments, and how much remains for this year?

**Answer (Thaysen):** High-volume customers who bought one or two X are starting to consider more. They use the first few to validate assays, then want more to run production, but "very few customers have still gone into production mode." He estimated fewer than half of high-volume customers have purchased an X so far. Some who bought one or two have ordered 10, so he sees big opportunities in 2024 and 2025.

**Answer (Goswami):** Illumina did not expect this fleet expansion before the 25B flow cell launched; these are "very early innings" and fleet expansions are expected this year.

### David Westenberg, Piper Sandler

**Question:** How does Illumina view competition seen at AGBT (the genomics conference), such as combined spatial and sequencing on one platform, and would it pursue that? How does it stack up against Ultima's $100 genome, whose specs look much better than a couple of years ago?

**Answer (Thaysen):** Illumina tracks the market and takes competition "very seriously." It intends to be in multiomics, starting with proteomics via its relationship with Stemologic (as transcribed), and believes many more modalities could run on the instrument. It will share when ready to market and speak less about future plans. The NovaSeq X offers an attractive price point across many applications, not just a few. Cost per sequencing is no longer the only measure; customers should consider whole-workflow cost, including manual steps, waste and instrument spend. Looking at details, he said Illumina's platform is "very attractive and very differentiated."

### Conor McNamara, RBC

**Question:** With Q1 operating margin guided to 18% and 20% for the year, what is the cadence, and how should the tax rate look in 2024 given the mid-20s core rate?

**Answer (Goswami; the transcript labels this as Jacob, but the content is financial):** The core Illumina tax rate should be relatively constant in 2024; no consolidated guidance yet. Operating margin typically rises with volume through the year as gross margin rises, but may dip in Q2 as merit increases and inflation take effect, then increase quarter on quarter after that. On R&D capitalization, the fix has passed the House but still needs the Senate and the President's signature; if enacted it would further improve the tax rate, but it is not built into assumptions.

### Sung Ji Nam, Scotiabank

**Question:** Is the MRD assay under development with Janssen tumor-informed or tumor-naive, and could it become an IVD (FDA-regulated diagnostic) assay?

**Answer (Thaysen):** The approach is "quite unique": a whole-genome-based MRD assay. It is initially tumor-informed (uses the patient's own tumor profile) but could move to tumor-naive with more data. Illumina has invested heavily in platforms and infrastructure that allow moving assays to IVD "when the time is right"; for now, the pharma agreement is mainly to explore the assay in various forms.

**Answer (Goswami):** Illumina has no intention of commercializing it through its own clinical lab (CLIA lab); that lab runs very few samples. It is interested in working with customers and partners to develop the assay.

### Patrick Donnelly, Citi

**Question:** What caused the Q4 gross margin step down, which was lighter than expected, and how will it progress in 2024? Also, how is Jacob thinking about pricing, given competitors at AGBT say they see discounting, particularly in mid-throughput?

**Answer (Goswami):** Several factors lowered Q4 gross margin: lower revenue (less absorption of fixed costs), higher-than-expected X contribution, more lower-margin partnership revenue, and a mix shift from 6000 to X consumables. He said COGS (cost of goods sold) productivity progress in 2023 is starting to show benefits into 2024 and beyond.

**Answer (Thaysen):** Illumina offers a premium product and customers accept premium pricing. Cost per gigabase alone is "too simplistic"; customers care about sample-to-insight workflow cost, including sample automation and especially informatics, a key cost driver. With the Dragon platform (as he described it) and comprehensive informatics, Illumina can offer a cost-attractive solution. In mid-throughput, with XLEAP-SBS, Illumina is coming out with "more attractive pricing, quality and more capacity" on the NextSeq 1k and 2k, driving elasticity at the new price point. He said Illumina will "move the nuts on many more elements than just one pricing element" (as transcribed).

### Eve Burstein, Bernstein Research

**Question:** Illumina ended the year with 885 NextSeq placements (as she described), above pre-pandemic but below the prior two years. How much is due to post-pandemic changes, capital and cash-flow constraints, or competition, and what is normalized demand?

**Answer (Goswami):** Major factors: (1) after the COVID-era boom in 2021-22 in mid- and low-throughput instrument sales, demand has normalized as the COVID threat receded; (2) 2023 macro conditions made customers more conservative with capital purchases; (3) China, in mid-throughput especially, hit by competition and macro conditions. Competition was a clear factor in China but not a huge one elsewhere. Illumina tracks competitive win rates closely, and they "stabilize[d] in Q3 and in Q4." He did not give a normalized demand figure.

### Dan Brennan, TD Cowen

**Question:** X pull-through was about $1 million in Q4, and his math implies roughly $850,000 in 2024. Is that reasonable and what drives the step down?

**Answer (Thaysen):** It is "way early innings" to look at X pull-through; Illumina has not done that math and will provide it when the situation is more stable.

**Answer (Goswami):** He said he disagreed that math hasn't been done, but "the math is not relevant" and Illumina doesn't want to confuse investors. Many customers are in validation, especially on the 25B. A number will come when things stabilize; so far results are encouraging.

### Dan Leonard, UBS

**Question:** After the customer listening tour, how healthy are diagnostics markets, and how will FDA regulation of lab-developed tests (LDTs) affect demand?

**Answer (Thaysen, health of clinical markets):** Illumina serves many clinical customers who build companies on its ecosystem, primarily in LDTs, and has its own offerings. Its TSO 500 (a tumor profiling kit) business "has been growing really fast," is now more than $100 million, and is expected to grow at a high double-digit rate in 2024 with "no end" in sight. He is "really bullish." Illumina is conducting a strategic review of how this business fits into core strategy, but wants to play in the space.

**Answer (Thaysen, regulation):** Illumina is monitoring closely and helping some customers prepare. Larger customers can use a single-site PMA (premarket approval pathway). Some Class III products may be moved down to Class II, which would reduce clinical-trial requirements; that is still under consideration. He said Illumina is well positioned "almost independent on where FDA is landing."

**Answer (Goswami):** Illumina is "about the only company" with ISO 13485 (medical device quality standard) classification on all instruments and most consumables, even for research-use-only products, and has a path to IVD if customers want it.

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord

**Question:** Guidance kept the prior range (from November) while risks remain; how much conservatism is baked in, and what are the main swing factors for Q1 and first-half versus second-half? Also, is $700,000 to $800,000 NovaSeq 6000 pull-through aggressive compared to the HiSeq decline after NovaSeq launched?

**Answer (6000 pull-through, Goswami):** When NovaSeq 6000 launched, Illumina had fewer clinical customers. Now about 50% of consumables revenue (the transcript says "Conn's revenue") comes from clinical customers with validated assays that won't change platforms in the short term, so the pull-through decline will be more muted than with HiSeq.

**Answer (guidance, Goswami):** Management has repeatedly called this a "prudent approach." Some markets show signs the macro may be turning, but this isn't assumed. The one pessimistic assumption is China, which recent news seems to corroborate. The main swing factor is X adoption; the team is spending a great deal of time helping X customers transition quickly. If markets pick up, Illumina is well positioned for upside; further macro headwinds reducing customers' ability to buy X or spend on consumables would hurt.

### Rachel Vatnsda, JPMorgan

**Question:** Following up on Q4 gross margin, is the consumables mix shift from 6000 to X a function of low volume leverage during ramp or are X consumables structurally lower margin? How will mix affect 2024 gross margin?

**Answer (Goswami):** X consumables were designed so margins at scale would be comparable to NovaSeq 6000 consumables (the transcript says "comparable to X consumables at scale," evidently a slip). Early in adoption, margins are lower because Illumina has not fully scaled; this should more or less normalize through 2024. For 2024 gross margin, the two largest improvements are a benefit from lower instrument placements (instruments carry lower margin) and COGS productivity actions.
