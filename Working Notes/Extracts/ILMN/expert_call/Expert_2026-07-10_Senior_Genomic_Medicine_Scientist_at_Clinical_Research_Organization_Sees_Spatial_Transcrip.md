# Channel check with a senior genomic medicine scientist at a clinical research organization: weak Q2 2026 sequencing volumes, spatial transcriptomics demand, Illumina loyalty

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/Expert_2026-07-10_Senior_Genomic_Medicine_Scientist_at_Clinical_Research_Organization_Sees_Spatial_Transcrip.md` · Date: 2026-07-10 · AI-interviewer channel check (expert interview 10 Jul 2026, published 15 Jul 2026); expert is a senior genomic medicine scientist / associate scientific director at a North Carolina clinical research organization (name not given) · expert call*

**Summary.** A long-time Illumina customer at a clinical research organization (CRO) says her/their lab's Q2 2026 sequencing output and consumables spending were down roughly 10-15% versus Q2 2025 because of budget and funding uncertainty, with a very weak May and a strong June rebound. The expert expects full-year 2026 output to be about 15% below 2025 (worse than the 10% first expected), is loyal to Illumina, sees Roche's AXELIOS as only a minor threat and PacBio losing traction, and calls spatial transcriptomics the hottest trend with proteomics (helped by Illumina's SomaLogic acquisition) next. Equipment upgrade plans have been pushed back because utilization is lower.

# Channel check: Senior genomic medicine scientist at a clinical research organization (interview 10 Jul 2026)

## Expert background and relationship to Illumina

- **Type of source:** an Illumina customer (a channel check, meaning a structured interview with someone who buys and uses the product). The interviewer is an AI, and the platform's perspective label for the expert is "Doctor/Medical Professional." The interview took place on 10 Jul 2026 and was published 15 Jul 2026.
- **Role:** the platform bio describes a "Senior Genomic Medicine Scientist at a Clinical Research Organization headquartered in North Carolina," focused on informatics analysis using sequencers. In the interview, the expert calls themself the "associate scientific director." They review and direct next-generation sequencing (NGS, high-throughput DNA/RNA reading) and "omics" systems (large-scale biological data such as genes, RNA, proteins) for clinical trials run for academia, pharmaceutical companies and research hospitals.
- **Prior roles:** director of the NGS Omics Facility Core at Colgate-Palmolive (running all sequencers and related materials), and earlier work at MD Anderson on NGS systems and workflows for immunotherapeutics and drug development.
- **Current duties:** omics research and protocol development, SOP (standard operating procedure) development, and data analysis for pharma. They also direct the sequencing. Work covers RNA and DNA sequencing for clinical studies, sometimes pre-clinical work, and "machine development." Customers are mainly pharma and research hospitals (especially cancer hospitals), with a little academia.
- **Illumina relationship and recency:** the expert says they have worked with Illumina "for over 10 years, going on 15 years," "graduated off of using Illumina sequencing," and gets a loyalty discount. They do this channel check every quarter, and their experience is current (Q2 2026 data, with a view into early July 2026).
- **Bias to keep in mind:** the expert is openly pro-Illumina ("gold standard of sequencing"). Much of the view on competitors is personal opinion rather than measured data.

## Q2 2026 volume drivers (direct experience)

- The expert said the quarter was "complicated" because the lab was "dealing with unknowns for the budget" and there were few trials early in Q2.
- April "wasn't too bad," but May was "a dead May, which was highly unusual" and "depressing." June saw a rebound with "a lot of studies and a lot of sequencing," bringing the quarter back to about average. The mix was DNA and RNA sequencing, bulk RNA, and spatial transcriptomics, which the lab is "starting to transition into" as the "hot topic."
- Cause of the May dip: budget uncertainty leading to fewer clinical trials. Q1 2026 was also slow, so the weakness may have carried over. Some pharma studies start 1 July, so the expert assumes sponsors lacked budget until then. Early July showed studies starting to rebound.
- Other factors: occasional trouble getting materials on time. Last year was mainly budget constraints, which "seems to be opening up." Lack of funding also mattered because many grants were not being approved, which is now spilling into academic hospitals. The expert said "I never really got a direct answer" on the cause, so this is partly inference.

## Sequencing output (gigabases of data) and forecast

- **Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025:** June was in line with the past two to three years of Junes, but May was "uncharacteristically" low. The expert estimated output down "somewhere like a decrease in 15%" year over year, noting the Q2 2025 and 2024 comparisons were "really high." The expert is compiling the metrics and delayed submitting them because of the June burst.
- **Versus the expert's own expectations:** "a little down than what I would have liked," though June raised hopes of meeting the target. The expert admitted uncertainty about their expectations because the year has been so unpredictable.
- **Full-year 2026 vs 2025:** the expert first expected output down about 10% (optimistically) and now thinks it is tracking nearer 15% down, "which is depressing." They said some carryover from last year's uncertainty is "hindering our progress."
- **Why the wider decline:** funding is lower; the expert has heard budgets and grants are being cut and that sponsors are merging studies, fitting low-priority samples into runs with high-priority ones. The expert's read is that clients are only funding high-priority work.
- **Versus 90 days ago:** more optimistic, driven by June. The expert expects Q3 to stay near normal levels of the last three years and hopes for the usual strong Q4 to get them "over the top." The expert promised to give an update.

## Consumables and reagents (reagent kits bought for each run)

- **Spending Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025:** down "maybe 10%," because there were fewer studies than last year.
- **Cause:** the expert said it is "just the lack of getting in projects," meaning volume-driven. Price increases also make labs cut back when they can, but this was secondary.
- **Pricing (last 90 days):** about the same; the expert could not "speak for Illumina." They get a loyalty discount, and holiday and trade-show deals are consistent across the board.
- **Inventory:** "sufficient." The expert used to hold excess stock but now keeps a leaner level, since few high-priority next-day projects have come in.
- **Lead times:** unchanged over 90 days; occasional backorders, but "I never had a problem with getting stuff in."

## Competitive landscape (opinion, with limited direct evidence)

- **Equipment pricing:** Oxford Nanopore has pushed to win users and "decreased a lot of the cost of things." PacBio was largely consistent, with a little promotion. Illumina is "so dominant that you're not going to overtake them in terms of making things lower because the quality is so good."
- **Platforms gaining favor:** NovaSeq, NextSeq and HiSeq (an older Illumina line) are being used more because spatial transcriptomics needs sequencers with their capacity. The expert was trained on NovaSeq and wants another one or an upgrade when budget allows. The expert expects NovaSeq, and maybe HiSeq, to gain traction over the next 12 months.
- **Platforms losing favor:** PacBio and other long-read platforms are "losing traction." The expert recalled long reads gaining popularity a few years ago as accuracy improved, but now people are returning to single-cell work and whole genome sequencing, which pharma is using a lot, especially in early-phase studies. The expert also said PacBio and Nanopore retain some value for broader transcriptomics views but will fall further behind Illumina.
- **Why short reads are favored (expert's personal opinion):** budget pressure makes people condense experiments. A single slide stained for protein can be imaged and then sequenced for RNA, giving protein and molecular-pathway information in one experiment instead of three separate ones (e.g., Western blot, single-cell sequencing and immunostaining). This saves time and lowers project cost.
- **Brand preference:** the expert would like to use Illumina "all across the board" for consistency in publications and client reporting, and says clients prefer Illumina over alternatives, "especially something like PacBio, which people aren't that crazy about."
- **Competitor products:** nothing has caught the expert's eye. Long-read tools are "pretty basic," and the PacBio Revio gives good, accurate long reads that suffice for the expert's needs. If upgrading, they would focus on Illumina.

### Roche AXELIOS

- Roche's new AXELIOS platform (based on "sequencing by expansion," per the expert) is claimed to do whole genome sequencing at an "extremely fast rate."
- The expert does not see it creating competitive pressure now or expecting to, and rated it "a minor threat." They want more publications and hands-on experience first. The expert noted that Illumina has "years of production and name out between colleagues" and said "You're going to have to step the game up a little bit more to get me off of that."

## Equipment purchase plans

- The expert plans to propose upgrading some Illumina sequencers by the end of Q4 2026, the first such opportunity in a few years, and is unsure it will "fly in today's economy." They want new technology they can sell internally.
- Over the past 90 days, plans have been pushed back because Q2 and overall output are lower than last year: "It's a hard sell to get the company to buy a product when we're not really using it."

## Illumina products and roadmap

- **Excitement:** no particular favorite; "I haven't been sold on anything yet."
- **NovaSeq X:** the expert thinks it will be "pretty cool." They recalled seeing an 18-month roadmap (around February-April, date uncertain) and say NovaSeq systems are great for spatial transcriptomics output.
- **TruPath Genome:** "very likely" to evaluate it. For its share of the lab's volume by end of 2026, the expert gave a loose range of 30-60%, "around 50-ish percent," while admitting they have not mapped it out. They see it as a premium upgrade of existing Illumina work and a way to consolidate away from long-read platforms.
- **SomaLogic acquisition (proteomics, the study of proteins):** improves Illumina's value proposition. Proteomics is "the weakest point of my core right now," and the deal would increase their chances of using Illumina more.
- **Support:** consistently satisfied for 10-15 years; customer service "has always been great," products on target, not "crazy expensive," and easy to use once learned. No recent changes noted.

## Market outlook

- Whole genome sequencing is increasingly used by pharma, particularly in early-phase studies.
- Spatial transcriptomics is "the hottest trend right now" at their lab, because it condenses studies and lowers costs.
- The expert thinks focus rotates "every three, four years," and predicts proteomics will be the next hot area, since RNA and DNA sequencing give a "roadmap" while proteomics shows what is happening in a body, drug response or disease. They said Illumina "did a good job" acquiring SomaLogic.

## Key takeaways

- **Fact-like data from one lab (direct):** Q2 2026 output about -15% and consumables spend about -10% year over year; May weak, June strong; stable pricing, inventory and lead times.
- **Expert forecast:** 2026 output about -15% (previously -10%); modest optimism for Q4.
- **Opinion or speculation:** AXELIOS a minor threat; PacBio losing traction; proteomics the next trend; TruPath at 30-60% of volume.
- **Caveat:** a single-lab, pro-Illumina view, with imprecise estimates ("maybe," "somewhere like").
