# Channel check with the CEO of Complete Genomics: Illumina expected to lose share to Element, Ultima and Roche (interview 26 Mar 2026)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/Expert_2026-03-26_Executive_at_Life_Sciences_Company_Sees_Illumina_Losing_Market_Share_Amid_Rising_Competiti.md` · Date: 2026-03-26 · AI-interviewed channel check (expert network "Channel Check" grid, published 29 Mar 2026); expert is the CEO of Complete Genomics (a sequencing competitor), classed as "Consultant" perspective; interviewer is an AI · expert call*

**Summary.** The CEO of Complete Genomics, a competing sequencing company with 13+ years in the field, says his customers' sequencing volume rose in Q1 2026 versus Q1 2025 because academic funding is less uncertain, but only "barely" met his expectations, and his full-year 2026 outlook is less optimistic than 90 days earlier. He sees consumable prices falling and all suppliers, Illumina included, discounting more to win share. He is confident Illumina will lose share as Element, Ultima and Roche (Axelios) bring new products, though he has no concrete evidence yet of who is winning.

## Expert background and how to weigh this source

- **Who the expert is.** The expert describes themself as the CEO of Complete Genomics, with "over 13 years' experience in genetic sequencing, including Illumina and Complete Genomics platforms," spanning R&D, manufacturing and commercial work. The grid bio calls them "CEO of a Life Sciences Company in California" with visibility into sequencing purchasing decisions and utilization. [Expert: CEO of Complete Genomics, 2026-03-26]
- **Relationship to Illumina.** The expert is effectively a competitor, not an Illumina customer. They once used Illumina's HiSeq (an older Illumina sequencer) but say it "has been obsoleted." They currently own and run no Illumina platforms and use mostly Complete Genomics platforms. They use no other competitor platforms (PacBio, Oxford Nanopore and so on). Their knowledge of Illumina is therefore indirect or historical, and their view of the market comes from their own customers' behavior and from public announcements.
- **Recency.** The interview took place 26 March 2026 (published 29 March 2026), so it covers Q1 2026 while the quarter was still running, and the expert says it was "less than three months into a year."
- **Their customers.** Complete Genomics has an office in San Jose, California, and a customer experience center near Boston, Massachusetts. Customers include academic institutions (Stanford and Yale named, plus other major universities), research institutions, in-vitro diagnostic (IVD, meaning commercial diagnostic test) companies and pharmaceutical companies. The expert did not name the IVD customers.
- **Caveats.** The format is an AI-run questionnaire with short answers. Several answers are explicitly speculation or "I don't know." Because the expert runs a rival to Illumina, comments on Illumina losing share may carry a self-interested view; the expert does not discuss this.

## What drives sequencing volume

- **Customer funding is the dominant factor.** The expert says the most important driver of their volume is customers' funding level. Customers with funding and samples run a lot of sequencing; uncertain funding or sample bottlenecks reduce volume. [Expert, Mar 2026]
- **Effects come with a lag.** Ongoing projects continue, but the next project or next batch of samples is delayed when funding is uncertain. This is most true of academic customers, whose money comes from research grants. IVD customers are less affected because they typically have steady flows of diagnostic samples. [Expert, Mar 2026]
- The expert said funding and sample availability were the main factors and named no others.

## Q1 2026 volume and the 2026 outlook

- **Q1 2026 output is up year on year.** The expert's total instrument output in Q1 2026 is increasing versus Q1 2025. The stated reason is that funding has "more certainty" than in Q1 2025, when President Trump taking office "creates certain uncertainty in the funding level for academics." [Expert, Mar 2026]
- **Only barely meeting expectations.** Q1 2026 output is "barely meeting my expectations, it's not exceeding. Definitely not exceeding."
- **Full-year 2026 forecast.** The expert expects a slight increase, "about single-digit increase compared to 2025" in sequencing volume or throughput. They stress this does not translate into higher revenue, because the sequencing price per gigabase (Gb, a billion bases of DNA read; the standard unit of sequencing cost) has been falling.
- **Less optimistic than 90 days ago.** The outlook is less optimistic than three months earlier. The expert says they are unsure of the reasons given how early in the year it is, but customer demand and needs look "weaker than we expected 90 days ago."

## Consumables spending and pricing

- **Spending flat, volume up.** Consumables (the reagent kits needed for each sequencing run) spending in Q1 2026 is the same in dollars as Q1 2025, but the sequencing volume obtained is higher, because the price of consumables has fallen versus a year earlier. The expert says they are "delivering more sequence in the volume on a similar dollar amount of consumables."
- **Cause of price declines.** The expert names competition as the main driver. Major suppliers including Element and Ultima have announced newer, higher-throughput and cheaper sequencing platforms that "put a competitive pressure on everyone." [Expert, Mar 2026]

## Equipment pricing environment

- Over the prior 90 days, the expert has seen Illumina and its competitors alike become "more willing to discount in order to gain market share" on sequencing equipment. [Expert, Mar 2026]

## Market share: what is known and what is predicted

- **No hard evidence yet.** Over the past 90 days there were major announcements from Illumina's competitors, and product announcements from Illumina, but because the competing products are not yet on sale, the expert has no concrete evidence of which platform is gaining or losing share. They say the trend in Q2 and Q3 2026 will be interesting to watch, in particular whether newly announced platforms win share, which would largely be at Illumina's expense. Today, "I just don't know."
- **Factors that will decide platform choice.** "It's about the data quality, cost and ease of use," which the expert calls the fundamental factors every sequencing lab weighs.
- **Prediction for the next 12 months (opinion, forward-looking).** The expert says: "It's difficult to predict which platform will gain market share, but I can predict that Illumina will definitely lose market share." The reason given is that Element, Ultima and Roche all have new, aggressive products coming. Incumbents such as Illumina "essentially have only one way to go, which is losing their market share." The expert does not know how much Illumina will lose or how much competitors will gain.

## Competitor products named by the expert

- **Element (Element Biosciences).** Its highest-throughput new instrument will directly compete with Illumina's NovaSeq (Illumina's high-throughput production sequencer family). [Expert, Mar 2026]
- **Ultima (Ultima Genomics).** Named with Element as having announced larger-throughput, cheaper platforms, and as a source of price pressure; no further product detail.
- **Roche.** Has announced a new platform, available "later this year" per the expert, that would compete directly with Illumina's medium- to highest-throughput instruments. The expert says it is "tailored for the oncology market with a faster turnaround time and easier sample prep," which will appeal directly to IVD customers who present a different kind of competition to Illumina.
- **Roche Axelios, asked about specifically.** The interviewer described Axelios as launching "next year" (this conflicts with the expert's "later this year"; the expert did not correct it). The expert expects a "major impact" for two reasons: (1) technology, as it is "a different technology from Illumina" and "a technological leap" relative to it, and a "fundamental change"; (2) Roche is a major player in IVD with its own clinical applications that can "jump-start" Axelios adoption within its existing customer base. The expert's transcript spells the product "Excelios"; it is read here as Axelios.

## Illumina's own products and strategy

- **Multi-omics focus (what the expert is most excited about).** Multi-omics means measuring several types of biological data (DNA, RNA, proteins and so on) rather than only DNA sequence. The expert sees Illumina trying to provide "data more than just a sequencing" and to become "the one-stop shop." The open question is whether Illumina's multi-omics data can be competitive with other providers.
- **Pipeline items the expert looks forward to.** Single-cell solutions and the proteomics (protein measurement) technology Illumina is acquiring from SomaLogic.
- **SomaLogic acquisition.** The interviewer said it is expected to close in the first half of 2026. The expert thinks it will help Illumina's multi-omics approach and looks forward to the offerings after closing. No further assessment was given.
- **Customer support.** The expert, speaking in the context of Illumina support, says it "has remained the same," they are "generally satisfied," and there are no changes. (Given the expert runs no Illumina platforms now, the basis for this is not explained.)

## Purchasing plans

- The expert has no plans to buy new sequencing equipment in the next 12 months, and plans have not changed in the last 90 days.

## Longer-term view of sequencing

- The expert believes genomic sequencing will grow over the next few years, especially in volume, but does not expect dramatic growth unless technological breakthroughs enable applications that use genomic data for individual diagnostics. Acceptance of such tests by clinicians is the critical piece: "The adoption by clinicians in more wider tests are the critical piece for the next leak" (the sentence is cut off in the transcript, apparently meaning "leap"). [Expert, Mar 2026]
