# Cancer-center genomics core director on ILMN: weak academic funding, price increases, Roche Axelios interest (Q1 2026 channel check)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/Expert_2026-03-31_Director_at_Cancer_Center_Sees_Challenges_and_Innovations_in_Genomic_Sequencing_Amid_Fundi.md` · Date: 2026-03-31 · Channel check expert interview (AI interviewer), published 2 Apr 2026; expert is the Director of a Genomics Shared Resource at a New York cancer center (unnamed) · expert call*

**Summary.** A director of a research-use-only genomics core facility at a New York comprehensive cancer center says Q1 2026 sequencing volume is slightly below Q1 2025 because academic funding is tight, with fewer and smaller projects and fewer exploratory studies. Illumina raised reagent, shipping, tariff and service-contract prices (new pricing mid-February), while single-cell and spatial work are growing. The expert would buy a NovaSeq X today, sees Roche's new sequencer (Axelios, formerly SBX) as the most exciting competitor, and rates Illumina's support as exceptional.

## Expert background and how to weigh it

**Who the expert is.** The expert is the Director of a Genomics Shared Resource (a "core facility", a central lab that provides sequencing services to many research groups) at a comprehensive cancer center in New York. They are a direct customer of Illumina, not a former employee. [Expert: director of genomics core, NY cancer center, interview 2026-03-31; published 2026-04-02]

**What the lab does.** The lab covers essentially all genomics: Sanger sequencing (older, single-fragment sequencing), microarrays (chips that read many known DNA variants), and a great deal of next-generation sequencing (NGS, massively parallel reading of DNA or RNA). Library prep (preparing samples for the sequencer) spans whole genome, whole exome (protein-coding regions only), RNA-seq, targeted sequencing, ChIP-seq, ATAC-seq and CUT&RUN. It also runs single-cell sequencing on the 10x Genomics platform and ParseBio, and spatial transcriptomics (measuring gene activity by location in a tissue) on 10x Visium. [Expert, 2026-03-31]

**Customers served.** It is a research-use-only facility. About 99% of users are academic researchers, from the expert's own institute or outside it, across the U.S. with a few from abroad. A small share are industry users. [Expert, 2026-03-31]

**Instruments.**
- The primary workhorse is the Illumina NovaSeq 6000, used for almost everything: RNA-seq, exome, whole genome, RRBS and whole-genome bisulfite sequencing (both DNA methylation methods).
- A NextSeq 500 is old and the lab is "pretty much done with that machine". The lab has a NextSeq 2000 (a neighboring lab's unit is used for small projects).
- The MiSeq (small benchtop sequencer) is used for metagenomics, small custom panels, and testing libraries before they go on the NovaSeq.
- The lab also runs iScan, Illumina's microarray system.
- Competitor use: the lab recently bought an Oxford Nanopore (ONT) device and is still in training. Planned uses are whole genome sequencing, adaptive sampling (selectively sequencing chosen regions) and 16S/microbiome sequencing. It has no other competitor systems.
[Expert, 2026-03-31]

**Direct experience versus speculation.** Statements on the lab's volumes, reagent spend, price increases, support and equipment plans are first-hand. Comments on Roche's sequencer, Element, Ultima, Singular, the SomaLogic deal and long-read limits are secondhand or opinion, and the expert says so on proteomics ("I really don't know that market"). Experience is current (Q1 2026).

## Demand and volumes in Q1 2026

**Funding is the main headwind.** The expert says academic and research funding is causing "a little bit of slowdown" in both the number and size of projects. Researchers who have money are "very judicious"; money already allocated is being spent, but they are cutting pilot and R&D projects. The expert sees "a drop in more of an exploratory studies." [Expert, 2026-03-31]

**Second negative: aging platform economics.** The lab runs a NovaSeq 6000, "now not the most cost effective sequencer." Both factors are called "slightly" negative. [Expert, 2026-03-31]

**Positives.** Single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptomics are increasing and keep the lab "very busy." [Expert, 2026-03-31]

**Output versus last year.** Gigabase output (gigabases are billions of DNA letters read, a measure of sequencing volume) on Illumina instruments is "slightly down" versus Q1 2025. The expert explains that early 2025, before funding changed, had a large pipeline of projects, and that funding has been difficult "for last one year." Q1 2026 volume is "certainly lower" than last year. Output is in line with the lab's own expectation, which was lower: "it's meeting our lower expectations." [Expert, 2026-03-31]

**Full-year 2026 outlook.** The expert expects 2026 output to be lower than 2025 if the lab keeps the same technology, citing rising reagent costs and less funding. They declined to give a percentage ("whether it's going to be 15%, 20%, it's very hard") and noted grants can suddenly lift volume. Nothing has changed in the last 90 days: funding "has been low since early January" with no significant change either way. [Expert, 2026-03-31]

## Consumables spend and Illumina pricing

**Spend.** Consumables (reagent kits needed for every run) spend is still lower than Q1 2025 because the lab runs fewer projects, but it is rising. Illumina implemented new pricing in mid-February 2026, raising costs a little. The lab ordered extra reagents in January in anticipation of the increase. Once that stock is used, the expert expects total spend to go up even if volume stays below last year. [Expert, 2026-03-31]

**Drivers.** Spend reflects a mix of reagent price, sample volume and pre-buy stocking. The "biggest driving factor is the number of projects"; a very large project would raise spend. [Expert, 2026-03-31]

**Price increases (recollection, not exact).** The expert says "Illumina prices have gone up," believes NovaSeq 6000 reagent costs rose, and says Illumina now adds shipping surcharges and tariff surcharges. A machine service contract price also increased. In summary: reagents, shipping, tariff surcharges and service contracts are all higher: "Pretty much every price is going up on every side." [Expert, 2026-03-31]

## Competitive landscape

**Pricing environment.** No notably more aggressive pricing in the past 90 days, but the market is "certainly getting more competitive" with Element, Ultima and now the Roche sequencer. Element and others price reagents "very aggressively compared to Illumina," but that matters only if a lab owns those machines. Owning many platforms is impractical because each machine costs $500,000 to $1 million, so the lab has no other systems. [Expert, 2026-03-31]

**What drives platform choice.**
- Cost per gigabase (or per million reads) is the main factor; every new entrant pitches itself as cheaper than Illumina.
- Illumina has countered by adding flow cells (the consumable slides where sequencing happens) and raising flow-cell throughput. The lab made no changes on the 6000, but NovaSeq X pricing is "a little bit more aggressive" as Illumina competes with others.
- End-user researchers care only about cost per gigabase. Core facilities that buy machines also weigh how long a machine has been on the market, vendor support (downtime makes a cheap system "not a good system") and running/operational cost.
- Long read (PacBio, Nanopore) is a specific application, not a general deciding factor. Short-read vendors are stretching read lengths to about 600 base pairs, also not decisive. "End users generally go with whatever is the cheapest."
[Expert, 2026-03-31]

**Share outlook (next 12 months).** The expert expects Roche's new sequencer to gain. There is a lot of interest because it is new and does "really cool things." Roche is expected to start selling "sometime in summer," with no exact date. As the biggest provider, Illumina would likely lose some share if Roche delivers on its promises; effects on Ultima and Element are unclear. [Expert opinion, 2026-03-31]

**Roche Axelios (formerly SBX).** Described by the interviewer as launching "next year". The expert says Roche has shared much but not all details; once it launches and assays are developed with customers, it will be "a very big player in the market." The expert calls it the most exciting competitor, able to do "amazing stuff very cheap, very fast." [Expert, 2026-03-31]

**Other competitors.** Element is trying spatial applications on its sequencer, and Singular is doing spatial transcriptomics on its. The expert says there are other dedicated spatial platforms, so this does not make them want to buy a sequencer. [Expert, 2026-03-31]

## Purchasing plans

- The lab is considering buying a sequencer in the next 12 months, undecided. Today's choice would be the Illumina NovaSeq X because of throughput, flexibility and familiarity: "if you give me a million dollars, I will go and buy NovaSeq X." The second choice would be Roche, if and when available. [Expert, 2026-03-31]
- Plans changed over 90 days: not for sequencing, but many other equipment purchases have been pushed back (the expert says "pushed forward," meaning delayed) due to funding uncertainty. The lab does not want to invest big in any platform; instrument purchases are on hold to wait and see how funding changes over the next few months. [Expert, 2026-03-31]

## Illumina products and pipeline

- **Five-base chemistry:** the lab is testing it. If it works, it "will solve one of the bigger problem": getting DNA methylation and whole genome from the same assay. [Expert, 2026-03-31]
- **Long read on short-read machines:** the expert is interested in Illumina's long-read-style product (referred to as "TruSeq", likely the interviewer-transcribed name; the expert says it gives long-read data on the short-read sequencer) as the next thing to test, calling it Illumina's attempt to enter that market. [Expert, 2026-03-31]
- **Wanted next:** more and higher-throughput flow cells on NovaSeq X, making sequencing "even cheaper"; the expert doesn't expect new flow cells on the 6000. [Expert, 2026-03-31]
- **SomaLogic acquisition** (Illumina's planned deal, expected to close in H1 2026, per the interviewer): the expert has no strong view and does not work in proteomics. People they talked to see it as an expensive investment for applications that may not pay back, needing sequencer plus automation. "I don't see that acquisition giving the Illumina big advantage." [Expert, 2026-03-31]

## Customer support

Support is "one of the best things about Illumina": exceptional sales, technical and engineering support, including on the iScan microarray. The expert says support is a reason many labs buy Illumina sequencers. No recent changes noted. [Expert, 2026-03-31]

## Long-term view of sequencing

- If costs keep falling, more applications will become whole genome sequencing, in research and clinical, especially single-cell, transcriptomics and cell-free DNA (DNA fragments circulating in blood).
- Today depth is limited by what the lead researcher (PI) can afford, which limits what biology can be seen. Cheaper sequencing should bring deeper sequencing and a broad switch to whole genome.
- Long-read platforms (PacBio, Nanopore) are "great" but have "slightly lower accuracy and lower throughput, more, higher cost."
- The expert wants high-throughput, low-cost short-read systems with read lengths of 800 to 1,000 bases, which they say is technically possible. That would "certainly change how we see the genome" and is what the market is missing. [Expert opinion, 2026-03-31]

## Key takeaways (as stated by the expert)

- Q1 2026 volume is slightly below Q1 2025 and in line with lowered expectations. Full-year 2026 is expected lower, with no percentage given. No change in outlook in 90 days.
- Illumina raised prices broadly in mid-February 2026; January pre-buying is masking the effect on spend.
- Funding, not competition, is the main reason for weak volume; competition is rising but the expert's lab remains Illumina-centric.
- The next purchase would be NovaSeq X, with Roche the alternative; Illumina support is a strong retention factor.
