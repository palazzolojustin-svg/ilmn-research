# Pharma NGS core lab director (customer) on Illumina volumes, pricing, competitors (Roche, Complete Genomics, Element, Ultima, 10x) and new Illumina products, Q2 2026

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/Expert_2026-07-07_Director_of_NGS_Core_Lab_at_Pharmaceutical_Company_Evaluates_Sequencing_Technologies_and_A.md` · Date: 2026-07-07 · Channel check expert-call platform (AI interviewer; published 13 Jul 2026, interview 7 Jul 2026); expert is an unnamed Director of the NGS core lab at a pharmaceutical company (customer perspective) · expert call*

**Summary.** A pharma NGS core lab manager with 15 years of experience, who sends roughly 90% of sequencing to Illumina, reports Q2 2026 volume and consumables spend up 3%-5% year over year, driven by bulk sequencing and gene therapy work, with single-cell softer and spatial stronger. Roche and Complete Genomics reagents are about 50% cheaper than Illumina, but the lab will not switch this year because of capital budget priorities, Roche's library prep complexity and Complete Genomics' lack of small flow cells; Roche's AXELIOS is rated a minor threat. The expert is excited about Illumina's TruPath, 5-Base and new spatial products, is lukewarm on SomaLogic, and sees Illumina's customer outreach improving.

## Expert background and how to read this call

- **Who:** The expert is the Director of the next-generation sequencing (NGS) core lab at a pharmaceutical company. A core lab is a shared in-house facility that runs sequencing for the company's scientists. The expert has managed it for 15 years. Their perspective is that of a **customer** of Illumina. The interviewer was an AI (interview 7 Jul 2026, published 13 Jul 2026). [Expert: pharma NGS core lab director, Jul 2026]
- **Recency:** Direct, current experience. Answers about volumes, pricing and purchasing cover Q2 2026 and the expert's current plans.
- **Lab setup:** The lab runs three NGS pipelines: (1) bulk sequencing (DNA or RNA libraries for many applications), (2) single-cell sequencing, mostly using 10x Genomics, and (3) spatial transcriptomics (measuring gene activity while keeping track of where in a tissue it occurs).
- **Customers served:** Mostly internal R&D scientists across immunology, oncology, metabolism and muscle, neuroscience, auditory, eye, genetic medicine and cell-based medicine. The lab also runs translational medicine and clinical projects, sometimes with outside collaborators who help obtain human samples.
- **Instruments:** Illumina MiSeq, NextSeq, NovaSeq 6000 and NovaSeq X Plus (low, medium and high throughput). Also Oxford Nanopore (ONT) and PacBio for long reads, and Ultima for some whole genome sequencing assays. About 90% of sequencing goes to Illumina (expert estimate).
- **Caveat on reliability:** The AI interviewer misheard the expert twice (on "falling below" versus "meeting" expectations, and on whether net price went up or down). The expert corrected both. The corrected answers are used below.

## Q2 2026 demand and volumes

- **Drivers by application [Expert, Jul 2026]:**
  - Bulk sequencing is up, with more samples from gene therapy-related projects (a newer area for the lab).
  - Single-cell sequencing is down. The expert's explanation is that people are shifting from basic research (where single-cell is heavily used) to pipeline-related (drug development) work. This is the expert's interpretation.
  - Spatial is up. People are seeing spatial readouts answer questions other technologies cannot.
- **Illumina output, Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025:** Up about 3%-5% on sequencing alone. Bulk is up; single-cell is lower. The primary driver is internal project demand, described as "natural growth" (more samples, projects, people, pipelines), plus gene therapy projects running new assays.
- **Versus expectations:** After a correction, output is matching expectations, because natural growth was expected.
- **Full year 2026 volume forecast:** Natural sample-volume growth of about 3%-5% (H1 view).
- **Full year 2026 spending forecast:** Total spend with Illumina could "jump" more than volume, if the lab adopts new Illumina assays (TruPath, 5-Base, new spatial technology). The expert said they would report back by end of 2026 whether this happens. This is a conditional expectation, not a certainty.
- **Sentiment vs 90 days earlier:** More optimistic. The reason is that the lab is running new things not expected at the start of the year: at least five or six new assays, some on Illumina sequencers and some on other types, plus new equipment and new directions for the lab.

## Consumables, pricing and inventory

- **Consumables spending (reagent kits needed for every run), Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025:** Up about 3%-5%, mostly from more samples, assays and pipelines (not from price or stocking).
- **List prices:** According to the expert, Illumina raises list prices every year after February 15 by about 3%-5%, probably around 4% [Expert, Jul 2026].
- **Discounts:** The lab's discount did not change. Discounts differ by reagent type (for example MiSeq versus NovaSeq X reagents) and are based on the whole company's Illumina spend, because other labs in the company, including another very large NGS core, also buy Illumina reagents.
- **Standing order:** The expert placed a purchase order before February 15 for all of 2026, with one shipment a month. This locks in 2025 prices for 2026. Ad hoc orders can be added when a project needs more. Net unit price therefore has not changed. Total spend is up slightly because of higher sample volume.
- **Inventory:** Sufficient.
- **Lead times (past 90 days):** Unchanged, because deliveries are on a fixed monthly schedule.

## Competitive pricing and evaluation of alternatives

- **Vendors being evaluated:** Roche (which just released its new sequencer) and Complete Genomics (CG). The expert is talking to both about total cost of equipment plus reagent. No action has been taken.
- **Why no switch yet:** The lab has a limited annual capital equipment budget. This year it is prioritizing non-sequencer technologies (notably the 10x Genomics Atera). Moving to a cheaper sequencer is likely pushed to next year. As a high-throughput core, the lab is "actively looking into the cheaper options."
- **Complete Genomics:** Reagent cost is about 50% of Illumina's (expert estimate), but the saving requires buying their very high-throughput sequencer, and the flow cell options are limited to very high throughput. The expert needs low, medium and high flexibility to guarantee fast turnaround; with only large flow cells, the lab would have to wait to fill them, delaying customers' data.
- **Roche:** Sequencing reagent is probably also about 50% of Illumina's (expert estimate). The main concern is library prep (preparing DNA for sequencing). The expert has heard it is complex and is still waiting for Roche's detailed protocol. The lab has roughly 50 (about 56) Illumina-based protocols, so switching all library prep kits would be a big challenge.
- **Illumina's flexibility:** NovaSeq X Plus offers 1.5B, 10B and 25B flow cells (B = billion reads), giving low, medium and high throughput on one machine. The expert said only Illumina and Complete Genomics span low to high throughput. Illumina is also adding 5B and longer-read options (see Product updates).
- **Broader market:** Element has sold "several hundred" instruments (expert's understanding), is medium throughput, and is attractive to smaller labs. Ultima is very cost-effective at very high throughput. The lab does not own an Ultima but a neighbouring lab does, and the expert is developing assays on it. The expert concluded that Illumina is "definitely gaining a lot of competition" from other vendors.

## Expected share shifts over the next 12 months (expert opinion)

- **MiSeq / MiSeq i100 (low throughput):** Strong. The lab has two older MiSeqs and wants to replace them with the new MiSeq i100, because Illumina's MiSeq still reads the longest of the low- and mid-throughput sequencers.
- **NextSeq (mid):** Losing. The lab is retiring most of its NextSeqs and would buy Element instead if it needed one, unless it had to stay on the same platform.
- **NovaSeq X Plus:** Competitive, and expected to replace NextSeq for labs needing medium or high throughput.
- **NovaSeq 6000:** The lab still uses it for longer reads but, with new X Plus options, "we don't really need it anymore."

## Equipment purchase plans

- **Next 12 months:** Yes, but still debating. If budget allows, the top priority is a MiSeq i100.
- **Change in past 90 days:** "Totally changed." New assays require non-sequencing equipment such as the 10x Atera for spatial, and possibly protein profiling. The lab delayed considering the new Roche sequencer, and new announcements in those three months changed its thinking.

## Illumina new products

- **TruPath (long-read-style genomic DNA sequencing):** Useful for verifying genomic changes or CRISPR-modified genomes. Likelihood of use: "somewhat likely." The expert needs more information. Illumina claims it works only on human samples, and the lab wants to run mouse samples; the expert said it was told this relates to analysis, and discussions continue. TruPath would be an add-on, not a replacement for PacBio or ONT, because it is specific to genomic DNA and the lab's long-read work is largely RNA. It might replace some PCR or other NGS verification assays.
- **5-Base (methylation, a chemical marker on DNA that affects gene activity):** Actively being looked at. The lab has compared two other vendors but has not finalized a choice, and is waiting to try Illumina's version.
- **Illumina spatial (the expert recalled the name as "StrataMap" or similar):** Illumina's NGS-based spatial transcriptomics, similar to 10x's Visium HD. On paper, based on the cost and data quality Illumina presented, it looks better than Visium HD. The expert ranks it number one of the three because spatial will contribute to the largest share of the lab's studies. The expert plans to try it when available.
- **Rapid mRNA library prep kit:** Illumina told the expert about it. The lab runs over 20,000 bulk RNA-seq samples and is comparing several vendors' products to replace its current assay. The expert said the lab historically did not use Illumina library prep kits, because "their library prep is not the most reliable," and used other vendors' kits. They want to evaluate this new one.
- **NovaSeq X Plus additions:** A new 5B flow cell and 2x500 bp sequencing on the 1.5B flow cell, called "very nice" add-ons.
- **ctDNA (circulating tumour DNA in blood) and MRD (minimal residual disease, detecting leftover cancer):** The expert noted Illumina announced a ctDNA sequencing solution for 2027. The lab is developing ctDNA assays, with options including MRD, methylation and Ultima's ppmSeq whole genome approach. The expert said ctDNA is growing because current solutions are costly, and the assays are used to monitor drug response and tumour progression.

## Competitor products

- **10x Genomics Atera:** Released around April 18 (at AACR, as the expert recalled). It is an in situ spatial whole-transcriptome technology. Its key advantage is compatibility with both FFPE (preserved clinical tissue) and fresh frozen samples, whereas Illumina's spatial assay currently runs only on fresh frozen. The expert is "waiting to acquire" it.
- **Roche AXELIOS:** The expert is unsure whether it pressures Illumina today but said it draws much discussion since Roche's announcement (expert recalled AGBT 2025). Advantages: cheaper and faster. Concerns heard from colleagues at AGBT 2026: library prep is tedious, which for a core lab "is also at some level of the killing point." Customer support: "Roche customer support is definitely not great... if I don't ask them, they will never reply." Rating: **minor threat**, because the expert is not switching soon. They want to see the workflow complexity and data quality from early adopters and will likely wait until next year, ideally attending AGBT next year.

## SomaLogic and multi-omics strategy

- **SomaLogic (proteomics, measuring proteins) acquisition:** "Adds value" to Illumina, but the expert is not excited. The lab is looking at Olink and Alamar instead. Using Illumina's protein solution requires buying a Tecan liquid handler (the assay is tied to the robot), which means large capital outlay and a need for high throughput. This is not suited to a beginner in protein profiling. Possible effect on Illumina usage: "Maybe."
- **Strategy view:** Illumina is moving from sequencing-only to multi-omics (single cell, spatial, protein, TruPath, methylation). The expert said Illumina told them it developed spatial late because it was focused on sequencing, and now wants to earn revenue from library prep plus sequencing. The expert's opinion is that sequencing revenue "hit the ceiling" at some level and "they are trying very hard to find ways to make more money." This is the expert's inference.

## Customer support

- **Satisfaction:** About 70%.
- **Past behaviour:** Illumina historically waited for the customer to ask, unlike other vendors who proactively inform.
- **Recent change:** This year Illumina reaches out more and holds on-site seminars. The expert learned about the new technologies there, and the seminar data changed their mind about trying spatial. "I think Illumina is doing better job."

## Industry trends (expert view)

1. **Cheaper and faster:** Cheapest option is Ultima at $80 per genome at 30X coverage (expert's figure). All companies are competing on cost and speed.
2. **Longer reads:** More requests this year for long reads, so the lab is spending more time on ONT and PacBio (several kilobases). Unsure whether Roche will change this, as Roche is also moving to longer, cheaper reads.
3. **Multi-omics:** Moving beyond RNA-seq to epigenetics (methylation, ATAC-seq), spatial, and measuring RNA and protein together.
