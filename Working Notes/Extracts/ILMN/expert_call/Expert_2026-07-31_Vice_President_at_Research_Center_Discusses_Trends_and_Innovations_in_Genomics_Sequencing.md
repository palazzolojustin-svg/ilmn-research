## Sequencing volume and project pipeline (direct experience)

- **Pipeline is the driver.** The expert says the project pipeline has been "relatively stable over the past year, pretty consistent." The only thing that really affects volume is the pipeline of funded projects. "The project pipeline is what predicts our volume."
- **What drives the pipeline:** Funding (from government or from customers wanting to buy more sequencing) and demand. The expert said "It's all of those things."
- **Utilization:** The facility has "plenty of capacity to do more" and is "holding steady at about probably 70% utilization overall," while "continuing to look for new projects."
- **Q2 2026 versus Q2 2025:** Total sequencing output (measured in gigabases, billions of DNA letters read) on Illumina instruments is "basically the same" year over year. Reason: a "consistent, steady stream of projects. Some projects roll off, then some projects come in."
- **Versus expectations:** Q2 2026 output is on plan against the facility's own forecast.

## Full-year 2026 outlook (expert forecast)

- **Forecast:** Output on Illumina instruments for full-year 2026 "might end up maybe 5% down, but not more than that" versus 2025.
- **Reasons:**
  - Projects are "not coming in at the pace we'd expect."
  - One of the larger federal awards is smaller than last year "by a couple million" (dollars, as stated) and the project is being slowed down. The expert calls this "probably the biggest contributor of why we're down."
- **Change versus 90 days earlier:** The outlook is "probably less optimistic." The expert has not seen much new movement out of the NIH (US National Institutes of Health) in funding new projects. In past years, summer NIH spending sometimes picks up as the agency supplements existing awards, but that has not happened this year. The NIH fiscal year ends in September, so the expert is "more pessimistic that there'll be a large influx of funding."

Consumables are the reagent kits and flow cells that customers must buy for every sequencing run.

- **Spending:** Illumina consumables spend in Q2 2026 is "roughly the same" as in Q2 2025, with "no big fluctuations."
- **Driver:** Purely volume. "We don't do advanced stocking," meaning the lab does not stockpile ahead of need. "Our pricing has been pretty consistent."
- **Pricing in the past 90 days:** To the expert's knowledge, list prices have not changed. The facility's negotiated volume price has not changed either.
- **Why no price change:** The expert says Illumina typically does not change prices at this time of year. They have more often seen price changes at the start of a calendar year.
- **Inventory:** "Sufficient." The facility orders on a regular cadence based on its forecast.
- **Lead times:** No notable change in the past 90 days.

## Sequencing equipment and competitive dynamics

### Pricing environment

- Neither Illumina nor competitors have become more aggressive on price concessions or incentives in the past 90 days "with us," and not to the expert's knowledge more broadly.

### Platforms gaining or losing favor

- The expert sees interest from some labs in acquiring Roche's AXELIOS, a new product that is "just getting commercialized" (launching "this summer" 2026). They have seen no notable changes for other vendors.
- **Why AXELIOS is drawing attention (expert's view):**
  - Data quality has been "shown to have good data quality."
  - Pricing: "aggressive pricing basically on par, if not a little bit cheaper than Illumina sequencing."
  - Flexible run setup and very fast run times, so a lot of data can be generated in a short time. This could suit labs that struggle with "batching and efficiently filling up NovaSeq X Plus, like the larger flow cells," because they could run fewer samples more often.
- **Market-wide shifts:** The expert does not think many shifts are happening. "I think Illumina still maintains a dominant position." Roche "will get some attention, but again, it's very early in its launch process." The market will need the coming year to see uptake and decisions based on hands-on experience once AXELIOS is in labs.

### Share outlook, next 12 months (opinion)

- AXELIOS "will start to have some share," which is new. Illumina "probably does not have too much exposure" or vulnerability to Roche in the next year. The impact will be "minimal" because adoption and deployment onto projects take time.
- No major shifts are expected in the coming year. Looking further out, "there could be some shift away from Illumina since there are other options now in the market."

### Competitive pressure from AXELIOS (opinion)

- Not "a tremendous amount of pressure in the first year," because evaluating and switching platforms takes time. Over time it could, in some settings, "exert more pressure than perhaps the other smaller companies with their launches."
- **Why it could be more threatening than smaller rivals:**
  - More distinguishing features: very rapid sequencing at prices very competitive with Illumina.
  - Roche is a very large company with large sales and support reach.
  - It has the potential to become even cheaper. It "should be very cheap given the sequencing chemistry that it's based on." Roche will take time to test "what the market will bear in terms of pricing."

### Equipment purchase plans (direct)

- The facility is "likely to acquire a couple AXELIOS boxes." It already has a beta (pre-release) unit that it will upgrade to the commercial version.
- It could buy more NovaSeq X Plus systems "if more volume appears," but has no plans now.
- Plans are unchanged over the past 90 days (nothing pulled forward or pushed back).

## Illumina new products and innovations (expert interest)

- **Multi-omics (measuring several types of biological molecules, not just DNA):** The expert is interested in a number of Illumina's new products here.
- **PIPseq (Illumina's single-cell offering):** The facility is working with Illumina on evaluating it. The expert thinks it "has some potential competitive advantage against 10x in some scenarios."
- **Five-base genome:** Of interest because the facility wants to detect methylation status (chemical tags on DNA that affect gene activity).
- **SomaScan / SomaLogic (plasma proteomics, measuring proteins in blood):** Potentially interested but not yet implemented. The facility currently uses Olink and "may have opportunity to switch over to Soma if some projects appear."
- **35B flow cell:** Expected to come out in January (2027, by implication). It may further reduce the price of sequencing, but the expert has no pricing yet.

### TruPath genome

- **Likelihood of use:** "Somewhat likely in some scenarios." A possible use is clinical whole genome sequencing in rare or developmental disease, to detect structural variation (large rearrangements of DNA) that is harder to detect with standard short reads.
- **Adoption limited by price:** There is a price differential that "may decrease over time once the ability to multiplex samples is developed." For now it looks like "an expensive, different version of Illumina sequencing, although interesting in some use cases."
- **Share of volume by end of 2026:** "Very small. Maybe one or 2%."
- **Positioning:** It would replace some long-read platform activity "but definitely not all of it." It is mostly "a premium add-on to some products where having that information is valuable."

## Competitor products the expert is watching

- **10x Genomics Flex:** The expert has interest in single-cell work. Flex chemistry "is working quite well and really lowering the cost of single-cell prep."
- **10x Genomics Atera (spatial profiling, mapping gene activity within tissue):** Interesting because it is "now a whole transcriptome" and increases throughput of spatial data generation.
- **AXELIOS:** Discussed above.
- **Atera as a long-term risk to sequencing (opinion):** Atera is "the 10X alternative" that over time could "eat away at some of the activities that people use sequencing readouts for." The expert says "it'll take a while before that threat appears," but people will keep an eye on it.

## Illumina's SomaLogic acquisition

- **Effect on value proposition:** "So far, no effect." The facility chose Olink for plasma proteomics before Illumina's SomaLogic launch.
- **Possible benefit for others:** For some labs, keeping everything on one platform and using the DRAGEN analysis and data platform "could be a value add" for labs without existing alternatives.
- **Effect on Illumina usage:** Increase is "Unlikely," unless a specific project requests SomaScan, which would add Illumina spending and sequencing. Nothing is lined up now.

## Customer support

The expert is satisfied. The facility gets "a pretty high level of support given the large installed base that we have." No recent changes in how Illumina supports the facility.

## Future of genomic sequencing (opinion)

- A shift toward more multi-omics.
- Potentially "fewer large biobank style programs being funded" (biobanks are large collections of samples and data used for population-scale studies).
- "The NIH funding situation is not good, so it may be harder to get large projects funded."
- A shift toward the interests of industry and biopharma in terms of what data they want generated. The expert says "we will follow what kind of demand comes from those types of customers."
