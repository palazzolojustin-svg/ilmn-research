# Channel check with a gene-therapy biotech strategic advisor: Illumina volumes down ~80% on pipeline shift; Roche Axelios seen as a major threat

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/Expert_2026-04-07_Advisor_at_Biotechnology_Company_Sees_Major_Impact_from_Roches_Axelios_on_Sequencing_Marke.md` · Date: 2026-04-07 · Expert network channel check (AI interviewer) with a Strategic Advisor at a gene therapy biotech in North Carolina; interview 2026-04-07, published 2026-04-09 · expert call*

**Summary.** A former director of genomics and data science, now a strategic advisor at a gene therapy biotech, describes an internal core lab whose Illumina NextSeq 2000 sequencing output and consumables spend fell about 80% year over year in Q1 2026, and are expected to stay down about 80% for 2026, because the company stopped early discovery and preclinical work to focus on Phase I clinical development. The drop is company-specific, not caused by pricing or a competitor switch. The expert expects Roche's upcoming Axelios sequencer to have a "major impact" on Illumina, mainly among high-volume providers, but says Illumina's installed base, support, switching costs and clinical validation protect it for now.

## Expert background and relationship to Illumina

- The expert is a strategic advisor at a gene therapy biotechnology company in North Carolina. Before that they were director of genomics and data science at the same organization. They have more than 10 years in genomics. The channel-check header says they have visibility into CRO-based genomic studies (CRO = contract research organization, an outsourced lab or trial service provider) supporting active clinical programs. [Expert: strategic advisor, gene therapy biotech, Apr 2026]
- Relationship to Illumina: a **customer** (direct, current user), not a former employee or competitor. The expert was tagged "Doctor/Medical Professional" by the platform. The interviewer was an AI.
- Platform experience: mainly Illumina, plus Oxford Nanopore and PacBio. Their uses of sequencing have included functional genomic screening, mechanistic characterization using transcriptomics (measuring gene activity) and epigenomics (chemical marks on DNA that regulate genes), whole genome sequencing and whole exome sequencing (reading all of the genome, or only the protein-coding parts).
- Recency: the views reflect the first quarter of 2026 and plans for the rest of 2026. The expert is now one step removed from day-to-day lab operations (advisor role), though they describe the lab's current activity.
- Most statements are direct experience of their own lab. Statements about Roche's Axelios, other labs, competitive pricing and market share shifts are explicitly speculation or opinion, and the expert says so.

## The expert's facility and customers

- The lab is effectively a core facility (a shared in-house sequencing service) inside the biotech. All customers are internal teams: early discovery, manufacturing and process development, preclinical, and clinical development.
- Platforms: the main Illumina instrument is the **NextSeq 2000**, on which "we run almost every assay." This includes stranded RNA-seq, 3-prime tag-based projects, epigenomics, single cell sequencing, whole genome and whole exome work.
- Oxford Nanopore is used for **viral genome sequencing** for product characterization of viral vectors (the delivery vehicles for gene therapy), not for preclinical or discovery work. PacBio was used in the past but there are no current PacBio assays.

## Volume, output and consumables trends (Q1 2026)

**Cause of the decline.** The company now focuses on supporting Phase I clinical development (first-in-human trials) and has stopped early pipeline development. As a result, Illumina spend and sequencing for early discovery and preclinical work "has been greatly reduced." Nanopore sequencing of viral vectors continues because manufacturing is still progressing. [Expert, Apr 2026]

**Magnitude.**
- Sequencing volume: "close to 80% decrease" versus the prior year.
- Illumina instrument output in gigabases (a gigabase is a billion DNA letters read) in Q1 2026 versus Q1 2025: decreasing; the expert agreed with the interviewer's suggestion of about an 80% drop.
- Versus the expert's own expectations: in line, because they knew the company was shifting focus.
- Full-year 2026 forecast: "still going to be around an 80% decrease" versus 2025, though it depends on how clinical development progresses. This is unchanged from 90 days earlier ("about the same").

**Why it stays low.** Phase I start-up timelines, patient enrollment, health authority feedback and regulatory review mean the expert does not expect a large number of clinical samples until potentially the end of 2026. When clinical samples do arrive, sequencing would be **outsourced to a CRO**, not run on the internal Illumina instruments. There is currently no sequencing for clinical development projects.

**Consumables** (reagents and kits that must be bought for every run).
- Spending in Q1 2026 versus Q1 2025: decreasing, because overall project volume "drastically decreased." The expert stressed it "does not have to do with price changes or us moving to a different provider."
- Pricing: no dramatic changes from Illumina in the past 90 days; list prices feel relatively stable. The expert's answer on discounts and incentives was cut off; later they said "On pricing we haven't really seen any differences."
- Inventory: described as sufficient.
- Lead times: unchanged in the past 90 days, "as far as I'm aware."

## Equipment purchasing and competitive pricing

- The expert has **no plans to buy new sequencing platforms in the next 12 months**, and did not have such plans going into 2026, so there was no change in the past 90 days.
- Competitive pricing: the expert has not priced new platforms in the past 90 days (speculation follows). They "would imagine" competitors are becoming more aggressive with concessions, "especially the Roche system that's going to be online within the near future."

## Platform share shifts

- **Illumina:** "Illumina is going to lose some traction," mainly on cost per gigabase at large sequencing providers, hospital systems and CROs with very high sample volumes, where price matters. In smaller biotechs or pharma the expert doubts cost per gigabase is the driving factor.
- **Why Illumina is sticky:** integrated support (reagents, technicians, customer service). "It would have to be a strong reason to shift our short read sequencing platform to another provider." Long-read platforms (Nanopore, PacBio) have distinct use cases, but the expert does not "see them displacing Illumina."
- **Clinical validation:** Illumina has validated platforms for clinical use, while the competing systems are for now research use only (RUO, not cleared for diagnostic use). The expert says competitors will eventually be more competitive but are RUO "right now."
- **Next 12 months (opinion):** Nanopore will gain share relative to PacBio because of ease of use and cost. Roche's system will "make at least a dent" in Illumina's share because Roche has a worldwide network and a clinical team that can place systems in many areas. The expert is unsure when Roche's system will be released and unsure about smaller players Ultima and Element.

## Roche's Axelios

- The expert is watching Roche's new sequencer. They expect **"major impact"** on Illumina once it is on the market.
- Reasons for the threat: (1) Roche's global reach; (2) a very fast workflow, which would be prioritized in hospital or clinical settings needing data quickly; (3) reusable sequencing cartridges, "a big benefit."
- Reasons Illumina keeps an edge: it is already in many centers, and switching has costs beyond buying a sequencer, since labs must revalidate lab workflows and data workflows, "a big undertaking for many centers." Illumina also has platforms that are not RUO, while Axelios is RUO "at least for the time being."

## Illumina products and strategy

- **Single cell prep:** the expert's team was excited by and used more of Illumina's newer single cell library prep (they could not recall the name), which works through vortexing and needs no separate instrument. They previously used the 10x Chromium instrument. Comparison given: 10x data "looks better" (more genes per cell, higher quality), while Illumina's has much faster hands-on lab time and can generate data on more cells than 10x.
- **TruPath Genome:** "Not at all likely" to use it, because the company does not do human whole genome sequencing. This could change with clinical development plans, but not in 2026.
- **SomaLogic acquisition** (Illumina's purchase of a proteomics company, i.e. protein measurement): no effect on their use of Illumina.
- **Customer support:** "very happy." The account team is responsive and technically knowledgeable and offers on-site help for both instrument and reagent issues.

## Industry outlook (expert opinion)

- Other sequencing types will move into clinical workflows, such as single cell sequencing; the expert notes 10x Genomics is trying to build out a clinical lab.
- Multi-omics (combining genome, RNA and epigenomic readouts on the same samples) will matter in both research and diagnostics, as many diagnostics companies move beyond variant calls on whole genome sequencing.
- Proteomics is "on the upswing." The expert notes Illumina has SomaLogic and Thermo Fisher has Olink, and that getting proteomic assays into the clinic is something many people are thinking about.

## Caveats for use

- The ~80% decline is specific to one internal core lab's strategic shift to Phase I clinical work, and the expert explicitly rules out pricing or competitive switching. It should not be read as an industry demand signal.
- Pricing, lead time and inventory observations are mild and unchanged; views on Axelios timing, pricing and share impact are speculative.
