# Channel check with a North Carolina biotech genomics director (Illumina NextSeq 2000, PacBio Revio, ONT customer): services-led growth, NIH SBIR uncertainty, PacBio ~30% price cut, Roche AXELIOS threat view

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/Expert_2026-10-09_Director_at_Biotech_Company_Sees_Growth_in_Sequencing_Services_and_Stability_in_Funding_Tr.md` · Date: 2026-10-09 · Expert-network channel check grid (AI Interviewer); expert is a customer, Director at a biotech company in North Carolina; interview 2026-10-09, published 2026-10-14 · expert call*

**Summary.** A research-use-only (RUO) sequencing services and product-development lab that runs 100% Illumina for short-read work describes flat Q3 2026 output, down about low-double-digit percent versus Q3 2025, and a full-year 2026 expected to be down about 5%. The expert expects roughly small-double-digit growth in 2027 driven by pharma services, with no instrument purchases planned, no interest in NovaSeq X, TruPath or SomaLogic, a freight price increase from Illumina of about 10-15%, and a roughly 30% PacBio consumable price cut (SPRQ-Nx). The expert views Roche's AXELIOS one as technically impressive and a possible threat mainly in clinical settings, but would not adopt it.

The interviewer was an AI (not a human analyst). The interview took place 2026-10-09 and was published 2026-10-14.
- **Relationship to Illumina:** a current customer. The expert oversees the sequencing instruments ("supervisor of genomics") and talks with vendor sales reps roughly every two months, by phone or in person. Experience is fully current (Q3 2026 data).

- **Scale:** mid-four-figure gigabases (Gb, a unit of sequencing data volume) per year of in-house output. The short-read side is about 150 P2 flow cells (Illumina cartridges; P1 to P4, higher number means more output), with some P3 and P4 cartridges mixed in.

## Drivers of sequencing output (headwinds and tailwinds)

**Positives [Expert: customer, Oct 2026]:**
- More services requests: the services department sells sequencing to mainly pharma, and more work means more sequencing throughput.
- Commercial launch plans for the company's own products raise internal sequencing.
- SBIR funding (Small Business Innovation Research awards, NIH grants for small firms), which partly funds the company.

**Negatives:** the inverse. Fewer services statements of work in a quarter, a light launch quarter or being between projects, or losing SBIR grants all lower output. The expert calls SBIR "the big variable".

**Why NIH funding has been unpredictable (last two years) [Expert: customer, Oct 2026, direct experience]:**
- Congress did not renew the SBIR funding mechanism on time; it was caught up in the continuing resolution budget (stopgap budget). This stalled approval and pushed back study sections, council reviews and notices of award.
- More recently, an executive order to scrutinize grants with any connection to China caught up academic and industry grants under the international conflict-of-interest clause.
- Both factors affected the expert's own SBIR awards and the academic customers served.

## Funding mix and Q3 2026 demand

- The expert's output is about 60% funded by pharma and revenue, and 40% government-funded. There are no institutional or philanthropic funds.
- Customer grant funding in Q3 2026: the expert cannot see it because customers do not share it. Their own NIH SBIR funding went down versus the prior quarter.
- Pharma/biotech-funded work in Q3 2026 was stronger than expected, because new service offerings were added.

**New service offerings [Expert: customer, Oct 2026]:**
- Short-read DNA methylation using enzymatic methyl-seq (EM-seq), analogous to bisulfite sequencing.
- Short-read ATAC-seq for chromatin accessibility (which parts of DNA are open and active).
- Epigenomics of FFPE material (formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded clinical biopsy tissue), still in soft launch; customers are "really excited".
- Fiber-seq, a long-read technology.

## Instruments and short-read versus long-read mix

- Fleet: two Illumina NextSeq 2000s, one PacBio Revio, and a couple of Oxford Nanopore (ONT) MinIONs (small, portable long-read devices).
- Output split: roughly high-double-digit percent short-read and low-double-digit percent long-read. Long-read was only recently added to services and is growing, but remains much smaller.
- 12-month outlook: long-read would probably stay in the low double digits, "optimistically a bit higher", so a high/low-double-digit split is expected a year from now.
- Three-to-five-year view: short-read keeps the majority because it is cost-effective, accessible and has many established assays.
- Why long-read excitement is growing: customers have built many single-cell technologies, but the expert says single-cell data is "poor quality". Long-read "single molecule" assays are non-PCR-based, with each molecule coming from a single cell and spanning tens of kilobases. The expert says they can answer many of the same questions as single-cell at much better signal-to-noise and increasingly lower cost.
- Shifting work is not simple: it needs customer demand for long-read assays, since it is not the same assay moving over. Persuading the same customer to move from a short-read to a long-read assay is the route. Lower cost per Gb helps "for sure" in that conversation.

- Why Q3 fell slightly short: despite services growth, commercial plans were between projects. The lab had just wrapped up a big push on short-read commercialization of ATAC-seq.
- Why down year over year: SBIR funding was down and commercial plans were down. Services were up (last year there were fewer service projects).
- The expert is more optimistic after Q3: the "rough patch with NIH" has stabilized, with improvement, and services are improving. At one point the expert said "maybe some small percentage growth" next year.
- Drivers of 2027: new offerings bring new pharma customers ("continually bringing in new pharma customers"), uncertainty in NIH funds, and launching the commercial product (once launched, internal sequencing falls, but services rise).

## Competitive landscape

- Short-read: 100% Illumina, zero Roche, Element, Ultima or others. There is "no chance" of another vendor entering within a year. The lab is "not in a position to consider another sequencing platform".
- Long-read by gigabase output (including third-party services): about 90% PacBio, 10% ONT. PacBio is used for 30X human genome coverage (roughly 100 Gb; the field standard for services customers). ONT is used mainly for shallow QC. The expert has purchase orders for more than 40 SMRT Cells (PacBio's sequencing chips) at more than 80 Gb each, which the expert corrected to mid-five-figure Gb.
- Vendor share change: long-read was previously 100% PacBio. Over the last year, partnerships with third-party ONT QC services raised ONT to about 10%. The 90/10 split is expected to hold, while total long-read grows.
- ONT sequencing is mostly through third-party services; the in-house MinIONs are rarely used.

### Roche AXELIOS one (launch announced August 2026)
- Expert has not adopted it; received the announcement. [Speculation and secondhand, not usage experience.]
- Views: "the technology is impressive"; the reusable sensor module is "a really nice touch because reducing cost is important." The instrument is a fairly high cost, similar to the Illumina NextSeq 2000 at "several hundreds of thousands of dollars". The expert lacks exact numbers.
- Threat view: because it is "fast, it's cheap, and it's accurate, it could pose a threat". The expert thinks Roche could make headway in clinical settings, where turnaround time matters, helped by real-time data processing during sequencing. The expert is happy with Illumina's infrastructure, environment and service contract costs.

## Illumina products and roadmap

- **NextSeq 2000:** "really nicely placed" in the lineup. Cartridges now range from P1 to P4, giving flexibility to sequence deeper or less per project. The expert is happy with it and would not consider moving up to the Nova platforms; the lab is not a genetics or production sequencing outfit, which are the customers who get the best use of NovaSeq and NovaSeq X.
- **NovaSeq X:** not adopted; interest "zero" because demand does not justify such high throughput. Illumina's committed roadmap (flow cell output up to 40% higher, whole genome speed about 30% faster, as stated by the interviewer) does not change this.
- **TruPath Genome** (launched February 2026, removes library prep from the whole genome workflow, list price about $395 per sample, per the interviewer): no incentive to upgrade. Most of the lab's assays are enrichment assays (sequencing only a fraction of the genome) with custom library prep, and it rarely does whole genome sequencing. The expert says TruPath "speaks to the genetics laboratories". It also does not move long-read work to Illumina because it lacks a single-molecule PCR-free readout.
- **SomaLogic** (acquisition closed January 2026, per the interviewer): no effect. The lab does little proteomics and is an epigenetics company.

## Instrument purchases and capital spending

- No placement plans were accelerated, delayed or cancelled in Q3 2026.
- 2026 instrument capex is flat versus 2025 (no instrument purchases in either year).
- The expert did not look at instrument discounts or terms, since there was no purchase planned.

## Consumables, inventory and pricing

- Q3 2026 consumables spending was the same as Q3 2025 (purchase order in place), but actual usage was slightly lower.
- Excess inventory on both Illumina and PacBio ("slightly excessive for both short and long-read"). Cause: usage came in lower than expected when or after the orders were placed (a demand-forecasting miss, per the interviewer). The lab typically places two purchase orders per year, and a ship schedule is wrapping up.
- Effect: the next purchase order will be delayed into 2027 until inventory is used, which the expert estimates at "maybe one or two months extra".
- No lead-time or stock-out issues in Q3 2026.
- Discounts, incentives and reagent commitment terms: unchanged in Q3 2026.

**Price changes in 2026 versus 2025:**
- Illumina: announced this summer that prices were increasing for freight and certain other items. The expert did not focus on details; the one number recalled was freight, "probably a 10%-15% increase". Item list and magnitude otherwise unknown.
- PacBio: a price reduction of about 30% from a new chemistry (SPRQ-Nx), achieved by reusing SMRT Cells. The expert compared it to Illumina's earlier NextSeq move that cut prices by about 30% per Gb. "It was really exciting."

## Vendor support and relationships

- Sales, technical and informatics support: "very positive for both" Illumina and PacBio, with levels unchanged in Q3 and "both high, consistent".
- Service contracts on the larger instruments (Illumina and PacBio) are "excellent". A technician usually arrives within a week when an instrument is down, parts ship quickly, and staff are friendly and communicative.
- ONT is the weakest: "If you can get a person, you're lucky," support pages are often insufficient, and technician turnaround is slower because there is no service contract on the small MinIONs.
- Rep changes: a new Illumina rep in Q3 2026. The expert has gone through three or four Illumina reps in two years. This has not affected support quality but has hurt the relationship, since new reps do not know the business. The PacBio rep is unchanged.
