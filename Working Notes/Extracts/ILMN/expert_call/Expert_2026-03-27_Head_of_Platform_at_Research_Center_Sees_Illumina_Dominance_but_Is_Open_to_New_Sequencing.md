# Channel check with the head of platforms at a Massachusetts biomedical research institute: flat Illumina demand, NIH funding drag, and openness to Roche and others

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/Expert_2026-03-27_Head_of_Platform_at_Research_Center_Sees_Illumina_Dominance_but_Is_Open_to_New_Sequencing.md` · Date: 2026-03-27 · Expert-network channel check (AI interviewer; published 31 Mar 2026), expert: Head of Platform / executive-team member at a large nonprofit biomedical research institute in Massachusetts (Doctor/Medical Professional perspective) · expert call*

**Summary.** A senior executive at a large nonprofit research institute that runs 15 Illumina NovaSeq X Plus instruments says sequencing volume, consumables spending and Illumina pricing were all flat in Q1 2026, with the only growth (about 25% more output) coming from a couple of added instruments. New NIH awards have largely stopped arriving, which makes the full-year 2026 outlook uncertain (hoped-for flat). The expert sees Illumina remaining dominant, long-read platforms losing share, Ultima possibly gaining a little, and Roche's AXELIOS as a credible future competitor on runtime; the expert would likely buy a Roche system when it is commercial if demand for their usual work grows.

## Document basics

This is a "channel check" (an interview with someone who buys and uses a company's products, used to gauge demand) published on 31 Mar 2026 and conducted on 27 Mar 2026. The interviewer was an AI. The expert's perspective is classed as "Doctor/Medical Professional." The topic grid is "Advanced Sequencing: Illumina, Inc. (ILMN)," covering sequencing output, adoption, consumables, competition, new products and support.

## Expert background and relationship to Illumina

- **Relationship:** The expert is a customer and user of Illumina, not a former employee or competitor. They also use competitor platforms. Their statements about their own lab are direct experience; statements about other users and the market are opinion or speculation, and I flag these below.
- **Role and seniority:** The expert is on the executive team of a large nonprofit biomedical research institute, described in the bio as a Head of Platform at a research center in Massachusetts, with visibility into operations, budget and spending decisions for genomics research. They oversee all of the institute's technology platforms, including the genomics platform where all next-generation sequencing (NGS, high-volume DNA/RNA reading) happens.
- **Experience:** They have worked at the institute for over 20 years, developing, implementing and scaling genomic technologies and applying them to disease research. They are also a principal investigator (lead scientist responsible for a grant) on federal awards that use large amounts of NGS. Their experience is current (interview in March 2026).
- **Customer base served:** Academic labs, commercial labs and pharmaceutical labs, both internal researchers and outside parties on a fee-for-service basis (paid by the project).

## Installed instruments

- **Illumina:** The expert has "all types of Illumina sequencers." The largest use is the NovaSeq X Plus (Illumina's top high-throughput machine), with 15 instruments in operation. They are used for whole genome sequencing, exome sequencing (reading only protein-coding regions), RNA sequencing, single-cell sequencing and many other applications. The lab also runs Illumina benchtop (smaller) instruments, the NextSeq and iSeq, for smaller studies.
- **Competitors:** The lab operates PacBio, Ultima and Element platforms, but does not really offer Oxford Nanopore. The expert said these are used far less than Illumina, "not really in high-scale production," but are available.

## Q1 2026 volume and demand

- **Volume:** Volume has been "pretty consistent, I would say, quarter-over-quarter for the past few quarters," with no big increases or decreases in Q1 2026. Demand was described as "largely pretty flat" and in line with expectations.
- **NIH (US National Institutes of Health) funding is the main headwind:** No new NIH awards have arrived at the institute "because of the problems with the NIH" in funding new awards. The institute has received only one new award since September 2025. The expert cited a government shutdown that affected the NIH, plus other problems issuing new awards. In normal times they would have expected new NIH money this quarter, but none came.
- **Offset:** A "decent pipeline of non-federal awards" keeps the sequencers relatively busy.
- **Planning:** Rest-of-year plans await how the NIH situation develops.

## Output (gigabases) trend

- Output on Illumina instruments is up roughly 25% year over year versus Q1 2025 ("approximately 25%"). The reason is simply that the lab has a couple more sequencers than a year ago. The expert said they added instruments only because they had enough samples and projects to use them, and that output "is just based on how many sequencers we have in place."
- Q1 2026 output met the expert's expectations (stated twice).

## Full-year 2026 outlook

- The expert said it is "really hard to estimate" because of federal funding uncertainty. As a guess, they "expect and hope" output is flat for 2026 versus 2025, which would require funding at 2025 levels, not assured.
- Optimism is unchanged versus 90 days earlier. Award flow "has not improved" since then, when the shutdown was still ongoing. They have yet to see grants actually flowing and hope for more movement around June-July 2026.

## Consumables and pricing

- Consumables (reagent kits needed for every run) spending is roughly flat versus the prior quarter (the interviewer framed it versus Q1 2025; the expert said "flat over the last quarter"). It reflects "stable demand and stable pricing," not stocking or destocking changes.
- No changes in pricing or discounts. The lab negotiates discounts annually based on projected volume and has seen no change from the normal approach with Illumina.
- Instrument pricing: no more aggressive price concessions or incentives in the past 90 days from Illumina or competitors. No changes in Illumina instrument pricing or in the other platforms the expert knows about.
- Ultima: at a recent conference in February 2026, Ultima introduced a newer, more streamlined instrument that is "potentially going to be offered at a lesser price," but the expert said they do not know the details (speculation).

## Competition and share

- **No platform currently gaining or losing favor** in the lab's observation, but there is interest in the Roche platform due out later this year. It is not yet available, and the expert does not know whether it is affecting current Illumina purchases. The expert's view is that their lab will have different use cases on those sequencers at least initially; other users might wait to decide whether to adopt Roche (speculation about others). The announcements were recent, so no market impact is visible yet.
- **Drivers of platform choice:** Cost, specifically total cost of ownership (consumables, instrument, required labor) plus switching costs from an existing platform. Also run speed (the Roche system "will offer quicker runtimes," which matters for some applications), and quality and support, weighed differently by different users.
- **12-month share view (opinion):**
  - Long-read platforms are "losing share"; the expert is not seeing many new big projects requiring long reads and is "not as optimistic about those platforms."
  - Illumina: "it's early to say the extent to which Illumina will lose share"; "they could lose some share," but it will take a while for people to evaluate new competitors.
  - Ultima "made some nice improvements" and could pick up share, but "still has a ways to go until they have a considerable base."
  - More real-world evidence is needed "before there will be big shifts away from Illumina as the dominant provider."

## Purchasing plans

- No specific purchase plans, and no change in the past 90 days.
- If new demand appears, the expert would most likely buy more NovaSeq X Plus.
- If demand for the type of work they generally do increases, they are "likely to purchase a Roche platform as it becomes commercial." The lab has a beta (pre-release test) version now that they would likely replace, subject to the commercial release.

## New products and strategy

### Illumina products of interest
- **PIPseq** (single-cell sample prep kits): potential to expand single-cell sequencing use. Features they want: scalability to as many as one million cells in a single experiment, and lower prep pricing.
- **Five-base sequencing** (reads methylation, chemical marks on DNA, alongside the normal bases): interest in methylation as a biomarker; sequencing offers ease of use because it is on the same platform with the same library prep (sample preparation) as standard whole genome.
- **Constellation** (longer-read information): to be examined, but "probably not as interesting" as the other two.

### Competitor products of interest
- **10x Genomics Flex v2** (single-cell prep): competes with Illumina's single-cell prep and has advantages for some cell types, for example working on fixed tissues that Illumina "isn't necessarily compatible with." The expert expects 10x to offer decreased costs over time and notes very good quality.
- **Roche:** interest in the fast runtime, which would let the lab generate data quickly and run smaller batches.

### Roche AXELIOS (formerly SBX)
The interviewer stated that Roche plans to launch AXELIOS next year. The expert expects it to have "a competitive position against Illumina," but "it remains to be seen how far that goes," depending on how much users value faster runtime and the cost of incorporating a new platform.

### SomaLogic acquisition
Illumina's planned acquisition of SomaLogic (protein measurement; interviewer said expected to close in H1 2026): the expert thinks it is "a good acquisition" that adds multi-omics (combining DNA, RNA, protein data) capability to Illumina's ecosystem. The institute has already invested in Olink, a competitor platform, but the expert is fine with Illumina acquiring SomaLogic.

## Customer support

The expert is satisfied. As a fairly large user they receive "very dedicated, on-site, daily support." No recent changes in how Illumina supports them.

## Long-term view of sequencing (opinion)

- Likely increase in clinical sequencing as more tests are developed and utility is shown.
- Oncology "will continue to expand and be interesting to the market."
- Hope for adoption of newborn sequencing in the clinic over time, and more biobank sequencing as more biobanks come online.
- Overall: "My outlook is positive, but of course there are related funding challenges."

## Key takeaways for an Illumina analyst

- Direct evidence from a large customer: flat demand, flat pricing and discounts, no destocking, in Q1 2026; growth is from instrument adds only.
- Main risk flagged is NIH funding (one new award since September 2025), with the expert hoping for improvement by June-July 2026.
- Competitive threat is seen as gradual: Roche (speed, likely future purchase by this lab), Ultima (cheaper instrument, unconfirmed), 10x in single-cell; long-read platforms seen as weakening.
