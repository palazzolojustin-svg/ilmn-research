# Channel check with a clinical research organization scientific director on sequencing output, Illumina dominance, Roche AXELIOS switching barriers and NovaSeq X budget constraints (Oct 2026)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/Expert_2026-10-06_Director_at_Clinical_Research_Organization_Optimistic_About_Sequencing_Output_Growth_and_I.md` · Date: 2026-10-06 · AI-interviewer channel check (expert call platform), published 2026-10-08; expert is an Associate Scientific Director at a North Carolina clinical research organization (customer perspective) · expert call*

**Summary.** A customer of Illumina, running a mostly CLIA-certified contract research lab, says roughly 98-100% of its short-read work runs on Illumina and expects that to continue. The expert says Q3 2026 sequencing output and consumables spending were 5-10% below an unusually strong Q3 2025, and expects full-year 2026 output to fall about 15% before rebounding about 20% in 2027 on clinical trials and pharma acquisitions. Roche's AXELIOS is seen as interesting but would take about two years to adopt, and a NovaSeq X purchase is desired but hard to get budget approval for.

## Expert background and how to weigh it

- **Who the expert is.** The expert is an Associate Scientific Director at a Clinical Research Organization (CRO, a company hired by drugmakers and others to run lab studies and trials) headquartered in North Carolina. The platform describes them as having visibility into operating sequencing platforms from Illumina, PacBio and Oxford Nanopore. [Expert: associate scientific director at a CRO, Oct 2026]
- **Relationship to Illumina.** The expert is a customer, not a former employee or competitor. They say they have worked with Illumina "for the past, 10, 15 years" and have "a good relationship with the company." They were "groomed since graduate school" on Illumina sequencers.
- **Recency.** The interview took place on 2026-10-06, and the expert comments directly on Q3 2026 and Q2 2026 at their own lab, so the experience is very current.
- **Format and caveats.** An AI interviewer asked the questions from a fixed script. It sometimes restated answers in slightly stronger terms than the expert used, and in a few places it supplied facts of its own (flagged below). The expert speaks mostly from direct experience of their own lab. Their statements about 2027, about Roche's AXELIOS and about the wider industry are expectations or opinion, not observation. This is one lab, so it is a single data point, and it is likely biased toward Illumina given the expert's loyalty.
- **Output size.** The expert said the lab generates "15" gigabases per year (gigabases are billions of DNA letters read). The AI interviewer repeated this as "roughly about 15 gigabases per year." The figure is small for a lab running a NovaSeq, and the expert did not elaborate.

## The lab and its customers

- **Customers.** The lab serves pharmaceutical companies, consumer goods companies (a newer segment, "now"), academic hospitals, and start-up and biotech companies.
- **Regulatory setting.** The lab operates under both research use only (RUO, not cleared for diagnostic decisions) and CLIA (the US clinical laboratory certification) conditions, with "CLIA for most of it."
- **Work mix.** The expert says they do a lot of clinical work and some preclinical work that leads into clinical trials. "We are never short on different avenues for NGS sequencing" (NGS is next-generation sequencing).

## Headwinds and tailwinds to output

**Tailwinds**
- Diversity of the customer base and the volume of clinical and preclinical work.
- Academic funding pressure. The expert says "academia is not getting as funded as much," so academic groups outsource their NGS work, and the lab picks some of it up.
- Pharma acquisitions. Workload is higher this quarter than last quarter, "roughly due to acquisitions that were done by pharmaceutical companies."

**Headwinds**
- "The market is slow. Things are more expensive."
- Q2 2026 was "very, very slow," which the expert says drags on the full-year result (see below).

## Application mix and growth outlook

The expert gave rough estimates of the lab's clinical sequencing output by application. The percentages were revised in the conversation and add up to about 100% once cardiovascular is included.

| Application | Approximate share of output |
|---|---|
| Oncology (tumor profiling) | ~55% |
| Immunology | ~20% |
| Rare disease | ~5% ("maybe") |
| Neuro and vaccines | ~10% |
| Cardiovascular | remainder (~10%, per the interviewer's restatement) |

- **Fastest growth (next 12 months).** Oncology and vaccine work. The expert cites studies involving cancer vaccines that are "leading to a lot of work being thrown our way." Immunology is also growing, and "spatial transcriptomics is starting to take control of that one" (spatial transcriptomics maps gene activity within tissue). The expert expects oncology and vaccines to dominate next.
- **Slowest growth.** Neurology: "I have not seen any more neurology work coming our way down the pipe," though a few projects may straggle in. The expert also says neuro and cardiovascular are likely to slow, noting they do that work mostly in early stages anyway. The focus for this quarter and next is oncology with some immunology.

## Instrument fleet and short-read versus long-read split

- **Fleet.** Short-read (reads short DNA fragments, Illumina's strength): Illumina MiSeq, NextSeq and NovaSeq. Long-read (reads long DNA stretches): Oxford Nanopore MinION and PacBio Revio. Much of the single-cell work (analyzing individual cells) also runs on the Illumina NovaSeq.
- **Split.** The expert says roughly 70-80% of output is short-read on Illumina-based sequencers, "just because I'm used to doing that. That is the gold standard." Long reads are used about 30% of the time. (The 70-80% and 30% figures are not perfectly consistent; the AI interviewer summarized them as 70-80% short and about 30% long.)
- **Long-read cost and quality.** The expert says MinION accuracy is "pretty good," as is PacBio's, at "about a few hundred dollars per run."
- **Which technology for which study.** For oncology, the lab normally uses short reads for RNA sequencing, while DNA sequencing is "more or less done with the long-reads."
- **12-month outlook.** The expert expects a slight increase in short-read (Illumina) use because incoming work favors it, and long-read to stay the same or decline slightly. They have seen nothing to suggest otherwise.
- **Q3 2026 share shift.** There was no real change in vendor share. The expert says it "primarily depends on what we're looking for." Q3 oncology studies skewed to short-read data, so there was perhaps a slight decrease in long-read usage "based upon circumstances."

## Utilization and demand trends

- **Q3 2026 versus expectations.** Q2 was very slow, which led the expert to expect Q3 to fall short. Q2 then picked up, and Q3 followed. Personally it "feels like it went over" expectations, but versus the expectations set at the start of the year, "it met the expectations."
- **Q3 2026 versus Q3 2025.** Output was about 5-10% lower. The expert explains that Q3 2025 "exploded," with a surge of work and contracts amid instability from "budgets and tariffs and things like that." The expert adds: "It still was a pretty good quarter, but it just wasn't that lightning in a bottle type of aspect from last year."
- **Full year 2026 versus 2025.** The expert expects about a 15% decrease in output. Their reasoning is that 2025 stayed steady apart from the Q3 explosion, while 2026 had a bad Q2. They said "a good 15% decrease." Their explanation was somewhat muddled, noting 2025 was otherwise "on target, maybe a little bit above."
- **Effect of Q3 on sentiment.** Q3 gave comfort because it landed near the expected range, so there were not "back-to-back horrible quarters," which "would have been an alarming issue in this company."
- **2027 outlook.** The expert hopes for and assumes "a big jump, about 20%" over 2026, a figure framed partly as hope ("I would hope"). Drivers:
  - Clinical trials moving from early-stage into Phase II and Phase III (larger patient groups needing more sequencing), which the expert says is projected to start moving next year.
  - Pharma acquisitions requiring more sequencing.
  - Work funneling in from academia, which lacks funding to do its own research.
  - Expecting no repeat of Q2's weakness, "based upon what I am looking at."
  - The expert cites "some positive results from our early onset clinical trials" and says "two good quarters will lead to optimism coming into 2027" (Q3 2026 and Q4 2026).

## Competitive landscape

### Short-read share
- The expert says 100% of short reads are done on Illumina, later "98%-100%." Reasons: it is "the gold standard," what appears "in a lot of the prestigious and high-impact journals," it fits the lab's standard operating procedures (SOPs), and a long, good relationship. "I don't see a reason to venture away from that."
- The expert named no use of Roche, Element Biosciences or Ultima for short reads.
- Over the next 12 months, "Illumina is going to continue to dominate," with Pacific Biosciences (PacBio) and Oxford Nanopore expected to have only minimal additional use.

### Long-read share
- Oxford Nanopore is about 70% of long-read output and PacBio about 30%.
- Oxford wins on speed and portability. The MinION "is quick" and can be used "anywhere, especially in clinics and hospitals." The expert says PacBio "you have to have a larger cohort to run." The lab uses PacBio mainly for DNA analysis.

### Roche AXELIOS (Roche's new sequencer)
- **Adoption.** The lab has not used it, but the expert has looked at it and thinks it is "pretty cool." They like the reusable sensor design and think it is relatively cheap and "really accurate." They call it "transformative in their NGS sequencing" and "worth looking into."
- **Barrier.** Switching would require analyzing the instrument, updating SOPs, and transitioning ongoing clinical trials. "It's a lot of red tape." The lab is deep into some trials.
- **Time to adopt.** The AI interviewer noted AXELIOS launched as research-use-only and would need CLIA validation (this is the interviewer's statement, not the expert's). The expert says evaluating the sequencer, budgeting, procurement and operations would take six to 12 months, with another year to change over clinical trials, SOPs and regulatory items, so "a good two years or so." They say, "For me personally, I don't feel like it is [worth it]... what we have is good enough."
- **Threat to Illumina.** It "could potentially give it a bit of a threat." If Illumina raises prices and Roche is available, "it is competitive." It will "give people an idea to look into this stuff." The expert compares it to "the other company that got absorbed with Illumina" (name not recalled). Roche would need time to compete because Illumina has years as the gold standard: "They have their work cut out for them. But they are doing pretty well."

## Illumina products and corporate developments

- **NovaSeq X (Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer).** The lab has an older NovaSeq, not the X. The expert says "I want a NovaSeq X" and calls it the sequencer they are looking into, saying it "seems to be better." The obstacle is justifying a large expense when the existing NovaSeq still works.
- **Roadmap.** The interviewer stated that Illumina has committed to raising NovaSeq X flow cell output by up to 40% and speeding whole-genome sequencing by about 30% (interviewer-supplied facts). The expert says it changes their own willingness, "You sold me on that, but to get [directors and VP] to do it is another question."
- **TruPath Genome.** The interviewer said Illumina launched TruPath Genome in February 2026, removing library preparation (the step that readies DNA for sequencing) from the whole-genome workflow, at about $395 list price per sample (interviewer-supplied). The expert thinks it would cut cost and workflow and speed turnaround, and could be raised with the board, though budget objections persist. "I have been working on it."
- **SomaLogic acquisition.** The interviewer said Illumina closed it in January 2026 (interviewer-supplied). The expert says acquisitions like it make it "an easy decision" to keep or shift work to Illumina, and "reiterates why they are the gold standard."

## Instrument purchasing and capital spending

- **Plans for next 12 months.** Ideally a NovaSeq X; realistically another NextSeq (NextSeq 2000), "because it's cheaper" and able to output a lot, especially for the growing RNA sequencing work, which "is still pretty good."
- **Procurement cycle.** There was no acceleration, delay or cancellation in Q3 2026. The lab has yearly reviews around Q1 to request equipment, with new machines typically arriving by the start of Q3 or the end of Q2.
- **2026 capex.** The expert expects instrument capital spending about 5% lower than 2025, because of the slow Q2, though it was originally expected to be flat.
- **Instrument discounts and terms.** They remained the same in Q3 2026 versus Q2 2026.

## Consumables spending and pricing

- **Spending.** Consumables (reagents and kits needed for each run) spending in Q3 2026 was down about 5-10% versus Q3 2025, driven by volume. The explanation is the same: Q3 2025 was unusually large, and this year is "a stable state of what we thought was going to happen."
- **Stocking.** No stocking or de-stocking. "Pretty straightforward."
- **Price.** The expert says prices for comparable consumables rose about 5%, then "5%-10%," versus last year, because "everything is just getting more and more expensive." The interviewer characterized this as inflationary pressure.
- **Discounts and terms.** No change versus the prior quarter. The expert saw no discounts.
- **Inventory.** "Sufficient," with a little extra in case of backorders.
- **Lead times.** Broadly unchanged in Q3, with a slight push-out in Q2 because there was less work.

## Vendor support

- The expert rates sales, technical and informatics support as "pretty good" across vendors, with no outliers. "They seem to just be a phone call away, which is very attractive for companies like us who may need some troubleshooting at times." Support levels were unchanged in Q3 versus the prior quarter, and no sales rep changes were flagged.

## Emerging technology watch
