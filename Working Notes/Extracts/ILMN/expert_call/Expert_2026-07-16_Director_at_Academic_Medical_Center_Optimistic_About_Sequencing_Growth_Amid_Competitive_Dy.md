# Channel check with an academic core-lab director (all-Illumina, New York): Q2 2026 sequencing volume up ~20%, library-prep share loss, Roche AXELIOS seen as minor-but-potentially-major threat

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/Expert_2026-07-16_Director_at_Academic_Medical_Center_Optimistic_About_Sequencing_Growth_Amid_Competitive_Dy.md` · Date: 2026-07-16 · Channel check expert call (AI Interviewer; published 20 Jul 2026, interview 16 Jul 2026). Expert: director and professor running a genomic sequencing core at an academic medical center in New York (anonymous); expert perspective "Doctor/Medical Professional" · expert call*

**Summary.** A director who has run an academic sequencing core for 14 years, using only Illumina sequencers (NovaSeq X Plus, NovaSeq 6000, NextSeq 2000), reports Q2 2026 sequencing output up about 20% year over year and expects about 15% growth for full-year 2026, helped by federal policy that steers work away from certain non-U.S. providers. Spending on Illumina consumables is falling because about 30% of the lab's library-prep volume has moved to other vendors on cost, miniaturization and performance, even as Illumina raised prices 5-10% in early 2026. The expert sees Roche's AXELIOS as a minor threat initially with potential to become major, expects Illumina to lose some share over 12 months, and is most excited about Illumina's StrataMap spatial product while being uninterested in TruPath Genome and SomaLogic.

## Expert background and how to weigh the evidence

- The expert is a director and professor at an academic medical center in New York. They have run a genomic sequencing core (a shared in-house service lab that sequences samples for other researchers) for 14 years [Expert: academic core director, Jul 2026]. The channel-check bio says they have visibility into operations, financials and strategic investment.
- The relationship to Illumina is that of a **customer**. The expert is a current user, so most statements are direct experience from the last few quarters.
- The facility is high-throughput. It does bulk RNA-seq (measuring gene activity across a whole tissue sample), DNA-seq, and single-cell and spatial "omic" assays (measuring molecules in individual cells or in their position within tissue). Clients are mostly academic medical researchers, with some commercial work.
- Instruments in use: NovaSeq X Plus, NovaSeq 6000 and NextSeq 2000, all Illumina. The expert uses no competitor sequencing platform. The lab has four Illumina sequencers.
- This is a single lab, heavily transcriptomic (RNA-focused) and academic. The expert says their own experience of academic cores is what they can speak from. Views on Roche, Element, Ultima, 10x's Atera and the future of sequencing are partly speculation, and the expert flags this at several points.

## Demand and volume drivers (Q2 2026)

**Top factors affecting volume.** The expert named external federal funding and tariffs as the main factors. They said these were the only main ones [Expert, Jul 2026].

- **Federal funding.** The lab saw a decrease in sequencing volume "between Q1 and Q3" (as stated; the period reference is unclear), but volume has largely started to rebound. Later the expert said federal funding "has become more easy for investigators to get."
- **Tariffs.** Tariffs raised the lab's overhead and its prices, making it less competitive against outside sequencing providers. The lab also keeps inventory very lean so it is "not carrying any of those tariff costs for too long."

**Output in Q2 2026.** Total sequencing output in gigabases (billions of DNA letters read) on Illumina instruments is **increasing, about 20% versus Q2 2025**. The expert said this was slightly above expectations but "around what we expected."

Reasons given for the increase:
- Easier federal funding for investigators.
- More external business from other academic centers, a larger footprint there and new partnerships with other institutions.
- The new federal rules, particularly the "1271 list," which bars some external non-U.S. entities from providing services to U.S. investigators. The lab has captured some of that market and internalized more projects than historically.

**Full-year 2026 forecast.** The expert expects about a **15% increase** in total output versus 2025. Drivers: new investigators, projects and funding opportunities, and further use of the federal policies that bar NIH (National Institutes of Health) funding from going to certain sequencing providers. The expert said they used this to get their institution to set policies that funnel projects internally rather than externally, capturing "a good percentage of the work that's been going out historically."

**Change in outlook.** The forecast is **more optimistic than 90 days earlier**. The reason is the updated 1271 list: certain providers on it were identified by the lab as "a significant outsourcing threat." The expert said these providers are not strictly barred but are now "suggested not to be used as a university supplier."

## Consumables, pricing and inventory

**Consumables spending is decreasing** versus Q2 2025. Consumables are the reagent kits needed for every run. The cause is vendor switching in library preparation (the step that readies DNA or RNA for sequencing).
- The lab has switched "the majority of our heavy workflows" from Illumina library preps to other vendors, citing cost, ability to be miniaturized (using smaller reagent volumes per sample) and performance.
- About **30% of total prep volume** has moved to other vendors, mostly bulk and total RNA and DNA whole-genome applications. The lab moved away from Illumina RNA and DNA preps to "the other vendor" (not named).
- The expert said the vendor change was the majority of the decline, not destocking or reagent price alone.

**Price changes.** Most Illumina reagents rose with new-year pricing, which the expert believes was **5%-10% depending on kit type**. Illumina generally raises prices in February. This was a main driver of the switch to cheaper vendors over the past 90-120 days.

**Discounts.** Some offsets exist but not for all products. The lab has seen more significant discounts on sequencing reagents than on library prep, though not as deep as historically available.

**Inventory and lead times.** Inventory is "sufficient." Lead times (delivery wait) over the past 90 days were "generally the same."

## Sequencing equipment and competitive pricing

- **Illumina is more aggressive.** The expert said Illumina "definitely" has become more aggressive on price. It offers deep trade-in credits on older sequencers; the expert believes the offer is now a **50% discount on trading in a NovaSeq 6000 for a new NovaSeq X Plus**. Other competitors are "fairly competitive in discounting," but Illumina has been generally more amenable to deeper discounts. The expert is unsure how this will change with the new Roche sequencers, Element and Ultima.
- **Platforms losing favor.** The NextSeq series (mid-scale Illumina benchtop sequencers) is "losing favor for sure." Reasons: Illumina is releasing smaller-output flow cells (the consumable on which sequencing occurs) for the NovaSeq X Plus, plus a new MiSeq series, leaving little gap for the NextSeq 2000. The expert sees little U.S. market for Complete Genomics and BGI given federal policies.
- **Platforms gaining favor.** More people are adopting Element platforms, especially AVITI, and the VITARI (Element's upcoming product) has aspects the expert finds unique. The expert thinks Illumina's StrataMap spatial offering may curb that somewhat. Beyond that, the expert sees little increase elsewhere. Over the next 12-18 months, Roche's new technology may gain traction "probably faster than Singular or Element or Ultima have," depending on whether Roche's promises hold in the field.
- **Drivers of platform preference:** cost, turnaround time and capacity.

## Share outlook

- The expert thinks **Illumina will lose some share over the next 12 months**, largely because of competitors' offerings. If Roche's technology is "80% of what they say," the expert can see quick adoption, especially in clinical markets, where Roche already has a significant footprint and clinical users are interested in the platform.
- If 10x's Atera does true single-molecule sequencing, it might also reduce Illumina share because much spatial work would no longer need Illumina sequencing. The expert said it is not an early-release technology, so near-term impact on Illumina is limited.

## Purchase plans

- Contemplating a **MiSeq i100 Plus** (small benchtop sequencer) in the next 12 months for small projects such as 16S (bacterial identification) and microbiome work.
- Wants to buy a 10x **Atera** at some point. The expert notes it is not necessarily a sequencer but has purported future sequencing capability.
- No other major sequencing purchases. Plans have not really changed in the past 90 days.

## Illumina products

- **StrataMap (spatial):** the product the expert is most excited about, based on the lab's own testing. Strengths cited: sensitivity, consistency, and the large usable area on the slide, which lets the lab batch disparate tissue types to reduce per-project cost. The expert thinks it may have benefits over current players.
- **TruPath Genome:** "Not at all likely" to use. Few of their investigators do DNA-based work; the lab is mostly transcriptomic, spatial and single-cell. The expert said clinical colleagues may be more interested, but on the research side there is little demand.
- **SomaLogic acquisition** (protein-measurement technology): "No effect to maybe a little bit of a worsened effect" on Illumina's value proposition. The expert called it not broadly applicable and questioned its effectiveness versus Olink and other proteomic players. It will not change Illumina use; the lab's protein work is Olink-based and limited.

## Competitor products the expert watches

- **10x Genomics:** the lab generally uses 10x over Illumina for single-cell work, largely for legacy data and flexibility. They are watching 10x's spatial and single-cell offerings closely, and are very interested in Atera, which can incorporate protein work the expert does not believe will be immediately available with StrataMap. In these two spaces the expert is currently more interested in Illumina's competitors than in Illumina.
- **Roche AXELIOS:** the expert expects competitive pressure in the future, not now. Threat level: "minor with the potential to be major," and "minor initially." To become major it would have to "show immense value over the current technology" and show a roadmap that eases switching concerns.
  - Reasons for caution: switching is "a very large ask," especially for a lab with four Illumina sequencers and established processes. New vendor onboarding creates "a lot of administrative burden and a lot of other operational headaches," and academic cores run lean.
  - Newer research labs coming online may find AXELIOS more interesting.
  - Singular, Element and Ultima "really have not been a very major threat to Illumina," and Illumina's existing share and the switching lift limit Roche out of the gate.

## Customer support

- **Sales rep support:** "very happy," no change in who supports the lab or in the energy behind it.
- **Technical support:** it takes much longer for staff to understand questions. Many staff seem new and ask the same questions repeatedly, "almost like they're just reading off of a screen," unlike earlier years. Overall the expert has few complaints.

## Expert's view of the future of sequencing

- Sequencing will become "more integrative." Platforms like Atera add proteomic and other measurements to sequencing. Bulk-level measurements may become less interesting as users move to spatial and single-cell resolution, though sequencing may always be needed to deconvolute (separate out) those signals.
- Over five years, more holistic instruments are expected. The expert sees Illumina's use of StrataMap to adapt flow cells and make the NovaSeq more versatile as part of this, plus higher-level AI-based data analysis.
