**Summary.** A manager of an academic sequencing core facility that uses Element's AVITI as its main short-read sequencer (and an Illumina NextSeq 550 only for methylation arrays) describes Q2 2026 conditions. Funding stress and competition from contract research organizations are hurting volume, and Element output is expected flat to up 5-10%. The expert sees Element's unchanging reagent prices as a real advantage over Illumina, expects Illumina's highest-output platforms to keep or gain share while lower-output ones lose it, and expects Roche's AXELIOS to pressure Illumina within about six months, though only as a "minor at first" threat.

## 2. Factors affecting sequencing volume (Q2 2026)

The expert named two main factors, both negative, and said these were the only main ones.

1. **Reduced research funding.** Researchers' access to funding has "become a bit reduced," with "a lot of stress and anxiety over funding cycles based on different regulations that have come to pass." The effects are:
   - researchers are much more cost sensitive;
   - they postpone some studies;
   - they hold onto funds longer and more tightly because they are unsure whether future funds will arrive.
2. **Competition from for-profit contract research organizations (CROs) and outside service providers.** The expert says this "has greatly reduced our sequencing volume due to the highly cost-competitive nature of these organizations." It has also cut the volume of the "more standard workflows," which lets the core process more "boutique-type preps" (specialized, non-standard sample preparations). The trade-off: "we lose throughput when we're doing non-standard workflows."

## 4. Consumables and inventory (Element)

- **Spending.** Spend on Element consumables in Q2 2026 is about the same as Q2 2025. Output is steady and "The Element consumables do not change in cost," which keeps spend level. [Expert]
- **Discounts.** Asked whether the stable cost reflects discounts or incentives, the expert said it is "Just steady pricing from Element."
- **Inventory.** The facility carries an **excessive** amount of Element inventory. The cause is the core's willingness to buy reagents that researchers have committed to use in the near future and to house them while projects arrive. [Expert]
- **Normalization.** The expert expects inventory to normalize in about 8 to 12 weeks.
- **Lead times.** Lead times on Element consumables over the past 90 days "stayed the same."

## 5. Pricing environment

- **Competitive discounting.** The expert has not seen Element or competitors become more aggressive on price concessions or incentives in the past 90 days: "Not in my experience."
- **Broader price direction.** The expert says pricing on many reagents, instruments and consumables is increasing. They cite increases on consumables from life science suppliers in library prep (turning DNA into a form a sequencer can read), array kits and sequencing reagents, **with the exception of Element's sequencing reagents**. Equipment costs "have gone higher, in my opinion," and they do not see much discounting for a facility of their kind. [Expert; personal observation]
- **Element's policy.** Element's reagents "do not change in price throughout the life cycle of the product." [Expert]
- **Competitive advantage versus Illumina.** Asked whether this stability is an advantage over Illumina, the expert said "I do." Reasoning: the core passes kit costs on to its users, and "when those change frequently and go up exclusively, it becomes hard for me to remain cost competitive with my sequencing offered at the core." This directly shapes platform decisions at the facility.
- **Discounting power of rivals (future view).** Some providers get "quite high" pricing discounts from reagent manufacturers, which "may serve to give them cost advantages over facilities that may not have the same sequencing power." [Expert, opinion]

## 6. Platform share trends

### Current (expert's observations, partly from the field and partly their view)
- **Gaining:** Illumina's highest-output platforms, such as the NovaSeq X, are gaining favor. [Expert]
- **Not gaining:** lower-output Illumina platforms "do not seem to be increasing market share because the cost per base is no longer competitive." [Expert]
- **Ultima:** "more and more folks become aware of the Ultima platform." [Expert]
- **Roche:** "certainly is chatter in the field" about what Roche's new platform will bring. [Expert]
- **Element:** increased familiarity and "some more adoption." The expert says Element's next advance will be its higher-output sequencer, which would bring its cost per base "into a little bit more of a competitive space" with higher-output offerings from others. [Expert]
- **Drivers of preference:** overall cost per base and the output of the sequencer at full capacity. "Everyone is really interested in having the lowest cost sequencing they possibly can." [Expert]

### Next 12 months (expert's expectations, forward-looking opinion)
- **Gain or hold:** Roche (lots of interest, but "I have yet to see that hit the market or perform"); Illumina will "continue to increase the share of output on their highest-output platforms"; Element will keep increasing share both domestically and internationally.
- **Lose share:** lower-output Illumina platforms; Element platforms not upgraded to the AVITI24 model ("potentially"); and any remaining Singular Genomics machines.

## 8. New products and competitors

### Illumina
- **Positive on:** shipping chemistry at room temperature (lower shipping cost, more environmentally friendly), and a move to smaller reagent cartridges and containers (easier to store, less waste). The expert calls this "fairly innovative." They said they are "not too sure exactly which [Illumina innovations] would have the most impact from a researcher standpoint."
- **TruPath Genome:** "Not at all likely" to use it. Reason: "We're not getting asked to do many genomes. I think that most of the genome work has migrated away from facilities like ours and into other facilities."
- **SomaLogic acquisition (Illumina's purchase of the proteomics company, i.e. a company measuring proteins):** the expert thinks it **improves** Illumina's value proposition because a multiomic readout is "a very exciting development" and diversifies the data Illumina outputs. But it would **not** make the expert use more Illumina: the sister facility on campus has a workflow where it is more relevant, and the expert would let them operate in that space without competition from their own core.

### What the expert watches among competitors and in general
- What other applications a sequencer can run, whether it has a multiomic readout, and whether it can be upgraded to higher output without new capital expense.
- Whether other reagent providers will learn to ship and store reagents without dry ice, lowering shipping cost and shrinking consumables for small facilities.

### Roche AXELIOS (Roche's new sequencing platform)
- **Pressure on Illumina:** The expert expects it to create pressure "in the next six months." [Expert, opinion; the product had not yet launched in their experience]
- **Why:** Roche has created "a good amount of excitement"; the potential cost per base "will be quite competitive"; and there may be interesting features such as reusing certain components to keep costs down. Competitively priced reads "will be an interesting challenge to Illumina."
- **Caveat:** "Again, it's early, so time will tell how this is received upon launch."
- **Severity:** "Somewhere in between" a major and minor threat. Quote: "Illumina certainly has a very large foothold in this market. I think that any competition and threat to them is going to be something that is minor at first." The expert adds that Illumina "has quite a stable share of the marketplace with their equipment placed widely throughout labs across the world."

## 9. Illumina customer support

- Satisfied. "The technical folks that we've worked with there have been great. The sales folks we've worked [with] there have been great."
- The one real issue: "it became very hard to keep up with their tech cycle from a capital equipment standpoint" (i.e. the pace at which Illumina releases new instruments).
- Recent changes: some shifting of personnel, which the expert says is normal and industry-wide (people changing careers, voluntarily or not), requiring new contacts. Support quality remains high, "not a lot of changes."

## 10. Outlook for the industry and closing points

- **Competition:** "remaining very high," with many providers.
- **Sequencing demand:** the expert sees sequencing as a whole "increasing and continuing to remain a powerful tool in research."
- **What the expert wants from vendors:** affordable sequencing at a good cost per base and platforms that deliver on "the promise of multiomic readouts."
- **LIMS (laboratory information management system, software that tracks samples and workflows):** a robust and affordable one is "a critical piece of operating as a serious player" in this competitive market.
- **Capital constraints:** for non-profit core facilities, margins are "very, very thin," institutions are challenged for excess resources, and the ability to afford new capital equipment "is as low as it has ever been or felt."
- **Implication for vendors:** the ability to **upgrade** equipment rather than make it obsolete will become more valuable, and vendors may need to be "a bit more creative" in giving small facilities access to newer, higher-end equipment, because "an outright purchase of a machine that's over $500,000 is just much less feasible for smaller facilities."
