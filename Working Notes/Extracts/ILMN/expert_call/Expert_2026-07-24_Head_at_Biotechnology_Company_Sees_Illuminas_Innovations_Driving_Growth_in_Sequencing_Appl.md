# Channel check with head of synthetic biology at a small biotech: Illumina volumes up 25-50%, stable pricing, SomaLogic seen as value-adding

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/Expert_2026-07-24_Head_at_Biotechnology_Company_Sees_Illuminas_Innovations_Driving_Growth_in_Sequencing_Appl.md` · Date: 2026-07-24 · AlphaSense channel check interview (AI interviewer; unnamed channel expert, head of synthetic biology at a ~50-person biotech); interview held 24 Jul 2026, published 29 Jul 2026 · expert call*

**Summary.** A customer-side expert at a small biotech says their Illumina sequencing output and reagent spending are both up roughly 25-50% year over year in Q2 2026, driven by more projects rather than price, and expects a similar full-year increase. Illumina pricing, lead times and support were stable over the prior 90 days. The expert sees Ultima as the main cost-driven competitor but expects only slow share loss at the high end because switching is costly, and views the SomaLogic acquisition as raising Illumina's value, though they have not yet used it.

## Expert background and how to weigh this source

**Who the expert is.** The expert is the head of synthetic biology at a small biotech company of about 50 people [Expert: head of synthetic biology, small biotech, Jul 2026]. Synthetic biology is the engineering of organisms or genetic constructs. The expert leads all sequencing at the company and is the decision-maker on purchases. They also decide which projects include sequencing, whether it is done on Illumina or Nanopore, and whether it is done in-house or outsourced to a partner.

**Relationship to Illumina.** The expert is a current customer, not a former employee or competitor. They also use competitor platforms. Their experience is current, as they describe Q2 2026 usage directly.

**What the company does.** It runs internal research and development and also acts as an external, customer-facing service business. It applies its platform technology to outside customers' requests and uses sequencing for both internal and external projects.

**Instruments used.**
- Illumina: MiniSeq and MiSeq (small, lower-throughput, faster-turnaround benchtop sequencers) and NovaSeq (high-throughput). The platform chosen depends on the depth of sequencing (how many times each DNA region is read) and the number of samples.
- Oxford Nanopore: used "quite a bit," specifically for plasmid and construct sequencing (checking engineered DNA) and for longer-read sequencing when a project needs it.
- PacBio: used sometimes, but less.

**Caveats on representativeness.** This is one small-company customer, so the figures are not a market-wide measure. The expert's growth is tied to their own company reaching platform maturity, as discussed below. The AlphaSense summary lists the interview as "Doctor/Medical Professional" perspective, but the transcript describes a biotech operator. The interview was run by an AI interviewer.

## Volume drivers

**Main driver: project prioritization.** The expert says sequencing throughput depends mainly on which projects company leadership prioritizes, plus projects arriving from external partners [Expert, Jul 2026].
- External customer projects have predictable sequencing needs set at the outset.
- Internal R&D projects have a wider and more variable range of sequencing needs.

**Reagent cost is a secondary factor.** The company's philosophy is to "do the science that is best for the questions at hand." Reagent cost still influences which platform is used and what sample number or sequencing depth is chosen. Sequencing is one of many elements used to keep a project within budget.

**Funding cycles act indirectly.** As a small biotech, funding affects headcount bandwidth, which limits how many R&D and external projects the company can take on. The expert says funding therefore affects sequencing only indirectly, and that it "comes down to project."

## Sequencing output and forecast

**Q2 2026 versus Q2 2025.** The expert says total Illumina output (measured in gigabases, billions of DNA letters read) is increasing, by roughly 25%-50% year over year (direct experience). The expert gave this as a range and not a precise figure.

**Why.** About a year earlier the company's platform was reaching maturity, meaning a version that could be reliably deployed and offered to customers. With a clear customer offering and more people, it can take on more external projects alongside internal R&D. The expert says the increase in throughput "is directly linked to just an increase in the number of projects."

**Versus expectations.** Output is meeting expectations relative to the number of projects being run. It is more than the expert would have predicted a year ago. In their words, "Things have really exploded in terms of just the number of things going on."

**Full year 2026 versus 2025.** The expert expects output to be higher again, in the same 25%-50% range (they briefly said "20%, 25%-50%"). The basis is the commercial pipeline: "Everything that we can tell from our commercial pipeline is that interest is continuing," and project counts should continue or increase from the current pace.

**Sentiment versus 90 days ago.** The expert called the outlook "about the same," then said "you could say more optimistic." The reason is that over the last 30 days more early commercial engagements have converted into executed, live projects, which raises confidence in the conversion rate. The AlphaSense summary characterizes this as slightly more optimistic.

## Consumables and reagents

**Spending trend.** Spending on Illumina consumables (the reagent kits needed for every sequencing run) is increasing, in line with throughput, at roughly 25%-50% year over year in Q2 2026 versus Q2 2025. The expert agreed when the interviewer suggested this range.

**Volume versus price.** The increase is primarily driven by sequencing throughput. Reagent prices rise every year, which contributes, but "it isn't increasing the full percentage increase that we're observing."

**How purchasing decisions are made.** Reagent and platform choices are made dynamically, weighing whether samples can be pooled (combined on one run to be more cost-effective) against turnaround time. Pooling onto a higher-throughput instrument can add a time lag, whereas lower-throughput instruments return data faster. Reagent costs therefore vary from time to time with project needs and timelines.

**Inventory.** The expert describes inventory as sufficient.
- On the recommendation of their Illumina field application scientist (FAS, Illumina's technical sales representative), they initially had a standing order of a set number of kits every quarter or couple of months.
- They moved to ordering as needed, at least one to two months ahead of need, which amounts to roughly the same quantity of kits.
- They keep a buffer of a couple of kits for re-runs or ad hoc runs.
- The goals are to avoid downtime, since "timelines are always tight," and to avoid wasting budget.

**Lead times.** Lead times on consumables were unchanged over the past 90 days. Shortages of certain kit types due to specific reagents or supply chain problems can happen, but the expert had not experienced one in the last 90 days. They plan sequencing needs well ahead so they can substitute other kit types if one is unavailable.

## Pricing

**Reagent pricing.** Nothing changed in the last 90 days [Expert, Jul 2026]. The expert understands Illumina raises prices every year, linked to inflation, which is the expert's understanding and not something stated by Illumina in this document.

**Discounts.** Illumina works with the expert on discounts based on their throughput.
- Discounts are generally volume-based, and buyers "end up in the same ballpark" for a given volume.
- The relationship with the FAS also matters. The expert said, "having the FAS not dislike you is not a bad thing when it comes to discounts applied."

**Equipment pricing.** The expert has seen no change in the last 90 days from Illumina or competitors in price concessions or incentives and called the environment "pretty stable."

## Competitive landscape

**Illumina's position.** For high-throughput NGS (next-generation sequencing), the expert has always used Illumina. They have considered newer vendors but admittedly have not used them. In their view Illumina "continues to be very popular and hasn't lost too much traction," though it has likely lost some as other platforms offering similar products have come online. They say Illumina's long dominance helps it capture the market.

**Ultima.** The expert named Ultima as the platform most likely gaining ground, based on what they have heard rather than direct use.
- Per-read costs are lower, but this requires very large runs on Ultima instruments.
- Versus NovaSeq and other high-throughput Illumina platforms, Ultima can be better.
- Versus lower-throughput, faster-turnaround MiniSeq and MiSeq, it "serves a different purpose."

**Oxford Nanopore.** Its long-read capability, which the expert says goes beyond Illumina's, is "super useful and interesting."

**Element Biosciences.** The interviewer asked about "Elio's" sequencing platform. The expert was not familiar with it and could not comment.

**What drives switching.** Familiarity makes moving off a platform difficult, and the expert says that is true for them.
- When companies do switch, it is likely cost-driven.
- At a biotech, headcount and timelines are tight, so it is hard to justify the time and burden of redeveloping bench protocols and reconfiguring downstream analysis pipelines.
- The only things that would make it tolerable are data that is faster, significantly cheaper, or both. The expert called this "a really tall order."

**12-month share outlook (opinion).** The expert could see NovaSeq and similar-throughput Illumina instruments "continuing to slowly lose traction" to competitor options. They expect this to be slow and not extreme, because of switching costs, which are "not financial cost necessarily, but cost of doing business."

## Equipment purchasing plans

The expert has no current plans to buy new sequencing equipment in the next 12 months, but it is possible, to handle anticipated higher throughput.
- For now they plan to lean on external sequencing providers, which work well if submissions can be planned on a reasonable timeline.
- External providers become difficult when rapid turnaround is needed or when a project suddenly becomes higher priority, because the company cannot influence the queue.
- Plans have not changed in the last 90 days and are consistent with needs rising slowly over time.

## Innovations and new products

**Illumina chemistry.** The expert is not following any specific new instrument or chemistry product. They know Illumina continues to innovate on chemistries and, not recently, modified the chemistry for its higher-throughput instruments, which lowered cost per read. The expert welcomes this.

**Protein sequencing and SomaLogic.** The expert named the protein-sequencing acquisition as the innovation they are most excited about, though they could not recall the name at first and later identified it as SomaLogic. They have not used it. Their description is that it makes protein information accessible within Illumina workflows, and they believe the acquisition happened about a year ago. (SomaLogic is described by the interviewer as a proteomics company, so the expert's "protein sequencing" label is the expert's characterization.)
- Opinion: the acquisition increases Illumina's value because it extends Illumina from nucleic acids (DNA and RNA) to proteins, and doing so at high throughput is "really enabling."
- Opinion: an offering carrying only the Illumina name "would reduce the barrier to entry for people to consider using it."
- If and when they start using it, it would be additive to existing DNA and RNA sequencing and would increase their throughput, but this has not happened yet.

**TruPath Genome.** The expert is not likely to use it in the near term, based on application type and not on any view of the product. Their sequencing is targeted, answering specific genetic engineering questions, so full-coverage whole-genome sequencing "isn't as useful to us."

**Competitor products of interest.** Ultima's throughput and cost per read, and Nanopore's long-read ability.

## Customer support and engagement

There have been no recent changes in how Illumina supports the facility or engages with them (communication frequency, technical support quality, partnership approach). The expert says Illumina is "super accommodating, very helpful" and that its customer support is "extremely effective," trying to make use of its products "very seamless."

## Future trends (expert opinion)

- Adding protein capability under Illumina "will be super enabling," moving users from only high-throughput nucleic acid data to also probing protein-side questions.
- Continued reductions in the cost of sequencing, which has already fallen "a ton even in the last decade or so," are enabling next-generation functional genomic tools such as single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptomics (mapping gene activity within tissue). The expert sees these as essential for precision medicine, drug development and better patient treatment.
