# Channel check with a single-cell and spatial genomics core specialist at a Massachusetts hospital on Illumina sequencing demand, spatial transcriptomics and competition (Q2 2026)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/Expert_2026-07-14_Specialist_at_Massachusetts_Hospital_Discusses_Trends_in_Sequencing_and_Spatial_Transcript.md` · Date: 2026-07-14 · AI-conducted channel check (AI Interviewer) with a Doctor/Medical Professional expert; interview 14 Jul 2026, published 17 Jul 2026; expert unnamed · expert call*

**Summary.** A single-cell genomics specialist at a hospital-based cellular profiling core in Massachusetts reports that their Illumina sequencing output and consumables spend are down about 15%-20% year over year in Q2 2026 and are forecast to fall a similar amount for full-year 2026, because users are shifting from single-cell work to spatial transcriptomics platforms (especially 10x Genomics Xenium and the coming Atera) that bypass the sequencer. Academic demand is weaker (grants shrinking) while biotech demand for large atlas projects is rising. Illumina pricing has been unchanged for at least two years in the expert's experience; the expert sees Illumina's coming spatial product and SomaLogic protein platform as exciting but sees cost and data-analysis usability as hurdles, and sees Roche's Axelios as a major threat only if Illumina does not cut prices.

## Expert background and relationship to Illumina

- **Who:** The expert is a single-cell genomic technology specialist at a hospital-based "cellular profiling core" (a shared service lab that runs single-cell and spatial sequencing for outside researchers) in the Massachusetts/Boston area. The platform labels them a Doctor/Medical Professional. They say they have been "a scientist in the field for around 15 years." [Expert: single-cell genomics core specialist, Jul 2026]
- **Relationship to Illumina:** Current customer and hands-on user. They are not a former Illumina employee. Their experience is current: they are describing Q2 2026 in their own lab.
- **Platform experience:** Short-read sequencing (reading short DNA fragments) on Illumina NovaSeq, NovaSeq X Plus, TruSeq and MiSeq; short-read sequencing on Oxford Nanopore's MinION (the expert's own wording); and long-read sequencing on PacBio.
- **Daily duties:** Preparing sequencing libraries (the processed samples loaded onto a sequencer) and setting up the right configurations to sequence them on Illumina platforms.
- **Customers of the core:** Academic users from hospitals and universities in the area, plus biotech companies from the region and other states. Samples come from humans, mice, Drosophila (fruit flies) and even cats, and from cultures, digestive tissue and blood, covering many cell types (for example T cells and B cells and their subtypes).
- **Equipment:**
  - All single-cell libraries and Xenium HD spatial libraries are sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq X Plus.
  - The core has multiple 10x Genomics Xenium machines for spatial work. Xenium does not include sequencing (it is probe-based, so it bypasses the sequencer).
  - The core does not yet have Illumina's upcoming sequencing-based spatial product.
- **Direct experience vs. speculation:** Volume, spending, pricing, inventory and lead-time answers come from direct experience in their own core. Comments on Atera, Illumina's spatial product, Axelios and SomaLogic are largely informed opinion or expectation (they say they have not seen Atera data or Illumina single-cell data).

## Demand drivers at the core (Q2 2026)

- **Academic weakness:** Academic grants "shrunk in Q1" and are "the same in Q2" (still depressed). The core has seen fewer academic users coming for single-cell or spatial work.
- **Biotech strength:** Large increase in Massachusetts/Boston-area biotech customers wanting very large atlas-style studies (big projects mapping many cells or tissues), using single-cell FLEX (10x's method for fixed cells that lets many samples be pooled and run together, which generates a lot of sequencing) and spatial. The core sees "a larger increase in these very large projects" and broad regional interest.
- **Shift to spatial reduces Illumina use:** Growing demand for spatial, including Xenium 5K and the soon-to-launch 10x Atera whole-transcriptome spatial system, means fewer people do single-cell sequencing. Because Xenium 5K and Atera "bypass the sequencer," this lowers Illumina sequencing demand. The expert says this applies to small academic labs, larger biotech labs and pharma using spatial on large numbers of archived fixed samples.
- **Net effect:** Negative for Illumina usage. "We definitely see a decrease in the Illumina usage." Xenium workflows that still need library prep and Illumina sequencing are being replaced by slide-based, probe-based spatial that does not.

## Volume and output

| Measure | Expert's statement |
|---|---|
| Q2 2026 sequencing output (gigabases) vs Q2 2025 | Decrease, "anywhere between 15%-20%" |
| Full-year 2026 forecast vs 2025 | About 15%-20% lower |
| Reagent and flow cell spending, Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025 | Down about 15%-20% (no precise spend data; inferred from volume) |

- The expert says the core has tracked this year to year from 2021 to 2026 and sees less single-cell sequencing and more spatial.
- FLEX samples and multi-library single-cell experiments (gene expression, TCR, BCR, hashing and protein libraries combined) produce the largest data. Despite some large projects late in 2025, total samples and data in 2026 are below 2025.
- **Versus expectations:** Output is "falling below our expectations" because the core has monthly single-cell sample targets it is not meeting. Spatial volume is well above target and compensates.
- **Outlook vs 90 days ago:** Less optimistic. Large single-cell projects ended in early summer. Three or four months earlier, the core was requesting "two or three, 10 billion flow cells every month or so" (flow cells are the consumable slides sequencing runs on; the "10 billion" refers to the 10B flow cell size as stated). Now few users do single cell, with fewer samples each.
- **Expected continuation:** Atera, announced and expected to release within the next few months, should accelerate the shift to spatial whole-transcriptome work and away from traditional single-cell sequencing.

## Pricing, inventory and lead times

- **Consumable and reagent prices:** No change in the past 90 days, and none "even since last year." Over at least two years, the expert has seen no significant change in reagent or per-run sequencing costs on the NovaSeq X Plus. Spending declines are driven by volume, not price.
- **Why no price change:** The expert does not know. They acknowledge it is costly for Illumina, but argue sequencing prices should fall given the very large data volumes Illumina's spatial platform will generate; otherwise per-sample cost could deter adoption.
- **Pass-through to the core's pricing:** The core cannot cut its own single-cell or FLEX service prices because of sequencing costs. If Illumina trimmed prices, the core could cut prices, be more competitive and win back single-cell users. [Expert opinion]
- **Equipment pricing:** No major change in sequencer pricing in the past 90 days that the expert knows of. It was "not easy to get any discounts or special pricing" for reagents or equipment.
- **Inventory:** Sufficient. The core orders monthly against planned projects and does not stock extra.
- **Lead times:** Unchanged over the past 90 days.

## Competitive landscape and platform shifts

### Illumina's spatial product
- Illumina has announced a sequencing-based spatial transcriptomics assay and platform. The expert finds it "exciting" for the field, especially labs that already own Illumina sequencers, which could run it in-house. Illumina would prepare the protocol and slides, with sequencing on Illumina machines.
- **Concern:** The sequencing required per slide is "huge," so each sample would be costly even though the assay is straightforward. They are watching adoption and whether Illumina lowers prices.
- Expected to be one of two platforms gaining interest over the next 12 months, because the wet lab protocol looks simple and is compatible with installed Illumina sequencers; cost is the disadvantage.
- "A lot of buzz in the community"; the expert wants more advertising, emails, webinars, seminars and conferences.

### 10x Genomics Atera
- 10x has announced Atera, a whole-transcriptome, single-cell-resolution spatial system that needs no external sequencer, coming "in the end of the year" (also described as within the next few months). The expert says there is "a lot of excitement" but they have not seen the data.
- The expert expects Atera to make Xenium (250+ genes) and Xenium 5K (5,000+ fixed-panel genes) and Visium eventually go away, since they offer less information.
- **Precedent:** Visium and Visium HT give a whole transcriptome but not at single-cell resolution; demand for Visium dropped once Xenium and Xenium 5K came out.
- Atera also bypasses Illumina sequencing, so it is a headwind for Illumina volume.

### Why spatial is winning, and its drawbacks
- **Advantages:** Cell localization, tissue structure and surrounding environment; full-transcriptome information; subtyping of cell populations by gene expression and detection of rare populations; use of FFPE (formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded) archived samples 10, 15 or even 20 years old; slides can be reused for protein or antibody staining; flexibility in handling and storage; very large information yield.
- **Disadvantages:** Cost (not cheap even with multiple sections per slide) and data analysis (needs skilled bioinformaticians and a very large server).

### Roche Axelios (referred to once as "AVITI" by the interviewer)
- The expert does not necessarily see it as a competitor, since Illumina is established and people are unlikely to switch.
- But they say it "could be a major threat" if pricing does not drop. They describe it as designed to disrupt the high-throughput segment dominated by Illumina's NovaSeq X and X Plus, priced below the NovaSeq X, with possible speed advantages through shorter run time. "Unless Illumina drop their sequencing prices, it will remain a major competitor or a major threat." [Opinion, not direct use; the core does not use it]

### Other competitor attention
- The core is watching spatial protein panels; it has tried a couple of vendors' protein assays for spatial slides and will decide what works best.
- Spatial proteomics is early, as are first attempts at 3D spatial transcriptomics, which the expert expects to become more popular in the next few years.

## Illumina products and strategy

- **Single-cell (beads and vortex method):** The core has not seen data from it and expressed interest to Illumina in trying it but did not proceed. They want user feedback, satisfaction and sample web summaries.
- **TruPath Genome:** Depends on the project. Because most users do transcriptomics (mRNA expression, not genome variation), it "will negatively influence" likelihood of use. The expert does not expect to use it in the next 90 days.
- **SomaLogic (Illumina's proteomics acquisition):** Appealing. The expert thinks it makes them more likely to use Illumina more, especially the DNA-to-protein translation approach, which reads protein profiles via next-generation sequencing by counting protein tags on DNA, instead of antibodies or mass spectrometry. Pairing Illumina spatial with SomaLogic "makes sense." They will watch for availability.

## Customer support and software

- Very satisfied with Illumina customer support; no major changes in recent months.
- **Complaint:** The data analysis website (BaseSpace Sequence Hub) is complicated; finding and downloading files, including public data, took a lot of time and is "not necessarily very user-friendly."
- **Request:** A friendly tool for single-cell gene expression analysis like 10x's Cell Ranger and Space Ranger, plus online tutorials and wider outreach on Illumina's analysis tools.
- **Promotions:** Promotional offers and discounts raise interest among users with limited funding; the expert would like to see similar offers from Illumina.

## Expert's outlook

- Short term: whole-transcriptome at single-cell level, with falling prices as competition and availability grow and more companies enter spatial.
- Within a few years: higher interest in protein panels (proteome and transcriptome), then 3D sequencing.
- Closing view: "I have a high confidence in Illumina," and they look forward to Illumina's single-cell and spatial tools spreading across academia and industry.

## Takeaways and caveats

- This is one core lab's view, concentrated in single-cell and spatial work, so it may not represent Illumina's clinical, population-genomics or high-throughput customers.
- The 15%-20% declines are approximations without hard spend data, and the three figures (output, forecast, spend) are all the same range.
- Several statements about Atera and Axelios are expectation, not firsthand use.
