## Factors driving the lab's sequencing volume (Q2 2026)

The expert named three main factors, and said there were no others [Expert, Jul 2026]:

1. **Capacity.** Capacity is "maybe limiting our sequencing."
2. **Pricing.** The cheapest sequencing comes from larger machines like the NovaSeq, so the lab pays more per base on its MiSeq and NextSeq. For high-volume work it is sometimes cheaper to send samples out, because external vendors pay less for reagents.
3. **Nature and stage of projects.** Whether the right samples have been collected and whether experiments are working determine whether sequencing is needed at all.

### In-house versus outsourced work

- Large projects go to external vendors. The expert's example is population-scale whole genome sequencing: 20 to 100 samples at 20X to 30X coverage (each base read about 20 to 30 times) in mammalian species. The lab lacks internal capacity for that, and vendors can do it faster and cheaper.
- Lower-volume work (amplicon, PIP-Seq, RNA-Seq) is handled in-house.
- Projects with tight turnaround times may be done internally.

- The interviewer summarized this as ONT taking share from PacBio rather than displacing Illumina's short-read dominance; the expert did not contradict that.

## Roche Axelios

- The expert expects Axelios (Roche's new sequencing platform) to create competitive pressure on Illumina "because it's definitely a lower price," but the barrier is validation: labs would need to spend significant resources validating it against legacy Illumina data.
- Threat level: "A minor threat."
- Outlook: New labs without legacy data could benefit from Axelios. Adoption would be "a big barrier for established labs," especially in the clinical space, where "it's going to be a very high bar."
- This is the expert's opinion; the lab does not use Axelios.
