# Illumina CEO Presentation at the 44th J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference (Slides, with Preliminary FY25 Results)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/deck/ILMN_2026-01-13_JPM_Healthcare_Conference_Slides.md` · Date: 2026-01-13 · Illumina, Inc. investor slide deck presented by Jacob Thaysen, PhD, Chief Executive Officer, at the 44th Annual J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference · slides*

**Summary.** Illumina's CEO used the JPM conference to pre-announce approximate FY25 results (revenue about $4.34B, flat in constant currency; Q4 revenue about $1.155B, up about 4%; non-GAAP EPS $4.76-$4.79) and to argue the company has returned to growth as customers move to the NovaSeq X sequencer. The deck reaffirms FY27 targets (high-single-digit ex-China revenue growth, 500+ bps of margin expansion, double-digit to teens EPS growth) and lays out three growth pillars: core sequencing, multiomics (multiple biological data types), and data/AI services. It also covers capital deployment, including the pending SomaLogic acquisition and about $740M of FY25 buybacks.

# Illumina at the 44th J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference (Jan 13, 2026): Slide-by-Slide Notes

This document is a slide deck, not a transcript. It contains no analyst questions or answers, no ratings, no price targets and no estimate changes. Everything below is management's own presentation [JPM, 2026-01-13]. All FY25 figures are described by the company as preliminary, unaudited and approximate. Illumina said it would report full Q4 and FY25 results on February 5, 2026.

Some terms used throughout:
- **NGS** (next-generation sequencing) is the technology that reads DNA in massive parallel batches.
- **Constant currency (CC)** growth removes the effect of exchange-rate changes so that underlying growth can be compared.
- **Non-GAAP** figures exclude certain items (such as acquisition costs and amortization) that Illumina believes obscure underlying performance.
- **Greater China** includes Taiwan and Hong Kong in all Illumina references.

## Slide 4: Illumina, the market leader in NGS

**Installed base (the number of Illumina sequencers in active use).** The company reports the largest installed base, at about 21,000 active instruments. This excludes decommissioned instruments and includes reagent-rental units (instruments placed with customers who pay through reagent purchases). The high-throughput count excludes HiSeq.
- More than 12,000 are low-throughput instruments.
- More than 6,000 are mid-throughput.
- About 2,000 are high-throughput.
- High-throughput (HT) capacity has increased about 2x from 2022 to 2025. This reflects theoretical maximum output on the active HT installed base.

**Market mix, measured as a share of sequencing consumables (the reagent kits customers must buy for each run).**
- About 60% comes from Research & Applied and about 40% from Clinical.
- An additional ~3% is "Other," mainly infectious disease testing, and is excluded from these percentages.
- Within clinical, about 51% is oncology testing, about 25% genetic disease, and about 21% reproductive health.

**Scale.** About 8.6k employees, and customers in about 160 countries.

**Financial profile (FY25).**
- Revenue is about $4.3B.
- Non-GAAP operating margin is about 23%. This is the latest disclosed guidance, given at Q3'25 earnings.
- Recurring revenue is about 90%. It is defined as consumables plus service and other revenue.

Management restates three FY27 targets and shows FY24 and FY25 progress toward them.

**1. Accelerating to high-single-digit (HSD) ex-China revenue growth by FY27 (CC).**

**2. 500+ bps of non-GAAP operating margin expansion (bps = hundredths of a percentage point).**

## Slide 8: Vision for NGS

Management describes four developments it expects:
- **Whole genomes** become the diagnostics standard of care and are routinely adopted in local hospitals around the world.
- **Multiomics at scale** (measuring several biological layers, such as DNA, RNA and proteins) catalyzes deeper biology and new breakthroughs.
- **Sovereign nations** move from "sick-care" to personalized health care, broadly adopting NGS across their healthcare systems.
- **Pharma** accelerates drug discovery and precision medicine using AI-powered large-cohort analysis.

## Slide 11: Three growth pillars

1. **Core sequencing and the NovaSeq X transition.** Complete application-specific workflows with superior turnaround time (TAT) and overall test economics.
2. **Scalable entry into multiomics.** More biological layers per sample to deepen insight and expand use cases.
3. **Expansion of services, data and software.** AI tools and scaled datasets that accelerate discovery and clinical translation.

These pillars serve pharma, research, commercial labs and clinical end-markets.

## Slide 12: Clinical market poised for accelerated growth

Management argues clinical NGS adoption has significant headroom. The deck footnotes this as based on internal market models and "directional."

- **Penetration.** A chart shows adoption below 20% for the clinical applications CGP (comprehensive genomic profiling), MRD (minimal residual disease), MCED (multi-cancer early detection) and GDT (genomic disease testing).
- **Deeper tests.** Physician demand is shifting from small panels toward large panels, whole exome sequencing (WES) and whole genome sequencing (WGS). The slide shows this as higher sequencing intensity, meaning more data per sample.
- **Data per sample.** A human genome is ~15x the Gb of an exome. Footnote: the human genome assumes more than 120 Gb of data per sample versus about 8 Gb for exomes.
- **Growth outlook.** The tailwinds are described as driving accelerated Gb growth, with a future CAGR (compound annual growth rate) of more than 30%, "Past" versus "Future," plus more samples being tested.

## Slide 14: Multiomics solutions add the next layer of growth

- **DRAGEN Single Cell** and **Illumina Single Cell.** Launched H1'25. Understand tissue biology at single-cell resolution, capturing cellular diversity and function.
- **Illumina Connected Multiomics.** Launched H2'25.

The extraction does not make clear which launch date goes with every product, so the Single Cell and Connected Multiomics dates should be checked against the original slide.

## Slide 15: BioInsight

BioInsight is Illumina's business for AI-enabled applications. It aggregates biology at scale to train proprietary foundation models that enable discovery and insights. The inputs the slide lists are:
- Genome-wide Perturb-Seq, which produces causal maps of gene function.
- Large-scale longitudinal datasets from sequencing at scale.
- Compute power.
- Standardized pipelines delivering model-ready data.

The outputs are foundation models, which advance drug discovery for pharma partners and produce clinical insights.

## Slide 16: Capital deployment

**Financial strength.** All figures are last twelve months (LTM) as of 9/28/2025.
- Return on invested capital (ROIC) is above 20%.
- Free cash flow (FCF) is about $1B per year, with FCF conversion above 140%. FCF conversion is FCF divided by non-GAAP net income.
- Gross leverage is about 1.7x (about 0.6x net), with a goal of maintaining an investment-grade credit rating.

**How management plans to use capital:**
- **Invest** to drive growth through innovation across the total workflow, data capacity expansion and multiomic investments.
- **Bolt-on acquisitions** that are key growth enablers, evaluated on serviceable addressable market (SAM) expansion, growth and EPS accretion, and ROIC. The SomaLogic acquisition is shown with a scheduled close in 1H'26, pending regulatory review.
- **Anti-dilutive share buy-back** and additional opportunistic repurchases, including about $740M in FY25.

### Footnotes to the reconciliations (selected)

- Acquisition-related costs mainly cover amortization of intangibles, GRAIL acquisition and divestiture legal costs, contingent consideration fair value changes and, for FY25, costs of the pending SomaLogic deal.
- Transformational initiatives in FY25 are mainly severance from restructuring and ERP system upgrade costs. In FY24 they were lease and asset impairments and severance.
- Strategic investment items are mainly mark-to-market adjustments and impairments, and for FY24, fair value changes on the Helix contingent value right (settled in 2024).
- "Other" in FY25 includes a donation to the Illumina foundation, costs related to board membership changes and legal contingency accruals.
- The FY25 tax line includes a one-time valuation allowance adjustment on deferred tax assets tied to U.S. foreign tax credits, following U.S. tax legislation signed July 4, 2025.
- Non-GAAP operating margin and EPS are key metrics used by the board to measure management's performance and determine significant elements of compensation.
