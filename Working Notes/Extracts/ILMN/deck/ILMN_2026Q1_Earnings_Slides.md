# Illumina Q1 2026 Earnings Presentation, April 30, 2026

This is the slide deck for Illumina's first-quarter 2026 earnings. The quarter ended March 29, 2026. Slides are presented by Jacob Thaysen (Chief Executive Officer) and Ankur Dhingra (Chief Financial Officer). A Q&A slide appears at the end of the main deck, but the document contains no questions or answers. The back of the document holds the financial reconciliation tables, which are summarized below.

## Definitions the company uses

- **Year-over-year (YoY)** compares with the same quarter of the prior fiscal year. **Sequential (quarter-over-quarter)** compares with the prior fiscal quarter. Unless stated, growth rates are YoY and reported.
- **Organic growth** removes the effects of currency and acquisitions. **Rest-of-world (ROW) organic growth** also removes the Greater China region. ROW therefore means everything outside China.
- **China** in the deck means the Greater China region, which includes Taiwan and Hong Kong.
- **Non-GAAP** figures exclude items such as acquisition costs and amortization of acquired intangibles. Illumina says non-GAAP operating margin and EPS are key metrics the board uses to judge management's performance and set management compensation. [Q1 2026 deck, Jacob Thaysen/company statement]
- Regions: USCAN is the U.S. and Canada. EMEALA is Europe, Middle East, Africa and Latin America. APAC is Asia Pacific.

## Slide 4: Q1 2026 results summary (Jacob Thaysen, CEO)

Total revenue was **$1.09 billion**, up **4.8% reported** and **1.2% organic** versus Q1 2025. ROW organic growth was **+3.5%**. The gap between reported and organic growth comes from acquisitions (1.7 points) and favorable currency (1.9 points); see the reconciliation below.

Illumina placed **more than 80 NovaSeq X instruments** in the quarter. NovaSeq X is its newest high-throughput sequencer.

Non-GAAP operating margin was **21.9%**, and non-GAAP diluted EPS (earnings per share) was **$1.15**.

## Slide 6: Q1 2026 revenue (Ankur Dhingra, CFO)

Slide 6 compares Q1'25 with Q1'26 for three revenue measures.

- **Total Illumina revenue** rose from $1,041M to **$1,091M**: +1% organic, +5% reported, with ROW organic growth of +3.5%.
- **Sequencing consumables** (the reagent kits customers must buy for each sequencing run) rose from $696M to **$726M**: +2% organic, +4% reported.
- **ROW sequencing consumables** (outside China) rose from $640M to **$687M**: +5% organic, +7% reported.

What to take from this: ROW consumables growth has accelerated through the last five quarters, from flat in Q1'25 to +7% in Q1'26. Research & Applied (academic, pharma and applied research customers) shrank 12% in Q1'26, worse than the roughly flat result in Q4'25. Sequentially, ROW consumables fell from $713M in Q4'25 to $687M in Q1'26.

### Footnotes explaining adjustments

- **Acquisition-related, Q1'26:** about $17M amortization of intangible assets (cost of revenue), $15M mainly legal and other expenses for the SomaLogic acquisition (operating expense), and $6M amortization of an inventory fair-value step-up from SomaLogic purchase accounting (cost of revenue), offset by $16M of fair-value adjustments on contingent consideration liabilities. SomaLogic is a proteomics (protein measurement) business Illumina acquired along with certain assets from Standard BioTools.
- **Acquisition-related, Q1'25:** $17M amortization, $7M mainly legal costs for the GRAIL acquisition, offset by $11M contingent consideration fair-value adjustments.
- **Transformational initiatives:** in Q1'26, mostly costs of upgrading the ERP (enterprise resource planning) system; in Q1'25, mostly employee severance from restructuring.
- **Strategic investment loss (gain):** realized and unrealized gains, losses and impairments on investments.
- **Other, Q1'25:** $3M for board membership changes and $2M for a legal accrual.

## Risk factors named in the deck

The cautionary notes list risks including: proteomics market growth; SomaLogic integration; Illumina's inclusion on China's "unreliable entities list" (a regulatory designation); U.S. tariffs and related court proceedings, including possible refund programs; manufacturing and third-party supplier reliance; competing products; litigation tied to the GRAIL acquisition; payor reimbursement and regulatory clearance; and economic conditions including armed conflict.
