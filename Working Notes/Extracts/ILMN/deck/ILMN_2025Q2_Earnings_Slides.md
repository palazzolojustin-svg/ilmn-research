# Illumina Q2 2025 Earnings Presentation (slides) – results, SomaLogic acquisition, NovaSeq X transition, revised FY25/Q3'25 guidance

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/deck/ILMN_2025Q2_Earnings_Slides.md` · Date: 2025-07-31 · Illumina, Inc. investor relations (Q2 2025 earnings slide deck; no individual speakers named) · slides*

**Summary.** Illumina reported Q2 2025 revenue of $1.06B (down about 3% year over year), non-GAAP operating margin of 23.8% and non-GAAP EPS of $1.19. The deck announces the planned acquisition of SomaLogic's proteomics (protein-measurement) business for $350M in cash plus milestones, updates the NovaSeq X transition targets, and revises full-year 2025 guidance to revenue of -2.5% to -0.5% in constant currency and EPS of $4.45–$4.55. Greater China revenue fell sharply and China remains a stated risk because of the "unreliable entities list" and an instrument export ban.

# Illumina Q2 2025 Earnings Presentation (July 31, 2025)

This is Illumina's slide deck for its second-quarter 2025 results. "Q2'25" is the quarter ended June 29, 2025. Unless stated otherwise, everything below is Illumina's own claim or reported figure [Q2 2025 slides, 2025-07-31]. "YoY" means versus the same quarter a year earlier; "QoQ" or "sequential" means versus the prior quarter. "CC" means constant currency (growth with the effect of exchange-rate changes removed). "Non-GAAP" means results adjusted to exclude items such as acquisition-related amortization and one-off charges. The deck states that all figures include pricing actions announced in Q1'25 (the deck does not describe those actions).

Comparisons in the deck are mostly against "Core Illumina," meaning Illumina excluding GRAIL. Illumina divested GRAIL (the cancer-detection test business) in June 2024 (the tables say it was spun off June 24, 2024), so consolidated Q2 2024 figures include GRAIL.

## Slide 3: Q2'25 results summary

- **Revenue:** $1.06B (precisely $1,059M), down about 3% YoY in both constant currency and as reported.
- **Non-GAAP operating margin:** 23.8%.
- **Non-GAAP diluted EPS (earnings per share):** $1.19.
- **NovaSeq X:** more than 50 instrument placements in the quarter. The NovaSeq X is Illumina's newest high-throughput sequencer.

Regional revenue (share of the $1.06B total in the chart):

| Asia Pacific, Middle East and Africa (AMEA) | $100M | 10% | -6% CC, -7% reported |

Europe was the only region that grew. Greater China was the weakest. Growth rates are described as approximate.

## Slide 4: Long-term targets

Illumina lists three key growth drivers: (1) core sequencing and the NovaSeq X transition, (2) scalable entry into multiomics (measuring several kinds of biological molecules, not only DNA), and (3) expansion of services, data and software.

## Slide 5: Announced acquisition of SomaLogic

Illumina announced it will acquire SomaLogic, Inc. and certain other assets (the "SomaLogic Business") from Standard BioTools Inc. Proteomics is the large-scale measurement of proteins.

- **What Illumina gets:** all existing SOMAmer-related technology and products (SOMAmers are the protein-binding reagents behind SomaLogic's tests), including SomaScan kits and services, SomaSignal Tests, and relevant technology development capabilities.
- **Purchase consideration:**
  - $350M in cash, payable at closing.
  - Up to $75M in near-term revenue performance-based milestones.
  - Potential performance-based royalties.
- **Strategic rationale (management claims):**
  - It builds on an existing partnership, combining SomaLogic's proteomics technology and expertise with Illumina's "highly scalable NGS ecosystem" (NGS is next-generation sequencing).
  - It "accelerates product timelines" by enabling a more seamless proteomics development roadmap.
  - Data-driven proteomics advances Illumina's multiomics strategy and positions it for growth in a "large and expanding proteomics market."
- **SomaLogic financials (management expectations):**
  - End markets are growing at high-single-digit-plus to double-digit rates.
  - It is a high-margin consumables business, as enabled on NGS.
  - Illumina expects the business to become profitable in 2027 on a non-GAAP operating income basis, and its non-GAAP operating margins to be in line with Illumina's in 2028.
- **Timeline:** projected to close in the first half of 2026, after regulatory clearance. The risk factors name Hart-Scott-Rodino Act approval in the U.S. and approvals from other governments as conditions, and list completion risk and integration risk.

## Slide 6: MiSeq i100 Plus

The MiSeq i100 Plus is a benchtop sequencer. As of Q2'25 it has had more than 500 instrument placements since launch. The deck also cites 18 proven workflows and 9 workflows from library prep (preparing DNA for sequencing) through analysis.

HT refers to Illumina's high-throughput sequencers, and Gb means gigabases (billions of DNA letters read). The slide tracks how much of the older NovaSeq 6000 base is shifting to the newer NovaSeq X. ## Slide 10: Q2'25 non-GAAP financials

- **Gross margin** (revenue left after direct costs): 69.4%, about flat versus 69.4% in Q2 2024.
- **Operating margin:** 23.8% versus 22.2%, up 160 basis points (1.6 percentage points).
- **EPS:** $1.19 versus $1.09, a reported increase of about 9%.

## Slide 11: Cash flow and balance sheet

- Cash flow from operations: $234M. Capital expenditures: $30M. Free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capex): $204M.
- **Share repurchases in Q2:** about 4.5M shares at a total cost of $380M, an average price of $84.66 per share.
- Cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments: about $1.16B.
- Gross leverage (gross debt to last-twelve-months EBITDA): about 1.7x.

## Risks flagged in the deck

The cautionary note lists, among others: slower growth in served markets including proteomics; SomaLogic closing, regulatory approval and integration; managing partner and customer relationships in proteomics; Illumina's inclusion on China's "unreliable entities list" and China's decision not to permit it to export sequencing instruments into China; U.S. tariffs and counter-tariffs; manufacturing and supplier reliance; competing products; GRAIL-related litigation; payor reimbursement and regulatory clearance; and economic conditions or armed conflict.
