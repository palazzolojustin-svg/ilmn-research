# GeneDx (WGS) Q2 2026 Earnings Call: record genome volume, flat reimbursement per test, push to raise collection rates

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/WGS/transcript/GeneDx_2026-08-03_Q2_2026_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2026-08-03 · The Motley Fool transcript of the GeneDx Q2 2026 earnings call; speakers Katherine Stueland (CEO), Kevin Feeley (CFO), Mark Gardner (President); analysts from TD Cowen, BTIG, Craig-Hallum, Piper Sandler, Canaccord Genuity, Jefferies, Guggenheim and Freedom Capital Markets · earnings call*

**Summary.** GeneDx, a large exome and genome testing lab and a major Illumina sequencing customer, reported Q2 2026 revenue of $114.4 million (up 11%) on a record 30,785 exome and genome tests (up 32%), returned to adjusted profitability a quarter early, and reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance. Revenue grows slower than volume because the mix shifted toward lower-priced genomes and only about 32% of outpatient genome claims are paid; management says raising collection rates toward about 70% is its biggest opportunity, with real uplift in Q4 2026 and mostly in 2027. Genome costs nearly twice exome costs, mainly because of reagents, and management expects step improvements in 2027.

# GeneDx Q2 2026 Earnings Call (3 August 2026)

**Background.** GeneDx is a lab that sequences patients' exomes (the protein-coding ~2% of DNA) and genomes (all of a person's DNA) to diagnose rare and inherited disease. It is a large buyer of Illumina sequencing instruments and reagents. Key terms used on the call:
- **ARR / "blended average reimbursement rate":** average revenue collected per exome or genome test.
- **Collection rate:** the share of claims that insurers actually pay.
- **Reflex testing:** a product that starts with an exome and, if needed, escalates ("reflexes") to a genome. It is reimbursed at the exome rate [Feeley].
- **NICU:** neonatal intensive care unit.
- **RCM:** revenue cycle management (billing, prior authorization and denials handling).

## Results and guidance

### Q2 2026 results [Q2 2026 call]

| Metric | Q2 2026 | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $114.4M | Up 11% year over year; above company guide |
| Exome and genome revenue | $100.3M | Up 17% |
| Exome and genome test volume | 30,785 | Up 32%; a record (">30,000") |
| Total gross margin | 70% | Up from 69% in Q1 |
| Adjusted net income | $0.4M | $8.6M better than Q1; returned to profitability one quarter earlier than previously expected |
| Blended reimbursement per test | $3,258 | Roughly flat vs Q1 and in line with expectations (the change was about $50, within 1%) |
| Operating expenses | About $80M | Slightly better than expected |

- Unit growth was broad-based across geneticists, pediatric neurology and NICU, with early contributions from general pediatrics and prenatal [Feeley].
- Management completed the full $25M of annual cost actions committed to on the prior call, while protecting investment in proven growth channels and operations [Feeley, Stueland].
- Feeley said the main driver of lower reimbursement versus 2025 is the mix shift into genome, and that there were "no meaningful changes to our contracted pricing." He called the Q2 blended rate "the new baseline for the remainder of the year."

### Balance sheet
- After quarter-end, GeneDx expanded its existing debt facility by $50M and received a concurrent equity investment from strategic partner Blackstone Life Sciences. Pro forma liquidity was about $188M as of 30 June 2026.
- Feeley said this leaves the company "fully capitalized well beyond sustained positive cash flow," with flexibility to invest in strategic opportunities.

### Guidance (reaffirmed for full year 2026)
- Revenue of $475M to $490M.
- Exome and genome volume growth of at least 30%.
- Exome and genome revenue growth of at least 20%.
- Gross margin of about 70%, and profitability for the full year.

**Q3 2026 guidance:**
- Revenue of $122M to $124M.
- Exome and genome revenue of $110M to $112M on about 33,200 tests, implying about $3,300 per test.
- Gross margin of about 70%.
- Adjusted net income of about $2M.

Feeley said the guide reflects July trends: genome mix kept falling, core-channel demand was healthy, and for the first time in a single month since launch (February 2026) reflex volume exceeded genome volume.

## Prepared remarks

### CEO Katherine Stueland
- Demand is strong ("Demand for our services has never been higher"). The business is organized around three imperatives: optimizing unit economics, growing exome and genome utilization, and delivering leading products at scale, all with responsible capital management.
- Unit economics comes first because "we're underearning on our potential." Getting paid more per test is "the single biggest opportunity." Over time she expects revenue growth to converge with or exceed volume growth.
- Long-term view: the genome will increasingly be the standard for diagnosing all heritable disease. In Q2 the mix moderated, with steady month-over-month gains back into exome products, including reflex.
- Carelon (a payer) announced a new policy that expanded commercial genome coverage from 47% to 87% of lives in one quarter. GeneDx credits its own advocacy and evidence generation.
- Strategy: serving the market ahead of coverage shows demand and medical necessity, and that unpaid volume can turn into revenue as coverage expands.
- Why clinicians choose GeneDx: result quality and reliability, leading turnaround times, deep data and expertise, and rare-disease customer support.
- Target: drive collections to what she called the industry standard of 70%.
- She welcomed Mark Gardner, who joined in June 2026.
- Closing: GeneDx led earlier transitions, from single-gene and panel tests to exome about 15 years ago, and now from exome to genome.

### President Mark Gardner
He agrees GeneDx is "underearning today on both exome and genome." Four operational levers aim to raise blended reimbursement:
1. **Mix management.** Managing exome vs genome mix in the near term buys time to build the processes needed to maximize payment on genome volume. Mix improved in Q2 and continued improving in July.
2. **Expand payer coverage.** Coverage is moving the right way but "still lags clinical demand and includes too much red tape."
3. **Payer-specific workflows.** Medical necessity criteria, documentation and prior authorization vary by payer. Better workflows should raise approval rates, cut avoidable denials and lift the share of tests paid. Exome collection will also improve.
4. **Technology.** AI and other systems across the revenue cycle, including internal controls and customer-experience features for billing information. This is longer term and still under development.

Progress has been made on all four, and he expects them to influence rates "in the quarters to come."

**Commercial:**
- GeneDx holds about 80% share among geneticists and has grown pediatric neurology share to roughly 50%. Geneticists moved from exome to genome but "did not leave GeneDx."
- Reaching a geneticist can take years, so GeneDx is diversifying call points (the physician types it targets).
- Based on updated American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) guidelines, it built out a general pediatrics team in early 2026. Q2 was the highest growth ever in that channel, with early signals of adoption: more field rep and medical science liaison engagement, new accounts and first orders from new clinicians.
- Sales coverage models will be adjusted to keep an appropriate return on investment.

### CFO Kevin Feeley

**Mix.** Genome was 32% of insurance-based outpatient volume in Q2, down from close to 40% in Q1 and up from 15% in 2025. This drives the gap between volume and revenue growth. Genome contracted rates are lower than exome, but "genome gross margins are healthy." Exome already runs at a gross margin above the company average.

**Payer coverage:**
- Commercial exome coverage: about 98% of lives, up from about 90% in Q1.
- Commercial genome coverage: about 87%, up from 47% last quarter, mainly because of Carelon.
- Medicaid: 39 states cover exome or genome, with Mississippi starting 1 July.
- Not all policies are equal: claims still get denied through restrictive eligibility criteria and administrative barriers.
- About 67% of outpatient genome volume in Q2 went to payers with an active positive coverage policy, versus 38% in Q2 2025 and 46% in Q1 2026. The trend continued up in July.

**Collection rates:**
- The underlying outpatient genome collection rate (percentage of claims paid) was about 32%, flat with Q1 and down from 43% in Q2 2025.
- Feeley attributes the decline to developing the market by accepting volume ahead of coverage.
- "If we were to double that 32% collection rate, what are zeros in our blended ARR today become paid units," giving uplift that "falls directly to the bottom line."
- Outpatient exome collection rates are comparable to genome, so there is room to improve. He said most denials are addressable.
- Q3 collection rates should stay roughly flat, since payer workflows are still being built. Revenue recognition uses collection experience over a trailing period, so improvements take a couple of quarters to appear. He expects meaningful improvement in Q4 2026 and the most significant uplift in 2027.
- Single-payer behavior (administrative changes, tighter policy, contract changes) could cause variability, but the payer base is diversified.

**Cost.**
- A whole genome costs nearly twice as much to produce as an exome, almost entirely from higher reagent costs. These should fall as genome utilization grows and manufacturers advance their technology.
- Cost per test should stay relatively flat in the second half of 2026, with step improvements in 2027.

## Q&A

### Dan Brennan, TD Cowen
- **Pacing:** Feeley said there is a natural lag between operational improvements and accrual rates. Collection should stay flat in Q3, with meaningful improvement from Q4, and "the upside is real" in 2027. "We're placing a premium on getting paid and guiding with that discipline."
- **Managing mix versus competitors pushing genomes:** Stueland said the future is genome, but about 80% of the time patients with genetic disease can be diagnosed with an exome. Clinicians can quickly reflex to a genome if needed. Because turnaround times are down, GeneDx can do this faster than other labs run a genome. She said the sales force is taking a "meticulous approach" so customers get what they want.
- **Price beyond 2026:** Brennan had modeled flat realized price after 2026 (the Street at low single-digit growth). Feeley replied that "to keep flat in '27 and beyond misses a core central opportunity." The 32% genome rate reflects coverage maturity, and volume was taken in deliberately to build evidence, shown by the jump from 47% to 87% commercial coverage. Exome and genome collection are both well below the roughly 70% typical of a mature diagnostic test, which would double today's rate. It will not be linear, but "in 2027 and beyond, we expect this work to pay off handsomely."

### Mark Massaro, BTIG
- **Carelon:** Stueland called it a "massive opening up of possible patients and revenue," covering about 56 million lives. "Coverage leads and payment lags" and payers do not operationalize immediately. Feeley said nothing is counted on for Q3, some contribution begins in Q4 2026, and it is fuller in 2027.
- **Medi-Cal (California Medicaid):** Feeley said California published a genome rate for fee-for-service a couple of months earlier, effective 1 July, so it is early. The bigger task is confirming managed care organizations (MCOs, the largest share of volume) follow Medi-Cal policy as regulation requires. Some uplift is counted based on recent trends, but greater uplift is expected in 2027.
- **General pediatrics:** Stueland noted it has been just over a year since the guidelines published and activity is early. Clinicians order predominantly exome. Gardner said developmental and behavioral specialists order more exomes than other general pediatricians. Practices are consolidating into corporate groups, which could aggregate demand and set policy. He said GeneDx is focusing on higher-productivity physicians and corporate accounts, and that "so far, the evidence is that it has a very high return on investment." Orders were not quantified.

### William Bonello, Craig-Hallum
- **Why ARR dipped despite better mix:** Feeley said the mix improvement is real (40% to 32% genome, with the difference flowing to reflex). Underlying exome collection was roughly flat, and slight payer-mix variation caused the roughly $50 move. Blended ARR has "stabilized" as the new base. He said "we're not happy with the 32% collection rate" and called it the company's largest earnings opportunity.
- **Exome collection of ~55% recalled:** Feeley clarified that the cited rates cover outpatient insurance-based volume only. NICU is collected at 100%, which lifts the total toward the 50% range. GeneDx will track the underlying outpatient rate.
- **Guidance reconciliation:** Feeley said the guide incorporates July trends: mix improving, healthy core demand, and reflex exceeding genome in a single month for the first time.

### David Westenberg, Piper Sandler
- **Visibility into Q4 collections and sales productivity:** Feeley said visibility was a commitment after Q1, and he has added financial leadership and revenue cycle leaders with experience at very large scale. Data through July informs the outlook. Gardner said traditional call points deliver at a high rate per rep, while emerging markets now represent a meaningful share of volume growth. Many reps were hired early in 2026 and should become more productive, accelerating in the second half.
- **Cash flow:** Stueland said the $25M OpEx cut is done and capital is organized around the three priorities. Feeley said Q2 operating cash flow will be the high point of the year, as it reflected a lag from the Q1 operating loss (heavy sales investment), a heavier capex cycle (the sequencing fleet was significantly expanded for genome volume, now leveling out), and some one-time payments. Three things improve the picture: completed cost actions, RCM gains from Q4, and COGS leverage from volume. He expects about $10M (cash use, in context) in Q3, a return to cash generation in Q4, and "strong sustained cash generation" by 2027. Blackstone financing provides runway.

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord Genuity
- **Reflex upside to guidance:** Feeley said the guide reflects expected mix evolution. Reflex is reimbursed at the exome rate, so reflex and exome interplay. Genome mix is expected at or slightly below today's level in the second half.
- **Which markets gained:** Reflex is a bridge offering. Interest is greatest among geneticists, whose genome orders are moving to reflex. The product is "operating better than initial expectations" since its February launch. Stueland said gen peds is predominantly exome.

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies
- **Competition:** Stueland said GeneDx is still dominant, with 80% share among geneticists and 50% in pediatric specialists. It welcomes competition because "more voices" open markets and shape payer policy faster. Gardner said competition turns on who can both measure and match variation best. The Infinity database is a unique asset that gives higher diagnostic yield, so customers are loyal.
- **1-minute ordering:** Designed by and for general pediatricians, and could unlock volume across all channels. It launches later in summer as an MVP (minimum viable product) with rolling features, and is on track. Gardner said emerging markets have different service expectations.

### Subbu Nambi, Guggenheim
- **Mix assumption:** Genome mix is expected to hover around 30%, perhaps slightly lower, in the second half, based on recent order flow.
- **What is different now:** Gardner said GeneDx historically has not invested enough in systems and process for payer-specific workflows. Industry hires bring a straightforward playbook: comply exactly with each payer's requirements. He sees dramatic improvement.
- **New rep productivity:** Gardner said return on investment is already positive and productivity should keep accelerating. 1-minute ordering and market-specific service complement the reps.

### Keith Hinton, Freedom Capital Markets
- **Volume by market:** Feeley said most unit growth came from geneticists, pediatric neurology and NICU, in that rank order, given larger bases. General pediatrics and prenatal are small but growing fast. He sees significant headroom in the core markets.
- **Gross margin up ~200 bps sequentially despite slightly lower ASP:** Feeley cited wet lab efficiency, lower input costs as genome utilization grows, and early deployment of advanced technology in the dry lab (computational side), plus economies of scale. Gardner said machine learning and AI, with GeneDx's data, drove "very significant improvements in dry lab productivity in Q2," and in some cases dry lab gains exceed wet lab gains.

## Notable points for Illumina and sequencing read-through
- Genome cost is nearly 2x exome, "almost entirely" from reagent cost. Feeley expects it to fall with genome utilization and manufacturer technology advances, flat in H2 2026 and stepping down in 2027 [Q2 2026 call].
- GeneDx significantly expanded its sequencing fleet over the past year for genome volume, and capex is expected to level out [Feeley].
- Competitors are pushing whole genomes, and GeneDx says its turnaround times let it reflex faster than others can run a genome [Stueland].
