# Barclays sector update (Sergott): AI-disruption fears in Tools and CROs, with rating changes on IQV, MEDP, AVTR, CERT and PACB and a lower ICLR price target

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/SECTOR/research/20260312_Barclays_SECTOR-Tools_U.S._Life_Science_-_Diagnostic_Tools-_UPDATE-_The_Bullet.md` · Date: 2026-03-06 · Barclays Equity Research (Barclays Capital Inc.), U.S. Life Science & Diagnostic Tools team: Luke Sergott (lead analyst and certifying analyst), Anna Kruszenski, Jacob Putman CFA, Salem Salem · sell-side research*

**Summary.** Barclays argues that the early-2026 sell-off in Tools and CRO (contract research organization) stocks, triggered by Anthropic's Claude life-science and clinical-research plug-ins, is largely overblown. It explains why AI should increase rather than replace wet-lab work and why CRO outsourcing is durable. It upgrades IQV to Overweight and MEDP to Equal Weight, downgrades AVTR (to Underweight), CERT (to Equal Weight) and PACB (to Underweight), and cuts the ICLR price target to $120 from $200. For Illumina it notes the stock fell only 5% since the plug-in news (short covering on a better-than-feared EPS print) and that Illumina's TruPath long-read launch could "freeze part of the long-read market" for PacBio.

The theme: given the pullback across Tools (companies that sell instruments, consumables and software to labs) and CROs (contract research organizations, which run clinical trials for drug makers), clients asked which names are most disrupted by AI and where fears are overblown. The headline says: upgrade IQV and MEDP; downgrade AVTR, CERT, PACB. The analysts have the most conviction in TXG, TWST, CRL and DHR because those names lack exposure to AI fears. Barclays says "the Claude updates had a dramatic impact on the complex, which led to some pretty interesting questions from generalists/AI bulls." It believes the sell-off "has reset multiples to attractive levels and presents good entry points for both short-term and longer-term investors."

## Positioning and conviction ranking for a recovery

### AI beneficiaries
TXG and TWST "are clear winners in our view."

### Indirect beneficiaries with more idiosyncratic (company-specific) risk
Barclays: "AI is not coming for bioprocessing and QA/QC applications."
- **DHR:** Being punished for doing an accretive deal "across the board." Barclays says DHR has "a much shorter time in the penalty box than WAT post-BDX." DHR is "likely our favorite over the shorter term (and longer term here too)," expected to be one of the first to recover if management shows progress toward a beat-and-raise year.
- **WAT:** Will struggle short-term; "it will take several quarters and maybe longer for investors to believe that management is going to turn around BDX in a durable way."
- **A (Agilent):** Barclays believes management will not do a disruptive or dilutive deal, but given how WAT and DHR traded after deal announcements, expects A to be "in the penalty box for a period or two post any transaction, depending on what it acquires."
- **MTD:** Barclays has "always liked MTD and management's history of execution." Fear over China after the Iran conflict weighs on MTD more than peers; "This is one of the only reasons we can give for the recent underperformance."
- **RVTY and BRKR:** Fall in this category because "they have historical margin ramps embedded in their guides."

### Neutral/indirect beneficiaries with overblown AI fears
These are wet-lab-focused names (companies whose business depends on physical lab experiments) that have sold off. They "likely will be solutions providers, so can see some cannibalization, likely more than offset by increased volumes."
- **CRL:** "Most dislocated and has the most wetlab exposure + the biggest NAM business in the world" (NAMs are new approach methodologies, non-animal testing methods), so "plenty of offsets to any pressure from AI cannibalization." Also: "This is not even a late-stage clinical services business but gets lumped in with the group."
- **TMO:** At the top of the list, but through PPD (its CRO) it also suffers from CRO fears. Barclays: TMO "has shown time and again that they can earn through, use any market disruption to their advantage." It is set up for a beat-and-raise year and its valuation is "set for a significant unlock once the AI fears on PPD subside." TMO would likely be at the top in the second phase of recovery as investors return to Tools; it could be in the first tier given long-term-investor interest, so "if we are wrong, it would likely be more on the timing surprising to the upside."
- **RVTY:** Fits because "they have drug disco + lots of software exposure."

### CROs
Barclays upgrades IQV to Overweight and MEDP to Equal Weight because fears are overblown and "valuations are baking in significant disruption." The key risk: "valuations do not hold in and continue to collapse, given there appears to be no backstop right now." These are "riskier calls and definitely for more longer-term, value-oriented investors."
- **IQV (U/G to OW):** Like TMO, IQV will be an AI solutions provider, which should drive share gains and offset AI-related erosion and margin pressure. Longer-term call "not without hair." Valuation should ultimately trade like service businesses in other industries.
- **MEDP (U/G to EW):** "Best in class with very specialized offerings that should catch tailwinds from biotech recovery." Elevated 4Q cancellations and decelerating out-year growth keep Barclays on the sidelines until clearer or cheaper.
- **FTRE (Fortrea, Equal Weight):** Added transparency on "605/606" revenues (the note does not define these, but they are the bookings/backlog/revenue disclosure categories Barclays has been pushing CROs to give) "helps build confidence in the underlying business, or at least sets a backstop." Real work takes time; with a clearer picture of margins one can see how management's initiatives play out. "Interesting setup" but needs more proof points on the turnaround; Barclays admits it "could (continue to) miss a move in shares."
- **ICLR:** Near-term "very favorable trade in the near term from an up/down perspective." Barclays thinks the audit and overstated revenues will be a modest impact: it does not appear to affect cash flow, and backlog is big enough that growth is not dramatically hit even if management restates backlog by 10% to use more conservative bookings standards. A backlog restatement would be viewed negatively, and Barclays has questions on share losses during this period, as seen with other CROs with past hiccups. Valuations are "well below trough levels" and the setup favors a trade into 1Q. After that, "ICLR has a long way to go to regain investor confidence and shares likely lag the group." Barclays waits for fundamentals clarity before making a call.

## Thesis: AI will not disrupt or replace wet-lab work

Barclays calls the notion that AI will disrupt or replace wet lab work "deeply flawed, if we were to put it lightly." Conviction "has only grown since the first Life Science Claude plugin was introduced."

Arguments:
- AI will make parts of drug discovery more efficient: AlphaFold has predicted protein structures for a few years; experiment planning has gone "from days to hours"; AI raises the ROI (return on investment) of drug discovery.
- Biology understanding "is incomplete at best and wrong at worst. This is why we do experiments." As soon as one gap is filled, "5 more open." AI can replace some experimentation, but where the science is poorly understood, experiments are still needed to test hypotheses.
- Very long-term, some wet lab use could be replaced by AI, but only after a near-to-medium-term acceleration in specific data collection. So the likelier near-term effect is MORE wet lab work to train AI models. Evidence: the boom in TWST's Drug Discovery business, led by AI data collection.
- Same dynamic in data analysis for large-scale proteomics/multiomics projects (measuring many proteins or molecular layers at once). AI tools create deeper insights faster. Claude's plug-in for the TXG single-cell workflow is "a perfect example."
- Within pharma: AI speeds experiment design and early molecule iteration, raising the chance a molecule reaches FDA approval (late-stage failure is expensive). Two routes to more wet-lab work: (a) less time on design leaves scientists more time for lab work; (b) more frequent early iteration means more experiments, whose results feed back into AI models, repeated until the molecule is optimized. Summary: "more time on scientists hands → more time feeding AI models and iterating on the molecule to increase the probability for FDA approval → more wet lab work."
- AI should raise pharma ROI (better-vetted molecules, less spending on failed trials) and increase the number of drugs in the pipeline. Barclays does not see why pharma "arbitrarily lowers its ROI targets because more molecules are coming through": pharma will spend on R&D if ROI is there, which dispels the debate that pharma will spend less on R&D and wet lab.

## Thesis: CRO fears are overblown (AI likely hits CROs more than core Tools)

1. **Regulation.** Clinical research is among the most regulated industries because it deals with life and death. The FDA and other global regulators will not accept a complete shift in trial paradigms in a few years; "The FDA only started allowing PDFs or digital submission of data in 2016/2017."
2. **Outsourcing is here to stay.** Some functions may be brought in-house, but pharma focuses on discovering, developing and commercializing drugs and lacks infrastructure for diverse pipelines. "Why would they invest in a back-office function, and build out their own AI system to do something that they have outsourced for a very long time?"
3. **Data access.** Biopharma's internal data covers only a narrow set of indications. Would they license data from IQV? IQV "would likely be totally fine with this agreement." Datasets are fragmented: ICLR management used to talk about sourcing 70 different datasets for its trials. AI helps pull them together but the company needs access.
4. **CROs are building their own AI tools.** The Claude clinical research plug-in is available to everyone, so IQV and larger CROs can add AI modules to systems pharma already uses. "It is safe to assume that anything that IQV and others make is going to be more comprehensive and seamless than a model vibe-coded by a pharma company."

## IQVIA (IQV): upgrade to Overweight, PT $210 unchanged

- Price $178.50; potential upside +17.6%. Upside case $250, downside case $160.
- Barclays acknowledges "investor hesitance to fight an AI narrative that likely has no end in sight, as it is impossible to disprove a negative." Two things would help backstop valuations: (1) more clarity on 605 bookings/backlog/P&L (few companies give this, only on the revenue side; Barclays will keep pushing, "likely in vain and to management's annoyance"); (2) management framing how AI could disrupt the business, what is at risk, and what they are doing internally. The second matters more for IQV because 606/605 questions are less relevant given its size, scale, reputation and execution history, which should earn a premium vs. late-stage peers (ex-MEDP).
- IQV shares fell more than 20% after the announcement versus SPX down 2%. Management framed the AI impact as ~5% of revenue; Barclays estimates that is ~15%-20% of TAS (Technology & Analytics Solutions) revenue and under 3% of corporate EBITDA. The AI threat is concentrated in consulting and lower-margin TAS businesses. Near-term, Barclays sees cannibalization that could shift those revenues to a higher-margin profile rather than IQV losing the business. R&D (clinical) is "not immune" but impact is "much more minimal than most fear." Investor fears and the share drop are "overdone."
- Value unlock: management delivering on its EBITDA growth guidance, showing no added pressure beyond pass-throughs (costs passed to clients) and FX. This is also the biggest risk: any EBITDA/margin wobble "will likely result in further multiple contraction, as there is no valuation support for services businesses at this time." Barclays admits its call "might be early as the show-me story needs to play out."
- Valuation: PT $210 = 12.5x EV/EBITDA on FY26E EBITDA of $3.98B (vs. 12.5x on $3.99B prior). Barclays says services businesses in other industries trade at low-to-mid teens EBITDA multiples.

## PacBio (PACB): downgrade to Underweight, PT $1.50 (from $2)

- Price $1.52; downside -1.3%. Upside case $3.00; downside case $1.00 (shown as "USD 100" in the garbled table).
- Barclays likes management's focus on core technology and its vision, but sees a tough near-term setup: a potential demand pause given competitive launches across sequencing, plus an "air pocket" on consumables in 1H as customers wind down research in anticipation of SPRQ NX (PacBio's coming lower-cost chemistry/sequencing product).
- **Roche Axelios:** Has longer reads than Illumina's SBS (sequencing by synthesis) chemistry, "but no where close to PACB." With limited funding, researchers who overlap between PacBio and Roche/short-read tech may put tight capex budgets toward Axelios. Likely a modest headwind to Revio (PacBio's long-read instrument) this year, after a tough 2025 for the platform.
- **Illumina:** ILMN has tried to enter long-read since its attempt to acquire PACB in 2020 was blocked by the FTC. The launch of TruPath would be "the third iteration/name change of ILMN's push into long-read sequencing." Given the mixed history, Barclays waits for a larger dataset from the community before concluding ILMN will win share, but near term "ILMN's launch could freeze part of the long-read market." Barclays says this likely gets more investor attention than Axelios.
- **SPRQ NX:** Launching later this year; "significantly brings down the cost of sequencing ($300/long-read genome)." By itself it could freeze PACB's market near term until customers burn off inventories ahead of the transition. Lower cost could be a "meaningful shift/inflection" even with a multi-wash step to reach those costs. Barclays hears less about the multi-wash being a hurdle in research than in prior years, but it could be a bigger hurdle on the clinical side, where PACB must penetrate to unlock a more material TAM (total addressable market).

Expects underperformance vs. the core Tools smid-cap group. Barclays "debated a lot internally" on the downgrade given enthusiasm for SPRQ NX and high regard for management, but "an illiquid stock with a 2H-weighted catalyst path is not likely to garner much investor interest."
