# Thermo Fisher Scientific (TMO) CEO Marc Casper at the Morgan Stanley 24th Annual Global Healthcare Conference, Sep 15, 2026

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/TMO/transcript/TMO_2026-09-15_Morgan_Stanley_24th_Annual_Global_Healthcare_Conference.md` · Date: 2026-09-15 · Seeking Alpha transcript; Morgan Stanley 24th Annual Global Healthcare Conference; speaker Marc Casper (Chairman & CEO, Thermo Fisher); interviewer Kallum Titchmarsh (Morgan Stanley Research, head of Life Science Tools and Diagnostics team) · conference*

**Summary.** Thermo Fisher's CEO says 2026 is shaping up as an outstanding year, with Q2 organic growth of 5% and full-year organic growth now expected at about 4%. He describes improving pharma, biotech and academic/government demand, a gradual China recovery, a tailwind from US manufacturing reshoring, and strong bioproduction and clinical research businesses. He reaffirms the 3% to 6% medium-term organic growth range and a path to about 7% from 2028. Sequencing, Illumina and diagnostics are not discussed.

## Overview and context

This is a fireside-style conversation on Day 2 of the Morgan Stanley Global Healthcare Conference, held September 15, 2026. Kallum Titchmarsh of Morgan Stanley interviews Marc Casper, Thermo Fisher's CEO. Thermo Fisher (TMO) is a large supplier of life science tools (instruments and consumables for labs), pharma contract services, and related products. Casper is joined by Raf Tejada (Investor Relations) and Sandy Pound (Chief Communications Officer). The conversation does not mention Illumina, DNA sequencing, competitors by name, pricing or diagnostics.

The interviewer opens by noting growing momentum through 2026. Q2 came in ahead of guidance and was the strongest organic growth (growth excluding acquisitions, divestitures and currency effects) since 2021.

## State of the business and 2026 outlook

**Casper's overall view [MS conference, 2026-09-15]:** 2026 "is setting up to be really an outstanding year," driven by improving end markets and strong operational performance.
- Q2 2026 organic growth was 5%, and earnings per share (EPS) grew 13% in the quarter.
- The company raised its full-year outlook and now expects organic growth of about 4% for 2026. Earlier in the year the guidance was described as "3% to 4%," and Casper says it is "likely to be 4%."
- Progress is occurring within the previously stated 3% to 6% organic growth range for 2026 and 2027, "progressing higher in the range."
- He says strong customer relationships are "paying off in a really strong way," and the acquisitions are performing well because customers want to adopt the added technologies.

**Second-half outlook [MS conference, 2026-09-15]:** The interviewer says guidance implies about 4% organic growth in the second half, balanced between Q3 and Q4. Casper confirms this.
- He says the company feels "very good" about the 4% second-half outlook and expects the two quarters to be similar.
- First-half 2026 organic growth was 3%, and the second half is expected to be 4%, giving about 4% for the full year. Casper says the company thinks of the business on an annual or six-month basis rather than quarter by quarter.
- Orders are running ahead of revenue, which he says bodes well for the future.
- The main puts and takes are execution against the backlog and continued strengthening of end markets, which "are playing out as we would expect." He says the company is confident of achieving the 2026 outlook and entering 2027 with "very solid momentum."

## Medium- and long-term growth framework

Casper refers to the Investor Day held in May 2026 [MS conference, 2026-09-15].
- Organic growth is expected to be in the 3% to 6% range across 2026 and 2027, and orders suggest growth will continue to strengthen into 2027.
- For 2028 and beyond, the company expects to operate in the "7% range." Casper says the 7% target "doesn't feel like very far from today's date."
- The drivers are stabilization of academic and government spending and improving biotech funding, both of which he says are playing out. He cites an aging population, scientific breakthroughs and demand for life science tools and Pharma Services as structural drivers, calling the industry "an outstanding neighborhood to live in" after a "painful last few years."
- Potential tailwind: China, because the company's assumptions there have been relatively modest.
- Potential headwind: macro risk, such as "something that really throws the economy off track." He says this is less a life science tools issue than a general economic one.
- On China, he says the company does not need China to be a meaningful contributor to progress through 3% to 6% and then to 7% or more in 2028.

## Pharma and biotech

Pharma and biotech is Thermo Fisher's largest customer set, about 60% of revenue. It grew mid-single digits in Q2 2026, and each of the last several quarters has been a little stronger than the one before [MS conference, 2026-09-15].
- Pharma customers are "quite positive," building pipelines, excited by the science, and expecting AI to improve returns on drug development. Casper says this fuels an investment cycle.
- Biotech is in a much better part of the cycle because funding and confidence are improving.
- Q2 strength was broad: clinical research, the research and safety market channel (Fisher Scientific, the company's distribution business for lab supplies), and bioproduction (making biologic medicines) all grew strongly.

**Funding-to-revenue lag:** Casper says that in 2025 the tone from emerging biotechs improved even though funding was not strong. Funding improved through 2025 and stepped up further in 2026, helped by industry M&A fueling venture capital investment, plus IPOs. Funding typically takes 2 to 3 quarters to flow into spending, depending on the business. The sequence he describes:
1. Clinical research first, because it gets companies to their next milestone and funding round. This shows in very strong authorizations (new contract wins) growth and revenue growth in clinical research.
2. The research and safety market channel (Fisher Scientific) next, as companies refill research pipelines and lab spend rises. This is starting now.
3. High-tech reagents (specialized consumables) are next, expected to strengthen over the next couple of quarters.

**Personalized mRNA cancer vaccines:** Asked about recent data, Casper says the opportunity is "less about the Moderna and Merck specific spending in two important customers but more about the interest in the area, which then spurs a reinvestment cycle." He describes it as a therapy addressing an individual's specific cancer and says capital will flow to support the area, as it has for antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) and other areas, calling it "yet another leg to drive growth in the industry."

**Reshoring (moving drug manufacturing to the US):** Casper says many medicines for the US are made in Western Europe (innovative medicines) or Asia Pacific, with generics mostly in India. Administration policies give economic benefits to US production, jobs and supply chain resilience. He sees three waves, all one-time benefits:
- **CDMO contracts (contract development and manufacturing):** Customers can meet US government requirements by committing to US production through contracts, without building a factory. Thermo Fisher has secured a number of large contracts. President Trump visited its CDMO site in Cincinnati (a high-tech oral solid dose facility) in March 2026. Existing factories produce, customers tech-transfer, and jobs and growth follow. Casper calls this the first phase.
- **Expansions of existing facilities:** Customers buy equipment, move medicines to the US, and stock labs and supplies. Activity is picking up, with a step-up probably in 2027.
- **Greenfield (brand-new) facilities:** Most of the announcements and dollars. These bring one-time purchases of equipment and lab fit-out, with Fisher Scientific supplying labs and inventory. Timing is 2027 to 2028.

He says the same amount of medicine is produced overall, so it is a one-time benefit that is "additive to how we've thought about our growth outlook," and it will normalize back to industry volume growth. He adds, "we'll take it."

## Academic and government

Q2 2026 was a strong quarter [MS conference, 2026-09-15].
- Academic and government returned to low-single-digit growth.
- Europe was strong, the US returned to slightly positive growth, and China remained a headwind in academic spending, though it was a better quarter. Growth was driven by adoption of high-end instrumentation, and the chromatography and mass spectrometry business did very well.
- Casper's view: funding has been "okay" but anxiety has been high. Academic and government spending follows funding flows over long periods, but in 2025 many pronouncements about changing policies made customers cautious.
- In 2026 the market is stabilizing but still muted. Funding is flowing, and he expects a return to growth in 2027.
- The anxiety is "literally only a U.S. dynamic." Europe was even stronger because it has fewer policy issues, and China remains muted because the government has been restrained on academic and government spending.

## Industrial and applied markets

This is about 15% of revenue and grew mid-single digits in Q2 2026 on broad strength [MS conference, 2026-09-15]:
- **Electron microscopy:** Very strong, driven by semiconductors. Casper says Thermo Fisher has leading, essential technology for developing next-generation chips and chiplets, where electron microscopes show what is happening at the atomic level. They are increasingly used in yield ramp (raising the share of good chips in production), with automation allowing near-line testing off the fab line, which speeds the ramp of a new fab. It is a long-cycle business, and "orders are far outstripping revenue." The number of new fabs being built should continue to drive strength.
- **Chemical analysis:** The smallest of the three instrument businesses, returned to growth. Safety and security spend (radiation and explosive detectors) recovered, and high commodity prices drive demand for spectroscopy instruments.
- The research and safety market channel also had a strong quarter.

## China

Casper visited China in August 2026, his second trip of the year. Thermo Fisher has been in China over 40 years, and Casper has been at the company 25 years [MS conference, 2026-09-15].
- China has been a drag on industry growth for several years. Thermo Fisher has generally performed better than others but was "flattish to down," and the business returned to growth in Q2 2026.
- He came away more positive. Innovative Chinese biotechs are investing heavily in R&D and want to work with Western companies to derisk licensing deals, since Thermo Fisher's name behind a product or trial data shows customers it works.
- He believes China can over time grow at least at the company average, if not better.
- Academic and government will remain challenged until the Chinese government changes its fiscal spending policy, but he says "I don't think that matters all that much."

## Bioproduction

Bioproduction is growing strongly, outpacing the market per the interviewer's data [MS conference, 2026-09-15].
- Casper credits a long record of share gains. Customers buy best-in-breed technologies to optimize the cost of producing medicines.
- Growth drivers: leadership in cell culture media and single-use technologies; the DynaDrive single-use bioreactor, described as a very efficient way to produce medicines; and a rapidly growing purification (resins) business, where molecule wins from 5 to 7 years ago are now becoming revenue.
- **Solventum filtration (purification and filtration business acquired from Solventum):** The one-year anniversary of the acquisition was just reached. The technology was great but commercial reach was weak, and customers wanted a trusted partner. Customer interest has been very high. It now counts in organic growth.
- Casper expects it to be a mid- to high-single-digit growth business, which is mathematically a slight drag on bioproduction organic growth but accretive to company organic growth. With a pipeline build and capacity expansion it should reach the bioproduction growth rate. It will be "highly accretive" to EPS.
- Long-term bioproduction growth is high single to low double digits in aggregate, as it was through 2019. Growth was supersized early in the pandemic, followed by an industry "hangover," and the market is now stepping up. The business is bigger with the addition and will be a more meaningful contributor to growth.

## Pharma Services

Growth was more modest in Q2 2026, but Casper expects a rebound in the second half [MS conference, 2026-09-15].
- In January the company said Pharma Services would grow modestly in the first half with a step-up in the second half. It has "the best visibility of any of our businesses" because customers indicate when they expect production. Contracts and shipment schedules support a "nice step-up" in H2 versus H1, and it is playing out as expected.
- The business has three parts: clinical trial packaging, logistics and distribution, where roughly 40% of all experimental medicines are touched by Thermo Fisher physically; drug product, mainly sterile fill finish (the final dosage form); and drug substance, where it is one of the largest producers of biologics using single-use technologies.
- Capacity has been expanded, particularly in sterile fill finish, so the business should be a "nice growth business" in 2027 and 2028.
- The interviewer suggests it benefits from reshoring. Casper agrees, saying the company won some contracts, visible in drug product and somewhat in drug substance.

## Clinical research and Clario

The clinical research business is performing very well, with authorizations growing "well in excess of our revenue," which bodes well for the trajectory [MS conference, 2026-09-15].
- **Accelerator Drug Development:** Focused on time to market. Since acquiring Patheon and PPD (five years ago), Thermo Fisher has combined them to help clients cut time out of the process. Casper explains that failing fast is cheaper and that getting successful products to market quickly lengthens the period of exclusivity. He says demand is "incredibly strong," and AI has been deployed "quite aggressively" to cut more time.
- **Clario:** One of the larger acquisitions, closed in March 2026, a $9 billion investment. It is a technology platform that generates and manages endpoints (the measurements that determine trial outcomes) in clinical trials and holds a leading position in three of the four major endpoints. Thermo Fisher's large central lab covers the fourth. The aim is a more seamless experience for sponsors and investigational sites, and to be the standard AI-enabled technology platform whether Thermo Fisher, another CRO or the sponsor runs the trial. Casper says the first six months have gone great.
- **Pipeline:** The CRO's view of early-stage innovation shows huge interest in neurology and continued oncology strength, plus adoption of new modalities. Casper stresses patient benefit.

## Capital deployment

Thermo Fisher will always focus on a very strong investment-grade credit rating, and Casper says it has plenty of financial and managerial capacity [MS conference, 2026-09-15]. The industry is fragmented, with many things to buy, and the company is very active in evaluating acquisitions. Activity in 2026 so far:
- $9 billion spent on M&A.
- $4 billion of share buybacks.
- A 10% dividend increase.
- Divestiture of the microbiology business, closed in August 2026, to redeploy capital into higher-value areas.

## AI and traditional tools spending

Asked whether pharma AI spend could crowd out traditional tools, Casper says drug-development ROI drives investment, and as AI improves ROI, customers will fuel pipelines [MS conference, 2026-09-15].
- More indications will run in parallel in clinical research, and better biological understanding will inform research spend.
- Demand is picking up in early research because of large-scale lab experimentation to populate foundational biological models; these high-throughput labs consume many reagents.
- He is "quite bullish that AI will actually be a facilitator of industry growth," and as industry leader with the most data and customer insight, Thermo Fisher views AI as an accelerator of its competitive position and "another tailwind."
