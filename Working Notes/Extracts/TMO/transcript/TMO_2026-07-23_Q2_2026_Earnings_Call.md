# Thermo Fisher Scientific (TMO) Q2 2026 Earnings Call, 23 July 2026

**Context for the reader.** Thermo Fisher (TMO) is the largest company in life-science tools: it sells instruments, lab consumables and services to drug makers, universities, hospitals and industrial customers. "Organic" revenue growth means growth excluding acquisitions, divestitures and currency effects. "Adjusted" figures exclude items such as acquisition-related charges. A "basis point" is one-hundredth of a percentage point. This call contains no commentary on Illumina, DNA sequencing or sequencing competitors; its main relevance is as a read on overall demand in life-science tools.

## Headline results

Management described the quarter as "outstanding" and said customer activity "continued to strengthen" across end markets [Q2 2026 call, Casper]. Surprises were clean on both the top and bottom lines. A Seeking Alpha banner on the transcript page states EPS of $6.03 beat by $0.32 and revenue of $11.99B (+10.49% y/y) beat by $292.04M (this is Seeking Alpha's comparison against consensus, not a statement by management).

**Versus the company's own prior guidance** [Q2 2026 call, Meyer]: revenue was about $300M ahead, including 2 percentage points stronger organic growth, a higher contribution from acquisitions and favorable foreign exchange (FX). Adjusted EPS was $0.30 ahead, driven by pull-through from the revenue beat, strong cost productivity and excellent acquisition performance, including Clario. Meyer said results were "significantly ahead of the assumptions included in our previous guidance."

**Margin drivers.** Strong productivity and favorable volume leverage (fixed costs spread over more sales) offset unfavorable product mix and funded strategic investments [Meyer].

### Organic growth by geography [Meyer]
- North America: low single digits.
- Europe: high single digits.
- Asia Pacific: high single digits, with China growing low single digits.

### Organic growth by end market [Casper]
Casper said there was "both sequential improvement and strong revenue growth across each of our end markets."
- **Pharma and biotech** (about 60% of revenue): mid-single-digit growth, led by bioproduction (making the biologic drugs customers sell), clinical research and the research and safety market channel (distribution of lab supplies).
- **Academic and government:** low-single-digit growth, driven by chromatography and mass spectrometry.
- **Industrial and applied:** mid-single-digit growth, led by electron microscopy and chemical analysis, plus the research and safety channel.
- **Diagnostics and health care:** mid-single-digit growth, driven by the health care market channel and immunodiagnostics.

### Segment results [Meyer]
The table summarizes each of the four reporting segments in Q2 2026 versus Q2 2025.

| Segment | Reported growth | Organic growth | Adj. operating income | Adj. operating margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Analytical Instruments | +7% | +7% | +30% | 23.0% (+420 bps) |

- **Life Sciences Solutions:** led by bioproduction ("another quarter of excellent organic growth"). Strong productivity was partly offset by the filtration and separation acquisition and unfavorable mix.
- **Analytical Instruments:** good growth across all three businesses, led by electron microscopy. Margin gain came from productivity, volume leverage, FX and favorable mix. (Casper described growth as high single digit.)
- **Specialty Diagnostics:** led by the health care market channel, immunodiagnostics and transplant diagnostics. Volume leverage and productivity were partly offset by mix.
- **Laboratory Products & Biopharma Services:** led by the research and safety channel and clinical research. Productivity and strong Clario performance were partly offset by mix and strategic investments.

## Cash flow, balance sheet and capital deployment [Meyer]
- Year-to-date (first half) operating cash flow was $3.3B; free cash flow (cash left after capital spending) was $2.5B after $800M net capital expenditures.
- In Q2 the company returned $1.2B to shareholders: $1B of buybacks and about $175M of dividends.
- Period-end cash was $4.1B and total debt $42.5B. Leverage was 3.6x gross debt to adjusted EBITDA and 3.3x net debt.
- Adjusted ROIC (return on invested capital) was 10.9%.

## Strategy and prepared remarks [Casper]

Thermo's growth strategy has three pillars: high-impact innovation, trusted-partner status with customers, and an "unparalleled commercial engine." It also relies on its PPI Business System (its continuous-improvement operating method), which Casper said is being accelerated by AI deployment across the company.

**Product launches.**
- At the American Society for Mass Spectrometry conference: Orbitrap Tribrid Apex mass spectrometer (multiomics, structural biology, biopharma characterization, small molecules) and Orbitrap Excedion mass spectrometer (reduces drug development risk, speeds time to market), plus AI-driven software including proteomics workflows.
- Vanquish Amplify UHPLC liquid chromatography system (less sample loss, better reproducibility), called "an important addition to our liquid chromatography offering."
- Applied Biosystems PowerFlex Thermal Cycler, a next-generation PCR platform.

**Commercial/partnership items.**
- April: opened a flagship U.S. Bioprocess Design Center in Massachusetts, a collaborative center working with pharma and biotech customers.
- Strategic collaboration with Precision Health Research, Singapore, combining Olink proteomics technology with the Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometer for a population health study.

**Acquisitions and divestiture.**
- Clario (digital endpoint data for clinical trials), closed late March: Q2 was the first full quarter, with the business delivering strongly, smooth integration and a building funnel of revenue synergies. Strategic fit strengthens the pharma/biotech partnership.
- Filtration and separation business (acquired from Solventum, formerly 3M's business): integration going well, positive customer feedback.
- Microbiology business: agreement announced late April to divest; expected to close in Q3. Anticipated net proceeds were used to repurchase $1B of shares in Q2. Casper said it "reflects our active management of the company."
- Capital deployment priorities remain strategic M&A, complemented by returning capital; Casper said there is "an active pipeline of M&A opportunities in our highly fragmented industry."

**Other.** Updated corporate social responsibility (CSR) reporting: net zero roadmap, more zero-waste certified sites, STEM programs benefiting over 185,000 students annually.

**EPS bridge (midpoint +$0.25)** [Meyer]: +$0.30 from Q2 performance, +$0.05 from a higher second-half revenue outlook, –$0.05 from the microbiology divestiture, –$0.05 from a second-half headwind due to recent FX rate changes.

**Microbiology divestiture details.** The business had $645M of 2025 revenue, with roughly a quarter selling through Thermo's own channel businesses; Thermo will retain that channel relationship and keep selling the products. With a Q3 close, it reduces 2026 revenue by about $200M (net of retained channel sales) and 2026 adjusted EPS by $0.05, and is expected to be about $0.15 dilutive to adjusted EPS in the first full year after close. Meyer said the company chose to buy back shares ahead of closing "based on an assessment of our valuation at that time."

**Phasing.** Organic growth was 3% in the first half and is expected to step up to 4% in the second half, with Q3 and Q4 similar. Q3 adjusted EPS is expected to be $0.35–$0.40 higher than Q2.

## Q&A

### Michael Ryskin, BofA Securities
*Drivers of the broad beat.* Casper said activity rose across end markets, as the company had expected it would pick up from 2025 levels during 2026. He cited continued progress in pharma, and noted biotech spending picked up and is now translating into revenue. Pharma and biotech is about 60% of revenue and "really progressing in a nice direction."

*Why Q3 and Q4 organic growth are similar* (Ryskin had expected a higher Q4 because of a days tailwind, i.e. more selling days). Meyer said the company is stepping up from 3% to 4% with a modest second-half improvement, and quarterly phasing "has really not meaningfully changed versus our original assumption." He did not address the days point or conservatism directly.

### Tycho Peterson, Jefferies
*Clinical research (PPD), Clario and Patheon.* Casper said clinical research had an excellent quarter, with strong organic revenue growth and strong authorizations (new contracted work), improving market conditions, and results largely as expected. Clario's first full quarter was very strong in authorizations, revenue growth and earnings; it does not show in organic results, and together with filtration and separation allowed the full-year acquisition contribution to be raised. "Accelerated drug development," an integrated offering spanning development, scale-up and clinical trial design, was "incredibly well received," especially by biotech, and has driven strong authorizations. Pharma services (the Patheon contract manufacturing business) had modest growth in line with expectations; the second half will be stronger based on shipment timing, and the business benefited earlier in the year from reshoring wins already in its outlook.

*Where the second-half improvement comes from.* Casper said all of the Q2 beat was embedded, plus a modest organic increase driven by pharma and biotech. Academic and government returned to growth, with the U.S. slightly positive, but the market is "going through a stabilization period," so the academic and government outlook was not changed. Sustained positive trends there would be upside over time.

### Jack Meehan, Operon Research
*Pharma/biotech spending, inventory, tariffs, reshoring.* Casper called it a "clean quarter," said the second half for pharma and biotech should be "a little better" despite the Q2 step-up, and cited broad momentum: bioproduction "a really excellent quarter," clinical research, and a very strong research and safety channel. Biotech picking up was "not surprising," consistent with the framework Meyer laid out at Investor Day: the big drivers of stepping up growth are recovery in biotech and in academic and government. Biotech's recovery showed in results; academic and government had a good quarter but is "not calling yet that that's the new level."

*Channels.* Health care market channel: Q1 had specific headwinds and Q2 was "incredibly strong"; Casper said to "take the average of the 2 quarters" as the right run-rate. Research and safety channel: driven by a strong competitive position serving pharma and biotech and by account wins (the share-gain part), with market improvement showing broadly; better academic and government helped but was not the main driver.

### Matthew Larew, William Blair
*Analytical Instruments strength.* Casper said growth was high single digit across all three businesses. Innovation was the most important driver: two mass spectrometers and AI-enabled software at the June ASMS conference, strong adoption of high-end instruments globally, especially among academic customers, and the UHPLC launch ("bodes well for the future"). His line: "irrespective of funding environments, if you have really relevant innovation, customers get money." Electron microscopy was another good quarter, with semiconductor and advanced materials strong and "very strong bookings growth."

*China.* China is about 7.5% of revenue; it grew low single digits, a return to growth, driven by pharma/biotech and industrial/applied. Academic and government in China remains "quite muted," neither worse nor improving. Casper plans more time in China in the second half; China is "not accretive to our organic growth as a company yet," but the company is positioning itself.

### Daniel Arias, Stifel
*Chemical analysis and macro-sensitive areas.* Casper said it is not a huge business but has key technologies. Two drivers: higher commodity prices raising demand from commodity-sensitive industrial customers, and increased demand for safety and security applications (largely radiation and explosive detection) given global conflict. "Market conditions are getting better."

*Pharma services phasing.* Meyer declined the assumption that Q4 will be the strongest quarter: first-half growth was low single digit, the second half "steps up meaningfully," tied to production schedules and customer campaigns, but this "doesn't necessarily imply that the fourth quarter grows over the third quarter."

### Daniel Brennan, TD Cowen
*Bioproduction above-market growth and customer delays* (Brennan noted a large peer had seen customer delays). Casper did not report any delays. He said the business is well positioned, with differentiated upstream and downstream capabilities: leadership in cell culture media and single-use technologies, a growing purification position, and filtration via the Solventum acquisition. Filtration and separation demand is strong and capacity is being increased, though it does not yet show in organic growth.

*Large pharma tone (IRA, most-favored-nation pricing, reshoring, AI).* Casper said he speaks regularly with executives, including a breakfast the day before, and the tone is positive: excitement about pipelines, focus on accelerating innovation productively, and customers with larger patent-exclusivity cliffs "really working their pipeline." Customers "have their arms around the macro" (IRA, MFNs, tariffs) and feel good about the outlook. He did not directly answer whether the guide leaves room for upside.

### Patrick Donnelly, Citi
*Academic and government cycle.* Casper said low-single-digit growth was driven most by chromatography and mass spectrometry and product launches, with strong global adoption; academics need the best tools to produce cutting-edge research. Geographically, Europe was very strong, the U.S. returned to growth, and China was flat to prior muted levels. "We're not calling a new trend based on Q2"; they want broader-based activity first. He cited good U.S. government support for academic research, and said "it's less about the headlines than it is actually about funding flow and the funding flow is improving," giving him confidence in "slow stabilization."

*Clinical research visibility and early biotech.* Casper said Thermo's biotech authorizations have been strong for a few quarters, with roughly a six-month lag from authorization to revenue. The business is performing as expected, and he expected a really good year in clinical research. Clario's endpoint data capabilities are drawing customer interest even from customers not using Thermo's CRO (contract research organization). He did not comment on the group overall.

### Luke Sergott, Barclays
*Bioprocessing competition (downstream, resins, peer pushouts).* Casper said there are "a number of fine players." Points he made: DynaDrive single-use bioreactors are becoming standardized across contract manufacturers (CDMOs) and adopted by innovative pharma, "quickly becoming the favorite technology," which bodes well for follow-on consumable sales. The resin business (used in purification) is smaller but has won many new molecules and is technology-differentiated. In filtration, the legacy 3M technology was well respected, and Thermo's commercial reach and reliable-supplier reputation are enabling many customer trials.

## Closing
Casper said the company is "on track to deliver a strong year" and is "well positioned to deliver a great 2026 and build an even brighter future for our company."
