# Barclays first take on Oxford Nanopore FY25 results: 2026 growth guide below consensus, gross margin guide above, EBITDA breakeven by 2027 reiterated

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ONT/research/20260306_Barclays_ONT-LN_Oxford_Nanopore_Technologies_PLC-_FY25_first_take-_Growt.md` · Date: 2026-03-02 · Barclays Equity Research (European Medical Technology & Services); analysts Hassan Al-Wakeel, CFA (lead, certifying analyst), Jonathon Unwin, Marco Pires-Cox, Rohit Rajan · sell-side research*

**Summary.** Barclays' same-day reaction note to Oxford Nanopore's FY25 results (published 2 March 2026). FY25 revenue was in line with the January trading update, but gross margin missed consensus. The 2026 constant-currency growth guide of 21-25% is well below the 27.5% consensus, 2027 growth is guided to be broadly similar, and the old >30% mid-term growth target is effectively dropped. Gross margin guidance of about 62% beats consensus, and adjusted EBITDA breakeven by 2027 is reiterated. Barclays stays Overweight with a 170p price target and expects the shares to be weighed on by the print.

## Overview and headline view

This is a short "first take" note from Barclays Equity Research, released at 08:29 GMT on 2 March 2026. It reacts to Oxford Nanopore Technologies PLC (ONT, listed in London) reporting its full-year 2025 (FY25) results the same day. Oxford Nanopore sells DNA/RNA sequencing instruments and the consumables (the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every run). A company analyst presentation was scheduled for 10:45am UK time.

Barclays' headline: "'26 growth guide below cons (21-25%, vs 27.5%), with '27 growth expected to be broadly similar (effectively removing mid-term growth guide). 2026 GP margin guide of 62% is ahead of cons, and adj EBITDA breakeven still expected by 2027. Expect print to weigh on shares." ("cons" is consensus, the average analyst forecast; "GP" is gross profit, revenue minus the direct cost of producing the product; "adj EBITDA" is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, adjusted for one-off items.)

## Rating, price target and valuation

- **Rating:** Overweight (Barclays' relative rating, meaning it expects the stock to outperform the average expected return of its European Medical Technology & Services coverage universe over 12 months). Industry view: Positive. No rating change is announced in this note.
- **Price target:** 170p (GBp). Price on 27 Feb 2026: 133p. Potential upside: +27.7%.
- **Price target history (Barclays):** 170p now, unchanged from 24 Nov 2025 (when the close was 124p). Earlier targets: 185p (3 Sep 2025, close 162p); 160p (5 Mar 2025, close 91p); 170p (21 Nov 2024, close 122p); 160p (24 Jun 2024, close 97p); 185p (7 Mar 2024, close 132p); 240p (17 Jan 2024, close 159p); 260p (28 Nov 2023, close 189p); 355p (16 Jun 2023, close 224p). On 2 Mar 2023 the target was 400p. All ratings in the history were Overweight.
- **Valuation method:** The target rests on about 5.7x EV/Sales for 2026 estimates (enterprise value divided by revenue), roughly a 20% premium to peers. Barclays argues a premium is warranted by the growth profile, "a now-clearer commercial strategy, improving execution and potentially significant upside to numbers given opportunities in applied markets." A discounted cash flow (DCF) cross-check, using a 7.5% discount rate (WACC) and 3% terminal growth rate (TGR), gives a similar value.
- **Risks to the target:** Poor execution on expansion, difficulty penetrating clinical markets, and increased competition.

## What Barclays expects from the share-price reaction

Barclays writes that consensus "was already expecting mid-term guidance not to be met," but that the 2026 and 2027 growth guides are lower than expected, so "we expect today's print to weigh on the shares, partially offset by improved GP outlook and the reiteration of adj EBITDA breakeven by 2027."

## FY25 performance by region

- **EMEAI (Europe, Middle East, Africa and India):** Revenue rose 26.1% to £100.4m (2024: £79.6m), driven by the UK and Europe. Growth came across all end markets, particularly Clinical and Research.
- **AMR (Americas):** Revenue rose 18.7% to £74.9m (2024: £63.2m), driven mainly by the US. The US Research environment remained uncertain, but growth was "underpinned by increasing demand in the Clinical markets."
- **APAC (Asia-Pacific):** Revenue rose 20.2% to £48.6m (2024: £40.4m), driven by a large population genomics programme in Singapore, plus higher revenue in Japan and China. The note says "China, which grew 15.2%" (the sentence is ambiguous, but it appears to give China's growth rate).

## FY26 guidance (issued 2 March 2026)

**Revenue growth:** 21-25% at constant currency, versus 27.5% consensus. This is the main negative surprise.

**By region:**
- AMR is expected to be the strongest region, driven by progress in non-Research end markets, despite a "stabilising but muted" NIH (US National Institutes of Health research funding) environment.
- EMEAI is expected to grow strongly but below FY25, because several large Research projects have completed and new projects start at different times.
- APAC demand remains solid, but growth may moderate versus FY25 because the Singapore PRECISE II programme has concluded, new projects are timing-dependent, and market challenges persist, particularly in China.

**Mix:** Growth is expected to be increasingly weighted to Applied end-markets (Clinical, BioPharma and Industrial) rather than academic Research.

**Gross margin:** About 62% for LSRT (the Life Science Research Tools business, which is the main part of the company), versus 60.9% consensus. Drivers are operational improvements (including flow-cell recycling) and tailwinds from the pricing model. (Flow cells are the consumable chips on which sequencing happens.)

**Adjusted operating expenses:** Overall adjusted cost growth of 0-5% in FY26, reflecting recent restructuring and continued focus on efficiency and cost discipline.

## Mid-term outlook: what changed

Reiterated: adjusted EBITDA breakeven by 2027 (versus consensus of a £14m loss in FY27E) and positive cash flow in FY28.

Supporting elements and changes versus prior targets:
- **Revenue growth:** Above-market constant-currency growth in FY27 "at a rate broadly similar to" FY26. This replaces the prior target of more than 30% revenue CAGR (compound annual growth rate) over FY24-FY27. Consensus was 27.5% for FY26 and 28.7% for FY27. Barclays describes this as effectively removing the mid-term growth guide.
- **Gross margin:** Further expansion in FY27 from FY26 levels. Consensus for FY27 was 62.3%, and the prior mid-term guide was more than 62% by FY27.
- **Costs:** "Continued tight cost control and operational leverage" across FY26 and FY27, compared with the prior medium-term guidance of 3-8% cost CAGR over FY24-FY27.
- **Balance sheet:** Support from a strong balance sheet, an improving cash profile and ongoing focus on working capital.

## Management commentary relayed by Barclays

After speaking with the company, Barclays reports that adjusted EBITDA breakeven by 2027 "was always the key target," and that the company will still reach it through the improved gross profit outlook and cost control, despite the lowered growth outlook.

## Estimate changes

This note does not publish Barclays' own revised estimates. It compares company results and guidance only against company-compiled consensus (February 2026), as in the table above.

## Risks and caveats

- Growth guidance is below consensus for both 2026 and 2027, and the long-held >30% CAGR target is no longer in place.
- Dependence on Research customers is a drag: NIH funding is muted, large European Research projects are ending, and the Singapore PRECISE II programme has finished.
- China remains a challenging market.
- Barclays' stated risks to its price target: execution on expansion, penetration of clinical markets, and competition.
