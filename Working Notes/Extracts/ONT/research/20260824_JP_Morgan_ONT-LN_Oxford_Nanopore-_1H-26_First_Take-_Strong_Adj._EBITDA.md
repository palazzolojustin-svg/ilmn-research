# J.P. Morgan Cazenove First Take on Oxford Nanopore 1H'26 results: Adj. EBITDA beat, diagnostics licensing deal, 2026 guidance raise, new 2030 targets

**Summary.** A same-day first reaction to Oxford Nanopore's 1H 2026 results. Revenue of £116.7m was about 2% below consensus, but the Adjusted EBITDA loss of £22.1m was 42% better than consensus because of tight cost control. ONT announced a licensing and cross-licensing deal with an undisclosed global diagnostics company, raised 2026 revenue and gross margin guidance, cut its OpEx guidance, reiterated 2027 EBITDA breakeven and 2028 cash flow breakeven, and set new 2030 targets (>$700m revenue, >15% Adj. EBITDA margin, excluding royalties). J.P. Morgan keeps an Overweight rating and calls the package "reassuring".

# J.P. Morgan Cazenove: Oxford Nanopore (ONT LN) 1H'26 First Take

## Headline view

J.P. Morgan's overall judgment [JPM, 2026-08-19]: "we see the confirmation of a significant agreement with a global diagnostics company today together with the 1H 2026 Adj. EBITDA beat, 2026 guidance raise, reiteration of 2027 Adj. EBITDA breakeven and issuance of 2030 targets as reassuring."

The main points the analysts make:
- Revenue of £116.7m for the first half of 2026 matched the trading update the company issued earlier, and the Adj. EBITDA loss of £22.1m was far better than the £38.1m consensus loss.
- 2026 revenue and gross margin guidance were raised because of the licensing agreement, and the OpEx guidance was lowered.
- The 2027 Adj. EBITDA breakeven target was reiterated. J.P. Morgan sees it as "well underpinned by the strong cost control seen so far in 2026" and as implying upside to consensus, which models a £23m Adj. EBITDA loss for 2027.
- A new 2030 target was set that excludes royalties from the new agreement. J.P. Morgan expects those royalties to be significant.

## 1H 2026 results

### Revenue

- **Total:** 1H'26 revenue was £116.7m (the "LSRT" revenue line). That was about 2% behind Company Consensus but in line with the trading update, which pointed to roughly £116.5m and 12% LC growth.
- **By end market** (all growth at constant currency for 1H'26):
  - Clinical revenue grew 37% LC, primarily from strong growth in EMEAI.
  - Biopharma grew 27% LC.
  - Applied Industrial grew 8% LC.
  - Research grew 7% LC, driven by growth in EMEAI and the Americas (AMR). This was partly offset by lower APAC revenue after the PRECISE II contract finished.
- **By region:**
  - **APAC:** Revenue fell about 8% LC. China revenue fell about 16% year on year, which the note attributes to tighter export control restrictions and changes to commercial operations in the region. The completion of the PRECISE II contract in Singapore in the prior year also weighed; it represented £3.6m of revenue.
  - **EMEAI (Europe, Middle East, India and Africa):** Revenue grew about 24% LC on continued momentum in Clinical and BioPharma and a strong Research performance. This was only partly offset by disruption in the Middle East and the roll-off of the NIHR and Genomics England projects (£4.7m of revenue).
  - **Americas:** Revenue grew 13% LC, below management's expectations. The note attributes this to the timing of customer orders and contract wins, including £0.9m of deferrals into July.

### Gross profit and margin

- Adjusted gross profit was £72.6m, in line with Company Consensus.
- The 1H'26 gross margin was 62.2%, about 101bps (basis points; 100bps = 1 percentage point) ahead of consensus.
- The gross margin rose about 400bps year on year, partly because 1H'25 included a one-off £3.3m inventory charge.

### Costs and Adj. EBITDA

- The Adj. EBITDA loss was £22.1m, 42% better than the Company Consensus loss of £38.1m and 32% better than JPMe.
- Adjusted OpEx (operating expenses) fell 7% year on year to £123m, about 9% below JPMe.
- Adjusted R&D was £36.9m, down 16% year on year and 16% (£7m) below JPMe. The note explains the £7m year-on-year fall as: £3.9m more development costs capitalised (booked as an asset rather than expensed); £2.1m lower headcount costs; and £4.8m lower materials and consultancy costs.
- Adjusted SG&A (selling, general and administrative costs) was £86.5m, down 2% year on year and 5% (£5m) below JPMe.
- Table 1 in the note compares the half with Company Consensus and JPMe, but the text of the table was garbled in the source. It shows SG&A of about £86.4m against £91.0m (a 5.1% difference), and R&D of £36.9m against £44.1m. Its other figures are unreadable and are not reproduced here.

### Cash

ONT finished the period with cash and other liquid investments of £234.5m.

## Licensing / cross-licensing agreement

- ONT announced a new agreement with an undisclosed global diagnostics company. The note calls it a "global cross-licensing agreement".
- ONT expects to recognise a **$20m licensing fee in 2H'26** (about £15m). The note also gives a further **$15m of product purchases over 2027–28**.
- ONT is also entitled to a **net royalty in the low-to-mid single digits** on revenues from life sciences and diagnostics products that incorporate the licensed intellectual property, for the life of the patent. The note describes the royalties as tiered on product sales.
- J.P. Morgan describes it as "a significant agreement" and expects the royalties to be a significant contributor to ONT's revenue and Adj. EBITDA by 2030.

## 2026 guidance

The table below sets out the changes. Revenue growth is at constant currency.

| Item | New | Previous |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth (LC) | 23–27% | 21–25% |
| Gross margin | 64% | 62% |
| Adjusted OpEx | 2% decline to stable | 0–5% growth |

- **Revenue:** The raise follows the agreement, which includes the $20m (£15m) fee in 2026. J.P. Morgan applies the 25% midpoint with a 2% FX headwind and gets 2026 revenue of £274m, about 4% ahead of Company Consensus.
- **Gross margin:** The raise reflects the contribution from the $20m licensing fee. It implies gross profit of £175.5m at the midpoint, about 7% ahead of consensus. J.P. Morgan treats the underlying 2026 gross margin as 62%.
- **Adjusted OpEx:** Guidance was cut "given the strong cost control seen in 1H 2026".
- J.P. Morgan's base for 2026 excluding the licensing fee is £259m of revenue.

## Medium-term targets

### 2027 Adj. EBITDA breakeven (reiterated)

ONT reiterated its target for Adj. EBITDA breakeven in 2027. Company Consensus has a £23m Adj. EBITDA loss, so J.P. Morgan sees upside. Its reasoning:
- **Revenue:** Mid-teens organic growth on the £259m 2026 base (excluding the £15m fee) gives £292.2m. J.P. Morgan adds an estimated $10m (£7.4m–7.5m) of product purchase revenue from the diagnostics contract in 2027, for 2027 revenue of about £299.6m (rounded to £300m elsewhere). That is about 5% below Company Consensus of £316.9m, which implies about 20% growth. The figure excludes any royalties.
- **Gross profit:** J.P. Morgan continues to expect a 64.0% gross margin in 2027, 200bps above the underlying 2026 margin of 62%. That implies gross profit of £192m, about 4% below Company Consensus.
- **Adjusted OpEx:** J.P. Morgan expects the strong cost control to continue, and a 2% decline in Adjusted OpEx in both 2026 and 2027 is, in its view, enough to reach Adj. EBITDA breakeven. It expects depreciation and amortisation to rise to £67m in 2027 from a JPMe of £58m in 2026.

### 2028 cash flow

ONT reiterated its target to become cash flow positive in 2028.

### New 2030 targets

- ONT targets organic 2030 revenue of more than $700m (more than £520m at GBPUSD 1.35) and an Adj. EBITDA margin above 15%.
- The targets exclude royalties from the new licensing agreement and any upside from additional business development opportunities.
- J.P. Morgan's own estimate is royalties of at least $50m by 2030. Taking $710m of organic revenue plus $50m of royalties gives $760m, or £560m of 2030 revenue, about 4% below the Bloomberg consensus.
- Starting from a 15.1% organic margin and assuming 100% drop-through on royalties (all royalty revenue falls to profit), J.P. Morgan calculates 2030 Adj. EBITDA of £116m, a 21% margin. That is about 3% below the Bloomberg consensus, which carries a 20.6% margin.
- J.P. Morgan concludes: "We therefore see this contract as a strong contributor towards ONT's mid-long term Revenues and profitability."
- **Longer term:** ONT outlined an ambition for more than $1bn of annual revenue.
