# Deutsche Bank: Oxford Nanopore (ONT LN) – DB UK Conference feedback

## Document overview and rating

This is a Deutsche Bank (Deutsche Numis Research) note dated 22 January 2026 by Research Analyst Kane Slutzkin, CFA. It summarizes a meeting with Nick Keher, Chief Financial Officer of Oxford Nanopore (ONT), at the dbAccess UK&I Conference. Oxford Nanopore is a UK-listed (London: ONT.L) maker of nanopore DNA/RNA sequencing devices. The sector classification is Healthcare & Medical Devices, Facilities & Services.

The overarching message, in the analyst's words, is "increasing momentum outside of core Research, driven by uptake in Clinical and Biopharma applications, and a clearer path towards profitability."

## DB view and valuation

[DB, 2026-01-22] Deutsche Bank sees ONT as "a UK champion with disruptive technology and large addressable markets to penetrate."

- Controlled costs and a shift to a capital pricing model (selling or placing instruments under a capital-style model rather than other arrangements; the note does not define it further) are described as sharpening the path to profitability and cash-flow break-even.
- A cash balance of about £300m means the company is, in DB's view, well funded to reach that point.
- Valuation: despite what DB calls superior growth, the shares trade at 4.5x EV/sales for FY26. That is a 20-30% discount to sequencing peers, which DB lists as Illumina (ILMN) at 5.5x and PacBio (PACB) at 6.3x. DB says the shares "continue to offer good value."
- "ONT remains one of our top picks in UK SMID Healthcare for 2026." (SMID = small and mid-cap companies.)

## Key takeaway 1: Diversification and high-growth segments

DB says revenue is increasingly diversified, coming from a "higher quality, less concentrated customer base." Research (66% of revenue, customers such as universities and government-funded labs) remains foundational, but DB sees a momentum shift toward higher-growth "applied" markets (Clinical, Biopharma, Industrial). Nanopore sequencing is "increasingly well validated," and applied markets "could be c.50% of group sales in time."

- **Research:** Grew 15% in FY25, driven mainly by government and academic funders. DB calls teen-percentage growth "realistic," with potential for better performance in the Americas. DB views it as a stable base.
- **Clinical:** Grew 60% in FY25. Particularly strong in infectious disease diagnostics, where rapid turnaround and long-read capability (reading long continuous stretches of DNA) are "proving highly disruptive." Key partnerships and eventual IVD launches (in vitro diagnostic products, i.e. regulated tests) signal an accelerated route to market, with products expected within 3 years (2028).
- **Biopharma:** "Poised for acceleration" as adoption in QA/QC (quality assurance/control) for mRNA, biologics and sterility testing gains traction. DB says it could contribute 20-30% of group revenue in time as evaluation phases convert and contracts materialize.
- **Industrial Applied:** Grew 27% in FY25, helped by synthetic biology and service labs.

## Key takeaway 2: Competitive differentiators – true long-read and speed

DB says ONT's small form factor, rapid turnaround, direct methylation detection (reading chemical modifications on DNA directly, without extra preparation) and ability to read "very long" DNA strands give it an advantage.

- **Illumina:** ONT differentiates clearly from Illumina's short-read limitations (Illumina reads short DNA fragments).
- **PacBio:** May have good long-read capability but is seen as lacking direct methylation and scalability for high throughput, and as requiring larger, more complex instruments.
- **Roche:** Actively developing and using a nanopore-based sequencing approach. ONT highlights Roche's limited read length and inability to do direct methylation, making it more aligned with Illumina's short-read approach. CFO Nick Keher noted that Roche's device could destabilize the short-read market on price, but ONT does not view it as directly competing with its core long-read advantages.

## Key takeaway 3: Path to profitability and cash-flow break-even

[DB, 2026-01-22] The path to profitability is sharpening, "albeit a couple years away."

- The company remains confident in achieving break-even by FY27. Consensus and DB estimates are FY28 (DB's own adjusted EBITDA turns positive in 2028E, see below).
- Support comes from a 30% CAGR target (compound annual growth rate, revenue), improving gross margins (from higher throughput and flow cell recycling; a flow cell is the consumable component that holds the nanopores), and tight cost control.
- Cash is about £300m, which DB believes is enough to reach break-even.

## Key takeaway 4: Leadership change

- As previously announced, Gordon Sanghera (founding-era CEO) will step down from the Board on 2 March 2026 and stay in an advisory capacity until early 2027.
- Nick Keher acknowledged past challenges with "launching things too early," which DB reads as a more mature, deliberate approach going forward and significant given the incoming leadership.
- Francis Van Parys was recently appointed as successor. His background is Danaher, GE Healthcare, Cytiva and currently Radiometer. DB believes he is a good fit given global experience at large companies across Biopharma and clinical diagnostics, plus regulatory experience. DB says this "bodes well" for the objective of accelerating growth in applied markets.

## Risks

The note's "Valuation & Risks" heading carries no separate risk text. Risks are only implied by the discussion: break-even timing (company FY27 versus DB/consensus FY28), the company's history of "launching things too early," the reliance on IVD launches expected by 2028 and on Biopharma evaluations converting into contracts, and Roche's potential price disruption of the short-read market.
