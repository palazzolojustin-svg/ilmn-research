# Deutsche Bank on Oxford Nanopore (ONT.L): FY25 results first take

**Source and context.** This is a short research note from Deutsche Bank (Deutsche Numis Research), authored by Kane Slutzkin, CFA, dated 2 March 2026, titled "FY25 in-line; lower FY26/7 growth; but reiteration of break-even target." It covers Oxford Nanopore Technologies PLC (London ticker ONT.L), a UK-listed maker of nanopore DNA/RNA sequencing instruments. (Nanopore sequencing reads genetic material by passing it through tiny pores and measuring changes in electrical current.) The note is filed under Healthcare & Medical Devices, Facilities & Services. The usable content is the front-page summary; the remaining pages are disclosures and boilerplate. The document does not contain a full financial model, estimate tables or a call transcript.

## Rating, price target and valuation

- **Rating:** Buy (unchanged). Deutsche Bank defines Buy as a recommendation to buy based on a 12-month view of total shareholder return (the percentage change from the current share price to the target price, plus any expected dividend yield).
- **Price target:** £225.00 (given as "GBP 225.00"; the stock page quotes prices in pence-style units, so the share price is shown as 133.10).
- **Share price:** 133.10 at 27 February 2026, against a 52-week range of 91.30 to 219.00. The target therefore implies substantial upside from the pre-results price.
- **Target history (from the disclosure chart):**
  - 5 March 2024: Buy, target £190.00, share price £110.10, analyst Paul Cuddon (no longer at Deutsche Bank).
  - 11 September 2025: Buy, target £210.00, share price £170.00, analyst Kane Slutzkin.
  - 22 January 2026: Buy, target £225.00, share price £172.90, analyst Kane Slutzkin.
  - The £225 target is therefore unchanged by this note; it was last set in January 2026, when the stock was about 30% higher than the 27 Feb 2026 price of 133.10.
- **Valuation view:** [Deutsche Bank, 2026-03-02] The shares trade on 3.2x EV/sales for FY26 (enterprise value divided by revenue, a way to price a company relative to its sales when it is not yet profitable). The analyst says this "remains good value," trading below sequencing peers despite superior growth. No explicit target-price methodology (such as DCF or multiple applied) is given in the note.

## Investment thesis

[Deutsche Bank, 2026-03-02] The "overarching message" is increasing momentum outside the company's core Research business, driven by uptake in Clinical and Biopharma applications, together with a clear path to profitability. The analyst cautions that the deceleration in guided growth "may raise doubts about the company's ability to achieve breakeven targets within the projected time frame," but still considers the shares good value.

## FY25 results (year ended December 2025)

All figures are as reported in the note, with growth at constant exchange rates (CER, which strips out currency swings so growth reflects underlying business).

- **Revenue:** Up 24% CER to £223m, described as "broad-based growth across geographies and customer end markets." The note says FY25 was "broadly in-line with Jan trading update" (the company's earlier January statement of preliminary figures).
- **Growth by end market:**
  - Clinical (hospital and healthcare uses, such as patient testing): +60%.
  - BioPharma (drug-industry customers): +30%.
  - Applied Industrial (non-academic uses such as agriculture, food and environmental testing): +27%.
  - Research (academic and government labs, the core historical market): +15%. The analyst notes this came "despite end-market funding pressures," points to improved performance in Q3 and Q4, and "in our view may point to some share gains in a soft market."
- **Adjusted gross margin:** 59.4% versus consensus of 59%. (Adjusted gross margin is the share of revenue left after the direct cost of making products, excluding some one-off items.)
- **EBITDA loss:** Narrowed to £87m versus consensus of £90.2m (the note's summary says £90m). EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, a measure of operating profitability. The analyst says the narrower loss indicates "improved profitability in H2" (the second half of 2025).
- **Cash:** Strong, at about £303m.

## Guidance and outlook

- **FY26 revenue growth guidance:** 21-25% at constant currency. Management said FY26 "started in-line with guidance expectations" [company statement, as quoted by Deutsche Bank].
- **Versus the Street:** The analyst writes that the 21-25% outlook "is likely to draw investor attention" and that Street (analyst consensus) expectations are above the top end of the range, "potentially leading to a negative market reaction." In the outlook bullet he says the Street is "at the upper end of this range" (a slightly softer phrasing than the front-page "above the top end").
- **Medium-term targets (unchanged):** Adjusted EBITDA break-even in FY27 and positive cash flow in FY28.
- **FY27 growth:** To be "broadly similar to FY26." Consensus had been forecasting mid-to-high 20s percentage growth for FY27, so this is below expectations.
- **Analyst inference:** Because growth will be lower than the Street assumed while the FY27 break-even target is held, "ONT will need to tinker with the cost side" [Deutsche Bank, 2026-03-02].

## Key risks and points of attention

- A negative share-price reaction to FY26/FY27 growth guidance sitting below consensus.
- Doubt about whether break-even can be reached on time given slower growth, which implies reliance on cost control.
- Continued funding pressure in the academic Research market (noted as a headwind in FY25, although Research still grew 15%).
