# Oxford Nanopore (ONT): Key takeaways from the 2026 J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference

## Document overview

This is a J.P. Morgan Cazenove (J.P. Morgan's UK research arm) note dated 20 January 2026, written by Zain Ebrahim (the lead analyst, European Pharmaceuticals & Biotechnology), with Richard Vosser and Sophia Graeff Buhl Nielsen listed as contacts. The note was completed on 19 January 2026 and disseminated on 20 January 2026. At the time, ONT shares closed at 151p (19 January 2026). The rating is Overweight (meaning J.P. Morgan expects the stock to outperform the average return of the analyst's coverage universe), and the price target (PT) is 190p on a June 2027 horizon.

The note summarises what Oxford Nanopore said at the 2026 J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference "last week" (the week before the note) and in investor meetings. ONT was represented by CEO Gordon Sanghera ("GS") and CFO Nick Keher ("NK"). Oxford Nanopore makes DNA/RNA sequencing devices that read genetic material by passing it through tiny protein pores (nanopores). Its devices are known for producing very long reads of DNA.

## Management takeaways from the conference

### New CEO: Francis van Parys

- Gordon Sanghera said that the new CEO, Francis van Parys, will officially take over on 2 March [JPM, 2026-01-20, citing GS at the conference].
- GS said van Parys brings significant commercialisation experience (the ability to sell and scale products). His background includes GE Healthcare, Cytiva, and most recently CEO of Radiometer, described as a multi-billion-dollar diagnostics subsidiary within Danaher.
- GS is particularly excited about the future clinical use of ONT's technology in infectious diseases, where he sees "significant applicability and runway for growth."

### Pathway to Adjusted EBITDA breakeven

Adjusted EBITDA is a profit measure that strips out interest, taxes, depreciation, amortisation and some other items. "Breakeven" means reaching zero loss on that measure.

- Nick Keher reiterated that, since setting the mid-term target, ONT's focus has been reaching Adjusted EBITDA breakeven. He said there are multiple levers to get there, given strong execution [JPM, 2026-01-20, citing NK].
- **Gross margin** (the share of revenue left after the direct cost of making products): the underlying gross margin in the first half of 2025 was 61%. This is approaching ONT's 2027 target of above 62%.
- **Revenue:** NK acknowledged headwinds in the end-market environment, particularly in Research (academic and government customers). But he sees drivers in the Applied markets (clinical, biopharma and industrial customers) that could still support the acceleration in revenue growth required to meet ONT's mid-term target of more than 30% growth over 2024-27.
- NK reiterated that Adjusted EBITDA breakeven is the "North Star" of the mid-term guidance and that the cost base can be "modulated further if required" (i.e., costs can be cut or slowed further if needed).

### Drivers of 2026 revenue growth

NK laid out the key variables for 2026 revenue growth [JPM, 2026-01-20, citing NK]:

- **Headwind: US federal-funding-exposed segment.** This segment represented 13% of revenue in 2024.
- **Headwind: China.** China is about 8% of revenue and has been hurt by continued export restrictions on trading.
- **Headwind: completion of EMEAI contracts** (contracts in Europe, Middle East, Africa and India that are finishing).
- **Offset: UK Biobank.** The ramp of the UK Biobank contract (sequencing 50,000 genomes) should offset the loss from completed EMEAI contracts.
- **Tailwind: Clinical and Biopharma.** NK is encouraged by strong Clinical and Biopharma revenue growth in 2025, particularly in the second half.
- **Americas (AMR):** AMR revenue was very strong in the second half of 2025, up about 30% year on year, despite a challenging backdrop. NK said there were changes in the US commercial team and key hires to help drive revenue in Applied markets. Applied markets now account for more than 50% of AMR revenue, which NK described as an encouraging exit rate into 2026.

### CapEx pricing model shift

ONT has moved from a leasing model (customers pay over time) to a CapEx model (customers buy larger devices outright, paying upfront).

- NK reiterated that the shift was accretive (added) to revenue by 3-4 percentage points in the first half of 2025, with a cash flow improvement of £20m.
- At the start of 2025, most sales went through the leasing model. ONT exited the year with close to 100% CapEx-model sales for larger devices.
- Consequence: the pricing-model impact should annualise in 2026 (i.e., a full year of benefit rather than a partial year), together with improved volumes. The headline of this section also states the shift has been accretive to gross margin and cash flow. (The source text is cut off mid-sentence at "improved volumes.")

### Product development expected in 2026

GS highlighted plans to improve and develop the nanopore platforms [JPM, 2026-01-20, citing GS]:

- **PromethION** (ONT's high-throughput sequencer): the plan is to sequence 4 genomes per flow cell (the consumable chip on which sequencing happens) to improve flow cell utilisation. Sequencing 2 genomes per flow cell is already in early access launch.
- **GridION Q v2** (a smaller benchtop system): in late-stage development, with R10 chemistry and RNA chemistries included, aimed at broader adoption by clinical and biomanufacturing quality control (QC) customers.
- **PromethION Q:** GS reiterated it is expected to launch at the end of 2026.

### Market share and market size

- NK said ONT gained share in the Americas, with customers placing smaller orders, leading to modest growth in the Americas research and government segment.
- GS attributed the share gains to an experienced salesforce, particularly the former head of sales from a large sequencing peer (not named in the note).
- GS cited an overall market opportunity of $20-25bn for ONT's technology, of which $13-14bn is unique to nanopore because of its differentiated platform capabilities.

### Competition: Roche SBX and Element Biosciences

- GS said ONT remains confident in its differentiation versus new entrants such as Roche's SBX (Sequencing by Expansion) technology.
- On Roche: SBX is expected to launch this year. GS noted it shares only the nanopore sensing element with ONT and is still fundamentally a sequencing-by-synthesis technology (the same broad approach used by Illumina).
- On Element Biosciences: it expects to launch an IVDR offering in 2026 (IVDR is the EU regulation for in vitro diagnostic devices), marking a potential entry into diagnostics.
- GS does not see either as a competitive risk to ONT, because both are focused on shorter reads.
- GS reiterated ONT's value proposition: richer insights, faster time to results, and broad accessibility. He said no competitor currently combines all these capabilities in one platform, leaving ONT "well positioned and highly differentiated, irrespective of recent or upcoming competitor product launches."

## Peer read-across for 2026 (J.P. Morgan's summary of other conference presentations)

Illumina and 10x Genomics are covered by J.P. Morgan US Life Sciences Tools & Diagnostics analyst Casey Woodring (PacBio is also noted as his coverage in the thesis section).

- **Illumina** (presentation Tuesday): said demand trends seen in the second half of 2025 are expected to carry into 2026, with clinical market demand continuing to be a key growth driver.
- **PacBio** (presented Monday): noted strong instrument placements in Q4 for both Revio and Vega (its long-read sequencers), despite ongoing uncertainty in the funding environment. The clinical market was its largest growth segment in 2025, growing 20%.
- **10x Genomics** (presented Monday): said overall market conditions in the first half of 2026 are expected to mirror the second half of 2025, with no major changes anticipated except for the Q4 budget flush (the year-end rush of customers spending remaining budgets).

## J.P. Morgan's investment thesis and forecasts

(These are the analyst's opinions and estimates, not company statements.)

- **2026 drivers:** operational execution on expansion into Biopharma and Clinical/Applied use of nanopore sequencing, which should raise confidence in ONT delivering strong double-digit revenue growth beyond its 2024-27 mid-term outlook.
- **Revenue forecasts:** a 2025-30 revenue CAGR (compound annual growth rate) of 27%, driven by Biopharma and Clinical expansion and growing Research use (including increasing use of methylation, a chemical modification of DNA that nanopores can detect directly). For 2026, J.P. Morgan expects more than 30% revenue growth, as academic and government funding headwinds abate and should now be reflected in the base. Biopharma/Clinical, Applied, and strong Research demand outside the US are expected to underpin acceleration.
- **Profit forecasts:** a 2026 Adjusted EBITDA loss of £56m, a £34m year-on-year improvement, driven by topline growth and 200 basis points (2 percentage points) of gross margin expansion. J.P. Morgan says this should increase confidence in the 2027 breakeven target.
- **Market focus:** J.P. Morgan expects attention to remain on the CEO search, resolution of which should lift a potential overhang on commercial strategy.
- **Relative valuation:** on J.P. Morgan forecasts, ONT trades at 3.5x 2026 EV/Sales (enterprise value divided by sales). This compares with about 5x for Illumina and about 6x for PacBio, despite ONT's superior growth: a forecast 2025-28 sales CAGR of 31% for ONT versus 5% for Illumina and 13% for PacBio.

## Rating, price target and valuation

- Rating: Overweight (unchanged; the rating history shows Overweight throughout).
- Price target: "We move to a Jun-27 PT of 190p," equal to 5.5x 2026 EV/Sales, which J.P. Morgan sees as "undemanding for a strong mid-term growth story." The note describes this as a move to a June 2027 target date; the PT history shows 190p was also the target set on 4 March 2025, so the level appears unchanged from that date's target, although the source does not state the prior PT in this note.
- Valuation method: EV/Sales multiple on 2026 forecasts.

## Risks to the rating and price target

J.P. Morgan lists these downside risks:

1. Competition from established short-read optical sequencing companies, in particular Illumina, with its very large installed base, competitive pricing and accuracy.
2. Future competition from other medium-to-longer-read sequencing companies, such as PacBio (a competing optical approach) or Roche with its SBX nanopore product, though at present SBX is short-read only and does not capture methylation.
3. Eventual competition from other long-read electrical sequencing companies when ONT's patents expire.
4. Failure to execute on margin improvement or reach profitability, either through pricing pressure (slower gross margin improvement) or higher operating expenses as the company continues to invest.
