## Overall view [JPM, 2026-05-13]

J.P. Morgan says it "continue[s] to see Roche as an indirect additional competitor to ONT." The stated reason is read length. Axelios is limited to around 1,000 bases per read (a "read" is one continuous stretch of sequenced DNA), whereas ONT can produce what the analysts call "universal reads," meaning reads of essentially any length.

On launch expectations, J.P. Morgan reports that:

- Roche refined its launch timing to **"summer 2026."**
- Initial customer feedback is supportive.
- Roche set an initial launch target of **100 instrument placements**. J.P. Morgan says this "looks modest" relative to earlier competitor launches: Revio 173, Element 112, Illumina NovaSeq X 352.
- Even allowing for possible conservatism in Roche's target, the analysts find this "reassuring" and conclude the near-term impact to ONT from the Axelios launch is "likely to be limited."

## Key takeaways

### 1. Roche's first-year target of 100 placements looks undemanding [JPM, 2026-05-13]

- Roche indicated that a **successful first year of Axelios launch would equate to 100 instrument placements** (instruments sold or installed at customers). Roche sees this as achievable given the positive customer reception so far.
- J.P. Morgan compares this with the first-year placements of three earlier launches, all in 2023:

| Launch | Company | Units placed | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revio (long-read sequencer) | PacBio (PACB) | 173 | Placed in 2023, covering 9 months of launch |
| Aviti (the note spells it "Avitiy") | Element Biosciences | 112 | 2023 launch |
| NovaSeq X | Illumina | 352 | 2023 |
| Axelios (target) | Roche | 100 | Target for first year of launch |

- The table shows that Roche's target is below all three comparison launches, lowest relative to Illumina's NovaSeq X, which is more than three times larger.
- J.P. Morgan's conclusion: the 100-unit target is "undemanding," and "even after allowing for likely conservatism" it sees "a potentially limited impact from Axelios on ONT's 2027 Revenue growth, which should be reassuring."

### 2. Roche targets "significant market share" but ONT is seen as more insulated [JPM, 2026-05-13]

- Roche is targeting "significant market share" in the sequencing market and sees Axelios as a **blockbuster peak sales opportunity** over the longer term (a blockbuster is conventionally a product with very large annual sales; the note does not give a figure).
- J.P. Morgan's view (opinion): Axelios is likely to take most of its share from **shorter-read incumbents**, and ONT's differentiated offering should "insulate against significant Axelios competition in the mid-long term."

### 3. Customer feedback is positive [Roche, 12 May 2026, as relayed by JPM]

- Roche reported positive feedback from "a few hundred customers" in the US and Europe on Axelios' **speed, throughput (data output per run), accuracy, flexibility and pricing**.
- J.P. Morgan (opinion) continues to see ONT as well positioned on accuracy, citing **99.8% accuracy using the Dorado basecalling model V5** (Dorado is ONT's software that converts raw electrical signal into DNA letters).
- ONT also has the ability to **detect native methylation** (chemical tags on DNA that affect gene activity, read directly without extra lab steps) at high accuracy, plus universal read-length capability.
- Axelios read lengths, per the note, are **400–600 bases on average and up to 1,000 bases**. J.P. Morgan says these ONT features "continue to differentiate ONT from peers."

### 4. Pricing: Roche reiterates $150 per genome [Roche, 12 May 2026, as relayed by JPM]

- Roche **reiterated** its expectation of Axelios pricing at **$150 per genome** (the consumable cost to sequence one human genome).
- Comparison points given by J.P. Morgan:
  - Illumina: **$200 per genome**.
  - Ultima Genomics: **$80–100 per genome** targeted.
  - ONT: at the higher end of pricing. Today's early-access configuration of **2 genomes per flow cell** (the consumable chip holding the pores) supports about **$345 per genome**. ONT has a roadmap to **4 genomes per flow cell**, or **below $200 per genome**.
- ONT's stated position (as relayed by J.P. Morgan): ONT expects to **maintain a premium pricing strategy** versus peers because it delivers a more complete dataset (methylation, longer reads and so on), i.e. a "fully loaded genome." The argument is that the total cost of a shorter-read platform plus a separate methylation assay would exceed ONT's price.

## Risks and caveats noted or implied

- The 100-unit target may be conservative, which J.P. Morgan acknowledges ("even after allowing for potential conservatism"); a stronger-than-targeted launch could pose more competition than J.P. Morgan assumes.
- Roche's price of $150 per genome is below ONT's current approximately $345 per genome at early-access throughput, and ONT's price falling below $200 depends on the 4-genomes-per-flow-cell roadmap, which is not yet delivered.
- Roche's stated ambition is "significant market share" and a blockbuster outcome in the longer term.
- The note's reassurance concerns near-term impact, specifically ONT's 2027 revenue growth. It does not quantify the impact.
