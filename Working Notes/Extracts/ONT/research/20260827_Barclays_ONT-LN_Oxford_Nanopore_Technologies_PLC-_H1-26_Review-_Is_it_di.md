# Barclays – Oxford Nanopore H1'26 Review: "Is it different this time?" (Overweight, PT raised to 182p)

**Summary.** Barclays reiterates Overweight on Oxford Nanopore after its H1 2026 results, which reset 2027 and medium-term expectations under new CEO Francis van Parys. The analysts argue the cut was deep enough to limit further downside after a history of downgrades, and they raise the price target 14% to 182p (from 160p) on a higher peer multiple. They see Biopharma as the biggest growth driver to 2030, helped by Moderna's positive Phase 3 melanoma news, but still expect adjusted EBITDA breakeven in 2028, not 2027 as guided.

# Barclays on Oxford Nanopore (ONT LN): H1'26 Review, "Is it different this time?"

Publication details: Barclays Equity Research, European Medical Technology & Services, 21 August 2026 (completed 20 Aug 2026, 18:15 GMT; released 21 Aug 2026, 03:00 GMT). Analysts: Jonathon Unwin (author and certifying analyst), Hassan Al-Wakeel CFA and Marco Pires-Cox. Oxford Nanopore (ONT) makes DNA/RNA sequencing instruments and the consumables (single-use reagent kits and flow cells) customers buy to run them.

## Rating, price target and headline view

- **Rating:** Overweight (unchanged). Industry view: Positive (unchanged). Overweight means Barclays expects the stock to outperform the average expected return of its European MedTech coverage universe over 12 months.
- **Price target:** 182p, raised 14% from 160p. The closing price on 20 Aug 2026 was 165p, implying +10.6% upside.
- **Scenario values:**
  - Upside case 220p. It assumes strong growth and more than £1.1bn of sales by 2030, against roughly £0.7bn in the base case.
  - Downside case 85p. It assumes a growth slowdown to roughly £0.3bn of revenue by 2030, against roughly £0.7bn in the base case.
- **Market data (Bloomberg, 20 Aug 2026):**
  - Market cap £1,604m; 975.36m shares outstanding; free float 46.95%.
  - 52-week average daily volume 1.3m shares; no dividend.
  - Return on equity (trailing twelve months) -25.63%; book value per share 43p.
  - 52-week range £0.96 to £2.16.
- **Share-price performance (comps table, pricing 19 Aug 2026):** +49.55% over one month and -21.96% over one year.

### Core thesis (Barclays, 21 Aug 2026)

The headline reads: "Cut sufficiently deep to limit downside risk and shift away from a pattern of consistent downgrades, in our view, given it was accompanied by new CEO's strategic update. Execution against new targets likely enough to drive a further rerating, with Biopharma key driver of growth."

- ONT reported H1'26 results on 19 Aug 2026 (Barclays first take: "H1 26 first take: Rebasing the midterm"). The report "was characterised by a reset of both 2027 and midterm expectations."
- Barclays frames the question explicitly: since its IPO, ONT has had "a history of consistent downgrades to expectations," so "is it different this time?"
- Barclays says it thought the last cut was the 25 March guidance cut (see "Guidance cut a clearing event?", 25 March). Yesterday's cut, it argues, "was sufficiently deep to limit further downside" and was supported by a detailed strategic review from new CEO Francis van Parys, in his first earnings call as CEO.
- Two near-term positives lift 2026 numbers:
  - Outperformance on opex (operating expense) containment.
  - $20m of licensing income from a new global diagnostics partner, at 100% gross margin because it carries no cost of goods.
- Adjusted EBITDA breakeven (adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, i.e. a proxy for operating profit) was reconfirmed by the company for 2027. Barclays reduces its 2027 loss but still sees breakeven as more likely in 2028.
- Given the downgrade history, Barclays believes that in the near term "simply achieving targets should be enough to support shares and close the discount to peers." It calls that discount unjustified given ONT's superior growth profile.
- Barclays sees further upside from Biopharma, citing Moderna's recent trial news as a positive.
- "Why Overweight?" (Barclays): it views market fundamentals as robust. It believes ONT's outlook in the Biopharma and Clinical end-markets can support an acceleration of growth over the medium term. Execution against updated targets "should drive a re-rating in the shares."

## Biopharma: the main growth driver

- Barclays sees Biopharma as "an exciting strategic area" and expects it to be the biggest contributor to growth to 2030. It contributes 770bps (7.7 percentage points) of Barclays' 19.7% average constant-currency (ccy, i.e. excluding exchange-rate effects) group growth over 2026-2030.
- Barclays had earlier flagged Biopharma as one of the areas with the most potential upside to its estimates (25 March note).
- Figure 2 shows the contributions to group ccy growth, 2026E-2030E. Only some of the values are legible in the source: 0.6%, 5.4%, 6.0%, 7.7% and the 19.7% group total. Biopharma is the largest at 7.7%, and the other three segments (Research, Clinical, Applied Industrial) account for the remainder. The source does not make clear which value belongs to which segment.

### Moderna read-across (Barclays, 20-21 Aug 2026; Moderna is covered by Eliana Merle)

- On 20 Aug 2026 Moderna (MRNA) announced that its Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial of intismeran in adjuvant melanoma (treatment given after surgery to prevent recurrence) met both endpoints at a first interim analysis. The endpoints were RFS (recurrence-free survival) and DMFS (distant metastasis-free survival).
- Intismeran is an mRNA-based cancer antigen therapy, called a "cancer vaccine" in the report.
- The data details are not yet known and are expected at a future medical meeting. The trial continues to evaluate overall survival.
- Barclays refers to its Moderna note, "Melanoma a major win in our view - where else could INT work?"
- ONT has not disclosed a contract with Moderna specifically. However, Barclays says ONT is known to have at least one Biopharma quality-control (QC) contract for mRNA vaccines, which ONT expects could drive "outsized revenue growth." Barclays says this supports its view of Biopharma as the largest growth driver to 2030.

### Notes from the CFO discussion on Biopharma QC (originally from Barclays' 25 March note, "Guidance cut a clearing event?")

- Current Biopharma contracts are each worth single-digit millions of pounds, and this "should build."
- There are two categories, mRNA vaccines and sterility testing.
- **mRNA vaccine QC** (testing that the manufactured product matches what was intended):
  - This has the potential to drive outsized revenue growth in Biopharma.
  - Current contracts, whose details were not disclosed, are for use in clinical trials of new mRNA vaccines with around 1,000 patients.
  - If the drugs are approved, ONT will be part of the patent approval process. This could lock in revenue for 20 years and lead to significant growth.
  - Each vaccine needs testing, so revenue is directly linked to the number of vaccines sold.
  - mRNA revenue comes from both the US and EMEA.
- **Sterility testing** is mainly in Europe today and is for an on-market product.
- ONT "remains the only player able to sequence native RNA," and the company commented that Roche cannot do this on its machine.
- Biopharma could still be 20% of group revenue by 2027, but Clinical has been growing quicker than the company expected.

## Valuation and estimate changes

- **Method:** The price target uses about 5.8x EV/Sales on 2026E (enterprise value divided by sales), in line with the peer average. This is up from 5.2x, reflecting the peer group's rerating, with no premium or discount to the group.
- Barclays says "We believe the company would merit a premium versus peers in light of its growth profile, but successive guidance cuts mean we risk adjust our conviction and value ONT in line with peers."
- Together with the c.2% rise in the 2026 revenue estimate, the higher multiple lifts the target from 160p to 182p.
- Barclays lowers revenue forecasts by 6-11% for 2027-2028.
- **2026 adjusted EBITDA excluding licence income and royalties:** a loss of £48.5m, versus -£55.3m previously. Barclays continues to see breakeven as more likely in 2028 than in 2027 as guided.

## Risks and valuation methodology

- **Methodology:** PT based on c.5.8x EV/Sales 2026E, in line with the peer average. Barclays believes a premium would be merited for growth, but it applies no premium because of the guidance-cut history.
- **Risks that may impede the target:** poor execution, further guidance cuts, and Biopharma contracts not materialising.
- Upside and downside cases are described above (220p and 85p).
