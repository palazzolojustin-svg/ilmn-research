# Barclays: Oxford Nanopore (ONT LN) investor trip feedback, 28 September 2026

## Headline view and rating

**Document and rating.** This is a Barclays Research note on Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT), a UK-listed company that makes DNA/RNA sequencers based on nanopores (tiny pores that read molecules as they pass through). It was written by the European Medical Technology & Services team after a 2026 MedTech & Life Sciences Investor Trip. During the trip the team visited 24 companies in 5 countries over the previous week. The note was completed 27 Sep 2026 and released 28 Sep 2026. It covers a meeting with CEO Francis Van Parys and CFO Nick Keher.

**Rating and price target.**
- Barclays rates ONT **Overweight**, meaning it expects the stock to beat the average expected total return of the analysts' coverage universe over 12 months. The industry view is **Positive**.
- The price target is **GBp 182** (pence). The closing price on 25 Sep 2026 was **GBp 182**, so the implied upside is only **+0.2%**.
- The rating was reiterated, with no change to the target in this note.

**Barclays' conclusion** [Barclays, 2026-09-28]:
- The analysts say they "walk away more constructive on the mRNA opportunity due to volume and pricing comments."
- They note ONT has a "history of downgrades", meaning past cuts to guidance. Given that, they believe that in the near term "simply achieving targets should be enough to support shares and close the discount to peers."
- They call the discount to peers "unjustified given ONT's superior growth profile" and point readers to an earlier note, "Feedback from CEO and CFO meeting, 8 Sept."

**Three key takeaways Barclays highlights:**
1. **mRNA.** List prices are $2,000-2,500 per test, but realised prices are closer to $1,000. Management said this is heavily discounted for initial customers. Recent clinical trial success de-risks demand, and clinical trial activity is more buoyant than first expected. The tests are "spec'd into" (written into) the BLA approval. A BLA (Biologics License Application) is the regulatory filing used to seek approval of a biologic drug or vaccine.
2. **Target applications and growth.** 18 focus areas represent 40-45% of the business today. The other 55-60% will still be served but is expected to grow at a mid-single-digit percentage rate (MSD%). Management considers the $700m 2030 target to rest on conservative assumptions. It also sees potential future demand from AI developers, especially for disease-prediction work where long-read sequencing could give unique insights.
3. **Adjusted EBITDA breakeven.** (Adjusted EBITDA is a profit measure before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation and certain adjustments.) More than half of group revenue now comes from PromethION, ONT's high-throughput sequencer, which is still growing rapidly. That drives a positive mix-shift effect on gross margin, and improved flow-cell recycling on PromethION also helps. (A flow cell is the consumable chip that holds the nanopores.) Commercial infrastructure and the product range are sufficient to drive growth, though incremental improvements will be made to both. R&D as a percentage of sales is to decline.

## Barclays' notes from the CEO/CFO meeting

### Strategy and growth framework
- Management began with about 50 potential sequencing applications. It assessed each for market attractiveness and for ONT's ability to realise revenue, then narrowed the focus to **18 target applications**.
- These 18 applications are about **40-45% of group revenue** today.
- The remaining **55-60%** of revenue is expected to grow at a mid-single-digit rate and is "not considered core" to the long-term target opportunity set.
- The ambition of **>$700m revenue in 2030** rests on what management calls conservative assumptions across the 18 applications.
- The target **excludes** upside from expanded partnerships, additional applications and future M&A.

### China
- China revenue **fell 16% in H1 2026**, and management expects similar trends for the rest of the year.
- China has declined from about **10% of group revenue historically to about 7% now**, and could fall further.
- Management cited both competitive and geopolitical pressures. There are **7-8 local competitors** across short-read and long-read sequencing.
- The long-term outlook includes **no meaningful China growth**, so any improvement would be upside.
- ONT believes Chinese rivals still struggle to replicate aspects of its wet chemistry (the biochemical reagents and processes). It acknowledges that this gap will likely narrow over time.

### mRNA vaccine quality control and biopharma opportunity
**Demand and adoption**
- Management is increasingly positive on mRNA quality control (QC) and believes demand has "de-risked materially" over the past 12 months.
- It first saw this as a clinical-trial market. It now sees stronger-than-expected clinical trial activity and more credible commercial adoption pathways.
- ONT supplies products into numerous mRNA vaccine clinical trial programmes. Management indicated that essentially all major players in the space are evaluating or using the technology.

**Value proposition**
- The key pitch is consolidating several QC workflows into a single sequencing-based assay.
- Existing workflows often need several orthogonal (independent) techniques, including Sanger sequencing, HPLC (high-performance liquid chromatography) and mass spectrometry.
- ONT believes nanopore sequencing can replace many of these by assessing multiple Critical Quality Attributes (CQAs) at once, including identity, integrity and purity.
- Customers can drop several legacy QC steps and sharply reduce the validation burden on manufacturing equipment.
- Management estimates the approach shortens QC timelines by roughly **3-4 weeks**.

**Regulatory status and roadmap**
- ONT is already used in BLAs for certain CQAs. Future adoption may need only limited bridging studies rather than full revalidation.
- Management sees a large long-term opportunity as more CQAs are approved and accepted by regulators.
- **CQA1** is commercially available today. **CQA2** is expected in **January 2027** and **CQA3** is targeted for **2027**.

**Pricing**
- List pricing for CQA testing is about **$2,000-2,500 per test**. Heavy early-stage discounting means realised pricing today is closer to **$1,000**.
- Customers are asking for long-term pricing agreements, including proposed **10-year fixed-price contracts**. ONT has deliberately declined these to keep future pricing flexibility.
- The company believes pricing could rise as more CQAs are validated and the strategic value of the workflow becomes clearer.

**Patient numbers**
- Management cited about **25,000 melanoma patients** as an initial addressable cohort and about **100,000 potential patients** across the broader opportunity set.
- There are five planned CQAs. Customers may not adopt all five, but adoption of **3-4 CQAs per patient** is viewed as realistic.
- ONT noted that personalised therapies need extensive patient-specific characterisation, which raises the value of its sequencing-based QC workflow.

### Product and commercial development
- The **Cepheid** product remains on track for a targeted **2027 launch**. (Cepheid is a molecular diagnostics company.)
- Management expects forthcoming product launches from the **bioMérieux (BIM)** partnership.
- Both Cepheid and bioMérieux operate in infectious disease testing and are potentially significant long-term routes to market.
- ONT believes its technology can greatly improve turnaround times in some infectious disease settings, potentially cutting workflows from days to hours.

### AI, data and population genomics
- Management believes long-read datasets will become increasingly valuable for AI-driven biological discovery. The central thesis is that richer genomic datasets create more useful AI models. Long-read sequencing, methylation data (chemical tags on DNA) and structural variant information (large rearrangements of DNA) offer capabilities short-read datasets cannot easily replicate. This matches Barclays' front-page claim that long-read provides better data for AI models than short-read.
- Management has high expectations for **UK Biobank** data releases expected later in 2026.
- ONT has fielded direct enquiries about datasets approaching **one million genomes**.
- It sees potential future demand from AI developers and organisations, particularly for disease prediction.
- Management believes future AI applications will increasingly add adjacent datasets such as spatial biology.

### Intellectual property and competitive position
- Management is confident in its patent portfolio and keeps expanding it. Individual patents last a maximum of 20 years, but management argues continual product innovation effectively refreshes protection.
- ONT recently won **favourable rulings against competition in Europe and Australia**. After the European ruling, certain competitor products are currently excluded from parts of the European market.
- Management plans to keep defending its IP vigorously and believes the rulings significantly reduce near-term "generic risk" (copycat competition).

### R&D
- Management is comfortable with current R&D spending and does not think major increases are needed.
- R&D is expected to stay **broadly flat in sterling terms over the next several years**, then grow with scale and volume. R&D as a percentage of sales will fall.
- The emphasis is on better allocation rather than higher absolute spend.
- Existing platforms are seen as sufficient, with investment going to incremental enhancements and performance improvements.

### M&A
- Management sees M&A as an important strategic lever over the medium to long term.
- Near-term focus is on partnerships, not acquisitions.
- Areas of interest include bioinformatics, sample preparation and other areas that extend ONT's core capabilities.
- Future deals are more likely to be capability-enhancing bolt-ons than transformational.

### Margins, consumables and operational leverage
- More than half of group revenue now comes from PromethION, which continues to grow rapidly.
- Management believes PromethION mix improvement can drive a meaningful gross margin uplift over time.
- A significant inventory of flow cells has already been manufactured and is held in stock.
- Further upside exists from higher flow-cell reuse rates, particularly on PromethION instruments.
- The company believes it has sufficient commercial infrastructure and can grow revenue faster than headcount, improving productivity over time.

### Other comments and catalysts
- Management expects to give a more detailed **strategy update in February** (2027).
- Possible catalysts before then: additional BLA filings and further partnership announcements.
