# Oxford Nanopore (ONT LN): J.P. Morgan Cazenove, 27 August 2026

## Headline view

J.P. Morgan Cazenove (JPM) rates Oxford Nanopore (ONT) **Overweight** (the bank expects the stock to outperform the average total return of the analyst's coverage universe over the price-target period). The note is dated 27 August 2026 (completed 26 Aug, disseminated 27 Aug). The share price on 26 Aug 2026 was 182p.

The **price target (PT) rises to 220p from 190p** and the date moves to **Dec-27 (previously Jun-27)** [JPM, 2026-08-27]. The PT is based on **5.5x 2027 EV/Sales**. EV/Sales means enterprise value (market value of the equity plus net debt) divided by annual revenue. JPM says the rise is "largely driven by the inclusion of 33p/share, primarily from the royalty stream on the diagnostics licensing agreement."

The note follows ONT's 1H 2026 results the week before (JPM published a separate take) and new CEO **Francis van Parys'** strategic update and issuance of a 2030 outlook.

JPM's one-line thesis: execution on Biopharma and Clinical, together with the transition to profitability, "should continue to support re-rating". Re-rating means the market paying a higher valuation multiple for the shares. JPM backs a strong 20% revenue compound annual growth rate (CAGR) for 2025-30.

## What changed in the forecasts

JPM cuts revenue forecasts by mid-to-high single digits for 2027-30, but makes a large upgrade to 2026 Adj. EBITDA. It remains confident that Adj. EBITDA breakeven in 2027 is achievable.

Reasons for the changes, as given by JPM:
- Revenue cuts reflect the new company outlook and JPM's updated growth assumptions across Research and Applied (see below).
- The 2026 EBITDA upgrade is large (+38%), with more modest changes beyond, because JPM stays confident that breakeven in 2027 is achievable.
- Royalty and licensing revenue from the diagnostics licensing agreement is newly added to the numbers.

## 2026 guidance and the 2027 EBITDA target "well underpinned"

JPM forecasts 2026 revenue of £275m, up 23.0% on FY25's £224m (the summary cover table shows 23.0%; the thesis text refers to 25% "LC" growth, which appears to be local or constant-currency growth). JPM expects a Adj. EBITDA loss of £29m for 2026.

2H 2026 is expected to look much better than 1H:
- JPM forecasts **36% revenue growth in 2H 2026**, a c. 24 percentage point (pp) step up on 1H 2026.
- A **13pp tailwind** comes from the **£15m ($20m) upfront payment** for the licensing agreement, recognised in 2H.
- A slightly **more favourable comparison base** in 2H, because the headwind from the **completion of the PRECISE Singapore contract** (a large national genomics programme) fell largely in 1H.
- An **increasing contribution from the UK Biobank contract** in 2H.
- **£0.9m of revenue for the Americas deferred** from 1H26 to 2H26.
- An **acceleration in Biopharma & Clinical revenues**: JPM expects total Applied (Biopharma, Clinical and Industrial) revenue growth of **35% in 2H 2026, up from 11% in 1H 2026**.
- JPM continues to forecast **Adj. EBITDA breakeven in 2027** (JPM: £4m in 2027E, 1.3% margin).

## Royalties on the licensing deal: worth c. 33p per share

- JPM estimates royalties on the diagnostics licensing agreement reach **$50m by 2030E**, and that the royalty duration is **more than 15 years**.
- JPM forecasts royalties make up **93% of the net present value (NPV) of the deal**, broadly consistent with ONT's commentary that royalties are **c. 90% of NPV**. NPV is today's value of all future cash flows. JPM's royalty model is available on request.
- The royalty stream adds c. **33p per share** to the PT.

## The 2030 mid-term outlook

ONT's 2030 target, issued with the strategic update, is revenue above **$700m** and an EBITDA margin above **15%**, both **excluding** the contribution from licensing royalties. JPM sees this as achievable, with its own ex-royalty 2030 forecasts at **£526m revenue** and a **17% EBITDA margin**. (JPM's total 2030 revenue including royalties is £563m, per Table 1.)

JPM's 2025-30 revenue CAGRs by segment:

| Segment | JPM 2025-30 revenue CAGR |
|---|---|
| Total ONT | 20% |
| Research | 8% |
| Applied (Biopharma + Clinical + Industrial) | 32% |
| Biopharma | 48% |
| Clinical | 36% |
| Applied Industrial | 8% |

Note that the thesis text also describes "Biopharma and Clinical" together as a 32% CAGR; the 32% figure is stated for Applied in the key points.

**Research.** JPM was encouraged by ONT's strategic emphasis on **structural variants** (large rearrangements of DNA), **phasing** (working out which variants sit on which copy of a chromosome) and **native methylation** (reading chemical modifications on DNA directly, without extra preparation). JPM says these are features "for which ONT's technology is particularly differentiated relative to competition."

**Biopharma.** JPM sees ONT as well positioned to participate in **mRNA quality control for personalised cancer vaccines**. It cites the **success of Moderna's (MRNA) Phase III adjuvant melanoma trial** announced the previous week as a boost to the mRNA personalised cancer vaccine opportunity.

**Clinical.** JPM was encouraged by the **expanded Cepheid relationship on infectious diseases** and by the **Myome agreement**.

JPM says Biopharma and Clinical growth drivers are "increasingly coming into view."

## Investment thesis (as stated by JPM)

- In 2026, ONT's performance should be driven by operational execution on expansion into Biopharma and Clinical/Applied use of nanopore sequencing, improving confidence in the 20% revenue CAGR for 2025-30.
- JPM forecasts a 2026 Adj. EBITDA loss of £29m, which should raise confidence in the 2027 breakeven target.

## Valuation

- **PT 220p at Dec-27, 5.5x 2027 EV/Sales.** JPM calls this "undemanding for a strong mid-term growth story."
- ONT currently trades on **4.5x 2027 EV/Sales**, versus **Illumina at c. 7x** and **PacBio at c. 5x**. JPM argues this discount is unjustified given ONT's superior growth: a 2025-30 sales CAGR of **20% for ONT versus 8% for Illumina and 12% for PacBio** (JPM forecasts).
- Coverage note: Illumina is covered by JPM US Life Sciences and Tools analyst Casey Woodring; Moderna by JPM US Biotech analyst Jessica Fye.

## Risks to the rating and price target

1. Competition from established short-read optical sequencing companies, particularly **Illumina**, with its very large installed base, competitive pricing and accuracy.
2. Future competition from other medium-to-long-read companies, such as **PacBio** (competing optical approach) or **Roche** with its **SBX nanopore product**, "albeit at present this is just short read and doesn't capture methylation."
3. Eventual competition from other long-read **electrical sequencing** companies when ONT's patents expire.
4. Failure to execute on margin improvement or reaching profitability, through pricing pressure (slower gross margin improvement) or higher operating expenses as ONT keeps investing.
