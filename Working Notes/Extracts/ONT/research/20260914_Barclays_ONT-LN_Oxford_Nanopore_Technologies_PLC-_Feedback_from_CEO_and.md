# Barclays note on Oxford Nanopore (ONT) after meeting with CEO and CFO: gross margin, mRNA vaccine QC, cost base, strategy and licensing

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ONT/research/20260914_Barclays_ONT-LN_Oxford_Nanopore_Technologies_PLC-_Feedback_from_CEO_and.md` · Date: 2026-09-08 · Barclays Equity Research (European Medical Technology & Services); analysts Jonathon Unwin, Hassan Al-Wakeel CFA, Marco Pires-Cox, Rohit Rajan; meeting with ONT CEO Frances van Parys and CFO Nick Keher · sell-side research*

**Summary.** Barclays reports on a meeting with Oxford Nanopore's CEO and CFO after the H1 2026 results. Management said it needs only about 200 basis points of gross margin expansion in 2027 (not 400) to reach adjusted EBITDA breakeven, expects a steady-state mix of 75-80% consumables, sees mRNA vaccine quality control as a moat because ONT reads RNA directly, and does not expect a large rise in costs. Barclays reiterates Overweight with a GBp 182 price target (GBp 161 share price on 4 Sep 2026, +12.8% upside).

## Overview of the document

This is a short Barclays Research note on Oxford Nanopore Technologies PLC (ONT, listed in London). Oxford Nanopore makes DNA and RNA sequencing instruments and the consumables (single-use kits and flow cells) that run on them. The note was completed on 7 September 2026 and released on 8 September 2026. It follows ONT's first-half 2026 (H1'26) results in August, which Barclays reviewed in a separate note titled "H1'26 Review: Is it different this time?". After those results, the Barclays team met CEO Frances van Parys and CFO Nick Keher. This note records what they said, with some Barclays commentary mixed in. Barclays does not give new estimates in this note.

Wording tags used below: [Barclays, 2026-09-08] marks the analysts' own views. [Mgmt meeting, Sep 2026] marks views the note attributes to the CEO and CFO.

## Rating, price target and valuation

- **Rating:** Overweight (the stock is expected to beat the average expected total return of Barclays' European medtech coverage over 12 months). Barclays reiterates this rating. Industry view: Positive. [Barclays, 2026-09-08]
- **Price target:** GBp 182 (pence), against a closing price of GBp 161 on 4 Sep 2026. That implies upside of 12.8%. (Price source: Bloomberg.)
- **Thesis in brief:** Because ONT has a history of guidance downgrades, Barclays believes that in the near term simply hitting its targets should be enough to support the shares and close ONT's discount to peers. Barclays calls that discount "unjustified given ONT's superior growth profile."
- **Valuation method:** The target rests on about 5.8x EV/Sales 2026E (enterprise value divided by estimated 2026 sales), in line with the peer average. Barclays says ONT would merit a premium to peers because of its growth profile. However, "successive guidance cuts mean we risk adjust our conviction and value ONT in line with peers."
- **Risks to the target:** Poor execution, further guidance cuts, and Biopharma contracts not materialising.
- **Estimate changes:** None are stated in this note.

## Three headline takeaways (summary at the top of the note)

The note's opening paragraph says gross margin expansion of about 200 basis points (bps; 100 bps equals 1 percentage point) would be enough for adjusted EBITDA breakeven in 2027, that the steady-state mix is more likely 75-80% consumables rather than 60%, that mRNA vaccine quality control needs direct RNA reading (which is the competitive moat), and that the new CEO sees no high additional costs. The three takeaways are:

1. **Gross margin.** [Mgmt meeting, Sep 2026]
   - ONT could see a similar level of gross margin expansion in 2027 as in 2026, but it does not need 400 bps of expansion to reach adjusted EBITDA breakeven (profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, with some items adjusted out). At about 200 bps, management would "feel comfortable." More than 200 bps is possible.
   - Biopharma gross margin will be much higher than the rest of the business.
   - Clinical is described as "a volume game with competitive pricing."
   - Consumables mix is likely to land around 75-80% of revenue, perhaps by 2028. Management had previously expected a 60:40 split of consumables to devices (instruments).
2. **mRNA vaccine quality control (QC).**
   - ONT's differentiation is its ability to read RNA directly, not the fact that it is a long-read sequencing technology (reading long continuous stretches of genetic material).
   - Measuring RNA directly lets customers see modifications, degradation and purity. Other technologies make a copy of the RNA, which "loses some of the detail necessary to ensure the vaccine is correct."
   - QC is performed on every batch, and one batch represents one patient (this is the personalised vaccine case). A patient may receive multiple doses, but all come from the same batch.
   - The list price of the test is $2,000. It is "fair to assume this is heavily discounted," but there is an opportunity to improve pricing over time.
3. **Cost base.**
   - The CEO does not see a need for increased costs. Key hires have been added to the C-suite. R&D is "more about product enhancement rather than new platforms." The sales force needs further specialisation, but that change is "more incremental."

## Detailed notes from the meeting

### mRNA vaccine QC
The note repeats the points above: direct reading of RNA is the differentiator; it reveals modifications, degradation and purity; copy-based methods lose detail; QC is per batch and each batch equals one patient; the list price is $2,000, probably heavily discounted, with room to raise pricing over time. [Mgmt meeting, Sep 2026; analyst wording "we can assume this is heavily discounted"]

### CEO views
- The CEO joined because they were excited by the technology, and this "has turned out to be justified."
- They have been impressed by the talent in the business, although many people have been at ONT for a long time and "haven't necessarily seen best-practice from other organisations."
- They are close to closing all gaps in the executive management team.
- Focus had been "a bit diffused" and led by what the platform can do or what is scientifically possible, rather than by what actually creates value. The aim now is to focus on the most value-adding applications.

### Prioritisation process
- ONT identified 47 applicable areas for its technology and narrowed them to 18.
- Of the 18, two are Research, eight are Biopharma (five in QC and three in development), and the remainder (eight) are Clinical. The note says "the rest are clinical"; the count of eight follows from the numbers given.
- Criteria used: market size and barriers to entry, reimbursement, readiness, and how quickly ONT can realise value.
- Management spoke to many customers and feels comfortable with the level of granularity of the work.
- More detail will be provided at the full-year results.

### Gross margin
- ONT does not need 400 bps of gross margin expansion in 2027 to reach adjusted EBITDA breakeven. About 200 bps would leave management comfortable.
- The Biopharma business will carry a higher gross margin.
- Management had thought a steady-state mix might be 60:40 consumables to devices, but it is likely to be closer to 75-80% consumables, perhaps by 2028. (Consumables are recurring and generally higher margin than instruments, so a higher consumables share supports margins; the note does not spell this out.)

### Costs
- Management does not think a lot of additional cost is needed in the short to medium term.
- ONT is "well on track to breakeven."
- Management does not expect much need for additional headcount.

### R&D
- Management sees product enhancement, not new platforms or many new consumables.
- Work will build out workflows and some areas of bioinformatics (software for analysing sequence data).

### Sales force
- The sales team is currently likely to be generalist and heavily focused on research and academia.
- It needs to focus more on Clinical and Biopharma customers, but management sees this as "more of a refinement."

### Competition in Clinical
- ONT sees Roche as "somewhat complementary."
- ONT wins on real-time results and rich insights. Counting applications (where the task is simply to count molecules) are not a focus.

### Clinical go-to-market
- Few global diagnostics companies can license ONT's technology, so "there won't be loads" of such agreements.
- For infectious disease, partnerships such as those with bioMérieux and Cepheid will be important, because these need IVD CE-Mark tests (in-vitro diagnostic tests approved for sale in Europe).

### Recently announced licensing agreement
- It is a cross-licensing agreement, so ONT also gets the benefit of the partner's patents.
- The $15 million of committed product purchases in 2027 and 2028 are not to be sold on to a third party. They are for the partner's internal use.
- About 90% of the agreement's value, on a net present value (NPV) basis, is in the royalties. The partner is not named in this note.

### Patents and competition
- ONT has a wide range of patents, from about 6-7 years remaining to close to 20 years. Each new nanopore (the tiny protein pore through which DNA or RNA passes to be read) brings a new patent.
- Some elements of the manufacturing process, in the wet chemistry part, are deliberately not patented, because patenting would make the process public. To date, competitors are "not close to getting the process correct," so there is no need to patent and show them how. Management will patent when competitors get close.
- Chinese competitors price at a 30% discount.
- In Australia, a competitor "agreed to infringement." (The note says "competition agreed to infringement"; the competitor is not named.)
