# Deutsche Bank note on Oxford Nanopore after the H1 2026 trading update: Buy kept, target price cut to 195p

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ONT/research/20260727_Deutsche_Bank_ONT-LN_Oxford_Nanopore_Technologies_PLC-_Post_H1_trading_update.md` · Date: 2026-07-20 · Deutsche Bank (Deutsche Numis Research), analyst Kane Slutzkin, CFA · sell-side research*

**Summary.** Deutsche Bank keeps its Buy rating on Oxford Nanopore but cuts its target price from 225p to 195p (-13.3%) after an H1 2026 trading update it calls softer than expected. FY26 guidance of 21-25% constant-currency revenue growth was maintained, but its quality is lower because it leans on H2 collaboration and licensing revenue, some of it non-recurring. The analyst argues the medium-term case is intact because of a low valuation (3.3x FY26e EV/Sales), strong Clinical and BioPharma growth, and early signs that US NIH research funding is stabilising. Revenue estimates fall 5%, 12% and 14% for FY26, FY27 and FY28, mostly reflecting the March 2026 guidance downgrade.

- [DB, 2026-07-20] The target price comes from a blended valuation. One part is a DCF (discounted cash flow, which values a company on its future cash flows brought back to today), using a WACC (discount rate, the required return on capital) of 9% and a terminal growth rate (Tg, the assumed long-run growth after the forecast period) of 3.5%. The other part is an EV/Sales multiple (enterprise value divided by revenue) of 5x.
- The cut reflects "reduced growth assumptions and current sequencing peer multiples."
- Rating history from the disclosure chart: Buy with a 190p target on 3 May 2024 (price then 110.10p, analyst Paul Cuddon, no longer at DB); Buy with a 210p target on 11 Sep 2025 (price 170.00p, Kane Slutzkin); Buy with a 225p target on 22 Jan 2026 (price 172.90p, Kane Slutzkin). This note lowers it to 195p.
- DB's Buy definition: based on a 12-month view of total shareholder return (TSR, the change from current price to target price plus projected dividend yield).

## Summary of the thesis

- [DB, 2026-07-20] The H1 update was "softer than expected" and "lowers the quality of FY26 guidance," but the analyst does not believe it "fundamentally changes the medium-term investment case."
- Consensus (the average of analyst forecasts) already sits towards the lower end of FY26 guidance. It also assumes adjusted EBITDA breakeven (operating profit before depreciation, amortisation and share-based pay adjustments) one year later than management's target.
- Valuation: the shares trade at just 3.3x FY26e EV/Sales. That is a 40-50% discount to key sequencing peers despite faster growth.
- Early signs suggest NIH (US National Institutes of Health) funding trends are beginning to stabilise.
- The analyst sees "attractive medium-term upside potential notwithstanding increased near-term execution risk."
- The analyst's opinion is that the depressed share price "could also increasingly attract strategic interest" (i.e. a takeover or similar).
- Near-term sentiment may stay subdued ahead of the interim results on 19 August 2026. A strategy update from the new CEO could be a catalyst at that event.

- Applied Markets (customers outside traditional academic research, covering Clinical, BioPharma and Industrial) was the main driver. This reinforces the analyst's view that ONT's "most attractive growth opportunities lie outside traditional academic research."
- Industrial: management highlighted ongoing pricing pressure and a potentially slower growth trajectory.
- Research grew 5%, which the analyst calls "a reasonable outcome given continued pressure on academic and government funding budgets."
- Recent NIH data and channel checks (checks with industry contacts) suggest US research funding may finally be stabilising, which would reduce a key headwind.

## Guidance and estimates

- FY26 guidance of 21-25% constant-currency (CC, excluding exchange-rate effects) revenue growth was maintained.
- Quality is lower: delivery relies more heavily on H2 collaboration and licensing revenues, some expected to be non-recurring. Excluding these, the analyst says growth would be closer to 16-20%. There is downside risk if they fail to materialise.
- The analyst has "belatedly updated" the model for the March 2026 guidance downgrade and the H1 update. He awaits clarity on H2 revenue opportunities and the implications for FY27.
- DB's EBITDA estimate remains towards the lower end of management's FY26 guidance range of a £45m-£55m loss.
- Conservatism: outer-year growth is anchored at the lower end of the company's 21-25% framework.
- DB forecasts adjusted EBITDA and cash flow breakeven in FY28/29 (no change). This is behind management's FY27/28 target.

## Research end-market: stabilising

- [DB, 2026-07-20] The US Academic & Government (A&G) market "may finally be bottoming."
- NIH data: Q2 2026 disbursements rose 5% year-on-year and 3% quarter-on-quarter to $11.4bn. H1 2026 disbursements rose 3% year-on-year.
- Recent channel checks by DB US analysts support the view that spending in academic labs and government-funded institutions is improving.
- DB's understanding is that ONT has about 10-15% exposure to NIH-funded research customers. This is no longer its primary growth engine, but weak research budgets have been a multi-quarter headwind and were again evident in H1 Research performance.
- Stabilisation would remove a drag on growth and reduce downside risk heading into FY27.
- Caveats: "The improvement should not be overstated." The environment remains challenging, and a broad-based recovery in capital equipment spending is likely a 2027 event rather than a near-term catalyst. Still, it is evidence the market may be moving "from worsening to stabilising."
- The significance for ONT is "less about driving an immediate acceleration in growth and more about removing an external headwind." Combined with Clinical and BioPharma momentum, it supports a more constructive medium-term outlook than the share price reflects.
- Figure 2 (quarterly NIH disbursement, $bn, chart labels as extracted): values shown include 13.5, 12.5, 12.9, 12.1, 11.5, 10.8, 10.9, 11.5, 11.0 and 11.4. The last is the Q2 2026 figure. ## Risks and caveats

- Increased near-term execution risk.
- Guidance relies on H2 collaboration and licensing revenue, some of it non-recurring; underlying growth ex-these is 16-20% (DB).
- Industrial pricing pressure and slower growth trajectory (management, via DB).
- Research funding recovery is modest; capital equipment recovery likely a 2027 event.
- Breakeven timing: DB assumes FY28/29, a year behind management's FY27/28 target.
- Subdued near-term sentiment until interim results on 19 August 2026.
- Disclosures: DB seeks to do business with covered companies and may have a conflict of interest. DB may act as market maker/liquidity provider in ONT, and has received compensation from ONT in the past year for non-investment-banking services (disclosure items 2, 14 and 24).

## Catalysts

- Interim results on 19 August 2026, including a strategy update from the new CEO.
- Further clarity on H2 collaboration and licensing revenue and FY27 implications.
- Continued NIH funding stabilisation.
- Possible strategic (M&A) interest at the depressed share price (analyst opinion).
