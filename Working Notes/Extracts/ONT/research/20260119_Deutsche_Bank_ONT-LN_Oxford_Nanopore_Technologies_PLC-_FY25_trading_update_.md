# Deutsche Bank on Oxford Nanopore FY25 trading update (12 January 2026)

## Document basics

This is a short sell-side research note (an analyst report from a bank) from Deutsche Bank, published under the Deutsche Numis Research label on 12 January 2026. The analyst is Kane Slutzkin, CFA. The sector classification is Healthcare & Medical Devices, Facilities & Services, and the company is Oxford Nanopore Technologies PLC (London ticker ONT.L, Bloomberg ONT LN). Oxford Nanopore (ONT) makes DNA/RNA sequencing instruments that read genetic material using tiny protein pores ("nanopores"). The note reacts to ONT's FY25 trading update (a short, pre-results statement of key figures for the year, before full "Prelims", the preliminary full-year results).

The headline of the note is: "FY25 trading update - slight beat on revs, cash ahead too."

## Rating, price target and market data

- **Rating:** Buy (unchanged in this note) [Deutsche Bank, 2026-01-12].
- **Price target:** £210.00 (unchanged in this note). The rating history chart shows the target was moved to £210 on 9 November 2025 (Buy, with the share price then at £170.00, set by Kane Slutzkin). Before that, an earlier entry on 5 March 2024 shows Buy with a £190 target when the share price was £110.10, set by Paul Cuddon, who is marked as no longer at Deutsche Bank.
- **Share price:** £140.80 at 9 January 2026 (the appendix table shows the same price as "1.41" in GBp-labelled units, i.e. the same level).
- **52-week range:** £91.30 to £219.00. So the share price sat well below the top of its range at the time of the note.
- **Valuation method and level:** The analyst cites an EV/sales multiple (enterprise value divided by revenue, a way to value a company relative to its sales). The shares trade on 3.7x EV/sales for FY26. The analyst says this "remains good value," trading below sequencing peers despite superior growth. No detailed price-target derivation is given.

## The main message

The analyst's headline view: "With shares unduly punished in H2-25, we think the slight beat on revenues, broad-based growth and strong cash position should be very well received by the market." ONT "remains a top pick for us in UK SMID Healthcare in 2026" (SMID means small and mid-cap stocks). The recommendation is Buy.

## FY25 revenue (company trading update, as reported by Deutsche Bank)

- ONT expects FY25 revenue of £223m-£224m. Consensus (the average analyst forecast) was £221m, so this is a slight beat.
- That equals 24% growth in constant currency (growth with exchange-rate effects removed).
- This slightly exceeds guidance. The November update had pointed to the top end of the 20-23% growth guidance range, so 24% is just above that range.

## Growth by customer end market

The analyst describes growth as broad-based across geographies and customer end markets:

- **Clinical:** about 60% growth.
- **BioPharma:** about 30% growth.
- **Applied Industrial:** about 27% growth.
- **Research:** +15% growth, despite end-market funding pressures (pressure on academic and government research budgets). The analyst says this points to improved performance in Q3 and Q4 of 2025 and, "in our view," may point to some share gains in a soft market. This is the analyst's opinion, not a company statement.
- **PromethION:** delivered more than 40% growth. (PromethION is ONT's high-throughput sequencer line; the note does not define it further.)

## Regulatory news

Oxford Nanopore's GridION Dx, its first In Vitro Diagnostic (IVD) device (a regulated device for diagnostic testing on samples taken from the body), has received CE and UKCA regulatory approval (the European and UK product-conformity marks). This allows its use in clinical settings across the UK and Europe.

## Cash and working capital

- Cash is strong at about £300m, ahead of consensus of £276m. The analyst says this "should be taken positively."
- The cash performance was helped by improving working capital (the cash tied up in day-to-day operations such as inventory and receivables).
- The analyst says "the move to the capex model is certainly helping, and we believe more benefits are likely to flow through in 2026." (The note does not explain the capex model; it appears to refer to a shift in how instruments are provided to customers, but this is not spelled out.)

## Outlook and estimates

- **Company outlook:** No steer on 2026 was given; the company will wait for Prelims (full-year results).
- **Mid-term guidance:** The analyst acknowledges that "some may need convincing on the acceleration in growth needed to meet the mid-term guide." (The specific mid-term target is not stated in the note.)
- **Profitability focus:** The analyst says "the main goal is profitability," and that if expectations around EBITDA break-even (EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a proxy for operating profit) by 2027 had changed, management would notify the market. In the analyst's reading, the absence of such a notice implies the 2027 break-even target is intact.
- **Deutsche Bank forecasts:**

The following compares the analyst's FY26 expectations with consensus, where given.

| Item | Deutsche Bank | Consensus |
|---|---|---|
| FY26 revenue growth | 25% | 28% |
| FY26 EBITDA loss | narrowing to about £60m | not given |

The analyst's FY26 growth forecast is therefore below the consensus level. No old-to-new estimate changes are described in this note.

## Valuation and risk commentary

The thesis rests on four points: a modest revenue beat, growth across all end markets, a stronger-than-expected cash position, and a depressed share price after a weak second half of 2025. The analyst believes the shares are cheap relative to sequencing peers on EV/sales despite faster growth. The risks the note explicitly flags are that some investors need convincing that growth can accelerate enough to hit mid-term guidance, and persistent funding pressure in the Research end market. No other risks are listed. No channel checks or survey data are included.
