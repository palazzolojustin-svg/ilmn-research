## Rating, price target and valuation (Barclays view)

- **Rating:** Overweight on ONT (Barclays defines this as expected to outperform the average expected total return of its industry coverage universe over 12 months). Barclays' industry view for European Medical Technology & Services is Positive.
- **Price and target:** The share price on 11 March 2026 was GBp 121 (pence). The price target is GBp 170, which implies potential upside of +40.6%.
- **Valuation method:** The target rests on about 5.7x EV/Sales for 2026E. EV/Sales is enterprise value divided by revenue, a way of valuing a company relative to its sales. This is about a 20% premium to peers. Barclays says the premium is justified by ONT's growth profile, a "now-clearer commercial strategy," improving execution, and potentially significant upside to numbers from opportunities in "applied markets" (the non-research uses of sequencing, such as clinical and biopharma). A discounted cash flow (DCF, a valuation from projected future cash flows) with a 7.5% WACC (discount rate) and 3% terminal growth rate (TGR) is used as a sense-check and gives a similar valuation.
- **Risks to the target (Barclays):** poor execution on expansion, difficulty penetrating clinical markets, and increased competition.

## Headline from Barclays

Barclays' headline summary: the purchasing model is expected to be a roughly 100-200bps (basis points; 100bps = 1 percentage point) benefit to gross margin (GM) in 2026. Contracts in EMEAI (Europe, Middle East, Africa and India) should "net off, but not grow," with a timing difference expected. China export restrictions are a low-single-digit-million-dollar backlog that is "getting worse not better." Potential internal execution improvements in China make it "APAC's wildcard for '26."

## Key takeaways from the fireside chat

### Purchasing model change

ONT changed its purchasing model, meaning how customers buy devices and consumables (the flow cells and kits needed for each run). The points below are from management's comments, as summarized by Barclays.

- **Past benefit:** The benefit to gross margin was +350bps in the first half of 2025 and +400bps for full-year 2025. The impact on the top line (revenue) was similar in size.
- **Timing:** The benefit "should all be felt by end of 2026."
- **Source of the benefit:** ONT now sells devices and consumables at a higher gross margin. This is partly offset by a mix shift toward more devices, which carry lower margin than consumables.
- **2026 outlook:** The gross margin benefit for 2026 could be 100-200bps.

### Contracts

- **EMEAI:** About $10m of contracts are rolling off. These are offset by the UK Biobank contract of about $10m in 2026 (the full contract is low-teens millions of dollars). The two net off, so there is no growth. There may be timing differences as UK Biobank ramps through the year, while the other contracts are a first-half 2026 headwind.
- **APAC (Asia-Pacific):** The PRECISE contract rolling off is a $3m headwind in H1 2026. The contract was $6m in total, split roughly 50:50 between H2 2024 and H1 2025. APAC will look "a little weaker" in H1 as a result.

### China

- China is a possible wild card for APAC growth in 2026. It is 44% of APAC revenue and grew at a teens percentage rate in 2025.
- Management believes it can execute better in China.
- Export restrictions have created a backlog of flow cell sales in the low-single-digit millions of dollars. The note says this is "getting worse not better."

### Elysion (automation product)

- ONT has stopped developing and selling Elysion. Management realised ONT was not the best provider of this, and that other providers could help customers build their own automation.
- It would have needed significant further development costs and SG&A (selling, general and administrative) expenses.
- The 2025 guidance was de-risked by this decision. It was dropped during ONT's prioritisation exercise.

### Biopharma

- The ViruSure contract announcement has limited financial impact because of its small size.
- ONT works with labs, and also directly with CDMOs (contract development and manufacturing organisations, which make drugs for pharma companies) and pharma companies.
- Pharma and CDMOs are not working together: pharma wants to see whether it can do more in-house, while CDMOs want to offer the capability to everyone.

### Clinical

- Most clinical revenue is from LDTs (laboratory-developed tests, run in a single lab rather than sold as approved kits).
- Management sees these moving from second-line to first-line tests in certain cases.
- ONT's "right-to-win" is greatest in rare disease, infectious disease and where methylation (chemical tags on DNA) is used.
- Reimbursement in the US is improving adoption in rare disease.

### Methylation

- Methylation can serve as a predictive marker of whether an organ is going to fail after transplant.
- It can be used to tell whether cancer is coming back or becoming active.

### Competition

- **Roche:** "Technology is very different – ONT doesn't need to take a copy." (Management view, as summarized by Barclays.)
- **Illumina:** The TruPath assay is "still short reads and stitching them together," and a separate test is needed for methylation. (Management view, as summarized by Barclays.)
