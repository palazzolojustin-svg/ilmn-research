## Core thesis in this note

Deutsche Bank's view is that Oxford Nanopore delivered "a material H1 beat on adjusted EBITDA losses" (adjusted EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, adjusted for one-off items; a loss means the company is still spending more than it earns). It also delivered improved operating-cost guidance and confirmation of a previously flagged H2 licensing opportunity. The analyst argues these together give "greater visibility on H2 delivery and the FY26 outlook" and strengthen "confidence in the path to FY27 adjusted EBITDA breakeven."

A central point is the gap between consensus and management guidance. FY26 consensus assumes an adjusted EBITDA loss of about £62m (Deutsche Bank's own estimate, "DBe", is £56m). Management's guidance is a loss of £45m to £55m (the source text renders this as "£4555m", which reads as £45–55m). Consensus is therefore "materially worse than management's … guidance, suggesting some scope for upgrades."

## H1 2026 results (six months ended mid-2026)

### Revenue
- H1-26 revenue was £116.7m, up 12.3% at constant currency (CC, which strips out exchange-rate effects).
- This was in line with the company's July update, so there was no revenue surprise.

### Profitability
The beat was in profit rather than sales. The adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to £22.1m, compared with:
- £38.1m consensus for H1-26 (so the loss was about £16m smaller than analysts expected), and
- £38.4m in H2-25 (the prior half-year).

The analyst attributes the improvement to "gross-profit growth and disciplined cost control." (Gross profit is revenue minus the direct cost of producing what was sold.)

### Cash
- Cash at the end of H1-26 was £234.5m, down from £302.8m at FY25 (a fall of about £68m).
- The analyst says the decline "largely reflect[s] operating cash outflow and a seasonal working-capital outflow" (cash tied up in inventory and receivables at that time of year).
- Cash conversion (how much of profit turns into cash) is "expected to improve materially in H2."

## Outlook and guidance

- **Underlying FY26 guidance is unchanged.** The note does not restate the revenue guidance figures.
- **H2 licensing deal confirmed.** The "previously flagged H2 licensing opportunity" is now confirmed as a $20m fee that will be recognised at 100% gross margin, so all of it drops into gross profit with no associated cost of sales. The note says the fee is nonrecurring and contributes roughly £15m to FY26 revenue.
- **Revenue consensus and implied H2 growth.** FY26 revenue consensus is about £264m. The analyst says this implies mid-20s percentage growth in H2, but roughly £15m of that comes from the one-off licensing fee, so underlying growth is lower.
- **Operating-cost guidance improved.** Operating costs are now guided to a 2% decline to flat year on year, versus growth of 0–5% previously. The analyst says this supports the FY27 adjusted EBITDA breakeven target.
- **FY26 adjusted EBITDA loss guidance:** £45–55m (management), versus consensus about £62m and DBe £56m. These compare as follows.

| Measure | FY26 adjusted EBITDA loss |
|---|---|
| Management guidance | £45–55m |
| Deutsche Bank estimate | £56m |
| Consensus | about £62m |

Because both the DB estimate and consensus sit at or beyond the worse end of guidance, the analyst sees room for upgrades.

## Strategic review and 2030 targets

The company's strategic review introduces new 2030 targets:
- Revenue above $700m, which the analyst converts to about £515m.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin above 15%.

Deutsche Bank's reading:
- The revenue target implies a compound annual growth rate (CAGR, the steady yearly growth rate needed to get from one number to another) of about 18% from the underlying FY26 consensus revenue base of about £264m.
- The analyst calls this "a more measured medium-term growth framework than in prior years."
- The analyst is cautious about estimates this far out, but notes the Bloomberg consensus for 2030 is £584m revenue and £120m adjusted EBITDA. Both are above the new targets (£584m versus about £515m revenue; £120m EBITDA is roughly 20.5% of £584m, versus the >15% margin target). This is a calculation from the note's figures, not a statement made in it.
- The 12pm company presentation is flagged as important for the assumptions behind these targets.
