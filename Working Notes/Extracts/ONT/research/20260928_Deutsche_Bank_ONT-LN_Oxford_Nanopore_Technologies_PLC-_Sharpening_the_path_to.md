- **Rating:** Buy, retained.
- **Price target (TP):** 210p, raised 7.7% from 195p.
- **Share price:** 174.40p at 18 September 2026. The 52-week range is 104.50p to 183.00p. (The disclosure page lists the price as 1.74 GBP, which is the same figure expressed in pounds.)
- **Rating history (from the report's chart):** 3 May 2024 Buy, TP 190p (price 110.10p, analyst Paul Cuddon, since departed). 11 Sep 2025 Buy, TP 210p (price 170.00p, Slutzkin). 22 Jan 2026 Buy, TP 225p (price 172.90p). 20 Jul 2026 Buy, TP 195p (price 110.00p). ## Summary of the thesis

Deutsche Bank says ONT's recent interim results and strategy update raise its confidence in the path to profitability and in the credibility of the medium-term framework. Points raised in the note's opening summary:

- The new CEO's focus on selected high-value applications is called "a welcome shift towards greater commercial discipline".
- Converting customer interest and device placements into recurring consumables revenue (the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for each sequencing run) "remains the key execution test".
- Underlying FY26 guidance was unchanged, with good H2 order coverage.
- The $20m licensing fee, lower cost guidance and further margin progress strengthen confidence in FY27 adjusted EBITDA breakeven. Adjusted EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, with some items such as share-based pay stripped out.
- Medium-term targets are "more measured than in the past, with upside excluded": more than $700m revenue and a more than 15% adjusted EBITDA margin by 2030.
- Growth should start in the mid-teens in FY27 and accelerate as BioPharma and Clinical gain weight, implying a mid-to-high-teens CAGR (compound annual growth rate) from the underlying FY26 base.
- Deutsche Bank's FY30 revenue forecast is broadly consistent with, but modestly below, management's ambition. It sees scope for margins to exceed 15% and move toward mature sequencing-company levels over time.
- The framework "appears more credible than previous targets". Future royalties and business development are potential upside. The balance sheet should fund the company through breakeven and into positive free cash flow (FCF).

## Company profile (as described by DB)

- ONT develops nanopore-based sequencing (reading DNA or RNA by passing it through tiny pores) for information-rich, rapid, accessible and scalable analysis of DNA and RNA. The platform is also being developed for proteins and metabolites.
- Its intellectual property comprises more than 2,500 active patents across more than 250 patent families.
- Headquarters and primary manufacturing are in the UK. It sells through direct sales teams and distributors in North America, Europe and Asia-Pacific.
- **Revenue by end market, H1-26:**
  - Research: 65%. These are mostly scientists studying fundamental biology or developing methods.
  - Applied Markets: 35%, made up of Clinical 15%, Industrial 12% and BioPharma 8%.
- The report's charts also show revenue by type (consumables 71%, devices and services 29%, as best read from the figure) and by region (Americas about 33%, EMEAI about 48%, APAC about 19%). These chart readings are approximate.

## Investment case

DB frames the new strategy as an attempt to close the gap between what the platform can do and the commercial value captured, by concentrating investment on applications where richer biological information, speed and flexible deployment improve customer outcomes.

- **Differentiated technology with a selective right to win.** ONT combines native DNA and RNA sequencing, long and ultra-long reads, methylation (chemical tags on DNA) detection, real-time analysis and flexible device formats. DB sees its strongest position where these capabilities change the biological or clinical answer, not in commoditised high-throughput sequencing. A selective strategy should help turn technology leadership into "complete, adoptable workflows".
- **BioPharma and Clinical provide the acceleration.** Research remains the largest market and should keep growing, but BioPharma and Clinical are expected to deliver most of the acceleration to the >$700m 2030 target. BioPharma is the nearer-term opportunity because validated quality-control (QC) workflows can become recurring and transferable across molecules. Clinical is the larger, longer-term opportunity but needs evidence, reimbursement, regulation and lab integration.
- **Path to profitable growth and cash breakeven.**
  - Adjusted EBITDA losses have narrowed materially. Management still targets breakeven in FY27 and positive FCF in FY28.
  - The levers are underlying revenue growth, gross-margin improvement and operating leverage (costs growing slower than revenue).
  - Management indicated consumables gross margins are already about 75%, with potential above 80%. Devices & Services gross margins are about 34%, with a longer-term path towards about 40%.
  - The licensing agreement adds high-margin upside but "is not required" to deliver breakeven.
  - Management expects net cash to remain above about £100m through breakeven, reducing financing risk. The balance sheet also gives flexibility for partnerships or selective M&A.
- **Superior growth at a peer discount.** Consensus expects about 17% sales CAGR for FY25-28. ONT trades at a discount to direct sequencing comparators because it is not yet profitable and carries more execution risk. DB expects delivery on strategy, consumables pull-through and breakeven to support a re-rating.
- **Key debate: commercial conversion.** DB says "The central risk is not whether ONT's technology is differentiated, but whether the company can translate that differentiation into scaled, recurring revenue." Progress should be judged on consumables utilisation, conversion of BioPharma evaluations, clinical workflow validation and recurring adoption, not on device placements or technical milestones alone.

## Medium-term targets (management framework, as reported by DB)

- **Targets:** more than $700m of revenue and more than 15% adjusted EBITDA margin by 2030, plus positive and growing FCF from FY28. Organic constant-currency (CC) growth is expected to start in the mid-teens in FY27 and accelerate as mix shifts to BioPharma and Clinical.
- **Market size:** a serviceable market of about $14-16bn across priority applications: about $4.1-5.5bn BioPharma, about $6bn selected Clinical applications and about $610-740m targeted Research applications.
- **Selective approach:** ONT will invest internally only where it has a clear right to win, and use licensing, OEM (supplying other firms' products), co-development and channel partners to speed adoption or reduce regulatory and commercial burden.
- **Growth shape (DB calculation):** the >$700m target implies a mid-to-high-teens CAGR from the underlying FY26 base, not a flat mid-teens rate. Growth must begin around mid-teens and accelerate later.
- **Mix in 2030:** BioPharma and Clinical could be about 65% of group revenue by 2030 (management indication). BioPharma gives the largest growth contribution through 2030. Clinical's full contribution likely extends beyond 2030. Research stays significant but loses weighting, and Industrial provides steady single-digit growth.
- **Margin target seen as prudent:** management sees "no structural reason" why group margins could not move towards peer levels in the 20s over the medium to long term, but considers >15% an appropriate 2030 commitment. The final margin depends on revenue, with revenue well above $700m giving more operating leverage.
- **Exclusions:** the targets exclude future business development, licensing and royalties. This includes the low-to-mid-single-digit royalty stream from the new diagnostics cross-licensing agreement, which management described as about 90% of the agreement's economic value and potentially lasting beyond a ten-year horizon. DB excludes royalties from its forecasts because it has limited visibility on the counterparty's product sales, but they could be high-margin upside.
- **DB's overall view:** the framework is more measured and credible than previous ambitions, reflecting clearer application prioritisation, a more customer-led roadmap and greater capital discipline. The scale of the strategic and organisational change still carries execution risk.
- **DB's own FY30 view:** revenue broadly consistent with but modestly below >$700m (about £500m), reflecting conservatism on BioPharma and Clinical adoption pace. It forecasts a 16% adjusted EBITDA margin in FY30 (the model table shows 17%, with the 16% likely rounding or reflecting a different basis) and believes margins can exceed 20% over time.

## End-market outlook (DB views)

- **Research:** about 65% of H1-26 revenue. Growth is currently mid-single-digit because of US research budget pressure and tough comparatives. DB expects improvement to low double digits over the medium term. Research falls to about 40% of group revenue by 2030 (the DB forecast table shows 44-45%). Latest NIH data are consistent with stabilisation, but evidence of a broad recovery is limited.
- **BioPharma:** DB's principal growth engine through 2030. ONT targets R&D and manufacturing QC workflows where one test can replace several assays with richer information. About 20 customers are evaluating the technology across mRNA, direct RNA, cell and gene therapy, and manufacturing QC. Validation can be lengthy, but once embedded in manufacturing processes or regulatory filings adoption becomes recurring and transferable across molecules.
- **Clinical:** the largest longer-term opportunity, focused on rare disease, rapid whole-genome sequencing, tumour profiling and acute infectious disease. Long reads, phasing (assigning variants to the right chromosome copy) and native methylation are differentiators, but adoption is limited by evidence, regulation, reimbursement and lab integration. DB expects strong growth through 2030, with a larger inflection after 2030 as workflows move from specialist labs to broader IVD (in-vitro diagnostic, regulated test) deployment.
- **Applied Industrial:** "no longer a material driver of group acceleration". Management expects steady mid-single-digit growth across plasmid sequencing, food safety, synthetic biology and biosecurity. DB assumes mid-single-digit growth and a small share of revenue through 2030.

## US research funding: NIH data (DB analysis)

- **Disbursements:** NIH (US National Institutes of Health) monthly disbursements rose from about $3.6bn in July to about $3.9bn in August 2026, so Q3-to-date is $7.5bn (September not yet available). Prior quarters were $11.0bn in Q1 and $11.4bn in Q2 2026. Q2 was up 5% year on year and 3% quarter on quarter, which DB calls the first clearer evidence that the funding deterioration may be bottoming. Quarterly series shown in the chart include 10.8, 10.9, 11.5, 11.5, 12.5, 12.9, 12.1, 13.5 for earlier quarters (axis labels are partly illegible, so assignment to quarters is uncertain).
- **Channel checks:** DB's July channel checks noted improving order activity in consumables and selected capital equipment, with a broader capex (capital equipment) recovery more likely in 4Q26 or 2027.
- **September outlook:** Q3 is tracking in line with recent sequential levels but below a demanding prior-year comparison. September could benefit from the fiscal year-end funding cycle: separate NIH awards data show 89% of the estimated FY26 extramural grants budget was obligated by 31 August, with $3.7bn left to deploy. DB notes this is not directly comparable with the Treasury disbursement series.
- **Read-across for ONT:** no meaningful near-term acceleration in Research demand yet, but reduced risk of further deterioration. Improving NIH trends matter more as removal of a headwind than as a growth catalyst. A stable Q3 would be modestly supportive for FY27.

## Path to profitability (DB analysis of company data)

- **Adjusted EBITDA loss, first half of each year:** £61.7m in H1-24, £48.3m in H1-25, £22.1m in H1-26.
- **Gross margin:** 62.2% in H1-26, helped by pricing, manufacturing yields and Flow Cell recycling. Management expects meaningful further improvement in FY27. Consumables margins about 75% (potential above 80%); Devices & Services about 34% (towards about 40%).
- **Operating costs:** adjusted operating costs fell 7% year on year in H1-26. FY26 guidance improved to a 2% decline to flat. The year-on-year reduction should narrow towards flat in H2, reflecting prudence and selective investment, with further efficiencies in logistics, IT and software.
- **Top line:** faster BioPharma and Clinical growth plus consumables pull-through from recent device placements should drive operating leverage. Converting evaluations and placements to recurring revenue is the key requirement.
- **Licensing:** the $20m FY26 licensing fee is recognised at 100% gross margin. Management said the agreement helps but is not required for FY27 breakeven.
- **Cash:** cash conversion should improve materially in H2. Management expects net cash to stay above about £100m through breakeven.
- **Targets:** adjusted EBITDA breakeven FY27, positive FCF FY28.

## Scientific validation: publications (PubMed data)

- DB cautions that publications lag adoption: customers buy instruments, generate data for months or years, then publish. They do not measure utilisation or revenue, and commercial applied work is less likely to be published. DB reads them as evidence of scientific validation and engagement, not near-term sales.
- ONT papers rose from fewer than 100 in 2014 to almost 1,900 in 2025, with acceleration from 2020 despite a tougher research climate. PacBio's growth has been slower, and ONT now produces about twice as many papers per year as PacBio.
- Illumina is far larger but its output has broadly plateaued since 2020-21. ONT rose from about 2% of Illumina's annual publication output in 2017 to more than 30% in 2026 (2026 is year to date).
- Commercial proof will need other measures: consumables pull-through, retention, workflow validation, recurring revenue.

## Competitive landscape (DB analysis, channel checks and preliminary expert feedback)

DB says the latest Illumina and PacBio calls validate the Clinical and BioPharma opportunities ONT targets but show intensifying competition. Channel checks and preliminary expert feedback support that ONT is genuinely differentiated where long and ultra-long reads, native DNA/RNA analysis, real-time results and flexible deployment change the outcome. However, the technology is "commercially underleveraged", with accuracy, output consistency, workflow standardisation and computational requirements cited as barriers to routine adoption. DB says management acknowledged the correct weakness: adoption is below potential and development was historically driven too much by scientific possibility rather than customer need.

- **Clinical validates the strategy but raises the bar.** Illumina's growth is increasingly Clinical-led and most PacBio Revio placements went to Clinical accounts. Customers need complete, validated, standardised workflows, so ONT needs clinical evidence, regulatory readiness, lab integration and dependable output.
- **Illumina is the scale and workflow benchmark.** Its installed base and ecosystem generate durable consumables demand. Channel feedback suggests ONT is complementary to short reads in many applications, not a broad substitute. Recent P2i placements (ONT's high-throughput PromethION device) must convert into sustained utilisation.
- **PacBio is the more direct long-read threat.** Its SPRQ-Nx lowers listed HiFi cost per genome by about 30%, strengthening it in whole-genome sequencing, rare disease and large cohorts. Preliminary feedback suggests PacBio may be preferable where high consensus accuracy and predictable output matter, while ONT leads on read length, native analysis, real-time results and flexibility. PacBio's uneven consumables pull-through gives no clear evidence of rapid displacement of ONT.
- **Utilisation matters more than placements.** PacBio customers are validating new workflows and working through inventory, while ONT had strong device growth but only 2.7% consumables growth (period not stated in the note, presumably H1-26).
- **BioPharma:** about 20 customers evaluating; conversion to validated, audit-ready recurring use is central to the 2030 targets.
- **Research is difficult sector-wide.** Illumina sees caution among academic customers and PacBio flagged US academic and government uncertainty. ONT has been comparatively resilient; a broader recovery should be upside, not base case.
- **Roche (AXELIOS 1):** a credible new entrant but initially more adjacent than direct. It offers same-day whole-genome sequencing in research workflows and is positioned for future clinical use. It currently threatens high-throughput sequencing more than ONT's core long-read applications. Roche brings resources, lab relationships, clinical workflow expertise and potentially attractive economics, but commercial proof is limited. Its speed, scale and cost could create overlap in some genome workflows and add pricing pressure, and its diagnostics footprint could aid long-term Clinical adoption. Open questions: independent data quality, workflow complexity, software maturity, placements and utilisation. DB says it is important to monitor but not a reason to change medium-term assumptions.

### Relative valuation view

- ONT's 5.6x 2026E EV/Sales is broadly in line with the 5.2x broader cohort and below the 6.6x sequencing average. It is roughly in line with PacBio and at about a 30% discount to Illumina.
- DB attributes Illumina's premium to scale, profitability and mature workflow ecosystem, and ONT's discount to pre-profitability and execution risk. It argues that delivery on growth and breakeven targets should support a re-rating towards sequencing peers.
- Sequencing peers are considered the more relevant benchmark given technology, instrument-plus-consumables model and similar Research and Clinical exposure.
- Quote: "ONT offers the strongest growth profile among sequencing peers but trades broadly in line with PacBio and at a material discount to Illumina, reflecting its earlier-stage profitability and execution risk."

### DCF (205p, 12-month forward)

- Assumptions: 9% WACC (weighted average cost of capital, the discount rate) and 3.5% terminal growth. Revenue rises from £274m in FY26 to £1.2bn (£1,199m) by FY35, with adjusted EBITDA margin reaching about 20% over time (the table shows 21-22%).
- Mechanics: present value of FCF to FY35 £237m plus PV of terminal value £1,393m gives implied EV £1,630m. Adding net cash at Dec-26 of £235m gives equity value £1,864m. Divided by 970m shares this is 192p per share today, or 205p on a 12-month forward basis.
- Forecast path used (£m): revenue 274 (FY26), 300, 355, 424, 513 (FY30), 621, 751, 892, 1,043, 1,199 (FY35). Adjusted EBITDA -39, -9, 20, 51, 89, 131, 160, 191, 224, 259. Adjusted FCF -106, -56, -32, -4, 30 (FY30), 62, 85, 110, 138, 166. DB's DCF therefore shows FCF turning positive in FY30, whereas the operating FCF line in the cash-flow table turns positive in FY30 and management targets positive FCF from FY28 (a definitional difference is possible; the note does not reconcile them).
- Sensitivity: value ranges from about 155p to about 315p per share across scenarios. For example at 9.0% WACC: 181p (2.5% growth), 192p (3.0%), 205p (3.5%), 220p (4.0%), 239p (4.5%). At 8.0% WACC and 3.5% growth: 255p. At 10.0% WACC and 3.5%: 170p.
- DB notes the valuation is sensitive to commercial adoption, margin delivery and discount rate. Upside would come from stronger growth, faster Clinical/BioPharma adoption and earlier EBITDA and cash breakeven.

### EV/Sales cross-check (215p)

- 6.3x 12-month forward EV/Sales, a 60:40 blend of PacBio and Illumina, weighted to PacBio because it is a closer comparable (pre-profitable long-read company), with Illumina as the reference for a scaled platform.
- Sales 12-month forward £293m × 6.3 = EV £1,849m; plus cash £235m = equity value £2,084m; ÷ 970m shares = 215p.
- ONT's 17% sales CAGR supports a multiple above the broader group, balanced against execution risk and less mature earnings.
- Blend: 50% × 215p + 50% × 205p = 210p.

## Risks (DB)

- **Commercial execution and adoption:** slow workflow validation, weak utilisation or delayed contract conversion in BioPharma and Clinical could cut growth and operating leverage.
- **Competition and pricing:** larger rivals with lower sequencing costs, higher throughput and broader ecosystems could constrain share, pricing and consumables pull-through.
- **Research and geographic exposure:** Research is the largest market, exposed to academic and government budgets. Export controls and commercial disruption in China, Middle East geopolitical uncertainty and weaker US research spending could cause revenue volatility.
- **Clinical and BioPharma execution:** needs clinical evidence, validated workflows, lab integration, reimbursement and regulatory capability. Longer adoption cycles would hurt the 2030 target. Progress has been made in clinical collaborations, but wider adoption depends on workflow maturity and execution.
- **Profitability and cash:** slower growth, adverse mix, higher compute or manufacturing costs, or extra investment could delay profitability and increase cash burn.
- **Technology, manufacturing and quality:** must keep improving accuracy, output, reliability and cost while scaling manufacturing; delays, lower yields or supply-chain disruption could hurt adoption and margins.
- **IP, partnerships and royalties:** patent disputes, weaker protection, partner execution or lower counterparty sales could reduce licensing and royalty income. The royalty stream is excluded from guidance because the counterparty's revenue cannot yet be reliably forecast.
