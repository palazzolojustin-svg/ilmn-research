# Barclays H1 2026 first take on Oxford Nanopore: new 2030 targets rebase the midterm outlook

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ONT/research/20260825_Barclays_ONT-LN_Oxford_Nanopore_Technologies_PLC-_H1_26_first_take-_Reba.md` · Date: 2026-08-19 · Barclays Equity Research (European Medical Technology & Services); analysts Hassan Al-Wakeel, CFA, Jonathon Unwin, Marco Pires-Cox and Rohit Rajan (the certification is signed by Jonathon Unwin) · sell-side research*

**Summary.** A same-day Barclays reaction note to Oxford Nanopore's H1 2026 results. Half-year revenue was in line with the July trading update. The company set new 2030 targets of more than $700m revenue and an adjusted EBITDA margin above 15%, which Barclays calculates are about 10% and 35% below consensus. FY26 guidance was raised (helped by a one-off $20m licensing fee) and the cost guide was cut. Barclays reiterated Overweight with a GBp 160 price target, expects the 2030 reset to weigh on the shares, but sees it as a way to end a pattern of repeated downgrades to expectations.

# Barclays, "H1 26 first take: Rebasing the midterm" (Oxford Nanopore Technologies PLC, 19 August 2026)

**Document basics.** This is a first-take note from Barclays' European Medical Technology & Services team, published on 19 August 2026 at 07:09 GMT. It was written on the day Oxford Nanopore (ONT, a UK-listed maker of nanopore DNA/RNA sequencing instruments and consumables) reported its first-half 2026 results. An analyst presentation was scheduled for 12:00 UK time. Barclays says it will look for more detail on the company call. Figures are in pounds sterling (GBP) unless stated, and the company's targets are set in US dollars.

## Rating, price target and valuation

- **Rating:** Overweight (Barclays' term for expecting the stock to outperform the average of its industry coverage universe over 12 months). Industry view: Positive. The rating was reiterated. [Barclays, 2026-08-19]
- **Price target:** GBp 160 (pence), against a closing price of GBp 124 on 17 August 2026, implying about +28.9% upside. The target is unchanged from the previous one on 13 July 2026 (GBP 1.60), which had cut it from GBP 1.72 on 25 March 2026.
- **Price-target history (GBP):**
  - 28 Nov 2023: 2.60
  - 17 Jan 2024: 2.40
  - 7 Mar 2024: 1.85
  - 24 Jun 2024: 1.60
  - 21 Nov 2024: 1.70

  - Before these, on 19 Aug 2023, the rating was Overweight with a target of 3.55.
  - The closing prices on those dates ranged from about 0.91 to 1.89, and the price was 1.16 on 13 Jul 2026.
- **Valuation method:** The target rests on about 5.2x EV/Sales for 2026E. EV/Sales (enterprise value divided by revenue) is a standard way to value a company that is not yet profitable. The multiple is set equal to the peer average.
- **Why only in line with peers:** Barclays says ONT would merit a premium to peers given its growth profile. But "successive guidance cuts" mean Barclays "risk adjust[s]" its conviction and values ONT in line with peers.
- **Barclays' view on valuation:** Barclays believes "valuation does not accurately reflect the growth opportunity relative to peers."
- **Risks to the target:** Poor execution, further guidance cuts, and BioPharma contracts not materialising.

## Headline view

- **Results:** H1 26 revenue was broadly in line with the trading update ONT published in July (Barclays' earlier note: "H1'26 Trading Update Review", 14 Jul). Gross profit was 2% behind Bloomberg consensus, but in line with comments made at the trading update.
- **New 2030 targets (the "rebase"):**
  - Revenue above $700m. Barclays says this is about 10% below Bloomberg consensus of £584m / $782m at spot exchange rates.
  - Adjusted EBITDA margin above 15% (adjusted EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, excluding one-off items). Barclays says this implies about $105m of EBITDA, roughly 35% below Bloomberg consensus of $161m.
- **Implied growth rate:** The top-line target implies a CAGR (compound annual growth rate) of about 19% over 2026-2030. Barclays takes a USD base of $345-$355m for 2026, "as per company".
- **Company wording:** ONT describes "organic constant-currency revenue growth of approximately mid-teens per annum and accelerating". The 2026 base excludes the $20m one-off licensing revenue.
- **Management clarification:** Barclays says that, "speaking to the company," mid-teens is "more a base" and that acceleration is expected as higher-growth areas become a bigger share of revenue. Barclays still looks for clarification on the call.
- **Consensus comparison:** Consensus expects constant-currency revenue growth of 20.8% in 2027 and 21.9% in 2028. The mid-teens language therefore sits below those expectations.
- **Exclusions:** The new guidance excludes potential upside from the new licensing agreement and "other uncertain elements."
- **Share price reaction (Barclays' expectation, not yet observed):** Barclays "expect[s] the below-consensus expectations for 2030 to weigh on shares today."
- **Barclays' counterargument:** The market has been looking for a rebase of targets "to shift away from a mode of consistent downgrades to expectations." Barclays believes the cut "may shift the co away from consistent d/g's to expectations".

## H1 26 segment performance

- **EMEAI:** Revenue grew 23.8% at constant currency to £55.6m (Barclays expected £54.1m). Growth was driven by continued momentum in the Clinical and BioPharma end markets, plus "robust" Research performance. This came despite disruption in the Middle East and the roll-off of large research programmes.
- **AMR:** Revenue grew 12.5% at constant currency to £39.1m (Barclays expected £39.8m). Growth was broad-based across end markets despite customer order timing headwinds, contract delays and continued pressure on the US research funding environment.
- **APAC:** Revenue declined 8.4% at constant currency to £22.0m (Barclays expected £22.6m). The drivers were a 15.7% decline in China and completion of the PRECISE II programme in Singapore, partly offset by growth elsewhere in the region.

## FY26 guidance (updated)

- **Revenue growth, excluding the licensing deal:** 16-20% at constant currency, versus Barclays' 16.5%.
- **Revenue growth, including the licensing deal:** about 23-27%, versus the prior guide of 21-25% and Barclays' 21.5%.
- **The licensing deal:** A $20m licensing fee will be recognised in H2 2026 at 100% gross margin (so all of it drops to gross profit). It comes from a new global cross-licensing agreement signed after the period end.
- **Underlying assumptions:** These continue to reflect weakness in China, disruption in the Middle East and customer order timing headwinds in AMR.
- **AMR second half:** It is expected to benefit from a "materially stronger" second half, supported by the ramp-up of secured business. First-half order timing headwinds are not expected to be fully recovered by year-end.
- **Mix:** Growth is increasingly weighted towards Applied Markets, particularly Clinical and BioPharma.
- **Gross margin:** About 62% excluding the licensing deal (consensus 62.0%). Including the deal, FY26 gross margin is expected to be about 64%.
- **Adjusted operating expenses (costs):** Guidance upgraded. FY26 adjusted operating cost growth is now expected at (2)% to 0%, versus previous guidance of 0-5%.

## New 2030 targets and medium-term milestones

- **Adjusted EBITDA breakeven during FY27:** Maintained. Consensus for FY27 is −£18.6m.
- **Positive free cash flow during FY28:** Maintained.
- **2030 revenue:** More than $700m.
  - Organic constant-currency growth of approximately mid-teens per annum and accelerating, measured from a FY26 base that excludes the $20m non-recurring licensing revenue.
  - Growth to 2030 is expected to be materially weighted towards BioPharma and Clinical.
  - Research is expected to remain a significant contributor through 2030, growing more slowly than Clinical and BioPharma but from a much larger base.
  - Industrial is expected to continue to provide steady single-digit growth.
- **2030 adjusted EBITDA margin:** Above 15% by 2030, with continued progressive improvement afterwards over the longer term.

## Risks and open questions

- Barclays' stated risks to its target are poor execution, further guidance cuts, and BioPharma contracts not materialising.
- The company-stated headwinds are weakness in China, Middle East disruption, AMR order timing and contract delays, US research funding pressure, and the roll-off of large research programmes and of PRECISE II.
- Barclays flagged the exact meaning of "mid-teens ... and accelerating" as something to clarify on the call. There are no analyst Q&A in this note, since it was published before the call.
