# Barclays on Oxford Nanopore (ONT): Guidance cut as a "clearing event", estimate changes, cash-flow analysis and management feedback (Mar 2026)

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ONT/research/20260406_Barclays_ONT-LN_Oxford_Nanopore_Technologies_PLC-_Guidance_cut_a_clearin.md` · Date: 2026-03-25 · Barclays Equity Research (European Medical Technology & Services); analyst Jonathon Unwin (coverage transferred to Unwin), with Hassan Al-Wakeel, CFA and Marco Pires-Cox listed as team contacts; PacBio and US peers covered by Luke Sergott · sell-side research*

**Summary.** Barclays reiterates Overweight on Oxford Nanopore and nudges its price target up to 172 GBp (from 170 GBp) after the company cut its medium-term growth guidance to 21-25% a year for 2026 and 2027 (from a >30% CAGR for 2024-27). Barclays calls the cut a "clearing event" that removes an overhang, cuts its revenue forecasts by 4% (2026E) and 8% (2027E), raises gross margin forecasts, and still expects adjusted EBITDA breakeven in 2028 (company guides 2027). It argues ONT can fund itself without a capital raise and that the shares are undervalued versus peers on growth, and it relays CFO/management comments on contracts, China, NIH, Biopharma, Clinical and product launches.

# Barclays: Oxford Nanopore Technologies, "Guidance cut a clearing event?" (25 March 2026)

## Headline view and recommendation

[Barclays, 2026-03-25] The note was completed 24 March 2026 and released 25 March 2026. It follows ONT's H2 2025 results (the second half of fiscal 2025) and Barclays' conversations with management, including its Global Healthcare Conference (Conference Takeaways, 11 March). Coverage of ONT is transferred to Jonathon Unwin.

Barclays' core message is that ONT's cut to its growth outlook is a "clearing event" for the stock. Management now expects growth of 21-25% in 2026 and 2027, versus the earlier target of more than 30% compound annual growth (CAGR) over 2024-2027. Barclays argues that lowering the target removes an overhang (a worry weighing on the share price), partly because consensus (the average analyst forecast) was already below guidance. It believes that delivering against new, achievable targets should drive a rerating (a higher valuation multiple) of the shares.

Key points of the thesis:
- ONT still targets adjusted EBITDA breakeven in 2027. (Adjusted EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, adjusted for one-off items; it is a rough measure of operating profit.) Barclays still thinks breakeven most likely comes in 2028, but expects growth to be the dominant narrative for the stock.
- Barclays forecasts a 27% constant-currency revenue CAGR for 2025-2030E (constant currency strips out exchange-rate effects). It also forecasts a 24% reported revenue CAGR for 2025-2028E.
- The biggest opportunity for outperformance is Biopharma (sales to drug developers and manufacturers). Management has said it could reach 20% of Group sales by 2027. Barclays' risk-adjusted forecast has it at only 9% in 2027E and 13% in 2028E, so its numbers "could prove conservative".
- The updated revenue, cost and cash-flow analysis makes Barclays "incrementally more positive" on 2026 gross margin expansion, on ONT's ability to contain operating costs, and on ONT meeting its cash needs without a capital raise.
- Barclays continues to see the shares as undervalued relative to peers given ONT's much higher top-line growth potential.
- Barclays says ONT would be the fastest-growing stock in its coverage.

**Method.** The price target rests on about 5.5x EV/Sales for 2026E (enterprise value divided by sales, a way to value companies that are not yet profitable), a premium of about 35% to peers. The average 2026E EV/Sales of the covered peers is 4.0x, versus ONT at 3.3x. Barclays justifies the premium by ONT's growth profile, a "now-clearer commercial strategy", improving execution, and potential upside to numbers from opportunities in applied markets.

**Why the premium could be larger.** PacBio (Underweight, covered by Luke Sergott), ONT's closest competitor, trades on 5.5x sales despite a 9.2% 2025-27E revenue CAGR, versus ONT's 24%. Barclays says the premium should arguably be higher, but it risk-adjusts to reflect ONT's history of guidance adjustments.

**Why the target rose despite lower numbers.** Updated cash-flow analysis now implies ONT is cash positive in 2026, so despite the number cuts and a trimmed multiple the target rises to 172p from 170p.

**Rating and target history (Barclays, Overweight throughout).** Targets in £: 4.00 (Mar 2023), 3.55 (Jun 2023), 2.60 (Nov 2023), 2.40 (Jan 2024), 1.85 (Mar 2024), 1.60 (Jun 2024), 1.70 (Nov 2024), 1.60 (Mar 2025), 1.85 (Sep 2025), 1.70 (Nov 2025), now 1.72.

**Risks to the target.** Poor execution, further guidance cuts, and Biopharma contracts not materialising.

## Why 2025 growth fell short of the original target

2025 constant-currency growth was 24%. The original mid-term guide, set at the 2024 results (March 2024), assumed more than 30% growth each year. Barclays reconciles the gap as follows:
- Purchasing-model changes and price increases, decided after the original guide, added about 4 points to 2025 growth. Excluding them, "underlying" growth was about 20%, one-third below the original >30% expectation.
- A 3-point headwind from US National Institutes of Health (NIH) funding and a 1.5-point headwind from China export restrictions. Both were unknown at, or have worsened since, the original guide.
- That leaves a net 5.5-point underperformance, which management attributes partly to a slower market and partly to internal execution issues: product launches not performing as hoped (the GridION Q for Biopharma, and the now-discontinued Elysion), and management has said it could have executed better in China [Conference Takeaways, 11 Mar].

## Barclays growth outlook and mix

Barclays expects about 24% growth in 2026E (broadly in line with 2025), 25% in 2027E and 28% in 2028E as higher-growth Clinical and Biopharma end-markets become bigger parts of the mix. From 2028E it expects further acceleration as Biopharma contracts move from the clinical-trial phase into production, with ONT included on the patent as part of the production process. Barclays risk-adjusts for trial success.

End-market mix: Research falls from about 66% of revenue (2025) toward about 41% (2030E) per the chart, with Clinical and Biopharma rising. Biopharma is 9% in 2027E and 13% in 2028E in Barclays' numbers. The mix chart also shows Applied/Industrial in low-teens percentages (the labels are partly garbled in the source, so exact splits are uncertain).

## Path to adjusted EBITDA breakeven

Company guidance is breakeven in 2027, defined as £0m or above. Barclays expects it in 2028E (adjusted EBITDA +£27m, 6.1% margin). It concedes this could be a headwind to the stock given guidance, but believes top-line execution supports the shares over the medium term. Historic adjusted EBITDA losses shown include roughly -£116m (2021) down to -£87m (2025), with Barclays at -£57m (2026E), -£20m (2027E), +£27m (2028E), then about £83m (2029E) and £149m (2030E) with margins reaching about 20.7% by 2030E.

### Gross margin

Gross margin (the share of revenue left after the direct cost of making and delivering products) was 58.6% in 2025, up from 54.6% in 2022 on a bridge Barclays sets out:
- Underlying improvements excluding the purchasing model change contributed 720bps, offset by a 560bps mix headwind, giving average underlying expansion of about 60bps a year over three years.
- In 2025 there was also a -190bps headwind from foreign exchange and -150bps of one-offs. Barclays expects the one-offs to reverse in 2026.
- The purchasing model change added 400bps in 2025. Barclays expects 150bps in 2026E (company commentary: 100-200bps), plus 60bps underlying, taking 2026E gross margin to 62.1%.

The CFO has previously said PromethION flow cell improvements (higher yield and recycling) could raise PromethION gross margin by 10 percentage points as 2027 exits, which would mean a 65% Group gross margin, at which Barclays thinks the business could break even. Because management has talked about these improvements since 2021, Barclays risk-adjusts to 63.5% in 2027E and 65% in 2028E. Historic gross margins: 41.2% (2019), 49.2%, 53.3%, 54.8% (2022-ish), 57.5%, 58.6% (2025); forecasts 62.1%, 63.5%, 65.0%, then 66.0% in 2029E and 2030E.

### Operating expenses

2025 was a proof point on cost control, with adjusted operating expenses growing just 1%. Barclays expects costs to grow below revenue, driven by SG&A (sales, general and administrative) containment and more selective R&D spending. Adjusted R&D is forecast to grow about 5% a year after a 10% fall. Capitalised R&D (development costs put on the balance sheet and amortised rather than expensed) is a growing share of total R&D, with the expensed/capitalised split moving from 77/23 (2019) toward roughly 59-62% expensed and 38-41% capitalised in recent and forecast years.

## Cash requirements: no capital raise expected

Barclays believes ONT can fund itself until adjusted EBITDA breakeven without raising equity or debt. Its reasons: selling down financial assets, a lower capex outlook (driven by the new capex-first sales model), and an inventory reduction that prevents large working-capital outflows in 2026E and 2027E. It expects positive OCF by 2028E (£9m) and positive equity FCF by 2029E. Company guidance is for positive free cash flow by 2028E, defined as OCF less capex and capitalised development costs.

Cash balance chart: £181m (2025), £193m (2026E), £141m (2027E), £96m (2028E). Financial assets (separate from cash) were about £258m at their peak and are run down toward zero. Capex is about £11m, £12m and £15m in 2026E-2028E, with capex around 4-5% of sales.

## Feedback from management conversations (CFO meetings and Conference Takeaways, 11 March)

### Purchasing model change
Moving to a capex-first model (customers buy devices up front rather than getting them as part of a subscription-like opex-first arrangement). H1'25 gross-margin benefit was +350bps and full-year 2025 +400bps, with a similar top-line impact. The benefit should be fully felt by end-2026, with a 2026 gross-margin benefit of 100-200bps. The main benefit is selling devices and consumables at a higher margin, partly offset by a mix shift to devices, which have lower margins than consumables. Under the old model, device revenue was recognised over one year, in line with consumables (typically about 12 months), while device costs were recognised over three years, which should be a gross margin tailwind in 2028E. Consumable list-price increases are not the realised average selling price (ASP) increase because ONT gives volume discounts. The capex model and consumables pricing together contributed roughly 4% of 2025 revenue growth.

### Contracts
- EMEAI: $10m of contracts rolling off is offset by UK Biobank of about $10m for 2026 (the full contract is in the low-teens millions). They net off, so no growth, with possible timing differences as UK Biobank ramps and a possible H1 headwind.
- APAC: the PRECISE contract rolling off is a $3m H1'26 headwind (the contract was $6m split roughly 50:50 between H2'24 and H1'25). APAC looks a little weaker in H1 as a result.

### Gross margin, EBITDA, margins
Improved PromethION flow cell yield and recycling can lift PromethION gross margin by 10pp, translating to a 65% group margin, possibly exiting 2027E. Further cost-base modulation is possible. Management sees "no reason" why adjusted EBITDA margin cannot rise to the mid-20s over time.

### NIH and US
NIH is 50% of the Americas business. It grew 3% in 2025 against 20% expected when the mid-term guide was set, a 3% headwind to Group growth. For 2026 the NIH environment looks stable, neither growing nor declining. H2'25 was more constructive in the US than H1'25 and management expects 2026 to be more constructive still.

### China
Export restrictions were a headwind of about 1.5% to 2025 growth, driven by restrictions on NVIDIA chips plus restriction lists of who ONT can sell to. Allowing NVIDIA chips to be sold in China would not bring instant full relief. China is 44% of APAC revenue and grew at a teens percentage rate in 2025; it could be the "wild card" for APAC in 2026. Management thinks it can execute better. The backlog of flow cell sales held up by export restrictions is in low single-digit $m and is "getting worse not better".

### Product launches
Elysion and GridION Q underperformed. The Mk1D MinION "has done okay". GridION Q 2.0 is expected in Q1'26. Q-line products are for regulated environments and must be "locked down" to comply with regulation, ensuring standardisation and repeatability.

### Elysion (discontinued)
ONT stopped developing and selling it, concluding it was not the best provider of this automation, with other providers better able to help customers build their own. It would have required significant further development and SG&A costs. 2025 guidance had been de-risked from it. It was stopped during the prioritisation exercise.

### Inventory and capitalised R&D
Inventory is targeted to fall to £80-90m by 2027E before rising again. COVID-era materials agreements to guarantee supply built up stock; ONT is no longer in them and is now buying only where needed. Capitalised R&D as a share of total R&D has risen as products reach launch; it should not increase further and could fall. Everything is amortised over three years.

### Strategy
An internal strategy review led to updated total addressable market (TAM) estimates in H1'25 (previous TAMs covered all of diagnostics) and some R&D projects were stopped.

### Biopharma
- Has not grown as quickly as hoped; potentially transformational growth is now more likely in 2028 than 2027. Current contracts are single-digit millions of pounds each, which should build.
- Two categories: mRNA vaccines and sterility testing. mRNA vaccine quality-control testing could drive outsized growth. Current contracts (details undisclosed) are for clinical trials of new mRNA vaccines with about 1,000 patients. If the drugs are approved, ONT would be part of the patent approval process, which could lock in revenue for 20 years. Each vaccine batch needs testing to check that what was intended was produced, so revenue is tied to vaccines sold. mRNA revenue is US and EMEA. Sterility testing is mainly Europe today and for an on-market product.
- ONT "remains the only player able to sequence native RNA" and management commented that Roche cannot do this on its machine.
- Biopharma could still reach 20% of Group revenue by 2027, but Clinical has grown faster than expected.
- The ViruSure contract announcement has limited financial impact given its small size. ONT works with labs but also directly with contract development and manufacturing organisations (CDMOs) and pharma companies, which are not working together: pharma wants to see if it can do more in-house, while CDMOs want to offer it to everyone.

### Clinical
Most revenue is lab-developed tests (LDTs). Management sees tests moving from second-line to first-line in some cases. ONT's right to win is strongest in rare disease, infectious disease and where methylation is used. US reimbursement is improving adoption in rare disease. Methylation (chemical marks on DNA) can predict whether a transplanted organ will fail and can show whether cancer is returning or becoming active.

### Competitors
- Roche: the technology is very different; "ONT doesn't need to take a copy".
- Illumina: its TruPath assay still uses short reads that are stitched together, and a separate test is needed for methylation.

### Partnerships and FX
The amPORE TB test will not be a significant revenue generator but shows ONT can develop a regulated test (currently only LDT or research use only) with the intention of IVD (in vitro diagnostic) approval. On FX: all USD headwind drops through to earnings because there is no manufacturing in the US.

## Risks
Poor execution, further guidance cuts, and Biopharma contracts not materialising [Barclays]. Barclays also flags that EBITDA breakeven in 2028 rather than the guided 2027 could weigh on the shares, and that management has repeatedly adjusted guidance (and talked about flow cell improvements since 2021), which is why it risk-adjusts.
