## Headline view

Oxford Nanopore's H1 update was weaker than expected. Revenue was £116.5m, up 12% in constant currency (CC, meaning growth with exchange-rate effects removed). That was about 7% below Deutsche Bank's own estimate of £125m (DBe). The analyst attributes the miss to:

- disruption in China,
- disruption in the Middle East,
- slower conversion of sales opportunities in the Americas.

Excluding China and the Middle East, group revenue growth was a healthier +16% CC. Within that, three areas grew strongly:

- Clinical: +35%
- BioPharma: +25%
- EMEAI (Europe, Middle East, India and Africa region): +23% CC

## H1 2026 results in detail

### Group revenue and cash

- **Revenue:** £116.5m, +12% CC, against DBe of £125m. [Deutsche Bank, 13 Jul 2026]
- **Underlying growth:** +16% CC excluding China and the Middle East.
- **Cash:** £234.5m at the end of H1 2026, down from £302.8m at the end of FY25 (a fall of about £68m over six months; my arithmetic).

### Geographic drivers of the miss

The analyst cites weakness in:

- **China:** revenue down 16%.
- **Middle East:** revenue down 14%.
- **Americas (AMR):** some order timing issues, with opportunities converting more slowly. The analyst describes "some AMR order timing" as a factor.

### Divisional performance

- **Applied Markets** (the commercial, non-academic customer groups) remained robust at +22%.
  - **Clinical:** +35%.
  - **BioPharma:** +25%.
  - **Industrial:** +5%, which the analyst calls "disappointing". He says it "may face some ongoing challenges as prices come down."
- **Research** (mainly academic and government customers) grew 5%, which the analyst describes as "somewhat expected".

### Regional performance

- **EMEAI:** +23% CC, described as "particularly strong".
- **AMR (Americas):** +12% CC.
- **APAC (Asia-Pacific):** −8% CC, due to China and the completion of PRECISE II. (PRECISE II is not explained in the note.)

## Outlook and guidance

### FY26 revenue guidance

- Guidance is maintained at 21–25% CC growth.
- Management now highlights that reaching this range **includes additional H2 collaboration and licensing revenues, some of which are expected to be non-recurring** (one-off, not repeatable in later years).
- Excluding those opportunities, FY26 growth would be **16–20%**. Management's stated reasons for the lower figure:
  - ongoing weakness in China,
  - disruption in the Middle East,
  - some lost AMR revenue that is not expected to be fully recovered this year.

### Other guidance items

- **Gross margin** (the share of revenue left after the direct cost of making products): guidance remains c.62%.
- **Adjusted EBITDA** (a measure of operating profit before depreciation, amortisation and certain adjustments): the target of adjusted EBITDA breakeven in FY27 is unchanged.
- **FY26 EBITDA loss:** management guidance is a loss of £45m–£55m. Management "remains comfortable with current EBITDA loss expectations".
- **Cash:** management expects some timing swings in cash flow but is comfortable with consensus expectations for year-end cash of c.£180m. That compares with £234.5m at the end of H1.

## Deutsche Bank's analysis and thesis

### Quality of guidance is weaker

The analyst's central point is that although the headline guidance is unchanged, "the quality of the FY26 outlook is weaker". It leans more heavily on H2 collaboration and licensing revenues, part of which will not recur.

### What consensus implies

- Prior consensus revenue for FY26 was £272m (the average of analysts' forecasts).
- Reaching that figure would imply close to 30% growth in H2, which the analyst presents as a demanding hurdle.
- There is "clear downside risk to consensus revenue forecasts if the additional H2 opportunities fail to materialise."
- The update will also likely prompt questions about how sustainable FY27 growth expectations are, since part of the FY26 revenue is non-recurring.

### Profitability reassurance

- Management reiterated the c.62% gross margin guidance and the FY27 adjusted EBITDA breakeven target.
- Consensus EBITDA is already at the lower end of management's £45m–£55m loss guidance. The analyst reads this as meaning expectations already reflect "a relatively conservative outcome within the range." (The note does not say whether "lower end" means the smaller or the larger loss.)

### Valuation

- The shares trade on c.3.5x FY26 EV/Sales (enterprise value divided by sales, measured before the update). The analyst calls this "a meaningful discount to sequencing peers".
- No peer multiples are given.

### Upcoming catalysts

- **August interims** (the half-year results report) and a **forthcoming strategy update under new CEO Francis Van Parys** are expected to give greater clarity on H2 revenue opportunities and longer-term growth priorities.

## Estimate changes

- No numerical estimate changes are published. The analyst says: "we will need to review our estimates."
- The direction of risk is flagged as downward for consensus revenue.
- The only estimate given is DBe H1 revenue of £125m, which was missed.
