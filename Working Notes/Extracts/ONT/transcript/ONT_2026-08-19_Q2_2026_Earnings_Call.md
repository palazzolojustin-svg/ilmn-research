# Oxford Nanopore Technologies Q2/H1 2026 Earnings Call (new CEO strategy, 2030 targets, cross-licensing deal)

**Summary.** Oxford Nanopore reported first-half 2026 revenue of GBP 116.7M (+12.3% constant currency), below its own expectations because of China, the Middle East and Americas order timing, but with a 62.2% gross margin and a 54% smaller adjusted EBITDA loss. It kept core 2026 guidance (16-20% growth, ~62% gross margin) and announced a cross-licensing deal with a global diagnostics company worth a $20M upfront payment, $15M of product purchases and a long-term royalty. New CEO Van Parys presented a strategy with a >$700M organic revenue target and >15% adjusted EBITDA margin by 2030, with clinical and biopharma becoming about two-thirds of revenue.

# Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) – Q2/H1 2026 Earnings Call, 19 August 2026

## Overview

This is the first results presentation by Francis Van Parys as CEO of Oxford Nanopore (ONT), about six months after he joined. ONT sells DNA sequencers based on "nanopores" (tiny holes that read DNA as it passes through). The call combined first-half results, a same-day cross-licensing deal, and a new strategy with 2030 financial targets. Figures are in pounds (GBP) unless marked $. "Constant currency" means growth with exchange-rate effects removed. "Adjusted EBITDA" is operating profit/loss before depreciation, amortisation and certain adjustments. "Gross margin" is the share of revenue left after the direct cost of making products.

## Results and guidance

### Headline H1 2026 results [Q2 2026 call, CFO Keher unless noted]

- **Revenue:** GBP 116.7M, up 12.3% at constant currency. Keher said this was below management's expectations, as flagged in the July trading update.
- **China:** revenue down about 16% year on year, due to "enhanced export control restrictions and changes to commercial operations in the region."
- **Middle East:** revenue down about 14%, attributed to the geopolitical situation.
- **Underlying growth:** excluding China and the Middle East, group growth was about 16% at constant currency, which Keher said is "a better indication of the underlying performance."
- **Americas:** timing of customer orders and contract wins hurt H1. Management does not expect to recapture those lost revenues in H2 (though Van Parys later said some delayed Americas contracts should materialise in H2).
- **EMEAI (Europe, Middle East, Africa, India):** grew 23.8% at constant currency despite Middle East disruption and research-market headwinds.
- **Gross margin:** 62.2%, in line with guidance and up 400 basis points (4 percentage points) year on year.
- **Adjusted operating expenses:** down 7% year on year, helped by the restructuring and efficiency actions taken in 2025.
- **Adjusted EBITDA loss:** GBP 22.1M versus GBP 48.3M a year earlier, a 54% improvement. It was also about GBP 16M better than H2 2025.
- **Net cash:** GBP 234.5M (cash, equivalents and other liquid investments, no debt), down GBP 68.3M from December 2025 (Keher also said "roughly GBP 70 million"). He attributed this to normal working-capital seasonality plus one-offs, and expects a "meaningful improvement" in H2.

### 2026 guidance

| Item | Guidance | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Core constant-currency revenue growth | 16% to 20% (unchanged) | Equals about GBP 260M–269M, or $345M–355M |
| Gross margin | About 62% (unchanged) | In line with original guidance and H1 |
| Adjusted operating expenses | -2% to flat year on year (improved) | Original March guidance was 0% to +5% growth |
| Revenue growth including $20M upfront licence fee | 23% to 27% (from 21% to 25% as stated in the call) | The $20M adds about 680 bps of growth |
| Gross margin including $20M upfront | About 64% | The $20M is recognised at 100% gross margin and adds about 200 bps |

- Keher said guidance on the core business is supported by "significant revenue coverage" from already confirmed business, proportionately the same coverage as going into H2 2025, before adding pipeline opportunities.
- Keher said the $20M upfront payment is excluded from the 16–20% guidance. Royalties are also excluded from 2026 and medium-term guidance.
- Adjusted opex: Keher expects the year-on-year reduction to narrow toward flat in H2, with further efficiency opportunities going into 2027.
- Targets reiterated: adjusted EBITDA breakeven in 2027, positive free cash flow in 2028, and net cash bottoming out above GBP 100M as the company passes through breakeven in 2028.

## Revenue mix in H1 2026

### By product

- **Devices:** up 32.6%, with every PromethION product line growing. The P2i (a mid-sized PromethION instrument) was "particularly strong," with revenue and placements more than doubling. Keher expects strong device sales to support future consumables pull-through.
- **Consumables** (the flow cells and kits customers must buy to run sequencing; MinION and PromethION): reported growth of only 2.7%, lower than anticipated. Keher called this "largely temporary" for three reasons:
  1. Large research programmes that added big consumables revenue in H1 2025 did not repeat.
  2. PromethION flow cell volumes grew over 20%, but average selling prices fell as customers moved into higher volume-discount tiers.
  3. Normalisation of ordering behaviour under the CapEx pricing model (customers now pay for devices outright, rather than getting them bundled), so they buy different volumes of consumables.
- Keher expects stronger consumables growth in H2 because new devices (especially P2i) will ramp, flow cell volumes keep growing, and comparisons get easier.
- PromethION was the group's growth driver, mostly through devices, especially P2i.

### By end market

| End market | H1 2026 revenue | Growth | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research | GBP 76M | +5.4% | Despite a GBP 8.3M (11.5%) headwind from NIHR, GEL 2.0 and PRECISE II contracts that ended in 2025; helped by the Sequence ME programme in EMEAI. Research is about two-thirds of group revenue. |
| Clinical | GBP 17.6M | +35.4% reported | Fastest-growing; driven by reimbursement-funded labs running assays or developing methods. A contract with MyOme (a Natera company) in rare disease was announced the same day. |
| BioPharma | GBP 9.5M | +25% | Uptake across R&D and QC (quality control) customers. |
| Industrial | GBP 13.7M | +6.2% | Driven by plasmid sequencing. |

Keher noted the mix is moving toward applied (commercially funded) markets, which are "more stable in nature."

### Gross margin bridge (H1 2026 vs H1 2025)

- The non-recurrence of last year's GBP 3.3M one-off non-cash inventory charge contributed +315 bps.
- Product and customer mix was a -160 bps headwind, expected to moderate as consumables/devices mix evolves and PromethION margins improve.
- Underlying improvement from the CapEx pricing model, yield gains and consumable recycling was +305 bps.
- FX was a -60 bps headwind, expected to moderate in H2 at current rates.

### Margin by category

- Consumables gross margin rose from 64% in 2023 to about 75% today, with potential above 80% (already achieved on some product lines). Keher later said MinION is already above 80% thanks to flow cell recycling; PromethION recycling is under way at some percentage and should lift margin as it rises.
- Devices and services gross margin rose from about 23% to about 34%, with a path to about 40%. Drivers so far: the new pricing model, better flow cell yields, MinION flow cell recycling and scale.
- Further upside: PromethION yield and recycling, SKU optimisation, services scale. None assumes business development contributions.
- Headwinds: inflation in compute and memory costs, being addressed through product changes, forward purchasing and pricing.

### Cash flow detail (H1 2026)

- Operating cash outflow before working capital: GBP 17.6M, about GBP 27.2M better than H1 2025, roughly in line with adjusted EBITDA.
- Working capital absorbed GBP 21.6M (versus GBP 6.2M outflow last year). The largest item was GBP 18.2M in payables, including GBP 25.7M of 2025 bonus payments that reverse in H2.
- CapEx and capitalised development: GBP 30.1M, of which GBP 24M was capitalised R&D (GBP 20M a year earlier), GBP 4M licences and patents, GBP 2.1M property, plant and equipment.
- Tax outflow GBP 1M; an R&D tax credit of about GBP 10M is expected in H2.
- Cash outflow on assets placed with customers: GBP 2.6M, versus GBP 14.4M in H1 2024 before the switch to CapEx-first pricing.
- Other investing/financing: net inflow of GBP 4.6M.

## Cross-licensing agreement (announced 19 Aug 2026)

- ONT signed a cross-licence with an unnamed "global diagnostics company," combining specific IP from both sides, providing freedom to operate under existing IP and strengthening ONT's IP position. Keher called it "potentially transformational for the group's outlook."
- Economics: a **$20M upfront fee** recognised in H2 2026 at 100% gross margin; **$15M of committed product purchases** recognised across 2027 and 2028 (modelled at current group margin); and an ongoing **royalty at a low to mid-single-digit percentage** of revenues from certain life science and diagnostic products of the counterparty using the licensed IP, for the life of the licensed patents.
- About 90% of the economic value sits in the royalty stream, based on market expectations for the counterparty's products.
- Royalties are additive as earned and excluded from 2026 and medium-term guidance because they are not ONT's products and cannot be reliably forecast. The $15M product revenue is included in the mid-teens 2027 growth outlook.
- No ONT cost is associated with the deal. Royalties drop through at 100% gross margin.
- Van Parys: "It's a sign up that we have a strong IP portfolio. It is one of the participation models that we feel is very relevant."
- The CFO said the counterparty's platform products have significant market-expected growth to 2030.

## CEO strategy presentation

### Diagnosis

- Van Parys has 20+ years in life sciences (GE Healthcare, Cytiva, Radiometer). He cited Biacore (a research platform he helped expand into regulated markets like biomanufacturing and QC) as a model.
- His four reasons for confidence: experience with similar opportunities, global reach, difficult technology work already done, and headroom between what the technology can do and the value captured.
- Weaknesses identified: adoption is below potential; customer experience has been inconsistent; product road maps were "driven more by what is scientifically possible than by customer problems"; breadth lacked portfolio focus and operating discipline; leadership depth needs strengthening. He said none of this requires reinventing the company.
- Self-description: "I'm an operator at heart. I'm competitive, pragmatic and focused on execution."

### Four pillars

1. **Customer-centric growth:** focus on high-value applications with a clear right to win.
2. **Focused innovation:** road maps tied to priority customer segments; near-term emphasis on product performance and workflow enhancements rather than entirely new products.
3. **Disciplined execution:** simplify the portfolio, clearer ownership, standard processes and measures. Portfolio simplification is already under way.
4. **High-performance culture:** build leadership depth and commercial, operational, regulatory and GMP-ready capabilities.

Detail was given only on pillars 1 and 4. Pillars 2 and 3 will be detailed at full-year results.

### Where to play and how

- Each application is judged on how differentiated ONT's value is and how readily the opportunity can be realised. Research is the "sweet spot"; selected biopharma workflows show strong differentiation; clinical carries heavier evidence, regulatory, reimbursement and market-building burdens.
- Participation spectrum: enable others via licensing, tech transfer or compatibility; OEM co-development or commercialisation; or own the full solution. Full ONT solutions are strongest in research and core biopharma QC; clinical is often partner-led because of validation, regulated deployment and channel access.
- ONT's core is library prep, sequencing and base calling; it will rely on third parties for sample collection, extraction, automation, analysis and integration.
- Serviceable market: about $14–16B, with three organic priority areas: research whole genome sequencing, biopharma and selected clinical.

### Priority areas

- **Research:** large genome centres, biobanks, core labs and disease programmes where long reads and native methylation change the answer (structural variation, phasing, difficult loci). Needs a stable production-ready PromethION workflow, prioritised bioinformatics, support, visible road maps, better output and cost per genome, and evidence of long-read utility.
- **Methylation** (chemical marks showing how DNA is being expressed): ONT captures sequence and methylation in one run with no separate assay. Four panels are shipping (methylation, pharmacogenomics, cancer digital panels), with tumour profiling expected from H2. A Prader-Willi example showed sequence alone gave no answer while methylation phasing found the cause.
- **BioPharma:** the fastest-growing priority market, focused on controlled R&D and QC. ONT will build core QC workflows directly and use channel and service-provider routes for broader R&D. Needs kits, productised bioinformatics, a CFR-ready (regulated-compliance) instrument path, comparative evidence and focused go-to-market. An mRNA QC case study showed one run consolidating identity, integrity and purity measurements into one report.
- **Clinical:** rare disease and rapid whole genome sequencing, tumour profiling, selected acute infectious disease. Model: ONT-led enablement in specialist centres and reference labs plus partner-led IVD (in vitro diagnostic) and channel routes. An acute leukaemia study consolidated 8 conventional assays into one adaptive sampling run producing 9 output categories, at an indicative $350–450 per sample versus about $1,650–2,050 for conventional assays.

### Leadership and culture

- New executives in the last three months: Tina St Leger (Chief People Officer), Andrew Watson (Chief Information Officer), Davide Manissero (Chief Medical Officer); Conor McKechnie joins in October as Chief Marketing and Communications Officer. Keher leads finance and operations; Lakmal leads science.
- Employee survey: 67% participation, 75% positive engagement, 73% would recommend ONT. Themes: focus, closer customer involvement, clearer accountability and faster decisions, stronger collaboration, simpler ways of working.

### Financial framework

- Research is about two-thirds of revenue today. Clinical and BioPharma, though smaller, are expected to grow faster and (with applied markets) reach about two-thirds of revenue by 2030. Industrial is expected to give steady single-digit growth.
- **2030 targets:** more than $700M organic revenue, adjusted EBITDA margin above 15%, and positive, growing free cash flow from 2028. Organic growth above mid-teens from FY2026, rising over time. Excludes the $20M upfront fee, royalties, and future business development/licensing, which are upside. Longer-term ambition: a $1B-plus revenue business.
- **Capital allocation:** (1) organic investment (innovation and manufacturing capacity); (2) partnerships as enablers, with a target return on invested capital above 15%; (3) selective M&A to accelerate adoption. All underpinned by a strong balance sheet.

## Q&A

### Zain Ebrahim (JPMorgan)

- **Royalty durability and size.** Keher said confidentiality limits detail. Royalty rate is low to mid-single-digit, and market expectations show significant growth in the covered products to 2030, so the royalty could be a large, "transformational" P&L contributor at 100% gross margin. On duration, he would not give dates but said to model beyond a 10-year horizon: patents typically last about 20 years, "It won't be that long, but it is towards that end rather than the 10." About 90% of value is in the royalties.
- **Clinical/BioPharma derisking.** Van Parys: these high-value applications are about 40–45% of group revenue today and should reach about 65% by 2030. Growth in them is high single digit currently (as transcribed), and as their weight rises overall growth should accelerate. Several clinical collaborations have been announced, more are under way, and about 20 unnamed biopharma customers are validating and adopting the technology, giving confidence. Keher added the split is about 35% applied markets today moving to 65% by 2030, and BioPharma has both QC (with evaluators) and a "very significant" R&D opportunity.

### Kyle Mikson (Canaccord)

- **2027 growth and beyond 2030.** Keher: "mid-teens and accelerating." Core growth in 2026 is 16–20% (23–27% with the $20M); for 2027 he expects mid-teens, accelerating toward >$700M by 2030. Street consensus for 2027 is 20%; he flagged that guidance assumes nothing for royalties or future business development, so these are additive. Clinical is the larger long-term opportunity; some development, particularly with partners, may only gain traction beyond 2030. Van Parys: through 2030 most growth comes from BioPharma (nearer term); infectious disease would likely move toward an IVD-type play through partnership, with product and regulatory requirements putting meaningful revenue in the outer years, accelerating "quite significantly" beyond 2030.
- **R&D and pipeline (PromethION 2, protein).** Van Parys: near-term R&D expense roughly where it is, with reprioritisation within it; spend to evolve with revenue from 2028; committed to the protein programme. Keher: innovation is fundamental; last year's strategic realignment stopped certain activities and benefits show now; more detail before full-year results; "we're not saving our way to greatness" but reallocating capital to higher-ROI activities.

### Jonathon Unwin (Barclays)

- **Why mid-teens in 2027 after 16–20% in 2026?** Keher: ONT analysed customer applications and pipeline; 40–45% of the business is in target applications growing very quickly, while for the other 55–60% it is being prudent. If a "halo effect" lifted growth there it could "end up getting caught up," and refocusing may take time. He hopes not to see deceleration to mid-teens but it would be inappropriate to say otherwise now; detailed in-year guidance will come at full-year results.
- **$15M product revenue:** included in the mid-teens outlook; royalties are not.
- **2027 gross margin:** no explicit number. He pointed to consumables at about 75% and MinION above 80%, PromethION recycling gains, devices/services scale, compute and memory cost headwinds being offset by development work (pricing may be considered in some instances), and a 40% devices/services long-term goal. With 62% in 2026 and a 400 bps gain this year, similar improvement in 2027 is "not unreasonable."
- **Is EBITDA breakeven dependent on the deal?** "Do we need this royalty agreement to hit EBITDA breakeven? No. Is it going to help? Yes."

### Veronika Dubajova (Citi)

- **R&D priorities and further pruning (Van Parys).** R&D spend is about the right level but must align with customer needs in target applications; a finalised plan on road map and portfolio simplification is targeted by full-year results. Near-term customer needs are driven by product enhancements and workflow completion on the current platform, not new products; he sees no need for new platforms in the medium term. Longer term, protein sequencing will become an important opportunity and investment continues. Portfolio simplification started last year and is mostly done, with "a couple of further adjustments of smaller programs."
- **Opex control (Keher).** Last year's "quite painful" realignments across R&D and SG&A (selling, general and administrative costs) are paying off. New savings: recovering more logistics costs (in sales and distribution) and eliminating duplicated IT/software programmes, executed over the next six months with benefits in 2027. He said the company would try to beat its opex guidance, and admitted disappointment with the H1 top line.

### Samuel England (Berenberg)

- **H2 visibility.** Van Parys: good visibility from scheduled orders for H2, a healthy pipeline, and no material improvement assumed in China or the Middle East; some delayed Americas contracts expected in H2. Keher: coverage of invoiced, shipped or scheduled revenue matches the same point last year, when ONT delivered 24% full-year growth; July was in line with expectations; Q3 is seasonally weaker than Q2 and Q4; pipeline is growing, with considerable coverage in the Americas.
- **Research assumptions.** Keher: mid-single-digit research growth assumed this year; NIH-related pressure exists, Europe remains healthy; medium-term should be stronger on target-application focus. The >$700M includes "prudence," no material market improvement, and "quite a significant headwind for China," so upside is possible.

### Kane Slutzkin (Deutsche Bank)

- **Infrastructure and capital intensity.** Van Parys: commercial infrastructure and R&D are about right; from 2028 investment scales with growth; no material change in magnitude, though projects and go-to-market need refocusing for specialisation. Manufacturing footprint is being reviewed and will "likely mean some expansion at some point," with decisions near term. Keher: main CapEx to 2030 would be flow cell manufacturing, spread over multiple years and not distorting the company's profile; a second facility is wanted partly for business continuity, and territories and funding sources are under consideration, to be worked through over six months.
- **Roche launch (Axelios).** Van Parys: ONT is seeing placements and has met customers investing; it is early days. He views it as adjacent, "a short read focused platform" aimed at higher throughput, not at the expense of ONT's target applications.
- **US research.** Keher: US research revenue is low single-digit growth, with a broad mix including difficult government-funded academic customers, but ONT still outperforms peers.

### Miles Dixon (Peel Hunt)

- **H2 opex.** Keher: guidance includes prudence and headroom, plus some build-out (e.g. the new leadership team); nothing is related to the licensing deal.
- **Capitalised development.** Rising because products mature from research into development as they become commercially available (accounting rules applied). Overall R&D spend broadly similar. Protein is mostly research today but may shift to development-led over 2027 and certainly 2028, which would raise capitalisation. Also adding automation to proven processes.

### Julie Simmonds (Panmure Liberum)

- **Why BioPharma before Clinical (Van Parys).** BioPharma validation and adoption is faster, though still thorough: once adopted into a QC process (e.g. as part of an FDA filing) it replicates across molecules because testing is repetitive, giving a step change. Clinical, especially beyond laboratory-developed tests into IVD, is longer term; he expects it to overtake BioPharma beyond 2030.
- **Consumables and CapEx model (Keher).** Under earlier project packs customers signed up for more flow cells than a normal year would use, creating a burn-through then drop; now they buy what they need, a "normalization event." Time for a new device to get going is generally under 3 months (training, installation, bioinformatics) followed by a ramp; ONT tracks utilisation per device (not published). Larger PromethION P24 utilisation is growing year on year. The GBP 8.3M headwind from NIHR, GEL 2.0 and PRECISE II was mostly consumables; new devices placed should ramp over the next 3+ months.

### James Orsborne (Stifel)

- **EBITDA margin beyond 15%.** Van Parys: ">15%" excludes cross-licence royalties and other partnerships; peers are in the 20s, and "no reason" ONT cannot reach that medium to long term, but 2030 commitment is prudently 15% or more. Keher: the margin depends on revenue; $800–900M would give a higher margin; royalties come in at 100% gross margin with no cost underneath.
- **GBP 100M cash trough.** Keher: still applies on an underlying basis, benefited by royalties. Capital allocation detail will be updated around year end. On M&A: it will not weaken the balance sheet, so M&A may come "a little bit later than '28."

## Closing

Van Parys said ONT will return at full-year results with more detail on pillars 2 and 3 (focused innovation and disciplined execution) and how the strategy will be operationalised.
