# Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT): Q4 / Full-Year 2025 Earnings Call, 2 March 2026

## Context and headline takeaways

This was Oxford Nanopore's results call for the fourth quarter and full year of 2025, held 2 March 2026. Oxford Nanopore makes DNA/RNA sequencing instruments and consumables based on nanopores (tiny protein holes through which a single molecule of DNA or RNA is threaded and read electrically). It was also the day co-founder Gordon Sanghera stepped down as CEO and Francis Van Parys started in the role.

Key points, all management claims unless stated otherwise [Q4 2025 call]:
- FY2025 revenue was GBP 223.9 million, up 24.2% at constant currency (the growth rate with exchange-rate effects stripped out). That was slightly above the 20-23% guidance set in March 2025.
- Reported gross margin was 58.6%, in line with the original guidance of 59%. Management says a "see-through" margin (excluding one-off items) of about 61% is the right base for 2026.
- The adjusted EBITDA loss (EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization; "adjusted" strips out certain items) narrowed by GBP 31.2 million, or 26%.
- Year-end cash was GBP 302.8 million with no debt.
- FY2026 guidance: revenue growth of 21-25% at constant currency, gross margin of 62%, and adjusted OpEx (operating expenses) growth of 0-5%.
- Medium-term targets were reiterated: adjusted EBITDA breakeven in 2027 and cash flow breakeven in 2028, with minimum cash of at least GBP 100 million. The earlier assumption of a revenue CAGR (compound annual growth rate) above 30% was replaced by growth at roughly the 2026 rate in 2027.

## Leadership transition

[Duncan Tatton-Brown, Chair, Q4 2025 call]
- Gordon Sanghera stepped down as CEO on 2 March 2026 after more than two decades leading the company he co-founded in 2005. He remains in an advisory capacity through early 2027 to support an orderly transition.
- Francis Van Parys became CEO on the same morning. He spent the day in Oxford meeting the team and did not join the call. The Chair said his experience is in scaling innovation-driven life sciences businesses, "particularly within regulated and commercial environments".
- The Board said it remains focused on supporting leading-edge science, driving strong growth in priority end markets through disciplined execution, and delivering sustainable profits.
- The Chair described the customer base as spanning more than 125 countries across research, clinical, biopharma and applied industrial markets.
- In Q&A the Chair said the Board has long recognized the quality of R&D talent and that real progress was made in the commercial team over the last couple of years. He said the executive team "now has a few gaps", which Van Parys has the opportunity to fill with more talent over the next few years, and that there is a lot of talent in the business with gaps mainly at senior level.
- Nick Keher (CFO) said he expects further additions to the executive team to support expansion into the higher-priority end markets.

## Gordon Sanghera: company history, vision and positioning

[Gordon Sanghera, Q4 2025 call]
- Origins: he began looking at Hagan Bayley's single-molecule "stochastic sensing" work in spring 2004. The company was spun out in 2005 with GBP 0.5 million from IP Group. Vision from the outset: "the analysis of anything by anyone, anywhere", which he called "a work in progress".
- Proudest moments: NASA taking the technology into space (slated for the Artemis mission) and Project NEEMO, in which NASA went down into the Mariana Trench and found over 7,500 new species.
- Scope of the platform: first application is DNA and RNA; extending to proteomics (protein analysis; more at the London Calling conference). The platform could also measure small molecules, chemicals, metabolites and volatile organic compounds.
- Timeline: DNA products launched 10 years ago; direct RNA about 5 years ago.
- Scale: over 125 countries, over 1,300 employees. Consumables, flow cells and reagents (flow cells are the consumable chips that hold the nanopores) are made in the company's own UK factory (Didcot), built to meet demand for the next 3 to 5 years of growth.
- Evidence of adoption: 20,000 publications in 10 years since the first products, "a phenomenal beat rate". Revenue has grown at a 28% CAGR since the IPO.
- Competitive claims: "There is no other company in this space... that sequences direct DNA, RNA." Nanopore reads native DNA (the molecule itself, with no copying or chemical conversion) from hundreds of bases (short) to tens or hundreds of thousands (long) to millions (ultra-long). "We are not just a longread company. It's that native DNA that makes us highly differentiated." He said no other nanopore company, including other nanopore players, does this.
- Workflow claim: sample preparation takes hours, not days, and there is no need to batch samples, enabling rapid, point-of-care, distributed and affordable use. One, two or all three attributes (native data, real-time speed, accessibility) drive growth.
- Market comparison: in a tough market where competitors "in the last 3 or 4 years have seen low growth, no growth or negative growth", ONT delivered 20%+ growth in every region.

### Closing reflections (Sanghera)
- Said it took 10 years to deliver the first products; the company has raised a lot of money and "people... think the return on the investment is low, but this company will be around in 100 years' time."
- Said T2T (telomere-to-telomere, i.e. fully complete) genomes are "the new gold standard" and that Craig Venter is building that standard.
- Recalled the doubters ("it will never work... you can't raise money in London... you will never be profitable"), said they made the team more determined, and said this is the right moment to hand over.
- Said "I'm not retiring", quoting David Bowie: "I don't know what I'll be doing next, but I can guarantee it will be interesting."

## FY2025 financial results

### Revenue and growth
[Gordon Sanghera and Nick Keher, Q4 2025 call]
- Revenue was GBP 223.9 million, up 24.2% at constant currency. This was marginally ahead of the 20-23% guidance set in March 2025 (Sanghera: 1% above the top end).
- Growth exceeded 20% in every region at constant currency, and the MinION range (the small portable sequencer) returned to growth as previously guided.
- About 70% of revenue comes from flow cells and kits made in Didcot.

By end market (the customer category the sequencing is used in):

| End market | FY2025 growth | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Life science research tools | +15% | 66% of revenue; "despite ongoing NIH pressures" (US National Institutes of Health funding); described as significantly outperforming competitors |
| Clinical | +59% (close to 60%) | Fastest-growing applied market |
| BioPharma | +30% | |
| Applied industrial | +27% | |

The applied end markets (clinical, biopharma, applied industrial) rose from 30% to 34% of revenue year on year, which Sanghera said is broadly what management projected at the capital markets day a couple of years earlier.

Keher's commercial colour: broader adoption in rare disease and oncology; strategic collaborations such as Cepheid and bioMérieux in clinical; QC (quality control) deployments, plasma sequencing expansion and higher PromethION utilization in biopharma and industrial; and stronger pricing discipline and contracting structures that improved margin profile and cash dynamics.

### Gross margin
Reported gross margin was 58.6% for FY2025, versus 57.5% in FY2024, in line with original guidance of 59%. The next table shows the bridge Keher described, from the FY2024 base to the FY2025 reported figure. Underlying improvements of 460 basis points (bps; 100 bps = 1 percentage point) were partly eaten by one-off and structural headwinds.

| Item | Effect |
|---|---|
| FY2024 base | 57.5% |
| Underlying improvements (new "CapEx-first" pricing model, plus yield improvements, especially on PromethION flow cells) | +460 bps |
| Foreign exchange | -70 bps |
| One-off obsolete-inventory charge (not expected to repeat) | -150 bps |
| Product and customer mix | -130 bps |
| Reported FY2025 | 58.6% |

- A restructuring charge of GBP 1.8 million (80 bps), almost entirely on the ElysION product line, is included. Excluding it, margin was 59.4%. Excluding one-offs taken in the first half of 2025, margin would have been 60.9%. The "see-through" margin is "closer to 61%", which Keher called "the right figure for investors and analysts to look at as we enter FY '26".
- Recycling: improved recycling of PromethION flow cells (reusing flow cells returned by customers) was largely offset in 2025 by lower recycling of MinION flow cells.
- The CapEx-first pricing model means customers pay for the sequencing device up front instead of the company placing it free and recovering the cost through consumables. It was announced in January 2025 and went live in February 2025.

### Costs and EBITDA
[Keher, Q4 2025 call]
- Adjusted OpEx grew only 1% year on year, reflecting cost control and two restructuring events: one in January 2025 to reallocate capital toward higher-growth opportunities, and one in November 2025 (a "strategic realignment") to align the business with high-priority end markets.
- The adjusted EBITDA loss improved by GBP 31.2 million (26%), of which GBP 18 million came in the second half (a 32% improvement for that half). Adjusted EBITDA has improved in every half-year since the start of FY2024.
- Currency headwinds (on both revenue and gross margin) have reduced adjusted EBITDA by GBP 9 million cumulatively since January 2024.
- Breakeven is to come from above-market revenue growth plus cost control, including efficiencies in IT, logistics and internal working practices (non-headcount spend).

### Cash and cash flow
Keher explained that the following items connect operating results to the year-end cash balance.
- Operating loss before restructuring: GBP 79.1 million, largely mirroring the EBITDA performance.
- Restructuring costs: GBP 13.8 million (GBP 5.2 million in H1 and GBP 8.6 million in H2, the latter from the November exercise).
- Working capital: an inflow of GBP 13.4 million, with total inventory down GBP 18 million.
- Assets at customers (devices placed with customers): GBP 10.1 million, down GBP 10.5 million from the prior year thanks to the new pricing model. A second-half increase of GBP 4.6 million relates mainly to devices for the UK Biobank contract and a few evaluation devices.
- CapEx and capitalized development costs: GBP 45.5 million, split GBP 41.5 million capitalized development and GBP 4 million CapEx. The unusually low CapEx reflects timing, with large projects such as the Spectrum Building now complete. Keher said this low level is unlikely to repeat in 2026.
- Tax income: GBP 18.9 million, from two R&D tax credits received in the year, again unlikely to repeat (the transcript says "FY '25"; context suggests the following year).
- Other investing and financing inflow: GBP 15.3 million, from the bond portfolio.
- Net cash outflow: GBP 101 million; year-end cash GBP 302.8 million, no debt. Keher said the cash burn was down GBP 50 million in the year.
- Minimum cash of at least GBP 100 million is expected as the company passes through adjusted EBITDA breakeven (2027) and cash flow breakeven (2028).

## Strategic review and portfolio decisions

[Keher, Q4 2025 call]
- In 2025 ONT completed a strategic review, using inside and outside perspectives. It identified roughly $13-14 billion of higher-priority segments across research, clinical, biopharma manufacturing QC and other markets, where richer data, speed and accessibility give a competitive advantage over peer technologies. Segments are ranked high, medium or low priority based on ease of access, differentiation and scalability, not size alone. This now shapes commercial resource allocation, roadmap decisions, capital deployment and organizational focus. (In Q&A, Keher referred to 47 end-market segments being targeted, with the high-priority ones evaluated against customer requirements.)
- Product decisions:
  - Sales of the P2 Solo (a single-flow-cell PromethION benchtop device) are discontinued from June 2026 (the transcript says "from June this year"), with efforts focused on the P2i.
  - Internal development of the ElysION platform (automated sample-prep/sequencing system) is paused and direct commercial efforts on it stopped.
  - Focus is on integrated systems such as the P2i and P24, and on "Q-Line" products (a product line aimed at biopharma QC) across GridION and PromethION.
  - A version 2 of the Q-Line product for GridION will launch in 2026 (later specified as the second half), serving a broader set of biopharma requirements. The PromethION Q-Line launch has been re-timed to late 2027.
- Operations: automation advanced, manufacturing capability expanded, and ERP and CRM (enterprise resource planning and customer relationship management software) transformations started. Keher called the result "a structurally more focused business with improving operational leverage."

## FY2026 guidance

[Keher, Q4 2025 call]

| Metric | FY2026 guidance | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth (constant currency) | 21% to 25% | FY2025 actual 24.2%; peers guiding largely to low-to-mid single-digit growth |
| Gross margin | 62% | 340 bps above FY2025 reported (58.6%); 110 bps above the ~61% see-through margin |
| Adjusted OpEx growth | 0% to 5% | Reflects November 2025 realignment benefits; range reflects hiring timing |

- Revenue drivers: the low end reflects expected continued subdued US research funding, specific dynamics in China, and some risk from the P2 Solo discontinuation. The top end is roughly the opposite of those factors, and the company could exceed guidance if it converts more customers to the P2i. Keher said the company tries to "risk adjust" guidance to de-risk external expectations.
- Regions:
  - Americas: strongest growth, driven by applied markets.
  - EMEAI (Europe, Middle East, India): consistent historic growth, but 2026 growth should be lower than 2025 because large research contracts are rolling off while new ones ramp up.
  - APAC: above-end-market growth expected but potentially slower, because of the end of the PRECISE II contract and China-specific factors. Keher said APAC "could be the surprise factor to the upside".
- End markets: growth strongest in applied markets, led by clinical and biopharma.
- Middle East: guidance does not include any risk from the weekend's events in the Middle East (the call was 2 March 2026). The region is about 3% of group revenue but is a central point in the global supply chain, so the full impact is "arguably unknown".
- Gross margin upside levers: further benefit from CapEx-first pricing, and better PromethION flow cell recycling, offsetting expected mix and currency headwinds.

### Phasing and EBITDA in 2026 (Q&A, Keher)
- Revenue is expected to be weighted roughly 45% first half / 55% second half, as in prior years.
- Gross margin is expected to be broadly consistent between halves, around 62%.
- The November restructuring should give a first-half cost benefit. Adjusted EBITDA loss will mirror the revenue profile.
- Based on the guidance, the market should arrive at an adjusted EBITDA loss of circa GBP 45-55 million for 2026, which Keher called a meaningful improvement and "good against where consensus is at the moment". (JPMorgan's Zain Ebrahim had cited consensus of an adjusted EBITDA loss of GBP 14 million for next year, apparently meaning 2027.)

### Medium-term targets
- Adjusted EBITDA breakeven in 2027 and cash flow breakeven in 2028 are reiterated, with "amended constituent drivers".
- Revenue growth in 2027 is expected to continue at a broadly similar rate to 2026. This is below the previously set medium-term CAGR of over 30%, reflecting a weaker end-market backdrop than when guidance was set, and partly execution, "which we will look to improve going forward".
- Improving gross margin, which has accelerated ahead of initial expectations, plus the restructurings and further efficiencies, keeps 2027 breakeven intact "even with a lower top line growth rate".

## Question-and-answer session

### Zain Ebrahim (JPMorgan)
Congratulated Sanghera. Asked (1) what separates the upper from the lower end of the 2026 guide, whether P2i conversion is the key driver, and what gives confidence that the Americas will accelerate versus 2025; (2) where gross margin is heading and what consensus (adjusted EBITDA loss of GBP 14 million) is underappreciating.

Keher's answers:
- Americas: pipeline coverage and the number of customers evaluating or about to take product in clinical and biopharma give confidence that ONT has "turned that corner". US research has always been hard to penetrate, but applied markets got there quicker: over 50% of Americas revenue now comes from applied markets and this looks set to continue.
- EMEA: the 5-year CAGR "has been a hell of a performance". In 2026, contracts such as NIHR and GL 2.0 (Genomics England) roll off, and the UK Biobank contract helps offset them, but a proportion of revenue is essentially static, so lower growth is expected. He said guidance is set prudently.
- APAC: a wildcard. Adaptive sampling (the sequencer decides in real time whether to keep or reject a DNA strand, enriching targets) is making inroads with large customers, particularly in Australia, with a healthy pipeline but a ramp-timing aspect. PRECISE II has ended. In China, export control restrictions "haven't got easier", leaving a backlog of potential revenue; there are more nanopore competitors in China, which ONT has evaluated and believes are technologically inferior, shown by high demand despite the price gap; and ONT believes it can execute better there. China is a substantial part of APAC, so guidance is cautious and may be revisited.
- P2i vs P2 Solo: across the 47 end-market segments, the P2i fits customer requirements better. ONT will push the P2i "stronger and harder", focusing sales, marketing, application and workflow development on the P2i or P24. There is a risk of weaker performance during the transition, which is factored into guidance.
- EBITDA breakeven: when medium-term guidance was set, ONT said it would modulate the cost base if top-line growth fell short. It did so in the two 2025 restructurings, so "we don't need to achieve over 30% CAGR to achieve that breakeven in '27". He called the updated constituents "a more realistic outlook".

### Veronika Dubajova (Citi)
Asked (1) how the gross margin gets above 62% to reach breakeven in 2027 and what builds confidence; (2) bigger picture, after months of bedding in the realignment, what excites management, how the organization is handling change, and what has gone better or worse than expected. Congratulated Sanghera.

Keher's gross margin answer:
- 62% in 2026 brings forward the original ">62% in 2027" constituent guidance by a year. He said it is fair for the market to think around 64% for 2027.
- Reasons: the pricing model changed (CapEx-first) after the original guidance and clearly helps margin. The remaining true tailwind is PromethION flow cell recycling. In 2025, the drop in MinION recycling cancelled out the PromethION benefit; for 2026 the MinION recycling rate is at a level that will step over that, and with PromethION recycling increasing there will be a meaningful improvement. He said that if PromethION achieved the MinION-style levels (the transcript is garbled: "if we achieved the levels that we did with the Prom to the Min"), it could be a 10 percentage-point improvement in PromethION gross margins, with "some way to go".

Big picture:
- Keher: prioritization "will always lead to better outcomes". With 47 end-market segments the technology could go after all of them, so having a focused list and "putting down tools" on some work lets more people work on bigger projects. Change has been hard; since the start of the year people are "starting to find their feet", though "not everybody is there yet".
- Sanghera: "success can be painful with infinite possibilities... and finite resource." The company is focusing on "the 5 or 10 big things", specifically 3 to 5 major items: direct RNA with modifications, direct DNA with modifications, adaptive sampling, and reuse by design. He said ONT is redesigning the PromethION chip for reuse (rather than reusing a chip not designed for it), with "radical transformative revenue and margin gains". Adaptive sampling reads the first seconds of DNA and decides on-target or off-target, making "2 days, $300 to $500 of target enrichment disappear", and native DNA also reveals methylation (chemical marks on DNA, the "fifth base"). He said "2026 will be the year of the methylome": UK Biobank and the Office of Life Sciences with Nanopore are sequencing 50,000 patient samples, which will give the first and most comprehensive 5-base genome. He described a shift from "a heavy R company with some D" to an R company with much more D, because of regulated applied markets that give "recurring sticky revenues over multiple years".

### Samuel England (Berenberg)
Asked about 2026 cadence of revenue, margins and costs, then progress in biopharma QC and scope for large contract wins in 2026. Congratulated Sanghera.

Keher's answers:
- Cadence: revenue roughly 45/55 H1/H2; gross margin about 62% in both halves; first-half cost benefit from the November restructuring; adjusted EBITDA loss of roughly GBP 45-55 million for the year (see above).
- Biopharma QC: he and Sanghera are "extremely frustrated" at not being able to name companies. In mRNA vaccine production, a contract has been signed and another is hoped for this year. In sterility testing, a large European biopharma uses the technology centrally for several sites it manages and should expand to those sites over time. A pipeline of companies is evaluating the technology. He said biopharma QC is "a big market opportunity for us to win".
- Sanghera: a fee-for-service contract with Eurofins covers sequencing in biopharma QC (sterility). In the first three weeks, Eurofins picked up a customer contamination that "saved several millions". The customer was not named.

### Charles Weston (RBC)
Asked (1) why 2027 growth should not accelerate versus 2026, given 2026 headwinds that are specific to that year and a higher base of faster-growing non-research markets; (2) timing of GridION Q-Line version 2 and of the PromethION 2 "genome per flow cell" chemistry/flow cell.

Keher: 2027 "could be higher than 2026", but guidance is for a broadly similar rate, which is all that is needed for 2027 breakeven; the aim is a prudent set of market expectations. He sees nothing in applied-market growth that would necessarily slow it, and will update 2027 guidance when the time comes. GridION Q-Line v2 will launch in the second half of 2026. The PromethION 2 genomes-per-flow-cell capability can already be demonstrated in customer beta testing; the question is how to roll it out as a complete product, and it will fit only certain customer segments at first (depending on sample type, read length and use), with further development needed for broader benefit.

Sanghera: competitors are in "a race to the bottom for whole genomes", which is not what ONT is about. ONT will deploy this throughput where it is competitive with incumbents, in biopharma, clinical and other applied uses; "everybody does not need that overcapacity". Some customers want lower throughput but higher accuracy, and ONT is beginning to segment markets and match products.

### Unknown analyst
- MinION recycling decline: Keher said "nothing untoward". MinION customers are typically lower-volume and getting flow cells back from them is harder than from PromethION customers, so stockpiles ebb and flow. Recycling also depends on internal manufacturing focus and is in ONT's control; it can be raised or lowered.
- Inventory: a major Board-level focus. The reported figure is GBP 80 million (net of provisions), so the gross number is higher. There is scope to work it down by "tens of millions over time on a gross level", particularly MinION flow cells while keeping PromethION stock where it is. Better yields reduce needed production; higher recycling reduces inventory consumption (so it "hurts" inventory reduction). "We've got cash stored up there. We should unlock it."
- The analyst noted the long time "from our first IP to IPO investment".

### Miles Dixon (Peel Hunt)
Asked (1) what really changed in R&D given similar headcount and costs, whether spend shifts to existing chemistries and away from blue-sky research; (2) the timeline and R&D spend for proteomics after the London Calling presentation; (3) what "clinical" means, given 60% growth and the possibility much of it is clinical research.

- Clinical definition (Keher): customers using the technology to provide a diagnosis to patients, judged by how the customer is paid. ONT lacks full visibility of use, so there may be some mix with translational work and "grayness"; better segmentation is being worked on. Sanghera added that the bulk of revenue comes from LDTs (laboratory-developed tests, run in-house by a lab), where the physician signs off on the test, not from ONT supplying a diagnostic tool and workflow. Keher agreed it was a good legal catch.
- R&D (Keher): R&D headcount includes investment in tech transfer, late-stage development and quality manufacturing, which sometimes sit in R&D. The January 2025 restructuring reinvested in these areas; the November reductions will lower average R&D headcount in 2026, but ONT is reinvesting. "We're not stopping innovation."
- Proteomics (Sanghera): excited about mapping peptides (true protein sequencing to come). An early open-access program/request for information has produced exciting applications, many from biopharma customers. He noted GLP-1 drugs are peptides that can now be read directly at the single-molecule level, which "nobody else can do". Keher: current spend is single-digit millions of pounds, since the platform technology already exists.
- Timeline (Sanghera): pure-play protein sequencing is medium term, 3 to 5 years; peptide mapping may produce revenue sooner, though ONT does not yet know what because it is "creating a whole new market space". There is both lone-wolf academic and significant corporate interest, and he expects it to go faster than DNA did.

### Andrew Whitney (Investec)
Asked whether the near-term spending tweak means ONT stays a nucleic-acid business for longer before moving into proteins and metabolites, and where senior hires will go.

- Sanghera declined to advise on recruitment ("As manager this season and not here next season, I'm not about to start telling Francis who he should recruit"). On strategy: ONT chose biology over chemistry. It did DNA, then RNA five years later, and now has peptide mapping as the start of proteomics, in a different way from mass spectrometry, with protein sequencing to come. It has not made a conscious effort to pursue chemistry (volatile organic compounds, small metabolites) and is focused on multiomics and biology, "the strategic rationale from day 1".
- Keher: the company is thinking carefully about what the protein product must do in its target end markets to win, so as to have a better launch profile.
- Tatton-Brown: see executive team gaps comments above.

### Kyle Mikson (Canaccord)
Asked about (1) 2026 device revenue and shipments through the P2 Solo-to-P2i conversion, plus pricing tailwinds from the CapEx model; (2) whether clinical, which grew nearly 60% in 2025, is getting more competitive with long-read and short-read rivals.

Keher:
- Devices: the pricing change (moving from free device placement to charging for devices) caught customers off guard and slowed device placements throughout 2025. Data suggest this should reverse in 2026, so he expects higher device revenue growth than consumable pull-through ("no doubt we'll get that wrong"). Margin should improve a bit more, particularly on larger devices, for accounting reasons he said he would explain offline.
- Clinical competition: new competitors are entering, but ONT wins on the richness of information (direct DNA and RNA, methylation, long reads and structural variants in one run) and turnaround time, which do not change with the landscape. Large names in clinical in the Americas are evaluating or underpinning the growth.

Sanghera on Illumina and the competitive landscape [Q4 2025 call]:
- "The competitive landscape is a real positive for Oxford Nanopore. The fact that Illumina has finally agreed long reads are important and they've launched a synthetic long-read play. This is the fourth one, I think. So good luck with that."
- ONT has "native long, ultra-long, super long, short. So we can give you any read length you want on native DNA."
- Illumina is also launching a methylation profiler, which he called recognition that the 20,000 publications and foundational multiomics work matter. "Bring it on, native, direct, all in one read and one price will outperform the market."

Sanghera thanked participants and said investors would see them in six months "with Francis and Nick leading the charge."
