# Oxford Nanopore (ONT) — Consensus Annual Financial Estimates

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ONT/consensus/ONT_Consensus_Annual.md` · Date: 2026-10-10 · Bloomberg consensus estimates · consensus estimates*

**Summary.** Bloomberg consensus projections for Oxford Nanopore's annual financials through 2031. ONT is currently unprofitable but consensus expects recovery to net profitability by 2030 and EBITDA positivity by 2028. Revenue growth accelerates from £183M (2024 actual) to £633M (2031E), with gross margins rebounding from a 2023 trough to 66%+. The company faces significant cash burn through 2027 but shows a clear path to positive free cash flow in 2030-2031. Key driver appears to be operating leverage as SG&A and R&D decline as a percentage of revenue.

## Overview

This document is a consensus estimates sheet compiled from Bloomberg, showing both historical actuals (2022–2025, in GBP millions) and forward projections (2026–2031E). All figures are in British pounds sterling (GBP). Historical years are marked "Y" (actual); forward years are marked "Est." The consensus represents the weighted average of analyst estimates.

## Key Assumptions and Risks

**Upside scenarios:**
- Faster adoption of sequencing in research and clinical diagnostics could drive revenue beats.
- Gross margin expansion to 66%+ assumes successful manufacturing scale-up or product mix shift; this is achievable but not guaranteed.
- Operating leverage in 2026–2031 depends on SG&A control and R&D productivity; consensus appears to assume headcount discipline.

**Downside/execution risks:**
- Competitive pressure from Illumina (dominant in short-read sequencing) and others could constrain pricing and mix.
- The company's cash position (£153.7M at 2026E, declining to £25.9M by 2031E) is concerning; any slowdown in cash burn or need to accelerate investment could force dilutive capital raises.
- Turning EBITDA positive in 2028E is two years away; if cash burn persists longer, liquidity may become a constraint.
- Profitability (net income) is not reached until 2030E; a six-year runway from 2024 to breakeven is long for a biotech and implies execution risk.

## Summary

Oxford Nanopore is a loss-making, cash-burning sequencing company at an inflection point. The consensus view sees a multi-year path to profitability, with revenue growing at 13%+ CAGR through 2031, gross margins rebounding to 66%+, and operating leverage kicking in via SG&A and R&D efficiency. EBITDA turns positive in 2028E, operating income in 2030E, and net income in 2030E, with diluted EPS reaching £0.06 by 2031E. However, the company faces a liquidity squeeze: cash is expected to burn to £26M by 2031E (and lower in intermediate years), requiring either faster profitability, capital raises, or debt financing. The path to profitability is credible but execution-dependent, particularly around gross margin expansion and cost discipline.
