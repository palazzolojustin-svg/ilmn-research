# Oxford Nanopore sets $700m revenue target for 2030

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/ONT/news/20260819_Proactive_Investors_Ltd_ONT-LN_Oxford_Nanopore_sets_-700m_revenue_target_for_2030.md` · Date: 2026-08-19 · Proactive Investors Ltd, Ian Lyall · news*

**Summary.** Oxford Nanopore announced new strategic targets under new CEO Francis Van Parys: revenue exceeding $700 million and adjusted EBITDA margins exceeding 15% by 2030, with profitability milestones in 2027–2028. H1 2026 revenue grew 10.5% to £116.7 million with strong clinical and biopharma growth offsetting a 15.7% decline in China sales. The company also signed a $20 million diagnostics cross-licensing deal and held £234.5 million in cash at the end of June.

## Strategic Targets and Long-Term Goals

Oxford Nanopore announced two new medium-term targets on 19 August 2026. The company aims for annual revenue exceeding $700 million by 2030, with adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization—a measure of underlying profitability that excludes non-cash and one-time items) margins exceeding 15% in the same year. Both targets are new to the guidance. The company reaffirmed earlier targets to reach breakeven on adjusted earnings in 2027 and to generate positive free cash flow from 2028 onward. Over the longer term, Oxford Nanopore is targeting annual revenue above $1 billion.

These targets accompany a strategic refresh under newly appointed chief executive Francis Van Parys, built around four priorities spanning biopharma, clinical testing and research. The company intends to concentrate investment on a smaller group of high-potential applications where its technology can command clear value [Announcement, 19 Aug 2026].

## H1 2026 Financial Results

Revenue for the six months to 30 June 2026 rose £116.7 million, representing growth of 10.5% reported, or 12.3% at constant currency (which excludes the effects of currency movements). Growth was uneven across segments: clinical sales grew 35.4% (the strongest segment), biopharma sales grew 25%, while sales in China fell 15.7% [H1 2026 results].

Gross margin (the share of revenue remaining after direct production costs) widened by 4 percentage points to 62.2%, indicating improved unit economics or higher-margin mix.

On the adjusted earnings basis preferred by management, the company's loss more than halved to £22.1 million from £48.3 million a year earlier. The reported GAAP loss narrowed to £48 million from £71.8 million in the same prior-year period. The gap between the two loss figures reflects add-backs for share-based compensation, depreciation, amortization, interest and other non-cash items [H1 2026 results].

## Cash Position and Burn Rate

Oxford Nanopore held cash and liquid investments of £234.5 million at 30 June 2026, down from £302.8 million at the end of 2025. This represents a net cash burn of £68.3 million over the first six months of the year [H1 2026 results].

## Diagnostics Partnership

After the close of the six-month period (i.e., after 30 June 2026), Oxford Nanopore signed a cross-licensing agreement with an unnamed global diagnostics firm. The deal is structured as a $20 million upfront fee paid this year, with $15 million in committed product purchases over the following two years [Post-period event, announced 19 Aug 2026].

## Share Price and Analyst Response

Oxford Nanopore's shares rose 4% to 126.9p on the announcement [19 Aug 2026].

Panmure Liberum, a broker, reiterated a 'hold' recommendation and a 170p price target. The analyst noted that the company trades at a "30% discount to the peer group" and expects that discount to narrow on these results. On the strategic shift, Panmure Liberum offered a nuanced view: "The strategic update indicates progression towards a more commercially focused business, which is both encouraging and welcome, though it indicates substantial changes which do not come without risk" [Panmure Liberum, 19 Aug 2026]. This characterizes the strategic refocus as positive but cautionary—a tightening of focus carries execution risk.
