# Pacific Biosciences (PacBio) Q1 2026 Earnings Call: record consumables, weak instruments, SPRQ-Nx launch, lowered revenue guidance

Speakers: Christian Henry (President and CEO), James "Jim" Gibson (CFO), Caylene Parrish (Investor Relations). Analysts: Pradeep Ambrose (TD Cowen, for Dan Brennan), Austin Kennedy (Wolfe Research, for Doug Schenkel), Alexandra Aylen (Canaccord Genuity, for Kyle Mikson), Karan Patel (Piper Sandler, for David Westenberg), Mason Carrico (Stephens). · earnings call*

**Summary.** PacBio reported Q1 2026 revenue of $37.2 million, flat year over year and about $2.8 million below consensus, as record consumables revenue (+9%) and more than 100% growth in clinical shipments were offset by weak instrument sales, especially the Vega system, caused by US academic funding pressure, a one-time Vega promotion and Middle East shipment problems. Management cut the top end of 2026 revenue guidance by $5 million to $165-175 million and said gross margin improvement will land at the low end of its range because of rising memory and compute costs. The company stressed the imminent late-May commercial launch of SPRQ-Nx chemistry (which makes HiFi long-read sequencing cheaper through reusable SMRT Cells), its clinical and rare-disease momentum, and a new 100,000-sample AI-related project with Basecamp Research. It also closed the sale of its short-read sequencing assets to Illumina for about $48.1 million net cash.

# PacBio Q1 2026 Earnings Call (May 7, 2026)

## Background and terms

Pacific Biosciences of California ("PacBio", ticker PACB) makes DNA sequencers that read long stretches of DNA ("long-read sequencing"). Its signature method is called HiFi sequencing, which produces long reads that are also highly accurate. Its two main instruments are:

- **Revio**: its higher-throughput system.
- **Vega**: its smaller, lower-priced benchtop system.

Customers run samples on disposable **SMRT Cells** (the chips where sequencing happens), using chemistry kits. Revenue comes from three sources: **instruments** (the machines), **consumables** (reagent kits and SMRT Cells that customers must buy for every run, and which recur), and **service and other**. **Pull-through** is the annual consumables revenue generated per installed Revio system. **ASP** means average selling price. **Non-GAAP** means results adjusted to exclude certain items (here, the CFO said the non-GAAP figures still include non-cash stock-based compensation). Unless noted, growth rates are year over year. [Q1 2026 call, 2026-05-07]

The Seeking Alpha summary header says the quarter's EPS of -$0.12 beat consensus by $0.01. It also says revenue of $37.18 million (up 0.07% year over year) missed consensus by $2.80 million. [Seeking Alpha header, Q1 2026]

### 2026 guidance (Gibson)

- **Revenue:** the high end was lowered by $5 million, and the new range is **$165 million to $175 million**. The low end was unchanged. Henry later said the company still expects to be in the range of the guidance given in February.
- **Revenue drivers:** the outlook continues to assume that consumables are the primary driver of growth. That growth is supported by continued utilization from clinical customers and the ongoing expansion of the Revio and Vega installed base.
- **Funding assumption:** the guidance assumes no meaningful recovery in academic and government funding, particularly in the Americas.
- **Non-GAAP gross margin:** improvement in 2026 is now expected toward the **lower end** of the previously communicated range of 100 to 400 basis points. A basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point, so this is 1 to 4 points of improvement. A higher consumable mix and SPRQ-Nx remain important margin drivers, but rising compute costs will "temper the pace of margin improvement in the near term."
- **Non-GAAP operating expenses:** $220 million to $225 million, down from 2025 levels.
- **Q2 gross margin:** management expects gross margin to improve in the second quarter. Gibson said Q1 margin pressure was "primarily driven by nonrecurring and timing-related factors."

## Prepared remarks

### Headline message (Henry)

Henry said the quarter was highlighted by three things:

- Record consumable revenue.
- More than 100% year-over-year growth in consumable shipments to clinically focused accounts.
- Significant strategic progress, including the first significant AI-related project, with Basecamp Research.

On the negative side, instrument revenue, particularly Vega, was lower than expected. He attributed this to continuing pressure on academic funding, particularly in the United States. He also said the company was unable to deliver some products to the Middle East because of the conflict in the region. [Q1 2026 call]

### Consumables

- This was the third consecutive record consumables quarter. Consumable revenue grew 9% year over year.
- Clinical shipments now represent a mid-teens percentage of total consumable shipments, a share that doubled from a year earlier. Henry expects them to keep growing as customers move "from testing and validation to full commercialization."
- Clinical growth offset the fact that some customers held off consumable shipments to wait for the SPRQ-Nx commercial launch.
- Demand was strong to join the SPRQ-Nx early access program during the quarter.

### Revio

- Shipments were 15 systems in Q1 2026, against 12 in Q1 2025.
- Henry said Revio demand remains constrained by the funding environment in the Americas. He was encouraged that half of Revio placements went to new customers globally and that multisystem orders from clinical accounts continue as they build capacity.
- Instrument revenue fell 12% mainly because of lower Revio ASPs, as the company prioritizes placements in strategic accounts, and lower Vega ASPs from the Q1 promotion. A higher number of Revio units shipped partly offset this. [Gibson]

### Vega

- Shipments were 27 systems against 28 a year earlier.
- Henry said Vega's revenue contribution was hurt by two factors: lighter US demand, because academic funding remains under pressure, and promotional pricing geared toward attracting new customers.
- The company ran a limited-time Vega promotion in Q1 to expand the installed base and unlock new accounts. It ended at the end of the quarter, and Henry expects Vega ASPs to normalize in Q2.
- More than 85% of Vega placements went to new customers, "expanding the reach of HiFi sequencing."

### Regions

- **EMEA (Europe, Middle East and Africa):** the highlight of the quarter, with 17% growth.
  - Revenue was $10.8M. Gibson said it was driven by consumables demand, reflecting account expansion and higher utilization, particularly in clinical settings where higher test volumes drove incremental pull-through.
  - This came despite challenges delivering product to the Middle East.
  - Henry said clinical customers who were in pilot and validation mode are moving into sustained production-scale sequencing, creating demand for more Revio systems and sustained consumable pull-through. The EMEA Revio pipeline "continues to be strong."
  - As in 2025, he expects EMEA to be the fastest-growing region in 2026.
- **Americas:** revenue of $16.7M, up 2%, driven by consumables growth from a larger installed base. The company is aggressively shifting strategy toward clinical and commercial accounts, where funding dynamics are more favorable. In Q1 its largest accounts were commercial service providers and clinical accounts.
- **Asia Pacific:** revenue of $9.7M, down 16%.
  - Henry attributed this primarily to its largest China customers waiting for the commercial launch of SPRQ-Nx kits, expected to ship later in May.
  - Gibson added that the decline also reflected a weaker academic funding environment.

### Outlook language

Henry said the company remains confident in delivering revenue growth for the year. He said that although Vega demand is softer than anticipated, "Revio opportunities are increasing with the imminent launch of SPRQ-Nx."

### SPRQ-Nx (new chemistry)

- Henry said SPRQ-Nx makes HiFi sequencing "the most affordable long-read sequencing technology." The economics come from two things:
  - The **Multi-Use SMRT Cell**, which will be commercialized with the ability to use the SMRT Cell 3 times.
  - An increase in SMRT Cell yield. Beta customers saw double-digit yield improvements.
- The beta program went so well that it was significantly expanded in Q1. Some customers are waiting for the full launch, which will occur later in May.
- Henry believes SPRQ-Nx will drive demand for both more Revio systems and more consumables.
- **Vega:** SPRQ-Nx chemistry is expected to launch on Vega later in summer 2026. It will enable significantly more throughput and unlock key features of the SPRQ chemistry, including lower DNA input quantities. Henry said this will increase the platform's utility and value and "accelerate demand for Vega."
- **Margins:** in Q&A, Henry said the multi-use design is a rare case where PacBio can improve customer economics and also raise its own consumables gross margin, with the benefit taking hold in the second half of 2026 and into 2027.

### Strategic actions in Q1

- PacBio closed the sale of its high-throughput short-read sequencing assets to Illumina, generating about $48.1M in net cash proceeds.
- It resolved outstanding litigation with Personal Genomics of Taiwan.
- Henry said these actions "sharpen our focus" and let the company concentrate entirely on long-read sequencing, which it calls its true competitive advantage.

### Clinical strategy and rare disease

- Henry called the clinical opportunity "the most compelling long-term driver" of the business, with clinical shipments up more than 100%.
- His core thesis is that HiFi is "the only commercially available sequencing technology that we believe can comprehensively characterize substantially all classes of variants in a single assay." Short-read approaches need multiple tests to achieve a similar result.
- He claimed faster time to answer, comprehensive output and lower total testing cost.
- Rare disease is expected to be a major driver. About 300 million people are estimated to live with a rare disease, many undiagnosed or misdiagnosed, which Henry sees as reflecting the limits of historic sequencing technology. He believes the market is early in its adoption curve ("early innings").
- Collaborations and studies in progress:
  - **Ambry Genetics:** on track to assess 1,000 patients in its ONCE study. Henry said it shows HiFi "has the power to find what other sequencing technologies have missed."
  - **n-Lorem and EspeRare:** HiFi sequencing across dozens of ultra-rare diseases. Henry said HiFi could help inform therapy recommendations, another validation point beyond diagnosis.
  - **University of Washington:** a program studying sudden unexplained death in childhood across 200 families is "well underway."
  - **DNAstack (announced late February):** a collaboration to launch the first global federated HiFi whole-genome dataset through the HiFi Solves consortium. The consortium has nearly 30 clinical and research institutions across 15 countries. Members have connected, or committed to connect, more than 10,000 HiFi whole genomes, which would be one of the largest and most diverse federated HiFi datasets for rare disease. ("Federated" means the data stay at their sites and are linked for secure international research.)
- **Newborn and carrier screening:** the Babies in Focus project, led by Eurofins Genomics UK (announced Q4 2025), will sequence at least 2,000 samples. It aims to show that long-read whole-genome sequencing gives clinically meaningful improvement in newborn screening, especially for complex and structural variants. It is advancing as planned. Henry expects 1,000 samples to be sequenced on PacBio technology between April and September 2026. He called it foundational evidence for potential inclusion of long-read sequencing in a UK national newborn screening program.

### Basecamp Research (AI project)

- PacBio signed a collaboration to deeply sequence about **100,000 metagenomic samples** (environmental DNA from mixed organisms). Henry called it the largest project using HiFi in PacBio's history and the first scaled use of HiFi for building a biological foundation model (an AI model trained on biological data).
- Basecamp believes model performance scales disproportionately with data quality and diversity, not just model size. It targets a "Trillion Gene Atlas," which could expand known genetic diversity by as much as 100-fold by sequencing up to 100-plus million species.
- The atlas will train Basecamp's EDEN model. Henry said it already shows the ability to go beyond prediction into generative biology, designing therapeutics from sequence and disease prompts, including gene insertion systems, antimicrobial peptides and cell therapies with high experimental hit rates.
- Basecamp chose PacBio because HiFi offers "the most accurate and comprehensive view of the genome." SPRQ-Nx supplies the economics to do this at scale.
- Sequencing is expected to scale up over the course of 2026.

### Gross margin drivers in Q1 (Gibson)

The non-GAAP gross margin fell to 37% from 40% for three reasons:

1. Higher computing component costs, specifically memory. These were flagged on the Q4 call as a possible 2026 headwind and are expected to persist all year.
2. The temporary Q1 Vega promotion, which compressed instrument margins.
3. One-time Q1 items, including inventory adjustments and warranty-related charges.

### Closing remarks (Henry)

Henry listed the quarter's accomplishments as:

- Record consumables revenue.
- Continued sequential strength in EMEA.
- Increasing clinical adoption.
- The Basecamp win.
- Promising SPRQ-Nx beta results, enabling full commercialization later in May.

He said HiFi is "increasingly becoming recognized as an obvious choice as large data sets are created to train advanced AI models for drug discovery." He said the company is focused on raising sequencer throughput and improving HiFi economics through SPRQ-Nx. He said he is "confident in the trajectory of our business and growth as we advance through 2026."

## Question-and-answer session

### Pradeep Ambrose, TD Cowen (for Dan Brennan)

**Q: What does the instrument guide imply, and what visibility is there?**

Henry said Revio is strengthening while Vega is uncertain. Vega, particularly in the Americas, is "more sensitive to the academic and government funding environment." As PacBio focuses on clinical and commercial accounts, it sees more Revio demand. On balance he expects the two to "somewhat balance out," which is why the company still expects to be within the range of the guidance given in February. Both platforms have sales funnels. The Revio funnel has been improving, and Vega in the Americas "has been a bit more challenging."

**Q: How is clinical traction in the US versus outside the US, and what is the outlook for 2026 and 2027?**

- **Outside the US:** Henry said EMEA shows "very, very strong traction." Vega is described as the platform for whole-genome sequencing in rare disease there. Customers are going from validation to increasing full commercialization, which he expects to be a core driver. (This remark was made about Vega, as stated in the call.)
- **In the US:** he said "much of the same thing." The biggest customers are now clinical and commercial accounts. Some have gone commercial, but many are ending their validation phase. He expects them to ramp into full commercial production with both carrier screening assays and whole-genome sequencing for rare disease.
- He expects clinical growth to continue in the US and Europe, noting the over 100% year-over-year growth in clinical consumables. No 2027 figure was given.

### Austin Kennedy, Wolfe Research (for Doug Schenkel)

**Q: What is the exposure to memory pricing in cost of product sales, and what is the margin impact and cadence?**

Henry said the instruments are heavy compute instruments, using DRAM (memory chips), storage and GPUs (graphics processors). The company has mitigated some 2026 risk but expects an impact on gross margin this year. As Gibson noted, gross margin growth should be at the lower end of the range because of these input costs. He said prices "increase pretty regularly" and there is "a lot of variability." PacBio is managing it with supply already on hand and by looking at R&D solutions, which take longer to implement. Over the long run, as DRAM prices normalize, those R&D solutions should help gross margin. He still expects 2026 gross margin to improve over 2025.

**Q: Where did Revio and Vega ASPs land, and are more discounts planned?**

Henry said there are no additional discount programs ongoing or planned. The Vega promotion was a "onetime promotion" aimed at new accounts, and it succeeded, with 85% of Vega sales going to brand-new customers. The company backed off the discount in Q2. Revio ASPs were "reasonably consistent" with prior levels. Vega ASPs were lower in Q1 because of the promotion and are expected to return to more normalized levels in Q2. No ASP figures were given.

### Alexandra Aylen, Canaccord Genuity (for Kyle Mikson)

**Q: Will PacBio launch more PureTarget panels, and what share of revenue could clinical become in the medium to long term?**

(The transcript spells the product "pure target.")

- Henry said the company is "very happy" with PureTarget performance, which is enabling entry into carrier screening. The fastest clinical growth, though, is in whole-genome sequencing for rare disease.
- PacBio is developing variations of the panel so customers can customize their panels somewhat, which he thinks will spread the opportunity.
- On the long-run clinical mix, he said "a very substantial proportion of our business, perhaps as much as more than half of our consumable revenue over time will be clinically driven," and declined to say when that would happen.
- Clinical is making up for weakness in the academic segment, particularly in consumables. Three sequential quarters of record consumables show platform power and, over time, should help gross margin as mix improves.

**Q: How should the upcoming ultra-high-throughput sequencer be factored in (a possible slowdown in Revio orders), alongside SPRQ-Nx and reusable SMRT Cells? Does PacBio see itself as a multi-product vendor, and what has customer feedback been?**

- Henry said the strategy is that three platforms in the market give customers choice across volume levels.
- PacBio will keep improving Revio through reagents and consumables (the SPRQ chemistry and now SPRQ-Nx), adding value for Revio customers.
- Customers who want to operate at very significant scale will find the ultra-high-throughput system the better route. It lowers cost through both sequencing economics and logistics.
- Over the long run he expects all three platforms to find a place: mid-throughput customers as long-term Revio users, and larger clinical accounts moving to ultra-high throughput.
- Vega will improve, with substantially more throughput later in summer and all SPRQ features, such as ultra-low DNA input. Henry said it has the right throughput for applications like AAV (adeno-associated virus, used in gene therapy) and microbial work. He thinks it will find its footing in academic settings and perhaps parts of the clinical market.
- He did not directly address whether Revio orders will slow ahead of the ultra-high-throughput launch, and gave no launch date or customer feedback specifics.

### Karan Patel, Piper Sandler (for David Westenberg)

**Q: What clinical applications drive EMEA growth: rare disease germline, or oncology?**

Henry said the 17% EMEA growth is "on the back of rare disease testing" becoming a first-line test in different countries. He said Europe is structurally a "perfect market" for Revio: a single-payer health system with innovative leaders who see that long-read, especially HiFi, can eliminate several other tests versus short-read approaches while increasing diagnostic yield. This is being demonstrated in multiple countries. EMEA grew substantially even though some shipments to the Middle East (which count in EMEA) were delayed. He fully expects it to be the fastest-growing region again in 2026. He did not mention oncology.

### Mason Carrico, Stephens

**Q: How much of the 2026 consumables guide rests on the existing installed base versus new placements?**

Henry said most of the guide rests on existing customers and their utilization. It is May, and new systems take time to ramp. SPRQ-Nx is "the one variable" being evaluated and will become clearer over the next 2 to 3 months. Some customers held off March shipments of regular SPRQ reagents in anticipation of SPRQ-Nx, so some may place bigger orders earlier once it launches. Visibility to the consumables guide comes mainly from the existing base expanding utilization and, to a lesser extent, new placements.

**Q: What was the feedback on the Vega promotion, how much of Q1 demand did it drive, and what should we expect for Vega placements for the rest of the year?**

- Henry said the promotion was successful, though it is hard to know which customers would have bought without it. A "substantial portion" of the 27 units shipped were under the promotion.
- It was most successful in APAC, a more price-sensitive market. Even with the promotion, few US customers took advantage, which he said reflects funding constraints and helped the company understand that tough academic and government funding environment.
- He believes the funnel allows the company to reach its guidance. He expects Vega to be volatile quarter to quarter, as it was last year, with ASPs moving toward normal levels. The company will see how unit volumes react.

## Points to note

- **Revenue versus consensus:** the call header shows a revenue miss of $2.80M versus consensus. The guidance cut came only at the high end.
- **Instruments:** management attributes the shortfall to three things: US academic and government funding, the Vega promotion (lower ASPs and margin), and Middle East shipment disruption. Management did not quantify the Middle East impact.
- **SPRQ-Nx timing effects:** customers in China and some others deferred consumable purchases in anticipation of the launch, and these deferrals hurt Q1 consumables and APAC revenue. The launch is expected later in May 2026, with Vega availability later in summer.
- **Illumina:** the only direct mention is the closing of the short-read asset sale for about $48.1M net cash in January 2026. No other comments on Illumina or on competitor pricing were made.
- **Memory and compute costs:** these are a gross margin headwind expected to last all of 2026, with only partial mitigation in the short term.
- **Not given:** no analyst ratings, price targets or estimate changes were included in this transcript.
