# PacBio Q2 2026 Earnings Call – CEO transition to Mark Van Oene, SPRQ-Nx launch, lowered 2026 guidance, cash breakeven pushed to 2028

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/PACB/transcript/PACB_2026-08-06_Q2_2026_Earnings_Call.md` · Date: 2026-08-05 · Seeking Alpha transcript of Pacific Biosciences (PacBio) Q2 2026 earnings call (Aug 5, 2026, 4:30 PM ET); speakers Christian Henry (outgoing CEO), Mark Van Oene (COO, now CEO), James "Jim" Gibson (CFO); analysts from Canaccord, Guggenheim, Operon, Stephens, TD Cowen, Piper Sandler, Jefferies, Barclays · earnings call*

**Summary.** PacBio reported Q2 2026 revenue of $39.0M (about flat to slightly down year over year, and a miss versus consensus), announced that CEO Christian Henry is stepping down in favor of COO Mark Van Oene, and launched its SPRQ-Nx chemistry, which cuts the list price of a 20x human genome by 30% to $345. Management lowered 2026 revenue guidance to $155-165M and gross margin guidance to 35-37%, citing a slower SPRQ-Nx adoption curve, higher compute and memory costs and Vega in-house manufacturing transition costs, and pushed cash flow breakeven from end of 2027 to 2028. A restructuring cutting about 40 jobs was announced; clinical (especially EMEA) demand and new population-scale Revio deals were highlights, while US academic funding and Asia Pacific were weak.

# PacBio Q2 2026 Earnings Call (August 5, 2026)

A note on terms used throughout. PacBio (PACB) sells long-read DNA sequencers (instruments that read long, continuous stretches of DNA). Its **HiFi** reads are its high-accuracy long reads. **Revio** is its high-throughput (large-capacity) sequencer. **Vega** is its smaller, lower-cost benchtop sequencer. **SMRT Cell** is the consumable chip on which a run takes place. **SPRQ** is the earlier sequencing chemistry (reagent set), and **SPRQ-Nx** is the new chemistry that lets a SMRT Cell be reused. **Consumables** are the reagent kits and cells that customers must buy for every run. **Pull-through** is consumables revenue per installed system per year. **Non-GAAP** figures exclude certain items (the company says it includes non-cash stock-based compensation in its non-GAAP expenses). **EMEA** means Europe, Middle East and Africa.

The Seeking Alpha header lists the quarter's headline versus consensus: EPS of -$0.14, missing by $0.01, and revenue of $39.01M (-1.91% year over year), missing by $1.57M. [Seeking Alpha summary, Q2 2026]

One housekeeping oddity: the operator and the call-opening speaker (Caylene Parrish, Gilmartin Group, investor relations) introduced the participants as Mark Van Oene "President and Chief Executive Officer," Jim Gibson as CFO, and Christian Henry as "PacBio board member and advisor." This reflects the leadership change announced the same day.

## Leadership change and reorganization

### CEO transition
- Christian Henry announced he is stepping down as President and CEO of PacBio, with Mark Van Oene leading the company as President and CEO "effective immediately." Henry stays on the Board of Directors and becomes an advisor to Van Oene. [Henry, Q2 2026 call]
- Henry said Van Oene joined shortly after he did and has led R&D operations and the commercial organizations. He said Van Oene's "deep understanding of the genomics and clinical markets will be invaluable to the company as we move deeper into supporting clinical sequencing around the globe." [Henry, Q2 2026 call]
- Henry said that in his nearly 6 years leading PacBio the company developed and launched "groundbreaking new long-read sequencers that have dramatically improved the scale and economics of long-read sequencing." He thanked employees, customers and collaborators. [Henry, Q2 2026 call]
- Van Oene said he joined in 2021 as Chief Operating Officer, led the R&D organization that built the Revio and Vega instruments, and more recently oversaw development and rollout of the multi-use SPRQ-Nx chemistry. His recent commercial focus leveraged "clinical account engagement," which he said has been particularly effective in EMEA. [Van Oene, Q2 2026 call]
- Van Oene's stated priorities: take what has worked in EMEA and scale it globally; drive SPRQ-Nx adoption across accounts; and grow understanding of disease biology and biomarker discovery by enabling greater HiFi throughput, cost efficiency and data access. In closing he said his focus as CEO is "Scale is driving growth, sharpen our execution, and run a leaner, more focused organization built around our highest conviction growth drivers," and that he has "spent 5 years in this business." [Van Oene, Q2 2026 call]
- Analysts (Canaccord's Kyle Mikson, Jefferies' Lauren Timmins, Barclays' Luke Sergott, Operon's Jack Meehan) congratulated Van Oene. Mikson said of Henry's tenure "There's been the highs and the lows, but it's been overall a really impressive job." Sergott joked he hoped to still see Henry at AGBT (the genomics industry conference), "perennial on the dance floor."

### Targeted reorganization and restructuring
- Van Oene said a targeted reorganization began "late last week" (late July / early August 2026). Marketing is being integrated more closely with the rest of the commercial organization to maximize clinical-market growth opportunities, and management spans and layers were reduced. He stressed "none of our key R&D platform projects were impacted." He described this as "operating with a leaner team focused on our highest priority growth drivers." [Van Oene, Q2 2026 call]
- CFO Gibson said the restructuring is expected to reduce the workforce by about 40 employees and lower the ongoing operating expense base while preserving investment in top growth priorities. Headcount at end of Q2 2026 was 492, versus 485 at end of 2025 and 491 at end of Q2 2025. [Gibson, Q2 2026 call]

## Q2 2026 financial results

### Revenue overview
Total revenue was $39.0M in Q2 2026, versus $39.8M in Q2 2025 (roughly flat year over year; Van Oene described it as a step up from Q1). Management attributed the result to growing consumables and new revenue from Vega placements as SPRQ-Nx rolled out, offset by weaker instruments and service/other. [Gibson and Van Oene, Q2 2026 call]

The table below compares the revenue lines for Q2 2026 against Q2 2025, as stated by the CFO.

| Line | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instrument revenue | $12.8M | $14.2M | -9% |
| Consumables revenue | $20.1M | $18.9M | +6% |
| Service and other | $6.1M | $6.7M | down |
| Total | $39.0M | $39.8M | roughly -2% |

- **Instruments:** The decline reflected a lower average selling price (ASP) driven by customer mix, including lower-priced strategic Revio placements to key accounts and fewer Vega shipments as academic and government funding constraints continued to pressure capital purchases. [Gibson]
- **Consumables:** Growth came from the larger installed base and continued Revio utilization, especially among clinical customers, partly offset by customers working through existing inventory and completing SPRQ-Nx workflow validation before broader adoption. Annualized pull-through was about $202,000 per system. [Gibson]
- **Service and other:** Revio service contracts grew with the installed base, offset by lower revenue as a population sequencing program was completed. [Gibson]

### Regional revenue
Americas was down slightly, Asia Pacific fell sharply and EMEA grew strongly, as the table summarizes.

| Region | Q2 2026 revenue | Change vs Q2 2025 | Drivers cited |
|---|---|---|---|
| Americas | $17.6M | down slightly | Ongoing NIH and broader academic funding uncertainty weighing on capital purchases; clinical and commercial activity resilient; Vega installed base expanding in public health labs |
| Asia Pacific | $7.0M | -45% | Conclusion of a significant population sequencing program; academic/government funding headwinds; lower consumables as customers completed SPRQ-Nx validation and used up existing reagent inventory |
| EMEA | $14.4M | +52% | Hospitals and clinical customers moving from pilots to routine production; growing Vega demand; a significant strategic multi-system Revio placement for a large national genomics initiative |

Van Oene said EMEA is expected to remain the fastest-growing region in 2026. [Q2 2026 call]

### Margins, costs, cash and earnings
- **Gross profit:** Non-GAAP gross profit was $13.9M, a 36% non-GAAP gross margin, versus $15.2M and 38% in Q2 2025. The decline was attributed to previously discussed compute cost inflation (higher cost of computing hardware used for data processing) and lower manufacturing volumes, plus $1.1M of costs for moving Vega manufacturing in-house from a contract manufacturer. Those transition costs are expected to end by the end of 2026. [Gibson]
- **Memory purchases:** Cash outflows were higher because of strategic purchases of memory components for future production. Gibson called these "largely timing-related" and said PacBio now expects to have enough memory to support operations through the end of 2026. [Gibson]
- **Operating expenses:** Non-GAAP operating expenses were $56.1M, down 3% from $58.1M in Q2 2025, reflecting expense discipline while maintaining top strategic investments. They included $8.6M of non-cash share-based compensation versus $11.0M a year earlier. [Gibson]
- **Net loss:** Non-GAAP net loss was $41.9M, or $0.14 per share, versus $40.0M, or $0.13 per share, in Q2 2025. [Gibson]
- **Cash:** About $236.9M of unrestricted cash, cash equivalents and investments at quarter end, versus $279.5M at December 31, 2025. [Gibson]

## Operating metrics

### Consumables and pull-through
- Consumable revenue was $20.1M versus $18.9M a year earlier. Shipments to clinical customers grew 67% year over year and were a mid-teens percentage of total consumable shipments. Management expects clinical shipments to keep growing as customers move into full commercialization across the installed base. [Van Oene]
- **Pull-through guidance lowered:** Full-year 2026 consumables pull-through is now expected to be $200,000 to $225,000 per Revio system, "due to the pace of demand we are experiencing today." Van Oene said the narrow range reflects purchase timing, since several accounts that received large Q1 shipments are working through inventory while evaluating the multi-use feature. He expects the range to rise as the SPRQ-Nx transition continues into late 2026 and 2027, and the first wave of SPRQ-Nx consumable reorders to come "in the coming months." Longer term, lower cost per genome should support higher utilization. [Van Oene] (Prior-range figures were not given on the call.)

### Revio (high-throughput sequencer)
- 20 Revio systems were sold in Q2 2026, versus 15 in Q2 2025. Cumulative shipments reached 366. [Van Oene, Gibson]
- Several multi-unit shipments occurred: one to a single new-to-PacBio customer, and two to existing customers expanding production fleets. Van Oene called this a "testament to the appeal of Revio technology and SPRQ-Nx economics." [Van Oene]
- 60% of Q2 Revio placements went to new customers, and 45% were sold as part of multi-instrument purchase orders. [Van Oene]
- PacBio closed and shipped a significant order of several Revio systems to a new population-scale customer expected to begin sequencing in Q3 2026. Together with the Basecamp opportunity announced in Q1, Van Oene said this signals PacBio's "entry into larger population-level genomic studies which have been unlocked with SPRQ-Nx." [Van Oene]
- Some clinical customers are "exit[ing] R&D mode" and moving into routine production sequencing with HiFi. [Van Oene]

### Vega (benchtop sequencer)
- 26 Vega systems were sold, versus 38 in Q2 2025. Cumulative shipments are 200. 81% of Q2 Vega shipments went to new customers. [Van Oene, Gibson]
- Customer conversations are "constructive," but U.S. funding uncertainty continues to constrain new orders. [Van Oene]
- Two reasons for continued conviction: (1) Vega ASP "has returned to normalized levels," showing demand can be driven without the promotional pricing offered in Q1; (2) U.S. public health labs, a segment PacBio deliberately built out, bought Vega instruments this quarter, and more consistent utilization is expected as they ramp. [Van Oene]
- In Q&A, Van Oene said Vega unit volume was consistent with the prior quarter and mostly to brand-new customers, in line with the aim of decentralizing HiFi sequencing. Applications remain mainly smaller targeted ones, though public health labs are beginning to use it for wastewater testing and some higher-throughput whole-genome applications. [Van Oene]

## SPRQ-Nx chemistry

- **Rollout:** Q2 featured the full global commercial rollout. Beta access was in high demand in Q1 and feedback was highly positive. In June, over a third of the installed base opted into the new consumable software that enables SPRQ-Nx, particularly multi-use. [Van Oene]
- **Multi-use:** SPRQ-Nx raises throughput per run, and new customers can use each SMRT Cell up to 3 times. This improves customer economics and allows PacBio to compete for much larger projects. [Van Oene]
- **Pricing:** U.S. list price falls to $345 per 20x HiFi human genome (20x means each position is read about 20 times on average), a 30% reduction versus the previous SPRQ chemistry, "without compromising the accuracy or comprehensiveness." Expanded methylation detection (chemical tags on DNA) and advances in DeepConsensus, an AI-powered consensus algorithm developed with Google, improve accuracy, run performance and biological information. [Van Oene]
- **Early data:** In the first full month of rollout, HiFi yield was "near identical across the first two uses with a slight decline on the third." [Van Oene]
- **Adoption targets:** Over half of the installed base is expected to have adopted the SPRQ-Nx software by end of Q3 2026, and the vast majority by year-end. More SPRQ-Nx usage should bring more throughput and expand gross margins. Many high-throughput customers are validating multi-use workflows in their own labs and are expected to ramp over the coming months. Van Oene believes SPRQ-Nx "will be a significant driver of volume in the second half of the year and beyond." [Van Oene]
- **Vega launch:** SPRQ-Nx launches on Vega later in August 2026, with up to 90 gigabases per run (versus 60 on the current Vega) and lower DNA input needs, harmonizing chemistry across both instruments. [Van Oene]
- Van Oene said SPRQ-Nx "has changed the math for high-throughput Revio customers who have been waiting for long-read sequencing to become economically viable at scale," with feedback "overwhelmingly positive."

## Scientific and clinical validation

- **NEJM (June 13, 2026):** "Clinical Long-Read Genome Sequencing for Rare Disease Diagnostics" (Bitter et al.). Concordance between long-read genome sequencing and standard of care was 96.4%. Long-read improved or refined diagnoses in 3.4% of cases; standard of care caught variants long reads missed in only 0.2%. Van Oene said it shows HiFi is a clinically effective first-tier test that improves diagnostic yield, simplifies lab workflow, reduces turnaround time and improves economics. He said a New England Journal of Medicine paper "carries real weight with payers and health systems" and accelerates the move away from legacy diagnostic workflows. [Van Oene]
- **Nature Genetics:** "Near-Perfect Genome Sequencing in Medical Genetics" (Sabbagh et al.). It proposes long-read sequencing as one pillar of a convergence with diploid genome assembly, pangenome references and AI-driven variant interpretation ("near-perfect genome sequencing"), with potential in post-natal, pre-natal and oncology settings and a staged implementation roadmap toward a "one-test paradigm." [Van Oene]
- **Subfertility preprint:** The HiFi Solves subfertility consortium (Asia Pacific) posted its first major study. Subfertility affects about 1 in 6 couples globally; current evaluation is fragmented, taking months or years, often without answers. HiFi genome sequencing can give a complete view in a single workflow, a long-term clinical opportunity. [Van Oene]
- **Basecamp Research:** Samples are in-house, with hundreds sequenced and delivered each week. Basecamp should contribute more meaningfully in 2027, when most samples will be processed. [Van Oene]

## 2026 guidance (updated Aug 5, 2026)

| Item | New guidance | Prior / comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $155M to $165M (lowered) | Prior figure not stated on the call |
| Non-GAAP gross margin | 35% to 37% | Lowered; Q2 actual 36% |
| Non-GAAP operating expenses | $215M to $220M | $5M lower than Q1 guidance; down from 2025 |
| Year-end cash | about $175M to $185M | Q2-end cash $236.9M |
| Consumables pull-through per Revio | $200K to $225K | Lowered, per Van Oene |
| Cash flow breakeven | 2028 | Previously end of 2027 |

- **Revenue assumptions:** Consumables remain the growth driver, supported by clinical utilization and the expanding Revio and Vega base. SPRQ-Nx adoption builds progressively through H2 as customers finish validation and use up reagent inventory. No meaningful recovery in academic and government funding is assumed, particularly in the Americas, and a more gradual recovery in China than previously expected. [Gibson]
- **Gross margin drivers:** Temporary Vega manufacturing transition costs of about $2.5M, elevated compute and memory costs, a more gradual SPRQ-Nx curve, and the margin effect of lower-priced strategic Revio placements. [Gibson]
- **Cash:** The year-end cash outlook reflects the revenue outlook, continued SPRQ-Nx investment, temporary manufacturing costs, working capital impacts and the cost reductions. [Gibson]
- **2027 outlook:** Cash burn is expected to "step down meaningfully." The restructuring should cut compensation-related expenses by about $15M to $20M next year, and a further ~$15M to $20M of annual savings should come as development spending on the high-throughput platform ends. Elevated compute costs and slower gross margin improvement remain. Gibson said existing cash is sufficient "to execute our strategic priorities, support the commercialization of our new high-throughput platform, and fund the business through cash flow breakeven." [Gibson]

## Question-and-answer session

### Kyle Mikson, Canaccord Genuity
**Question:** How many of the new Revio and Vega customers are clinical, and how is that shaping up?
**Answer (Van Oene):** Revio had a "good rebound" with 20 shipments, mostly to new customers and including multi-system placements, which is central to the SPRQ-Nx thesis (economics both scale existing customers and bring in new ones). The majority of Revio placements are to clinical accounts, showing momentum on instruments as well as consumables. Vega units were lower than last year but consistent with last quarter, mostly to brand-new customers, for decentralizing HiFi; applications are still mainly smaller targeted ones, with public health labs picking up wastewater testing and some whole-genome use. He also thanked Henry, saying his "industry knowledge and experience really has transformed PacBio."

### Thomas VonDerVellen (for Subbu Nambi), Guggenheim Securities
**Question:** Given population-scale Revio placements, consumables timing and clinical accounts, what should sequential pull-through look like in Q3 and Q4?
**Answer (Van Oene):** PacBio will name the population-genome customer "when the timing's right." Clinical customers scale with faster consumables uptake driven by SPRQ-Nx economics, starting to scale through the back half. Consumables revenue was relatively flat quarter over quarter in Q2; he expects that to continue through Q3 and then scale toward the end of the year, as customers optimize workflows, get used to running SMRT Cells multiple times and harden validation.

### Jack Meehan, Operon Research
**Question:** How much memory supply is locked in for H2 and 2027, what is the headwind if spot prices hold, and when will multi-use SMRT Cells help gross margin?
**Answer (Gibson):** PacBio is supplied with memory and GPU coverage through the end of 2026 on current forecasts and will probably re-enter the market late in 2026. The gross margin cut to 35-37% assumes memory impact persists through the rest of 2026; there is no good visibility into 2027, which partly drove the restructuring. Unanticipated Vega transition costs are also hitting margin, though they accelerate the in-house manufacturing move, cutting suppliers and cost long term and helping 2027. The SPRQ-Nx ramp is expected to build more in the latter half of H2, offsetting some memory and transition costs. He does not expect memory prices to fall but hopes for stabilization; spot-market fluctuations caused the unanticipated impacts, and "early indicators" suggest stabilizing.

### Mason Carrico, Stephens
**Question:** Early signs that the 30-40% cost reduction will expand sample volumes enough to offset the price discount, and the sequential pacing?
**Answer (Van Oene):** SPRQ-Nx is critical for establishing PacBio in larger programs (seen in multi-system Revio deals) and for gross margin expansion. He expects to overcome the 30% price decrease through H2 growth, and the timing is "relatively short term." One-third of customers have already upgraded the software. The Asia Pacific consumables drop in Q2 was customers burning down SMRT inventory ahead of SPRQ-Nx shipments at scale. He expects the gap to close during Q3 and as the year ends.

### Pradeep Ambrose (for Dan Brennan), TD Cowen
**Question:** Will Vega consumables see the same inventory and validation transition after launch as Revio?
**Answer (Van Oene):** No, mostly because Vega utilization is lower than Revio's. Instead SPRQ-Nx on Vega (90 gigabases versus 60, lower DNA input) should expand applications: larger gene panels, larger amplicon sets (targeted DNA fragments), the occasional whole human or non-human genome, and drive utilization upward.

### Skye Gilbert (for David Westenberg), Piper Sandler
**Question:** As the industry shifts to multiomics, what is long-read's advantage and what are biopharma partners seeking?
**Answer (Van Oene):** He called this where PacBio is "most differentiated," with the most comprehensive clinical genome. Interest is rising in repeat expansions, structural variation and diploid genomics (as in the Near-Perfect Genome paper); methylation reference sets and signatures are expanding demand, including chromatin architecture. In RNA, demand for isoforms (different versions of transcripts) across development and conditions is pushing the field toward true transcriptomics rather than simple gene expression, and DNA plus RNA works well together. The comprehensive, multiomic data is attractive for future-proofed AI model development, as with the Basecamp deal, and comes up increasingly with biopharma and the research community.

### Lauren Timmins (for Tycho Peterson), Jefferies
**Question:** Guidance was lowered despite the SPRQ-Nx launch on both systems, H2 ramp, a new population-scale customer and accelerating Revio placements. What isn't showing up in the positive signals?
**Answer (Van Oene):** The $155-165M range "does reflect the confidence in our ability to execute," but traction sits in the context of the SPRQ-Nx transition, so the range is appropriate. The compute cost impact is "real"; PacBio is seeking mitigations and has pre-purchased some, and the gross margin guide reflects uncertainty and real headwinds. SPRQ-Nx transition and compute cost headwinds are why cash flow breakeven moved out to 2028; he called it "a really balanced view."

### Luke Sergott, Barclays
**Question:** What spurred the commercial rework, what will the new structure look like, and what other investments are needed given AI and population-sequencing customers?
**Answer (Van Oene):** The clinical team is doing well, and the aim is to take European clinical traction into the rest of the world as a combined motion. The restructuring mostly concerned marketing, focusing it on clinical workflows and on educating the market about HiFi's usefulness; sales and marketing are aligned toward the clinical opportunity. "Don't expect a massive change"; PacBio will keep supporting plant, animal and research communities if budgets unlock. Biopharma and data-strategy deals are large, lumpy and handled in business or corporate development, so he does not see them as a distraction.

## Key themes and risks (as stated by management)
- **Funding:** NIH, academic and government funding constraints in the Americas and for Vega orders; no meaningful recovery assumed; slower China recovery than previously assumed.
- **Transition timing:** Customers burning down SPRQ inventory and validating multi-use workflows depress near-term consumables (flat sequentially through Q3), with a ramp expected late 2026.
- **Cost pressure:** Compute and memory cost inflation, spot-market volatility, no visibility into 2027 memory, and Vega in-house transition costs (about $2.5M in 2026, $1.1M in Q2).
- **Mix and pricing:** Lower-priced strategic Revio placements lower instrument ASP and margin; the 30% genome list price cut must be offset by volume.
- **Opportunities:** Clinical adoption (consumable clinical shipments +67%, EMEA +52%), population-scale genomics, AI/data deals such as Basecamp (more meaningful in 2027), and payer-relevant clinical evidence such as the NEJM paper.
- **Illumina and competitors:** Illumina and other competitors were not named in this call. The only competitive reference is Van Oene's statement that SPRQ-Nx economics let PacBio "compete for substantially larger projects where competitive economics are crucial to winning" and are aimed at customers waiting for long-read sequencing to become economically viable at scale.
