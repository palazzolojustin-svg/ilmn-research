# Barclays on Pacific Biosciences (PACB) 3Q25 results: consumables strong, instruments and Vega placements weak, Equal Weight and $1.50 target maintained

*Source file: `Working Notes/Sources/PACB/research/20251113_Barclays_PACB_Pacbio-_For_Every_2_Steps_Forward_in_Consumables-_Instru.md` · Date: 2025-11-05 · Barclays Research (U.S. Life Science & Diagnostic Tools); analysts Luke Sergott, Salem Salem, Anna Kruszenski (Jacob Putman listed on last page) · sell-side research*

**Summary.** Barclays reviews Pacific Biosciences' (PACB) third-quarter 2025 results. Total revenue missed the Street because instruments and Vega placements were light, but consumables beat and gross margin (42.1%) was well above expectations. Management trimmed the top end of full-year 2025 revenue guidance to $155M-$160M, and Barclays kept its Equal Weight rating and $1.50 price target (4x 2026 revenue estimate of $169M) while waiting for proof of faster growth and broader adoption.

# Barclays on PACB, 3Q25 Results

Publication: Barclays Research note dated 5 November 2025 (released 6 November 2025, 00:51 GMT). Authors: Luke Sergott, Salem Salem and Anna Kruszenski, U.S. Life Science & Diagnostic Tools team. The note is titled "For Every 2 Steps Forward in Consumables, Instruments Take a Step Back."

## Rating, price target and valuation

**Rating and target.** Barclays rates Pacbio (PACB) Equal Weight, unchanged. This is a relative rating, meaning Barclays expects the stock to perform in line with the average expected return of the other U.S. life science and diagnostic tools companies it covers over 12 months. The industry view is Neutral, also unchanged. The price target is $1.50, unchanged. The stock closed at $1.92 on 5 November 2025, so the target implies about 21.7% downside.

**Valuation method.** The $1.50 target applies 4x EV/revenue (enterprise value, meaning market value plus net debt, divided by sales) to Barclays' 2026 revenue estimate of $169M. The prior target used 4x on $172M, so the 2026 revenue estimate was cut by about $3M while the multiple stayed the same.

**Why the target did not move.** Barclays says it maintains the target "given the volatility in shares due to the illiquidity of the stock and as our estimates did not change that much."

**Why Equal Weight.** Barclays says it remains "on the sidelines." It likes the underlying technology, the revamped business and story, the strong management team and the innovative launches. However, the stock trades at a 20% premium to peers. Barclays also cites a volatile macro backdrop (the broader economic and funding environment) and says it wants to "see more proof points" of material growth acceleration and more material market adoption of the key platforms.

**Stated risks to the target.**
- Upside: (1) macro conditions ease and investors return to growth names; (2) the funding environment improves and customer spending accelerates; (3) demand and use cases for long-read sequencing ramp faster than expected, with scientists flocking to PACB as the premier long-read provider.
- Downside: (1) macro headwinds persist longer than expected or worsen, causing investors to shy away from high-multiple names; (2) customers tighten their belts further and demand weakens.

## Barclays' core view of the quarter

**Overall.** Barclays calls it "a mixed quarter with few surprises given the tough macro." The note's framing is that consumables (the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run) gained ground, while instruments (the sequencers themselves) lost it. Investor focus remains on the soft capital-equipment spending environment and on Vega placements declining quarter over quarter.

**Instruments.** Instruments came in light. Vega (PACB's lower-priced benchtop long-read sequencer) declined sequentially in the first year of its launch. Barclays says it does not want to "make too much of this dynamic," because a handful of boxes were pushed into 4Q. It adds that, given how tough the academic and government (A&G) market is, it does not make much of the quarter's instrument results, though the sequential declines stood out.

**Consumables and mix.** What "really stood out" to Barclays was consumables growth and the resulting mix benefit to gross margin (more high-margin consumables in the revenue mix). Revio (PACB's high-throughput long-read system) pull-through (the consumables revenue each installed system generates) ran at the high end of management's range. Barclays attributes this to "all the market education and marketing the team has done over the past 2 years to get HiFi out there in the hands of researchers/clinicians." HiFi refers to PACB's high-accuracy long-read sequencing method.

**Clinical traction.** Barclays says clinical utility is starting to show up, citing an EU consortium paper showing HiFi long-reads are "much better than standard testing technologies for carrier screening and hard to sequence genes." Carrier screening tests healthy people for gene variants they could pass to children. Barclays thinks this gives PACB "a good foothold in the clinical market for them to expand upon."

**Cost and remaining hurdles.** SPRQ-nx (PACB's next-generation chemistry and consumables, launching in beta) brings the cost of sequencing down to be on par with high-throughput peers. Barclays says the remaining hurdles are up-front workflows and costs, and simplifying and streamlining data analysis. These will require further innovation in addition to the focus on broadening the sequencing portfolio.

**Long-term framing.**
- Barclays sees early signs of market penetration and expansion from the consumables pickup, but says "we are still at the early days and the story will take time to play out."
- PACB's platform is a "substitution model for an already existing market." Barclays says that if PACB gained 2% market share, "this would essentially double revenues from here."
- On quality, long-reads are clearly better than short-reads, but "from a cost, speed, and ease-of-use perspective they have never been on par." Management is addressing this, but "it [takes] a lot to dethrone the entrenched technologies."

**Near-term outlook.** A&G capex (capital spending by academic and government labs) demand is expected to stay soft or get worse, which will weigh on investors' views of 2026.

**Margins.** Gross margin (GM) came in "quite strong" at 42% and should keep improving from the low-40s as management drives efficiency and product improvements on the consumables side. Barclays says this "could eventually lead to material GM expansion when vols reach critical capacity utilization" (when manufacturing volume is high enough to spread fixed costs).

## Guidance update (management, 3Q25 call)

- **FY25 revenue:** $155M-$160M, versus $155M-$165M previously. Management lowered the high end by $5M.
- **4Q25 revenue:** about +10% sequentially (versus 3Q25).
- **Placements:** Revio and Vega placements are expected to exceed the quarterly placements of the year to date, so both should hit highs for the year in 4Q.
- **Gross margin exit rate:** above 40%, unchanged. Barclays notes margin should remain above 40% in 4Q with room for improvement afterward.
- **2026:** no formal guidance, but management said revenue should grow in 2026, based on a growing installed base, an improving sales funnel, steady instrument placements like those seen in 2025, and strong consumable utilization.
- **Cash flow:** positive cash flow by the end of 2027 (reiterated). FY25 cash burn reiterated at $115M.

## Key takeaways from the call

### Placements and the Vega miss
- About 75% of Revio shipments went to new customers, and about 60% of Vega placements went to new customers.
- Vega shipments came in below forecast, particularly in Europe, because procurement processes extended beyond the end of 3Q. Some delayed purchase orders have already been received and are expected to ship in 4Q.
- One Revio failed installation testing and shifts to 4Q revenue recognition. Management said this unit had a "much higher ASP" (average selling price).

### Pull-through and pricing
- Revio pull-through was $236k per system on an annualized basis, at the high end of management's expected range. Management cited durable demand and strong utilization at top accounts.
- Instrument ASPs dipped in 3Q because PACB placed systems at lower prices at key institutions, with the intent of driving high throughput and consumable use (strategic placements). Management expects ASPs to recover going forward.

### Regional performance (year over year)
- EMEA (Europe, Middle East and Africa) led growth at +18%, driven by 50% consumables growth, partly offset by the Vega miss from procurement delays.
- Americas fell 10% as A&G funding remains soft, though revenue grew 2% sequentially.
- APAC (Asia-Pacific) fell 11%, although China performed better than management expected and the region continues to show strength.

### SPRQ-nx
- Expected to lower the cost of human whole-genome sequencing (WGS) to below $300 per genome at scale, making long-read economically competitive with many short-read platforms.
- More than 100 customers expressed interest in the beta. Beta testers pay for consumables, starting this month (November 2025). Early access begins in early 2026, followed by a broad rollout in 2026.
- Barclays' headline also mentions new partnerships, "pop seq" (population-scale sequencing) programs and clinical penetration as updates, but the note gives no further detail on them.
