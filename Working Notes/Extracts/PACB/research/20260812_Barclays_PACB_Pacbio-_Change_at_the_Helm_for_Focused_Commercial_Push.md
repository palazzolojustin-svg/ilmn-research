**Summary.** Barclays reacts to Pacbio's Q2 2026 results, the immediate handover of the CEO role from Christian Henry to COO Mark Van Oene, a reorganization of about 40 jobs, and a cut to 2026 revenue guidance and gross margin guidance. Barclays keeps its Underweight rating and lowers its price target to $1.25 from $1.50, but argues the guide cut reflects a temporary "air pocket" from the SPRQ-Nx chemistry transition rather than weak demand. ## Core thesis and the analyst's commentary

### The management change

Barclays says it "truly enjoyed" its time with outgoing CEO Christian Henry. It credits him with instilling a new vision and bringing the technology "into the forefront of genomics." In a lighter note, Barclays says his tenure "wasn't always smooth sailing, but he always made it fun."

Incoming CEO Mark Van Oene is described as continuing the strategy of penetrating the clinical opportunity "in a focused way." Barclays says he laid out specific key performance indicators (KPIs, measurable goals) for the next year:

1. Drive clinical adoption and convert users from pilot programs and validation stages into production volumes.
2. Convert customers to SPRQ-Nx quickly. SPRQ-Nx is Pacbio's new sequencing chemistry, which management says improves economics for large-scale sequencing.
3. Get the high-throughput (HT) platform out next year, so it can handle volumes from Basecamp at an "approachable cost." Barclays' headline says the aim is "to complete the HT platform capable of handling the expected volumes from Basecamp." The note does not explain Basecamp further; the call notes call it a population-scale program, with a separate new population-scale customer.

### Barclays' view of what is hardest

Barclays thinks the harder goal is converting clinical customers into production-stage volumes, "particularly in the US and in smaller labs."

- **Europe and national health systems:** management "has done a good job" getting HiFi (Pacbio's high-accuracy long-read sequencing method) adopted for rare disease testing.
- **US:** the market is "very different and more fragmented." The reasons clinical labs have not scaled vary by customer. Barclays lists these:
  - an inability to handle the data analysis;
  - a desire for more automated upfront (sample preparation) workflows;
  - getting used to the technology versus a legacy bias toward short-read sequencing.
- **Implication:** this will be "more hand-to-hand" for the commercial organization. Barclays says management has already begun changing the commercial team, becoming "more targeted and strategic with marketing," and "bringing in the right people for the right clinical customers to press the conversion."

### Why the guidance cut is "not as bad as it looks"

Barclays says change is good and understands the management transition came together with the reorganization, the guidance cut, and a tighter strategy. Its reasoning on guidance:

- The cut is more about an "air pocket" caused by the SPRQ-Nx transition than poor demand for the technology.
- Most existing Revio customers who can convert should be ready to transition by year-end 2026.
- As a result, the air pocket is likely to drag out to a trough in 3Q and 4Q 2026, which led to the guide cut.
- Barclays calls the headline "disappointing," but says "when we dig deeper it is not as bad as it looks." It describes the quarter as one with "a lot of changes" where "time will tell on the success of the new strategy."

## Key notes from the call (Q2 2026, as summarized by Barclays)

### CEO transition effective immediately

- CEO duties passed immediately from Christian Henry to COO Mark Van Oene. Van Oene previously led R&D, operations and commercial execution, and the rollout of Revio, Vega and SPRQ-Nx.
- Henry remains on the Board and serves in an advisory capacity to Van Oene.
- Van Oene highlighted the EMEA (Europe, Middle East, Africa) clinical commercial success as the operating model he wants to scale globally. His priorities are clinical sequencing adoption, SPRQ-Nx utilization, and enabling larger-scale genomics programs.

### Revio placements beat expectations

Revio is Pacbio's main high-throughput long-read sequencer.

- Pacbio shipped 20 Revio systems in 2Q26, versus Barclays' estimate of 15. Of these, 60% went to new customers and 45% were sold as part of multi-instrument orders.
- Several multi-system orders were tied to customers scaling production sequencing programs. These included clinical accounts and population-genomics initiatives unlocked by SPRQ-Nx economics.
- Management highlighted a significant new population-scale customer, incremental to Basecamp. Barclays reads this as evidence of traction in large-scale sequencing markets.
- Revio pull-through was $202K in the quarter, pressured by the SPRQ-Nx transition, which led to the lowered annual pull-through guide.

### Vega still pressured by academic and government weakness

Vega is Pacbio's smaller, lower-cost benchtop sequencer.

- Vega shipments were 26 systems, versus 38 in the same quarter a year earlier. A&G (academic and government) funding constraints continued to pressure capital spending.
- Despite lower volume, 81% of Vega placements went to new customers. Management said Vega average selling prices (ASPs) returned to normal levels after promotional pricing used in 1Q26.
- Public health laboratories were an area of strength. Wastewater testing and other smaller or targeted applications are beginning to contribute to adoption.

### Reorganization

- The restructuring will cut the workforce by about 40 employees and realign resources to high-priority growth initiatives.
- Marketing was integrated more closely with the commercial organization to improve engagement with clinical customers and replicate successful EMEA go-to-market practices globally.
- Management explicitly said no key R&D platform programs were affected.
- $15M–$20M in annual compensation expense savings are expected to benefit 2027.

### Clinical momentum offsets academic weakness

- Clinical consumables shipments grew 67% year over year in 2Q26 and represented a mid-teens percentage of total consumables shipments.
- Multiple publications in the New England Journal of Medicine and Nature Genetics were highlighted as strengthening the evidence for HiFi long-read sequencing as a first-line clinical diagnostic approach.
- A&G funding constraints remained the primary headwind across the Americas and parts of APAC (Asia-Pacific). No meaningful recovery is assumed in guidance.

### EMEA is the growth engine

- EMEA revenue rose 52% year over year, driven by hospitals and clinical customers moving from pilot projects to routine production sequencing.
- Pacbio pointed to this as the model for the Americas and APAC. Clinical account engagement strategies developed in EMEA are being integrated into the wider commercial organization.

### SPRQ-Nx adoption

- Over a third of the installed base adopted SPRQ-Nx-enabled software during June 2026.
- Management expects more than half of the installed base to upgrade by the end of 3Q26 and a majority by year-end.
- Validation cycles (customers verifying the new chemistry before switching) delay pull-through, which drove the guide-down.

## Risks

- **Downside risks to Barclays' view (from the scenario text):** macro headwinds lasting longer or worsening; a guidance miss causing multiple contraction; an extended demand pause on capital purchases from weak academic markets.
- **Execution risks flagged in the commentary:** converting US and small clinical labs from pilot to production is difficult and customer-specific; the SPRQ-Nx "air pocket" could deepen in 3Q/4Q 2026; academic funding shows no meaningful recovery; China recovery is slower than expected; and the new strategy and management are untested ("time will tell").
- **Upside risks:** see the Rating section above.
