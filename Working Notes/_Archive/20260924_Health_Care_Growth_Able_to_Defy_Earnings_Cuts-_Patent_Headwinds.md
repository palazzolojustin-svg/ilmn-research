# **European Health Care: Quality Intact, Momentum Missing** 

Read Research Report: European Healthcare Autumn Outlook 



Laurent Douillet Simbarashe Gumbo Team: Strategy Team: Strategy BI Senior Equity Strategist BI Associate Analyst 

Health-Care Growth Able to Defy Earnings Cuts, Patent Headwinds 

(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- European health-care 2H and 2027 consensus earnings look conservative following a solid 1H, even with the headwinds of intensifying patent expirations, US pricing pressure and Novo Nordisk's setbacks exerting a drag. Our operating-leverage assessment and mid-single-digit sales growth and M&A tailwinds point to sustained resilience. (09/24/26) 

## 1. Patent-Cliff Hit Eased by Robust European Pharma Growth 

European pharma faces a significant patent cliff, with Bloomberg Intelligence industry analysts calculating generic competition could erode about $21 billion of sales over 2026-28 for the top seven companies, equivalent to 5.6% of their 2025 revenue base. BI's analysis suggests that more than $60 billion of sales are exposed, with GSK, AstraZeneca and Roche facing the greatest risks. So far, recent launches and existing growth products have cushioned part of the expected blow, with the MSCI European Pharmaceutical Index delivering 8.2% sales growth in 1H ($ terms), exceeding the pace of the prior two years and market expectations by about 1 percentage point. 

Consensus points to sales growth slowing to around 5% in 2H and 2027, though estimates may still prove conservative after Sanofi, UCB, Merck and GSK all raised their sales guidance during the 2Q reporting season, and no companies lowered their outlooks. (09/24/26)

## 3. Margin Expectations Demanding, But Achievable 

European health-care earnings growth through 2028 is expected to be driven by a combination of 6% annual sales growth and solid margin expansion of 180 bps, based on consensus. Cost-control initiatives at AstraZeneca and Sanofi, restructuring at Novo Nordisk and Novartis, and rising Roche royalties from Eli Lilly's oral obesity drug mean the pharmaceutical segment is seen achieving 170 bps of margin expansion. Expectations are for a more cautious 140-bp improvement for health-care equipment, life sciences and services, implying operating leverage of 28% below the recent 31-39% range. 

Biotech looks like the biggest source of risk, with analysts seeing 870 bps of margin expansion on 21% sales growth, implying 55% operating leverage vs. the recent 33-50% range. The segment's small index weight -- just 2.5% of overall operating profit -- nevertheless limits the risk. (09/24/26)

## 5. Strong Cash Flow, Lower Leverage Support Pharma M&A 

Strong cash generation and falling leverage should give pharmaceutical companies more M&A firepower through 2027 as they look to offset looming patent expirations and replenish drug pipelines. Free cash flow is expected to post a 7.7% CAGR over 2025-27, broadly in line with the pace of the past five years, while dividend expectations remain achievable. The payout ratio relative to free cash flow is seen holding near 42% in 2027, below the 2020-25 average of 47% and 46% in 2025. 

Improvements across all segments point to European health-care companies' balance sheets strengthening further, with consensus looking for net debt-to-Ebitda to fall to 0.9x in 2027. Big pharma has so far completed $17.3 billion of deals this year and awaits approval of about $14 billion more, with GSK accounting for almost half that sum. This follows roughly $50 billion of M&A in 2025, with activity already above 2022-24 levels. (09/24/26)
