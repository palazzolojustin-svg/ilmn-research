# **EU Healthcare: Growth Fears Priced, Visibility Missing** 

Read Research Report: European Healthcare Autumn Outlook 



Laurent Douillet Simbarashe Gumbo Team: Strategy Team: Strategy BI Senior Equity Strategist BI Associate Analyst 

European Healthcare Lacks Earnings Catalysts as Discounts Deepen 

(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- European healthcare lacks the earnings visibility needed for a rerating, with our reverse-DCF analysis pointing to barely any free-cash-flow growth beyond 2030 and the defensive P/E premium disappearing vs. staples and utilities. Negative earnings revisions and weak fund flows suggest valuation alone won't revive investor sentiment. (09/29/26) 

## 1. Current Valuations Discount Barely Any FCF Growth Beyond 2030 

Using a reverse DCF model to analyze pharmaceutical and healthcare-equipment segment pricing suggests there's barely any free-cash-flow growth beyond the 2026-30 consensus. That points to either the current market estimates being too high, or the sector entering no-growth territory after this decade. Our reverse DCF model -- using an 8% weighted average cost of capital -- suggests an inperpetuity growth rate of 0.6% after 2030 for the pharmaceutical segment and 1.5% for equipment, life-science and services providers. 

Sanofi, Argenx, Novo Nordisk and Philips are the companies whose share prices discount the worst growth outcomes beyond 2030, with rates ranging from minus 1% for Philips to minus 7.5% for Sanofi. The latter is also the only company whose enterprise value is below its current book value and discounted 2026-30 free cash flows, implying shareholder-value destruction once its Dupixent patent expires. (09/29/26)

## 3. Europe Healthcare Discounts Deepen as Earnings Visibility Wanes 

European healthcare needs greater near-term earnings visibility for sentiment to improve, given patent expirations, lower US drug prices and negative headlines for trial failures continue to squeeze valuations. After a 20% derating over the past two years, the sector's blended forward P/E multiple sits 1.2 standard deviations below its 10-year average. This discount is broad-based, as healthcare equipment trades about 2.3 standard deviations below its historical norm, while pharma is 1.1 standard deviations less. Much of the pharma P/E derating (17.5x to 14.2x) reflects Novo Nordisk setbacks, without which the industry's valuation is broadly in line with its historical level. 

Across the sector, just 16% of constituents trade above their historical valuation averages. In terms of standard deviations, the following companies trade at sizable discounts to their long-term norms: Sanofi (minus 2.5), EssilorLuxottica (minus 2.2) and Novo Nordisk (minus 1.7). Meanwhile, Roche (1.6) and Novartis (1) stand out as trading above theirs. (09/29/26)

## 5. Sector Needs Earnings Catalyst to Revive Investor Flows 

Sentiment toward the European healthcare sector still needs to improve for asset flows to turn decisively positive. The largest European healthcare ETFs recorded about €823 million of net outflows this year, though the pace has moderated from roughly €1.1 billion in 1H when concerns about US regulatory and pricing pressure were most acute. Institutional positioning tells a similar story. Sellers of European healthcare stocks have cumulatively outnumbered buyers since 2023, and demand has yet to recover fully from the 2024 deterioration when Novo Nordisk's drugs began losing momentum relative to Eli Lilly's in the GLP-1 market. Buying improved in 2H25, but low valuations alone probably won't be enough to revive market sentiment. 

A more durable recovery likely requires better news flow, with new blockbuster drugs and stronger pipeline readouts supporting improving earnings revisions. Until then, better flows may signal stabilization vs. a decisive return of investor conviction. (09/29/26)
