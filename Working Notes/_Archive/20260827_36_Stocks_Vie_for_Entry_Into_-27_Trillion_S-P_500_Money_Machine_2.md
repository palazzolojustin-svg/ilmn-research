36 Stocks Vie for Entry Into $27 Trillion S&P 500 Money Machine 

(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- Passive investing has a dirty little secret: somebody is still making the decisions, making it the most influential "active" manager. Nearly $27 trillion is tied to the S&P 500, where next Friday's announcement will determine which stocks receive Wall Street's most valuable golden ticket. Thirty-six companies appear to qualify for entry, while more than 110 existing members no longer meet the index's market-cap hurdle. Yet only a handful of changes are expected because the committee can override the rulebook whenever it chooses. 

While it's still early, Bloom Energy and Cheniere lead our candidate list that also includes Astera Labs, Credo, Everpure, Twilio and Illumina. (08/27/26) 

## 1. Nearly $27 Trillion in S&P 500 Linked Products 

Additions to the S&P 500 Index matter because they can spark billions of dollars of buying pressure as passive S&P 500 trackers with trillions in assets seek to replicate the index. According to S&P Dow Jones, about $13 trillion of assets were in products tracking the S&P 500 at the end of 2024. Adjusting for index performance and fund flows, we calculate that the figure has likely risen to at least $17.7 trillion (including derivatives). An additional $9.2 trillion may be held by funds benchmarked to the index, bringing the total to nearly $26.9 trillion. 

Changes were officially announced on Sept. 4, and index-tracking products must gain exposure to any newly added stocks around market close on Sept. 18. Stock weightings are determined by market capitalization as of the close on Sept. 9. (09/17/26)

## 2. Big Buying Pressure Follows Addition to S&P 500 

Among the 36 stocks that appear to meet all inclusion criteria, even smaller firms would face billions of dollars in buying pressure if added to the index. Our calculations are based on an estimate of nearly $12.4 trillion in assets in S&P 500-tracking funds, which likely understates buying pressure because it excludes exposure to derivatives and active managers. 

Cheniere's theoretical $11 billion in buying pressure is the second largest after Bloom's $11.4 billion, but it would be over 1,800% of average daily trading volume. Still, Bloom and Cheniere would be relatively small additions compared to the largest recent rebalance additions like Marvell and Vertiv.

## 3. At Least 36 Stocks Meet S&P 500 Criteria 

At least 36 stocks currently appear to meet inclusion criteria. The top candidates ranked solely by their free-float size are Bloom Energy, Cheniere, Astera, Credo, Everpure, Twilio and Illumina. Everpure, Twilio and Illumina are the three largest upgrade-eligible S&P 400 members. Cheniere, Bloom and Astera are Bloomberg 500 Index (B500) members, which has looser inclusion criteria and often adds stocks before the S&P 500. Credo is only a member of the Russell 1000. We track an additional 53 stocks that are nearly eligible but fail due to their domiciles, market caps, profitability, or other metrics. 

Just because a stock meets the criteria doesn't mean it will be added; the committee decides, and stocks must be removed to make room for new ones. The committee monitors the index's sector balance and may avoid a move involving the largest eligible stocks. (08/27/26)

## 4. Sector Balance Likely Matters to S&P 500 Committee 

One potential tailwind for Cheniere's prospects of joining the S&P 500 is that it's an energy stock under the GICS format. The S&P 500 index committee cares about sector balance and index concentration. As a percentage of market capitalization, the energy sector is now just 3.4%, and currently it includes 21 stocks, which is about 4% of the S&P 500. That's well down from its most recent peak above 13% in 2008. 

Bloom Energy is an industrials stock, the largest sector by number of stocks at 83. The next four names on our list were all technology stocks, the largest sector by weight at 37% and the third largest by number of stocks at 73. Illumina rounds out the top seven by size and is a healthcare stock, the fourth-largest sector by weight and number of stocks. (08/27/26)

## 6. Relevant Dates and Requirements to Be Added to S&P 500 Index 

Changes to the S&P 500 will be based on market values as of the reference date, the Wednesday before the second Friday of the month. That means the market cap and respective relevant values are based on the close of business on Sept. 9. 

The index changes go into effect after the close of business on the third Friday of September (Sept. 18). Funds tracking the index will have to update their exposure to these new stocks and away from the removals before the market opens on Sept. 21. In the vast majority of funds, the trades will be done on Sept. 18, at or around the close of trading. 

Eligibility for the index is based on data from the last business day of the month before the rebalance date -- Aug. 31 in this most recent case. The expected money movement should be mostly priced into these stocks at this time. (09/17/26)
