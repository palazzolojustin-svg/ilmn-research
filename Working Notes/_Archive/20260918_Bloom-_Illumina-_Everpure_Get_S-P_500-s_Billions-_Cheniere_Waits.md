Bloom, Illumina, Everpure Get S&P 500's Billions; Cheniere Waits 

(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- Bloom Energy, Illumina and Everpure can draw roughly $29 billion of passive buying following their addition to the S&P 500, while Cheniere Energy, Astera and Alnylam are among the candidates we see for future rebalances. Molson Coors, Trade Desk and Builders FirstSource may face billions of dollars in selling after their removal. We identify at least 30 additional companies that meet or are close to meeting the index's size and profitability criteria. Snowflake and Cloudflare might move higher on that list if they reach the required profitability thresholds. At the other end, more than 130 members no longer meet the size threshold, including Norwegian Cruise Line, A. O. Smith and Mosaic. (09/18/26) 

## 1. Index Additions Set for $29 Billion in Buying Spree 

Bloom Energy could attract about $21 billion in purchases from the more than $17 trillion in products tracking the S&P 500. As a first-time entrant to the S&P suite of indexes, it won't face offsetting selling pressure. By contrast, Everpure and Illumina are top holdings in the midcap S&P 400 and may face roughly $4.3 billion and $4.8 billion, respectively, in selling from products tracking the index. Still, buying from S&P 500 products should more than offset that selling, leaving both with over $4 billion in net buying. The flows are large relative to trading activity. Estimated buying equals about 15.9x Everpure's average daily trading volume, 14.8x Illumina's and 7.9x Bloom Energy's, based on 2026 averages. 

Despite the projected inflows, Everpure is down 1.9% since the announcement, while Illumina and Bloom Energy gained 4.9% and 6.8%. (09/18/26)

## 2. Exiting Stocks Face About $3 Billion in Net Selling 

The three stocks being removed from the S&P 500 will bypass the S&P MidCap 400 and move directly into the S&P SmallCap 600, mirroring transitions seen in the prior three reconstitutions. The three are expected to face about $2.8 billion in combined net selling, with the pressure amounting to roughly 15% of each stock's free float. Molson Coors will face the most selling relative to trading volume, with estimated S&P 500 sales equal to almost 7x its 2026 average daily volume vs. 4.7x for Builders FirstSource and 2.1x for Trade Desk. The figures are large, but they're likely to be absorbed without major disruption.

## 3. 30 More Candidates for Future Index Rebalances 

At least 30 stocks, including the three chosen, were technically eligible to be added to the S&P 500 for this quarter's rebalance, based on our analysis. Another 25 are big enough to qualify for the index but fail to meet one or more of the other criteria. A small subset of five might be permanently left out due to potential issues with non-US domiciles. Some are on the cusp of being large enough, while most others on the list need to improve quarterly or trailing 12-month profitability to be considered by the index committee. Cheniere Energy, Astera, Twilio, Alnylam and Okta are among the leading contenders for next quarter's rebalance. Snowflake, Cloudflare and Natera may also be considered if they reach the required profitability thresholds.

## 4. Stocks That Could Exit in Future Rebalances 

At least 133 stocks in the S&P 500 don't meet the size requirement to be added to the index (a market capitalization of $22.7 billion), and 64 of those are below $15 billion. Many more fail to meet other criteria, such as profitability thresholds. But the index committee considers more than the largest profitable names and sector balance. It also weighs turnover and seeks to keep membership as stable as possible. That's why it added and removed only three stocks, yet could added 30 by our count. We believe the committee ought to consider removing more than just a handful of names in future rebalances. 

Based on current market capitalization, Norwegian Cruise Lines, A. O. Smith and Mosaic are among the leading candidates for deletion next quarter, though much can change in three months. (09/18/26)

## 5. Nearly $27 Trillion in S&P 500 Linked Products 

Additions to the S&P 500 Index matter because they can spark billions of dollars of buying pressure as passive S&P 500 trackers with trillions in assets seek to replicate the index. According to S&P Dow Jones, about $13 trillion of assets were in products tracking the S&P 500 at the end of 2024. Adjusting for index performance and fund flows, we calculate that the figure has likely risen to at least $17.7 trillion (including derivatives). An additional $9.2 trillion may be held by funds benchmarked to the index, bringing the total to nearly $26.9 trillion. 

Changes were officially announced on Sept. 4, and index-tracking products must gain exposure to any newly added stocks around market close on Sept. 18. Stock weightings are determined by market capitalization as of the close on Sept. 9. (09/17/26)

## 6. Relevant Dates and Requirements to Be Added to S&P 500 Index 

Changes to the S&P 500 will be based on market values as of the reference date, the Wednesday before the second Friday of the month. That means the market cap and respective relevant values are based on the close of business on Sept. 9. 

The index changes go into effect after the close of business on the third Friday of September (Sept. 18). Funds tracking the index will have to update their exposure to these new stocks and away from the removals before the market opens on Sept. 21. In the vast majority of funds, the trades will be done on Sept. 18, at or around the close of trading. 

Eligibility for the index is based on data from the last business day of the month before the rebalance date -- Aug. 31 in this most recent case. The expected money movement should be mostly priced into these stocks at this time. (09/17/26)
