Roche Launch Execution Key as R&D Day Promises Busy 2027/28 

(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- Roche must successfully launch giredestrant (breast cancer) and fenebrutinib (multiple sclerosis) to lift near-term pipeline sales expectations. Beyond that, Roche's R&D day suggested that 2027 and 2028 feature a multitude of late-stage readouts due that, if positive, could drive upside to post-2030 growth expectations. (09/29/26) 

## 1. Roche Pharma Day Unlikely to Drive Sales-Estimate Increases 

Roche long-term sales growth outlook will be heavily influenced by the outcome of numerous Phase 3 studies set to report in 2027 and 2028. Estimates are unlikely to alter significantly following its Sept. 28 annual Pharma day, leaving Roche's three-year (2025-2028E) and five-year (2025-2030E) sales growth at almost 7%, in line with the US/EU large Pharma median. Yet though many of the studies scheduled to report are in indications with significant sales potential, they're often in highly competitive areas or where Roche is undertaking head-to-head comparisons with current standardof-care, increasing the likelihood of trial failures. 

With as many as 20 new drug launches targeted by 2030 (five of which have reported positive Phase 3 data), it's more likely than not that consensus will need to increase over time. Still until those data are delivered and Roche is seen also to provide strong marketing execution on near-term product rollout, sales estimates may see limited change. (09/29/26)

## 3. Competitor Oncology and MS Readouts of Most Interest 

Tecentriq's approval in a subcutaneous formulation competes with similars from Bristol Myers and Merck. Uptake of all will be closely watched, most notably to assess the settings where the formulation is preferred. We expect the subcutaneous formulation to benefit patient switches rather than drive incremental sales. Breast cancer readouts continue for the oral SERD class, with potential implications for giredestrant -- AstraZeneca's SERENA-4 study of Etcamah/camizestrant recently failed, its outcome unsurprising given a similar data trial design to Roche's failed persevERA trial, yet adjuvant data are due next year. 

Liver safety issues for Sanofi's tolebrutinib heightens sensitivity around fenebrutinib's regulatory challenges in multiple sclerosis (MS) -- initial indications are that liver safety may be manageable though increased mortality in the fenebrutinib group will likely attract regulatory scrutiny. Novartis reported a Phase 3 MS win for remibrutinib but provided no data -- that's due at the upcoming ACTRIMS-ECTRIMS meeting (Toronto, Canada, Oct. 23) which should allow for further assessment of that product's profile. (09/29/26)
