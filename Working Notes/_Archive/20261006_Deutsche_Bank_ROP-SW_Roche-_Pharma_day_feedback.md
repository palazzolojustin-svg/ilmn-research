##### Summary generalist takes 

**We attended Roche’s annual pharma day in London yesterday and provide key takes and feedback. The summary is that notwithstanding the underwhelmed market reaction (stock -LSD), we actually viewed the update as a very minor positive** for reasons we briefly discuss below – though perhaps that reflected our limited expectations into the event relative to some others. Specifically, after the mid-term group guide provided at the Diagnostics day ( _<u>ROG: Dia day/sellside meet feedback + fene feedback takes</u> (14/05/26)_ ), we were were not anticipating any new group/divisional guidance; nor do these events typically provide the forum for material novel data disclosure. However, they have occasionally provided a forum for novel peak asset-sales guidance and project plans and whilst we did a handful of the former, these did not cover giredestrant (likely part of the disappointment given the present focus…). We also did get plenty of the latter (new trials), which we viewed as mostly positive. **We also got a helpful and credible overview of the way Roche is embedding AI into its R&D enterprise – timely after our recent review (** **_<u>EU Pharma: 2026 AI Biopharma review: the dream is still Morphic, not yet mortal*</u> (14/09/26)_ ) and perhaps the most convincing from a large pharma company so far. Finally, we also got a clearer view of the 2027 catalyst list** , which will comprise P3 data for afimkibart and zemocimig (but not trontinemab), making those two, along with the giredestrant, launch the likely determinant items for the foreseeable future.

The new asset guidance encompassed recently in-licensed tam-peli at >CHF3bn and the same for also in-licensed BTK degrader bexobrutideg (which would be a very good return on the $700m upfront in June) plus CHF1-2bn for the VEGF/IL6 dutafab. The most notable new non-H2H trials were an expansion of the giredestrant program, comprising KALITERA (broadly an equivalent to CAMBRIA1/EMBER4), a scientificallyinteresting MRD-guided academic study AFT70-NAVIGATE and a curious combination trial with PFE’s atirmociclib. We say 'curious' because the comparator for the latter was unconfirmed and will be key, and we also did not fully understand the rationale for pushing ahead into a P3 with PFE’s CDK4/2 asset rather than one of the internal Regor programs – particularly since it was confirmed yesterday that CDK4/2 asset RG6794 would be proceeding into P2. For context we’ve covered both atirmociclib/Regor before: most recently see _<u>EU Pharma: Breast cancer: Updates on SONIA + CDK 2/4 class (AZN/NOVN/ROG)</u> (20/04/26)_ 

- n **The key new H2H trials included confirmation that Roche will take enicepatide H2H with tirzepatide in diabetes** (but refused to be drawn on obesity), bexobrutideg in 2L CLL with LLY’s pirtobrutinib (but refused to be drawn on 1L plans…) and the VEGF/IL6 dutafab will go H2H Vabysmo in DME (but refused to be drawn on AMD). 

- n **Beyond the above we got a good AI R&D overview from gRED head Aviv Regev that clearly impressed much of the audience, albeit one that remains hard to parse** – both relatively and absolutely in our view. In absolute terms: Roche was keen to admit that the first wave of output will only be proven in the clinic and that process is only starting now i.e will take years play out. In relative terms: because it remains opaque what peers are truly up to (disclosure remains limited and no company has provided an equivalent presentation of such detail). Beyond that, we also add some questions we didn’t get time to ask: Roche is clearly embedding use of some public tools (such as Boltz etc), **but made it clear not only is the data input/extraction proprietary but so are the models – which raises many obvious questions** e.g. whether Roche is building out a software division; how it is avoiding these agents becoming (relatively) obsolete and keeping pace with the best that are out there. Moreover, some of these agents were clearly being developed as master agents that will ultimately guide all decisions made in the Roche portfolio (e.g. “ _TargetNexus_ ”), so how the company plans to manage the data excellence, flow and sandbox/security risk here, become obvious questions. 

- n **Finally, we also got around 300 questions in the various Q&A sessions on giredestrant, mostly comprising analysts trying to parse** approval/ labelling, pricing, access and commercial launch curve prospects, and mostly to little avail. On the key topic of labelling Roche doesn’t expect an Adcom and appeared to imply it will escape Etcamah’s black box ( _<u>AZN: S6 addendum: the dulcet ode soured by a pestilent congregation of vapours*?</u> (22/09/26)_ ); we got little on pricing, whilst there was some confusion on 2027 expectations: the company’s view was that street is at CHF600m (which is the number we see vs. a much higher one that was suggested…) and it seemed very comfortable with this – we would hope so since buyside expectations are now clearly much higher (perhaps that is what the question was referring to…).
