**_Below, we highlight our key takeaways from the event:_** 

### **1) Giredestrant for Hormonal Breast Cancer:** 

Management reiterated giredestrant's intended positioning across both early and refractory metastatic HR+/HER2- breast cancer. Beyond the completed adjuvant **lidERA** study (PDUFA 30

New / upcoming studies highlighted included 1) **Ph.3b novERA** (all-risk patients switching from CDK4/6i), 2) **Ph.3b kalitERA** (AI-intolerant patients), 3) the MRD-guided **AFT-70 NAVIGATE** study with Natera/Alliance, and 4) a new Pfizer-led **giredestrant plus atirmociclib** (CDK4 inhibitor) programme. 

ROP reiterated strong confidence in the launch profile, emphasising low discontinuation rates in lidERA, broad physician reach, and patient-support infrastructure, all helping the asset to enable high uptake and chronic use. 

Further updates include a delay to **pionERA,** the Ph.3 first-line HR+/HER2- metastatic breast cancer study, which has been pushed back to an expected 2028 readout (from 2027), as a result of increasing enrolment to strengthen powering. ROP additionally reiterated that **persevERA** (ET-sensitive 1L mBC) missed its primary endpoint but showed a numerical PFS improvement, while **pionERA** (ET-resistant, ESR1m-enriched 1L mBC), **heredERA** (HR+/HER2+ mBC maintenance) and the Ph.1b/2 **MoonROSE** giredestrant/CDK4-2 programmes remain ongoing. 

Management ultimately characterise giredestrant as its critical growth driver, representing a key contributor to future sales expansion. BARC currently forecast **SFr8.4bn peak sales (un-risked SFr8.9bn)** , marginally behind company guidance of >SFr9bn. The company expressed confidence in delivering at least c.SFr0.6bn in '27 sales, c.50% above BBG cons of c.SFr0.4bn, even with initial free product, to aide payor coverage. 

### **<u>2) Metabolic / Obesity Portfolio:</u>** 

ROP's metabolic update was broadly positive, driven primarily by **Ph.2 data for GLP-1/GIP asset enicepatide.** 

In **T2D** , enicepatide reduced HbA1c by **2.65% from an 8.1% baseline** , **and body weight by 15.5% at 48 weeks** (pbo-adj. figures not available), with no apparent plateau observed. This compares to LLY's (covered by Emily Field) tirzepatide, which delivered 2.3% HbA1c reduction, with 14.7% (11.6% pbo-adj.) weight loss at 72 weeks. We believe efficacy could increase over time, with no plateau reached to date. 

In **Obesity** , enicepatide delivered **22.7% (18.3% pbo-adj.) weight loss at 48w** , with no apparent plateau. Comparatively, LLY's tirzpedatide delivered 20.9% (17.8% pbo-adj.) weight loss at 72 weeks. We view this as relatively favourable for enicepatide, but not as clear of a win as in T2D, again noting the caveat of trial cross-comparison. 

**ROP echoed this view, flagging enicepatide's larger opportunity as T2D, particularly through its ability to simultaneously address weight loss as a comorbidity.** Obesity was flagged as having strong combination therapy potential (e.g., with petrelintide). 

Mechanistically, we believe enicepatide may be differentiated from tirezpatide through **improved selectivity for biased agonism, providing more selective amplification of cAMP pathways whilst limiting β-arrestin-mediated receptor desensitization.** 

BARC currently forecasts **risk-adj. sales of SFr1.5bn (50% PoS on SFr3.1bn)** , with ROP guiding for combined peak sales with CT-996 of >SFr3bn. 

ROP confirmed its intentions to **advance oral GLP-1 asset CT-996 into Ph.3 for obesity (H1'27 start), flagging it as the third key obesity/T2D asset in the metabolic portfolio.**

### **<u>3) Fenebrutinib BTK for Multiple Sclerosis</u>** 

ROP continue to position fenebrutinib as a sizable opportunity across both RMS and PPMS, noting it as the first and only BTK inhibitor with positive Ph.3 results in both indications, filing having now taken place in the US. 

Management emphasised the opportunity to address both ARR and disability progression, presenting fenebrutinib as a potential expansion of ROP's MS franchise beyond CD20 Ocrevus. 

ROP increased its long-term expectations for the asset upgraded to >SFr3bn from SFr2-3bn. It was noted that BBG cons carries only c.SFr1bn of peak sales, which ROP believes could be achievable in PMS alone, RMS offering upside potential. 

We remain somewhat cautious on fenebrutinib, given potential competition from NOVN's remi BTK in RMS, that may be cleaner and more effective on disability, though we still see a role for fene in PMS, where there is no competitor. 

We currently forecast **SFr0.9bn and SFr1.7bn risk-adj. sales in RMS and PPMS respectively for fenebrutinib** , coming in under management's updated target. 

### **<u>4) Tambotatug Pelitecan ADC for B7-HC added tp potential NME launches by '30</u>** 

Tambotatug pelitecan (Tam-Peli, B7-H3 ADC) emerged as one of the more notable oncology pipeline additions at Pharma Day, with **Roche adding the asset to its list of potential NME launches by 2030** . 

Management highlighted encouraging data presented at the World Conference on Lung Cancer and described the B7-H3 ADC as a potentially important new growth driver across both SCLC and NSCLC. 

**Roche announced a planned Ph.3 start in 2L+ SCLC during Q4'26** and positioned the programme as part of its effort to strengthen its lung cancer franchise beyond divarasib. 

ROP highlighted activity across SCLC, NSCLC and other solid tumours. 

Tam-Peli is now one of only a handful of oncology assets specifically called out as having materially enhanced Roche's long-term growth outlook, alongside divarasib (KRAS) and giredestrant.
