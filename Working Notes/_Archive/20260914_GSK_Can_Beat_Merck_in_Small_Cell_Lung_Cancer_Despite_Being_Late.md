# **WCLC 2026: ADCs in SCLC** 

Read Research Report: WCLC 2026: Summit, GSK Take Spotlight

Sam Fazeli Team: Pharma BI Director of Research, Global Industries 

GSK Can Beat Merck in Small-Cell Lung Cancer Despite Being Late 

(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- GSK and potentially Roche will lock horns with Merck's B7H3 antibodydrug conjugate for second-line small-cell lung cancer, based on first-ever Phase 3 data presented at the World Conference on Lung Cancer 2026. GSK partner Hansoh Bio's risvutatug rezetecan posted median overall survival of 18.5 months vs. 13.3 for Roche partner MediLink's tambotatug pelitecan in similarly sized trials. Data for Merck-Daiichi's ifinatamab deruxtecan, likely to be the first drug to hit the market in 4Q26 based only on Phase 2 data, don't look as compelling with lower tumor response rates reported at WCLC 2024. Consensus has no sales for Roche's tam-peli (it has no active global Phase 3), $270 million for ris-rez and $360 million for I-DXd in 2030. The current standard of care, Amgen's Imdelltra, is seen hitting $1.3 billion in 2026 sales. (09/14/26) 

## 1. Ris-Rez Provides Higher Survival But Comparison Tough 

The 18.5 month median overall survival (mOS) in GSK partner Hansoh Bio's Artemis-008 Phase 3 trial of risvutatug rezetecan is impressive compared with the topotecan chemotherapy control arm at 10.3 months, a 54% reduction in the risk of death, based on data presented at WCLC 2026. Roche partner MediLink's tambotatug pelitecan showed median overall survival of 13.3 months vs. 9.4 months in a similarly sized China-only Taishan-302 Phase 3 trial, achieving identical risk reduction of 54%. Although on the face of it ris-rez appears to result in better survival, there are several caveats that make us cautious in coming to that conclusion for these B7H3 targeting antibody-drug conjugates. 

Median follow-ups in the two trials are different, with Taishan-302 about three months shorter than the 12.2 months of Artemis-008, suggesting mOS could improve with more time, though it's unlikely to reach the same level as in the Hansoh trial. We also note patients enrolled in the MediLink trial were sicker than those in the Artemis-008 trial, based on the number of patients with liver or brain metastases, and the percentage of patients who had progressed on chemotherapy (chemotherapy treatment-free interval, CTFI) after fewer than 90 days. Also, the dose of drug used in Artemis-008 was fourfold higher. (09/14/26)

## 2. Merck-Daiichi I-DXd Likely to Be First to Market 

Merck and Daiichi Sankyo's ifinatamab deruxtecan (I-DXd), also a B7H3 targeting ADC, is likely to be the first drug in this class to be approved, with an FDA action date of Oct. 10, 2026. The filing is based on data from a single-arm Phase 2 trial reported at WCLC 2024, IDeate-Lung01, which don't look as strong as figures reported by GSK-Hansoh and Roche-MediLink in their Phase 3 trials at WCLC 2026. This is also the case compared with the Artemis-001 ris-rez Phase 1 data. The overall response rate (ORR) of 48% in IDeate-Lung01 was meaningfully lower than 58% in Artemis-008 and 61% in Artemis-001, even though patients in IDeate-Lung01 had better prognostic factors such as rates of liver and brain metastases. The same is the case when data is compared with Taishan-302, which had an ORR of 59%. 

If this holds true when the Phase 3 IDeate-Lung02 trial reports in the next two years, then Merck's I- DXd might be superseded by the Roche or the GSK drug. Roche doesn't appear to have any active global Phase 3 trials posted on trial registries, suggesting it would be at best third to market. (09/14/26)

## 3. Tam-Peli and I-DXd Seem to Have Safety Edge Over Ris-Rez 

One of the issues with ADCs is their therapeutic window -- the dose at which good efficacy is seen without causing too much toxicity. Although the safety profiles of Hansoh's ris-rez and MediLink's tampeli are clearly better than topotecan chemotherapy, the former appears to have higher toxicity. This could be due to three things. Firstly, the drug and linker used in each ADC were different, which could have impacted the amount of drug that escapes into general circulation, resulting in systemic toxicity. It's also possible that the fourfold higher dose used in Hansoh's trial led to higher side effects. Lastly, the median follow-up period in the MediLink trial was three months shorter than in Hansoh's trial; with a longer follow-up, the number of reported side effects and drug-toxicity driven discontinuations would likely have increased in Taishan-302. 

Merck-Daiichi's I-DXd seems to have a better toxicity profile than the other two drugs, but this interpretation is complicated by the fact that the data are from a relatively small Phase 2 trial. Discontinuation rates due to adverse events and rates of interstitial lung disease (ILD, a serious side effect of several ADCs) are the same or higher. (09/14/26)
