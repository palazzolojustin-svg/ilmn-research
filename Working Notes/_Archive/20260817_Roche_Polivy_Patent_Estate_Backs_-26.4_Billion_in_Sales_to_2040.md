# **BI Patent Analysis: Polivy** 



VaNae Hamilton Team: Pharma BI Patent Specialist 



Aude Gerspacher Team: Litigation BI Director of US Industry Research 

Roche Polivy Patent Estate Backs $26.4 Billion in Sales to 2040 

(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- Roche's patent protection for antibody-drug conjugate Polivy can extend exclusivity beyond expiry of the primary composition-of-matter patent by eight years in the US and six years in Europe and Japan, supporting $26.4 billion in risk-adjusted revenue from 2034-40. Consensus revenue peaks around 2030, between composition-of-matter expiry in China in 2028 and in the US, Europe and Japan in 2033. We expect formulation patents to extend exclusivity to 2041 in the US and 2039 in Europe and Japan. (08/17/26) 

## 1. Polivy Patent Portfolio Expands in Treatment Coverage 

Roche's patent protection for Polivy, its anti-CD79b antibody-drug conjugate, is growing through secondary coverage. The portfolio consists of at least 12 global families, with a nearly-even split of 29 patents and 24 applications. Of the 12 families, three cover compositions of matter including polatuzumab vedotin, one covers Polivy's formulation and the remaining eight cover methods of treatment. 

Across the US, Europe, Japan and China, Roche has its strongest existing protection in the US and Japan, with 20 patents between the two jurisdictions. Growth potential is greatest in Europe and China, where 15 applications are under examination. The life of the global portfolio spans from 2012 to an estimated 2042. (08/17/26)

## Seminal Family Builds Core Protection Through 2033 

Composition-of-matter (CoM) coverage of polatuzumab vedotin anchors Roche's portfolio. There are 12 CoM patents within the seminal family, five in the US, three in Europe, three in China and one in Japan. China is the only jurisdiction with an application under examination in the family. Multiple filings within the jurisdictions cover the structural components of the antibody-drug conjugate, with the broadest coverage being of polatuzumab by complementary determining-region sequences. The earliest patent expiry within the family is July 2028 in China, while CoM coverage in the US, Europe and Japan extends until mid-2033, inclusive of term extensions. 

Patent-term extended coverage in the US, Europe, and Japan surpass the regulatory exclusivities granted in those regions expiring in June 2031, January 2030 and March 2031. (08/17/26) 

## 3. Formulation Extends US, Japan Exclusivity; Europe Pending 

Roche's secondary CoM patents covering the formulation of Polivy extend protection eight years beyond the seminal CoM patents in the US and six years in Japan. Similar coverage is pending in Europe, where a grant could extend projected loss of exclusivity six years beyond our existing calculations. Though formulation coverage is pending in China, a grant would be inconsequential to exclusivity because formulation patents aren't listable with the National Medical Products Administration. 

The US formulation patent received patent-term (PTE) adjustment, extending expiration to October 2041, while its Japanese counterpart received one through October 2039. In Japan, additional indication-based PTE pushes expiration as late as March 2041, though our analysis assumes the earliest possible biosimilar market entry date. Formulation protection in Europe and China will expire in April 2039 if the pending applications mature into patents. (08/17/26)

## 4. Treatment Coverage Limited in China, Strong In Other Key Markets 

Method-of-treatment (MoT) families, which constitute two-thirds of Polivy's portfolio, bolster protection in the US and Japan, with six and five patents in each jurisdiction, respectively. At least one of the families provides defensive protection for treatment of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) with Polivy in combination with bendamustine and a rituximab product, with patents in the US and Europe. Though applications in China preserve potential for expanded protection, pending claims either don't track Polivy's label or lack dosing features. 

Only four of the 20 applications pending across the key jurisdictions are in China, while none of the 12 patents are in the region. This gap is particularly limiting because exclusivity in the region expires first, as it hinges on composition coverage. Even if the pending applications mature into patents, they're unlikely to affect exclusivity. (08/17/26)

## 5. Robust Secondary Coverage Underpins US Exclusivity to 2041 

Roche's US patent protection for Polivy is robust, with 12 patents and six applications under examination. Protection is layered, with the seminal CoM patents expiring in June 2033, a labeltracking method of treatment patent expiring in September 2035 and the formulation patent in October 2041, 10 years after the expiration of the 12-year FDA regulatory exclusivity. Within the seminal family, five patents cover polatuzumab vedotin and two broadly cover methods of treating cancer with combination therapies. 

Of the seven patents outside the primary composition layer, four provide commercially relevant secondary protection alongside six pending applications. In addition to key method of treatment and formulation patents, two additional patents and pending applications claim combination therapies that aren't currently approved, but may cover future label expansion. (08/17/26)

## 6. Pending Coverage Can Extend European Exclusivity to 2039 

Europe has the biggest pipeline of pending patent coverage for Polivy, with eight applications under examination. Of the six patents in the region, three cover various structural features of polatuzumab vedotin as well as methods of broadly treating B-cell proliferative disorders. CoM coverage extends three years beyond Polivy's data and market exclusivities, which expire in January 2030. 

Supplementary Protection Certificates were granted in France, Italy, Spain and the UK, extending the expiration of the second CoM patent until July 2033. MoT patents and applications support the existing label and potential future expansions, while formulation coverage can extend exclusivity until April 2039 if the pending application matures into a patent. (08/17/26)

## 7. Japan's Patent Strength Contrasts Limited China Upside 

Polivy's protection in Japan mirrors the US, with staggered expiration of regulatory and patent coverage. The re-examination period for Polivy expires in March 2031, two years before the CoM patents with indication-based patent-term extensions expire in July 2033. Later exclusivity rests on the formulation patent, which expires in October 2039, adding six years of exclusivity in the region. Japan has the second-largest number of patents in the portfolio, with eight vs. 12 in the US, and the fewest applications pending among the major markets. 

We anticipate biosimilar entry in China following expiration of CoM coverage in July 2028. With all three patents in the region arising from the seminal family, China’s portfolio is almost the inverse of Japan’s, with seven applications pending. Despite an apparently robust patent pipeline, claims in the pending applications don't cover label-indicated methods of treatment. As a result, none of the pending coverage provides a clear path to extending exclusivity beyond the seminal CoM patents. (08/17/26)

## 8. Polivy Patent-Portfolio Analysis Methodology 

The BI patent team uncovers the patent portfolio for biologics and drugs by searching patent and regulatory databases, selecting the relevant families and calculating expiration dates across regions, including all extensions. The team analyzes each patent's coverage, identifying it as primarily composition-of-matter (compound, formulation, crystals), method-of-treatment (disease indication(s) and dosing-regimen(s)), process(es) (manufacturing) or diagnostic. 

Not all patent portfolios have all patent types. The relative strength of the portfolio is assessed and the likely loss of exclusivity based on the patent most likely to hold off generics or biosimilars is projected for each region. (08/17/26)
