Europe Earnings Upgrades Broaden as AI, Energy Lift Momentum 

(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- Europe's strong 2Q earnings season is translating into broader 2027 upgrades, with AI-led spending and decade-high management confidence supporting further momentum. Energy, financials and healthcare drove much of the 2Q upside, while technology leads 2027 estimate increases as rising AI capex lifts earnings expectations. (08/13/26) 

## 1. Europe Earnings Beat Expectations as Energy, Banks Lead 

European companies delivered their strongest earnings growth since 2022, up 18%, with about 57% beating consensus. Energy (BP and Shell), healthcare (Roche and Novartis) and financials (BNP, Munich Re) drove 87% of the positive earnings surprise. Healthcare defied expectations that it would be a drag, helped by stronger revenue growth and easing currency headwinds. Technology largely lived up to lofty expectations, while our broader AI basket contributed more than a tenth of the overall earnings upside, boosted by ASML and Siemens. 

By contrast, autos and components were the biggest detractors despite a low base, weighed down by large misses from Volkswagen and Mercedes-Benz. Transportation has also disappointed, as elevated oil prices weighed on earnings. (08/13/26)

## 2. 2027 EPS Upgrades Broaden, Led by Tech and Energy 

More than half of European companies have had their 2027 earnings estimates raised since 2Q results, as energy and AI spending support the outlook. Energy had the broadest revision ratio at 46%, followed by technology at 38.5%, which also had the largest upgrade at 4.4%, driven by AI capex. ASML's estimates rose 9.3%, alongside upgrades to ASM International and Nokia. In our AI basket, which also includes industrials and materials benefiting from AI infrastructure spending, 78% of companies had 2027 earnings upgrades after results, including Atlas Copco and Schneider, though cuts to Glencore and Rio Tinto tempered gains. Industrials had the third-highest revision ratio at 36.4%, led by upgrades to Rolls-Royce and Leonardo, with higher defense spending supporting the sector outlook.

## 3. AI Leads as Management Sentiment Reaches Decade High 

Management sentiment has reached its highest level since at least 2015, as strong earnings bolster executive confidence. Commentary has been broadly positive across prepared remarks and analyst Q&A, with management most positive on AI benefits and buybacks. AI is also the most discussed topic along with foreign exchange and China, especially in the consumer discretionary sector. Financials leads on AI enthusiasm, with the Lloyds, HSBC and Aberdeen citing it as a major cost-reduction driver, while energy and materials are the least positive. 

Sentiment has also improved across most other topics, including China and foreign exchange. Chinese household consumption is stabilizing and even showing some signs of recovery in some segments such as high-end cosmetics, while a weakening euro is increasingly viewed as a 2H earnings tailwind. ## 4. Pharma Defies Contraction, Lifts Healthcare Growth 

Healthcare, driven by pharma, defied expectations for a contraction and expanded 7%, as easing foreign-exchange pressure and demand for key products, including GLP-1s and cancer medicines, drove beats across large caps. The impact of lower US drug prices under the Inflation Reduction Act and Most Favored Nation Prescription Drug Pricing was less severe than expected, while lower R&D costs supported earnings at Sanofi and Roche.
