Amgen Small-Cell Lung Cancer Trials Raise Bar for Roche-MediLink 

(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- Replacing chemotherapy with antibody-drug conjugates has merit, based on data from MediLink, licensed to Roche for global development, and Qilu at the World Conference on Lung Cancer 2026. Yet, they -- and companies such as GSK-Hansoh -- face changing standard of care as Amgen reports data from its Phase 3 trials of Imelltra in first-line small cell lung cancer. The DeLLphi-305 trial, which added Amgen's Imdelltra to Astra's Imfinzi, achieved its primary endpoint of overall survival. Amgen is easing Imdelltra's use by cutting post-dose monitoring times and increasing the treatment interval to three weeks. ADCs may need to be tested with Imdelltra and Imfinzi, or Boehringer Ingelheim's obrixtamig -- the top risk for Amgen if successful. Consensus expects Imdelltra sales of over $4 billion sales by 2036. (09/17/26) 

## 1. MediLink-Roche Tam-Peli Faces Uncertain Future in Early SCLC 

MediLink reported encouraging initial data at WCLC 2026, aiming to replace chemotherapy in treating patients with previously untreated small-cell lung cancer with an antibody-drug conjugate, tambotatug pelitecan, or Tam-Peli (YL201), licensed to Roche for ex-China development. Still the therapy's forward path for this patient population is unclear. Tam-Peli, combined with PD-1 inhibitor serplulimab from Henlius in a Phase 1 trial, showed an objective response rate of 85% compared with 80% in the Astrum-005 Phase 3 trial of serplulimab plus chemo at a similar median follow-up of about 12 months. Though that isn't a significant improvement, median progression free survival seems much higher, with the caveat the trial cohort was small. 

The issue for this approach is that the standard of care in SCLC is likely to change in the next few years. Amgen's DeLLphi-305 Phase 3 trial of Imdelltra plus AstraZeneca's Imfinzi -- a PD-L1 inhibitor with a similar mechanism action as serplulimab -- in the maintenance setting, following four cycles of Imfinzi plus chemo, has already reached what Amgen called a clinically meaningful and landmark impact on overall survival, with data expected in the next few months. Amgen's DeLLphi-312 Phase 3 trial is testing Imdelltra in combination with Imfinzi and chemotherapy for 4 cycles therapy, followed by Imdelltra plus Imfinzi maintenance until progression, essentially repeating the Astra's Caspian trial with Imfinzi and chemotherapy, which led to its approval. Data is likely in2028, which means MediLink and Roche need to think hard about their late-stage development strategy. (09/17/26)

## 2. Qilu Pharmaceuticals's QLC5508 Data Replicate MediLink's 

Qilu's QL5508, a B7H3 ADC, showed encouraging activity when combined with PD-1 inhibitor QL2107, its biosimilar of Merck's Keytruda, in a Phase 1b/2 trial at WCLC 2026. The 81% ORR was similar to the 85% for Roche-MediLink's Tam-Peli plus Henlius' serplulimab. Median follow-up for this trial was just 3.6 months, suggesting efficacy and optically lower toxicity are likely to change as more adverse events are recorded over time. The lack of extra efficacy in the 16 patients, for which the combination drug was QLC1706, a PD-1xCTLA4 bispecific, isn't surprising after AstraZeneca's experience in the third arm of its Caspian trial, which included its CTLA4 antibody tremelimumab. 

Another issue for this trial and the MediLink Tam-Peli study is the lower number of patients with brain metasteses (4.7% and 4.9%), which may mean a healthier patient population vs. those in trials such as Caspian (10%) and Henlius' Astrum-005 (12.9%). (09/17/26)

## 3. Imdelltra Extended Dosing Remains Unconvincing

## 4. Legend's Solid Tumor CAR-T Still Doesn't Beat Amgen's Imdelltra 

Legend Biotech's LB2102 is an engineered immune cell therapy (CAR-T) targeting SCLC by binding to DLL3 on cancer cells. It's designed to enhance its activity in the disease, though updated data at WCLC 2026 failed to show a significant in efficacy with longer follow-up. Though ORR improved to 29% at 17.6 months from 20 months at 12.5 months, there were no complete responses. The median progressionfree survival wasn't impressive either, even though the data was from the higher dose group -- dose level 3 and above. 

Data on the outcome of the engineered cells in patients showed the CAR-T cells nearly disappeared by day 60, even at the highest dose of 16 million infused cells. That aligns with the low median duration of response of 6.5 months, underlining the fact that LB2102 may be inferior to Amgen's standard of care T-cell engager tarlatamab (Imdelltra) in the same setting. 

LB2102 is an autologous CAR-T product, produced by engineering the patients' own T-cells to recognise DLL3 on tumor cells. The cells are made to inhibit the function of TGF-beta, which is thought to be a significant barrier for T-cells to kill tumors in solid cancers. (09/17/26)
