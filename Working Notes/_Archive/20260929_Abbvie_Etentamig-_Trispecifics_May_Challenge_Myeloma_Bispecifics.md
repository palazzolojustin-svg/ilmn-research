# **IMS Annual Meeting 2026: T-Cell Engagers** 

Read Research Report: IMS 2026: Myeloma Advances Point to $47 Billion Market Sam Fazeli Cindy Wu Team: Pharma Team: Pharma BI Director of Research, Global Industries BI Associate Analyst 

Abbvie Etentamig, Trispecifics May Challenge Myeloma Bispecifics 

(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- AbbVie presented pivotal data from etentamig at the International Myeloma Society (IMS) annual meeting, which leaves it well-positioned to challenge approved bispecific antibodies in community settings but with a safety edge. J&J's ramantamig leads an emerging wave of trispecific antibodies eager to move into earlier lines, reporting early efficacy that outperforms bispecific combinations. (09/29/26) 

## 1. Etentamig May Threaten Class Lead via Community-Friendly Safety 

Data from AbbVie's Phase 3 registrational CERVINO trial positions etentamig as a challenger in myeloma to J&J's Tecvayli and Pfizer's Elrexfio. Its differentiated safety profile could drive uptake in outpatient settings, likely due to a low-affinity CD3 domain, monthly dosing, and improved safety management -- about 10% of patients received prophylactic immunoglobulin replacement therapy to manage infections and tocilizumab to manage cytokine release syndrome (CRS), respectively. 27% of trial patients were treated in non-academic centers. 

Etentamig delivered a class-low 28.3% CRS rate with a single step-up dose, and CRS drops to 0% with prophylactic tocilizumab. This reduces the burden of inpatient monitoring, especially in community settings, and may allow a US approval without Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategies (REMS), which the other bispecifics require, according to a presentation by Wake Forest professor Peter Voorhees. 

Monthly dosing doesn't compromise the efficacy. The overall response rate achieved by etentamig appears slightly higher than the class, though we note Tecvayli (MajesTEC-1) and Elrexfio (MagnetisMM-3) were evaluated in more heavily pretreated populations. Given its slightly shorter median follow-up vs. other trials, adverse events may see some increase over time. (09/29/26)

First results from one arm of MajesTEC-5 trial evaluating Talvey plus Darzalex and lenalidomide (TalDR) as front-line induction in transplant-eligible newly diagnosed patients showed high response depth, with a 90% minimal residual disease (MRD)-negativity rate post-transplant/pre-maintenance, yet its 70% complete response rates lagged the 100% rate seen in the Tecvayli-DR arm. ## 3. Roche's Cevostamab Triplet Heads to Phase 3 in Second Line 

In the Phase 1b CAMMA 1 trial, Roche's FcRH5/CD3 bispecific antibody, cevostamab, plus pomalidomide and dexamethasone, delivered encouraging depth in BCMA-naive patients with at least one prior line, validating its 70-mg dose and triple step-up dosing schedule for the Phase 3 CEVOLUTION trial. The triplet's response rates and 18-month progression-free survival rate track similarly to Tecvayli monotherapy in MajesTEC-9, though we note less proportion of patients in this trial were exposed to CD38. However, high discontinuations, driven primarily by pomalidomide, raises questions over the value of using a triplet over single-agent bispecifics like Tecvayli. 

The CEVOLUTION trial mandates prior anti-CD38 and lenalidomide exposure, so it may not replicate CAMMA 1's results given the difference in baseline treatment history. (09/29/26)

## 4. J&J's Trispecific Ramantamig Aims to Top Bispecifics 

J&J's subcutaneous trispecific ramantamig targeting BCMA, GPRC5D and CD3, achieved a 94% overall response rate and a 79% complete response rate in patients not previously treated with BCMA- or GPRC5D-targeting therapies, at 21.8-month follow-up. A two-year progression-free survival (PFS) rate of 81% compares favorably to the 18-month PFS rate of 70% seen with Tecvayli-Talvey in the RedirecTT1 trial, though we note the latter had a higher extramedullary disease burden. 

Improved safety protocols -- including prophylactic tocilizumab in 52% of patients -- allowed over half of the patients to receive step-up dose and first treatment dose in outpatient setting. Infections were also manageable, with grade 3+ events plateauing after the first year. 

J&J has launched three Phase 3 studies, including two head-to-head trials against bispecific antibodies in relapsed myeloma, and one combining ramantamig with Darzalex in transplant-ineligible newly diagnosed patients, though no data has been reported in the front-line setting. (09/29/26)

## 5. Innovent IBI3003 Leads China Trispecific Class in Myeloma 

Early data for trispecific T-cell engagers from Chinese drugmakers, with a similar mechanism as ramantamig, highlight the potential for licensing deals. 

Innovent leads the class with over 100 patients treated with IBI3003, and a Phase 3 TriadicMM-1 trial has been initiated in China. In the selected optimal-dose cohort, an 85% overall response rate including a 42% complete response rate was reported at a median follow-up of 7.6 months, with response deepening ongoing. Qilu's QLS4131 showed encouraging responses even in BCMA/GPRC5Dexposed groups, with objective response rates of 100% in small nine-patient cohorts. Mabworks' MBS314, in collaboration with Pfizer, appears to trail in efficacy. All three agents show manageable, class-consistent safety profiles. 

Updates are also pending for Simcere's SIM0500, another China-developed trispecific that secured a $1 billion licensing deal with Abbvie. (09/29/26)
