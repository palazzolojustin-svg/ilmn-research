Roche, Bristol-BioNTech ESMO Data Can Reset Lung Cancer Care 

(Bloomberg Intelligence) -- Roche's divarasib looks poised to challenge KRAS G12C therapies in second-line lung cancer and expand into first-line treatment, while Bristol-BioNTech's pumitamig can strengthen the case for PD-(L)1xVEGF combinations. Upcoming ESMO 2026 data will test both strategies, with pumitamig's pairing with a B7-H3 antibody-drug conjugate offering the more consequential test of a potential future standard combination. (10/05/26) 

1. Divarasib Poised to Reset Second-Line KRAS G12C Lung Care 

Roche's next-generation KRAS G12C inhibitor divarasib is poised to become the new standard for second-line treatment of KRAS G12C-mutant lung cancer, with progression-free survival potentially double the five to six months achieved by approved rivals. KRASCENDO 1 compares divarasib with Amgen's Lumakras and Bristol's Krazati, which together are set to generate $650 million of 2026 sales. Earlier divarasib data showed more than twice the progression-free survival of those therapies, a benefit we expect it to replicate in the comparative trial. That should displace rivals and expand the market opportunity, with consensus' $1.4 billion peak-sales estimate looking conservative as divarasib expands to first-line treatment. KRASCENDO 3 is recruiting, with primary completion estimated in 2032. 

Lilly's olomorasib and Merck's calderasib pose a risk to divarasib, yet early market positioning will be important if differences in effectiveness narrow between the next generation of G12C inhibitors. (10/05/26)
