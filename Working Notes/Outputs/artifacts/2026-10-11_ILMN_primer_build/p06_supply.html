3Suppliers, customers and unit economics by product

Illumina makes its own chemistry and flow cells and buys mostly components, so its supplier risk is about cost inflation rather than dependency. Its customer risk is the opposite: a small number of very large clinical labs now drive most of the growth, and their contracts let them leave on short notice.

3.1 Suppliers: who Illumina pays

Illumina's filings name no supplier. They say most components come from multiple sources but some raw materials and sub-assemblies come from a single source, and that Illumina could redesign around them or make them in-house D. Purchase commitments are small: $184M of non-cancellable supply and licence obligations at end-2025, more than half due in 2026 D. The table lists what we can identify.

3.2 Customers: who pays Illumina

No customer is more than 10% of revenue D, but the FY2025 10-K added a new risk sentence saying revenue is "increasingly derived from a small number of large, centralized laboratory customers," which may bring "greater pricing pressure, reduced negotiating leverage" D. Experts put it more bluntly: roughly 10–20 large labs drive most clinical volume, and Illumina is "just the supplier" to them X.

How this compares with rivals. Roche lists its AXELIOS consumables at $150 per genome with no volume discounts, and $0.06 per million reads in its high-output mode against $0.68 on Illumina's 25B list E: UBS. Ultima markets about $80 per genome. Element claims about $1 per Gb on VITARI, half the X's $2. Oxford Nanopore and PacBio sit at about $345 per genome for long reads. Large Illumina customers already pay about $125–175 per genome after discounts, according to one expert relayed by UBS X, so the headline gap to Roche is smaller than the list prices suggest.
