7Management guidance in depth

Since mid-2024, CEO Jacob Thaysen and CFO Ankur Dhingra have set quarterly guidance they reliably beat, but they have twice set a first full-year revenue guide that later had to be cut. The 2026 guide has been raised twice. The 2027 targets (high-single-digit growth, a 26% operating margin, double-digit to teens EPS growth) have been kept in name but softened in definition twice. This section lists everything management guides, what the current guide implies, and how far to trust it.

The annual record is weaker. The first full-year revenue guide was too high in both 2024 and 2025. In 2024 management guided flat revenue and delivered −2.4%, cutting twice along the way. In 2025 it cut the revenue guide in March and May, mainly for China and tariffs, before raising again; the year ended within $3M of the January midpoint, but only after a round trip. In both years the first EPS guide proved too low: 2024 EPS of $4.16 beat the first guide's midpoint by 7.4%, and 2025 EPS of $4.84 beat it by 5.8%. The read-through is that this team protects earnings and is less reliable on revenue, especially when external shocks hit. The 2026 guide is the first under this team that has only been raised.

Margin beats are shrinking. Operating margin beat the guide by 250–420 basis points in 2024, by 250–280 in mid-2025, by 145 in Q1'26 and by 50 in Q2'26. Revenue beats have held steady while cost inflation (tariffs, memory chips, freight) absorbs more of the upside.

What the 2027 targets imply in numbers

Translating the targets onto the 2026 guide gives a range for 2027 that brackets consensus.

  • Revenue. HSD growth ex-China, read as 7–9%, on 2026 ex-China revenue of about $4.41B, plus a shrinking China of about $190M, gives roughly $4.90–4.99B. Consensus is $4,918M, at the bottom of that range. Note that 2027 compares against a 53-week 2026, which costs about 0.5 point or more.
  • Operating margin. "Within 50 basis points" of 26% means about 25.5–26%. Consensus is 25.2%, slightly below.
  • EPS. Double-digit to teens growth on the 2026 midpoint of $5.35 gives $5.89–6.37. Consensus of $6.11 (+14%) sits in the middle.
  • The volume–price model. Management's own long-term model is volume growth in the mid-20s and price declines in the mid-teens, which gives high-single-digit consumables growth. Volume has run above 30%, so the model has room if price behaves. The 2027 question is whether the 35B and contract pricing push the price decline back above the mid-teens.

7.6 Where management's statements matter most for the debates

Management's positions on the key debates, paraphrased, with the date. Short quotes are kept where the exact wording matters.

  • "Not a cliff, a wave." The CEO has rejected the idea of a clinical "cliff" (a sudden revenue drop when large labs move to the X) in November 2024, July 2025 and July 2026. His description of a converted customer: the first year after moving is "flattish", and growth returns in the second year. The CFO said 40 fully converted clinical customers saw volume offset price in year one and both accelerate in year two (Oct 2025).
  • The tail. About 20% of tests will stay on the 6000 (Feb 2025); "some of them… will never move" (CFO, Nov 2024); the 6000 transition should be "mostly substantially done" by end-2026 (CFO, Feb 2026). These statements sit uneasily together.
  • Pricing. Pricing headwinds should "start to dissipate" through 2026 and "shouldn't be worse than '25" (CEO, Mar 2026). Illumina has moved from one global price list to pricing by application and geography (Aug 2024), and offers separate pricing lines that let a test run at higher volume rather than cutting prices across the board (Feb 2025). On the 35B: no price until closer to launch.
  • Competition. "We don't see any substantial change in the competitive environment" (CEO, Apr 2026, after an analyst cited Roche's $150 genome).
  • Volume. Clinical volume growth "above 30%" should continue "for a while"; the whole business should settle near 25% (CEO, Jan 2026).
  • New products. "1 to 2 points of growth next year" (CFO, Jul 2026); not meaningful in 2026 (CEO, Feb 2026).
How to use the guidance

Treat the quarterly guide as a floor: the realistic outcome is 1–3% above the revenue midpoint and 5–10% above the EPS midpoint, with the margin beat shrinking. Treat the first annual guide (due early February 2027) as the real event. History says it will be set cautiously on revenue, which in February 2026 cost the stock 10%. And treat the 2027 targets as achievable only at their lower edge: about 7% growth ex-China, a margin near 25.5%, and EPS around $6.00–6.15.
