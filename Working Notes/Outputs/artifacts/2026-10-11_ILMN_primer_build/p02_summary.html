The answers up front

Illumina sells the machines that read DNA and, far more importantly, the chemicals every run consumes. Clinical labs testing for cancer and inherited disease now drive almost all of its growth. The business is improving. The stock has run well ahead of it: it trades at 52 times this year's earnings because the market is paying for faster growth than either management or the sell-side forecasts.

The business in five sentences

About three-quarters of Illumina's $4.6B of 2026 revenue comes from consumables, the flow cells and reagents used in every sequencing run, and about 90% is recurring. Customers are sequencing more than 30% more DNA each year, but consumables revenue grows only about 5%, because Illumina's newer NovaSeq X sequencer sells each gigabase for about a third of what the older NovaSeq 6000 did. Clinical labs now buy about 65% of sequencing consumables and grew 15–20% in recent quarters; research customers are shrinking on US funding cuts but stabilising. Management targets high-single-digit growth, a ~26% operating margin and double-digit to teens EPS growth in 2027, and has beaten every quarterly earnings guide since 2024. Competition has so far shown up in price rather than share, and the main new rival, Roche's AXELIOS, has only just launched.

Where we differ from the Street

Variance summary
  • Near term, upside. Illumina has beaten its revenue guide by about 2% and its EPS guide by about 13% on average. We expect Q3 near $1.17B and $1.42–1.45 (consensus $1,154M and $1.36) and FY26 EPS near $5.45–5.50 (consensus $5.36). The extra week in Q4 may be worth more than consensus models.
  • 2027–28, downside. Consensus needs sequencing consumables to accelerate to +8.8% and +9.3% and gross margin to rise to 69.5%. That requires revenue per gigabase to fall more slowly than it has, just as the 35B flow cell, Roche's AXELIOS and Medicare cuts arrive. Our base is about +7% consumables growth, 2027 EPS near $5.90 (consensus $6.11) and 2028 EPS of $6.50–6.70 (consensus $7.01).
  • Watch items. The X pull-through guide ($1.35–1.40M) looks stretched, though a small miss would mostly reflect more boxes placed; free cash flow and buybacks are running behind; research stabilisation and new products are the main upside risks to our view.

Valuation

Illumina trades at 52 times 2026 and 40 times 2028 consensus earnings, against a median of about 26 times for large tools companies and about 17 times for Roche. Peer-based methods give $155–265 a share. On our estimates a fair range is about $190–220; consensus numbers support about $225–245; only our bull case (2028 EPS of $7.60 at 40 times) reaches today's price. The average sell-side target is $222–234.

What decides it

On 29 October, watch clinical consumables growth against a +12% comparison (15% or more rebuts the slowdown, below 12% confirms it), the X's share of high-throughput revenue, and gross margin. X pull-through is disclosed only once a year, so its test comes with Q4 results in February 2027. In early 2027, the 35B flow cell's price and the first 2027 guide matter more than anything else. History says that first guide will be cautious; in February 2026 a cautious guide cost the stock 10%.
