6Variance to the Street: where we would differ from consensus

This is the written list of places where we think the Bloomberg consensus (10 October 2026) is mis-set. Near term, we expect Illumina to beat again. Further out, we think consensus is too high on 2027–28 sequencing consumables growth and gross margin, because the price per gigabase keeps falling faster than the Street's numbers allow. Each item gives the direction, the rough size, the reasoning and what would prove us wrong.

First, our numbers next to consensus. The table summarises where the items below net out. Our base case is our own judgment, built from the evidence in sections 2–5; the bull and bear cases bracket it. The main message: we are slightly above consensus for 2026 and about 3–7% below it for 2027–28 earnings.

  1. Below consensus · FY27–28 · ~$35–85M revenue, ~$0.15–0.35 EPS

    Sequencing consumables growth in 2027–28 is too high at +8.8% and +9.3%. Consensus needs consumables to accelerate from +4.5% in the first half of 2026 to almost +9%. Using the arithmetic in section 8, if gigabases grow 30%, consensus needs revenue per gigabase to fall only about 16% a year, against about 19% recently; if gigabases grow only 20–25%, the allowable price decline shrinks to 9–13%. We expect revenue per gigabase to fall 12–18% in 2027 because the 35B flow cell arrives in the first half, clinical work keeps moving from the 6000 (about $90–130M of 2027 drag, section 5.2), and large-lab contracts only let prices fall. We put 2027 growth near +7%.

    What would prove us wrong: the 2026 X placement wave (about $300M a year of potential consumables once fully used) arriving faster than the 6–9 month ramp, and research turning positive. When we will know: the first 2027 guide in early February 2027, and the 35B price.

  2. Below the bull narrative · H2'26 and 2027

    Clinical consumables growth is decelerating, and the guide already says so. Clinical outside China grew +20%, +20% and then +15%. The raised "mid-teens" full-year guide implies about +11–15% in the second half, centred near +13%, because the comparisons get harder (+12% in Q3'25, +20% in Q4'25). The market's narrative (RBC: "durable mid-teens") assumes the Q2 dip was purely the Middle East. Consensus does not publish a clinical line, but its +8.8% total consumables number needs clinical in the low-to-mid teens in 2027 while research stops shrinking. We expect low teens.

    What would prove us wrong: Q3'26 clinical growth of 15% or more. When: 29 October.

  3. KPI risk · Q4'26 deck (Feb 2027)

    The NovaSeq X pull-through guide of $1.35–1.40M for 2026 looks stretched, though less than our raw numbers suggest. Pull-through is consumables revenue per installed instrument per year. On our derived figures and the average installed base, the X's first-half pull-through annualised at about $1.21M, and the second half would need about $1.46–1.55M to hit the guide S. Two things narrow the gap. Our method understated the disclosed 2025 figure by about 3–5%, and the CFO said in July that pull-through "increased year-over-year" while our numbers show a slight fall, so our derivation is probably biased low. And the 14-week Q4 adds roughly 4% to the second half. Adjusted, the second half needs roughly a 10–15% step-up, which the ramp of about 280 boxes placed since Q4'25 could partly supply. Our best guess is a full-year figure near $1.30–1.35M: the low end of the guide or a small miss. The miss would come mainly from placing more boxes than planned, which lowers the per-box average while raising total consumables, so it is a weaker bear signal than it looks. Our quarterly figures are derived; Illumina publishes pull-through only once a year, in the Q4 deck (with a JPM-conference preview in January). The Q1'26 and Q2'26 decks did not repeat the guide, which is normal, and the calls gave only qualitative comments ("pull-through increased year-over-year"). The test is therefore the FY26 actual in the Q4'26 deck in February 2027: a figure below $1.35M, or a missing table, would feed the "price per gigabase" bear case. The reason is benign in part: record placements dilute the average because new boxes take 6–9 months to ramp.

    What would prove us wrong: an FY26 X pull-through of $1.35M or more. When: JPM conference (mid-January 2027) or Q4'26 results (early February 2027).

  4. Above consensus · Q3–Q4'26 · ~$15–35M revenue, ~$0.08–0.15 EPS

    Illumina will probably beat the second half again. In the ten quarters with a revenue guide since 2024, revenue beat the midpoint by an average of 1.9%, and never missed the low end. Earnings per share beat the midpoint in all eight quarters with an EPS guide, by an average of 12.9%, though the size of the beats has shrunk to +10% and +7% in 2026. Consensus sits exactly on the guidance midpoints ($1,154M and $1.36 for Q3; implied $1,220M and $1.54 for Q4). A typical recent beat puts Q3 at about $1.17B and $1.42–1.45, and FY26 EPS at about $5.45–5.50.

    What would prove us wrong: memory-chip and freight inflation, which management said in July was worse "than we anticipated", absorbing the upside at the margin line. Q4 needs an operating margin of about 25.5%, the highest in the company's recent history.

  5. Above consensus · Q4'26 / below on FY27 growth optics

    The extra (53rd) week in Q4'26 may be worth more than consensus models. Illumina's fiscal 2026 has 53 weeks. The CFO said the extra week is in Q4 and is "largely a consumables story", worth about 0.5 point of revenue. Mechanically, a fourteenth week is one-thirteenth of a normal quarter, about 2% of full-year revenue, though holiday weeks sell less. Consensus Q4'26 sequencing consumables growth of +7.9%, against +5.6% in Q3, embeds only about $17M of extra-week benefit. If the week is worth more, Q4 beats, and 2027 faces a harder comparison, which makes reported 2027 growth look weaker. Bloomberg's own period dates wrongly put the extra week in Q3; investors comparing Q3 against a 14-week consensus could misread the print.

  6. Below consensus · FY27–28 gross margin · ~$20–35M, ~$0.10–0.20 EPS

    Consensus gross margin rises from 68.5% to 69.1% in 2027 and 69.5% in 2028; we expect it to be flat at best. The rise assumes no net price erosion at the margin line. Against that: the 35B flow cell lowers price per gigabase on Illumina's highest-margin product; memory-chip costs rose more than expected and Illumina is now "sharing some of the increase with customers"; freight costs rose with the Middle East conflict; SomaLogic is lower-margin; and Q2'26's margin was helped by $22M of government incentives, against $4M a year earlier. Gross margin was 68.2% in Q2'26, down 120 basis points year over year. Barclays models gross margin falling to 67.8% in 2027. Each 50 basis points is about $25M of profit or $0.13 of EPS.

    What would prove us wrong: X consumables reaching "6000-like margins at scale" as management designed, and cost programmes running ahead.

  7. Swing factor · both directions · FY27

    Research and applied customers are the swing factor, and our base case already assumes they recover. Research consumables fell 12% in Q1'26 and 7% in Q2'26, but rose about 9% from Q1 to Q2. About 90% of research volume is already on the X, so most of the price step is behind it, and J.P. Morgan's academic respondents expect consumables spending up 9% in 2027. Research is about 35% of sequencing consumables, so each 7 points of research growth moves the total by about 2.5 points. Our +7% base assumes clinical grows about 11–12% and research about 2% in 2027, less about 1.3 points for China's decline and the extra week in 2026. If research recovers to mid-single digits, our number rises to consensus; if it stays negative, as one genome-centre expert now expects for 2027 because of US funding, it falls to about +5%.

  8. Above consensus · H2'26 instruments · small EPS effect

    Consensus has instrument revenue falling 8% in the second half of 2026 and 6% in 2027. Placements ran at about 83 and 95+ X systems in Q1 and Q2'26, against a 50–60 a quarter framework, and management says they remain "elevated" in the second half. The 35B and 14B flow cells (H1'27) and the end of 6000 support could also drive upgrades. Offsets: volume discounts, and leases and reagent rentals that defer revenue. Instruments earn only about a 35% gross margin, so a $20–40M beat matters for the placement story, not for EPS.

  9. Below consensus · FY28 and beyond

    2027–28 brings a cluster of pricing risks that no consensus line shows. Roche's AXELIOS launched in June at $150 a genome with no volume discounts and about 100 systems targeted in year one; Element's VITARI ships in the second half of 2026 at about half the X's price per gigabase; Medicare's proposed 2027 cut to genomic-sequencing codes pushes clinical labs to cut costs; and the GRAIL-era "open offer" pricing terms may expire around August 2027 (unverified), which could reopen contract negotiations with the largest customers. Barclays expects "another round of pricing headwinds in 2027 and beyond". We think consensus FY28 EPS of $7.01 (+14.7%) is too high; our base is $6.50–6.70.

    What would prove us wrong: large labs validating new tests on the X rather than on rivals, and a gentle 35B price. JPM's survey found only 8% of customers would consider buying AXELIOS in the next 6–12 months.

  10. Below consensus · FY26 cash flow and buybacks

    Free cash flow and buybacks are running behind. Consensus expects $997M of free cash flow in 2026. The first half produced $413M, so the second half needs about $584M, against $520M in the second half of 2025. Q2'26 free cash flow fell to $162M because Illumina stockpiled memory chips for several quarters. Buybacks slowed sharply above $140 a share; at $278, the $420M modelled for 2026 buys few shares. We expect 2026 free cash flow of roughly $900–950M.

  11. Optional upside, not in consensus · FY27

    New products could add about 1 point that consensus does not obviously include. Our base case for multiomics and data is about $59M (1.3 points) in 2027, of which $31M is evidence-backed (section 5.3). Consensus 2027 growth of 6.3% leaves little visible room for it. If it arrives on top of a 6% core, revenue could beat by $30–60M. We treat this as an option, because nothing is disclosed.

  12. Small upside, not in consensus · 2027

    GRAIL royalties restart at the end of 2026. GRAIL's filings show its royalty to Illumina (9%, falling to a 7% floor with offsets) was suspended until 24 December 2026. It then resumes. GRAIL's revenue is small today, so this is a few million dollars a year unless the multi-cancer test wins Medicare coverage, which a House committee advanced 43–0 in September. Illumina also owns about 14.5% of GRAIL. Separately, the GRAIL supply agreement's first non-renewal deadline falls around 31 October 2026.

How the list nets out

Near term (Q3 and Q4'26) the evidence favours upside to consensus: a long beat record, a possibly under-modelled extra week and strong placements. Medium term (2027–28) it favours downside: consensus assumes consumables accelerate and gross margin expands while price per gigabase falls more slowly than it has. That combination, a likely beat followed by a cautious first 2027 guide, is the same pattern the stock showed in February 2026, when a strong Q4'25 beat met a conservative 2026 guide and the shares fell 10.4%.
