10Valuation: where Illumina trades, and where it should

At $278 Illumina trades at 52 times 2026 earnings and 9.3 times 2026 sales, roughly double the median of large life-science tools companies on earnings. Every peer-based method we tried gives $155–265 a share. A cash-flow model says the price needs revenue to grow about 16% a year from 2029 to 2035. On our estimates, a fair range is about $190–220, consensus numbers support about $225–245, and only the bull case reaches today's price.

The fairest single yardstick is the growth-adjusted P/E. It gives Illumina credit for its faster earnings growth, about 39 times rather than 26, and still lands at $230–237. The case for paying more rests on four things: earnings growth of about 14% a year from margin expansion, clinical consumables growing in the mid-teens, the 2027 high-single-digit target, and the scarcity of a clinical-sequencing franchise with high switching costs. The case against: Roche's $150 genome, discounting to defend share, and dependence on 10–20 large clinical customers.

10.4 The reverse DCF: what the price assumes

A discounted cash flow (DCF) model values a company as the present value of all its future free cash flow. A reverse DCF runs the model backwards: it takes today's price and asks what growth is needed to justify it. We hold consensus free cash flow for 2027–28, then solve for the growth rate needed from 2029 to 2035, with a discount rate (WACC, the return investors require) of 9–10% and long-run growth after 2035 of 3–4%.

Cells show the annual FCF growth needed in 2029–35 to justify an EV of $42.8B Der. About 70% of the value sits in the years after 2035.

Expressed as revenue: if the operating margin rises to 30% by 2035, the price needs revenue to grow about 16% a year from 2029 to 2035, reaching about $14.9B in 2035, 3.4 times 2025 revenue. Consensus has revenue growing about 7% a year to 2028, and management targets high single digits. Put the other way, if revenue grows a strong 10% a year in 2029–35, the model gives $184–222 a share; at 8% it gives $166–199. J.P. Morgan's own sensitivity table reaches $273 only with a discount rate of 8.1% and long-run growth of 5%, the most generous corner of its grid.
