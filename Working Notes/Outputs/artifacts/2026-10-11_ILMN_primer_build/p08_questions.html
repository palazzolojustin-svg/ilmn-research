5The three Illumina questions, and what the buy side is implying

These are the four questions the stock turns on. For each one we give the answer first, then the evidence, then what would change our mind. Where the answer is our own judgment rather than a disclosed fact, we say so.

5.1 What is the true go-forward growth in clinical sequencing volume, and is management's explanation for the slowdown right?

Answer

Clinical sequencing volume is still growing fast: we estimate about 35–50% a year in gigabases over the past year, centred near 42%, and we expect roughly 25–35% in 2027. But clinical consumables revenue is decelerating, from +20% to +15% in Q2'26 and, on management's own full-year guide, to roughly +13% in the second half. Management's explanation (a temporary Middle East and Latin America disruption) is true but incomplete. The bigger reasons are tougher comparisons in the second half, the continuing move of clinical work to the cheaper NovaSeq X, and a narrowing base of very large customers. We expect clinical consumables revenue to grow in the low teens in 2027, not the mid-teens the bulls assume.

Volume and revenue are different things

Illumina reports two kinds of growth, and the gap between them is the whole story. Volume is measured in gigabases (Gb), billions of DNA letters read. Management says gigabases sequenced on its connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30% year over year in every quarter since Q3'25, and that clinical volume grew "well above that" in both Q1 and Q2'26 D. At the January JPM conference the CEO was more specific: clinical volume growth is "above 30%" and should stay there "for a while", while the whole business should settle at about 25% MGMT. Revenue grows much more slowly because the price Illumina earns per gigabase keeps falling as customers move from the NovaSeq 6000 to the NovaSeq X, which sells a gigabase for roughly a third of the price.

Illumina does not publish a clinical gigabase number. We can back one out. Clinical consumables revenue outside China grew 15–20% in the last three quarters. If revenue per clinical gigabase fell about 20–28% (the range implied by the X's share of clinical work rising from about 64% to 78% over the year), clinical gigabases must have grown about 35–50% SDer. A second check, built from the gigabase and revenue shares of the X and the 6000, says total high-throughput gigabases probably grew 33–45% in Q2'26, because any lower figure would require the 6000's price per gigabase to have risen, which its falling list prices rule out S.

What the customers are doing

The bottom-up evidence comes from Illumina's largest clinical customers, who report test volumes every quarter. In Q2'26 Natera's MRD tests (minimal residual disease, blood tests that look for cancer coming back) grew 56%, Guardant's oncology tests 63%, GeneDx's exomes and genomes 32%, Tempus's oncology tests 31%, Caris's cases 18% and BillionToOne's tests 35%, while Myriad fell 1% S. Test counts are not gigabases, because tests use very different amounts of sequencing. A prenatal screen (NIPT) uses about 1–2 Gb, an exome about 8 Gb, a tissue tumour panel about 15 Gb, and a 30X whole genome or a liquid-biopsy panel 100–120 Gb or more. Natera's amplicon-based MRD test uses less than 1 Gb per blood draw S. Weighting the Q2'26 test growth rates by gigabases rather than by revenue gives clinical gigabase growth of about 31–34% S, a little below the revenue-implied range but consistent with management's "above 30%".

Two structural drivers support continued high volume growth. First, intensity: moving a rare-disease case from an exome to a whole genome uses about 15 times more sequencing, and MRD patients are tested three or four times a year rather than once MGMTX. Second, penetration: management's slides put comprehensive tumour profiling, MRD, multi-cancer early detection and genetic-disease testing each at less than 20% of their eventual market MGMT. Against that, several drivers are maturing. Experts say NIPT already reaches 70–80% of US pregnancies and tissue tumour profiling is "quite saturated" X. Whole-genome testing is still "much, much smaller" than exome testing, so the 15-fold intensity jump applies to a small base today MGMT. And J.P. Morgan's June survey of 32 clinical labs that use Illumina expects only +15% volume growth in 2027 and +19% in 2028, measured in runs and samples rather than gigabases E: JPM.

Is management's explanation for the Q2 slowdown right?

On the Q2'26 call the CEO explained the drop from +20% to +15% as "some in and outs" in the Middle East and Latin America, with US clinical still growing faster than 20% MGMT. The regional data back the claim: in Q2'26 the US and Canada grew 13% organically while Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Latin America were flat D. So the explanation is accurate. It is not complete, for three reasons.

  1. The full-year guide already assumes further slowing. Management raised the 2026 clinical consumables guide to "mid-teens". The first half grew about 17.5%. For the year to land at 14–16%, the second half must grow about 11–15%, centred near 13% Der. That is a guided deceleration, not a one-off.
  2. The comparisons get harder. Clinical consumables grew 8%, 10%, 12% and 20% in the four quarters of 2025 D. Q1 and Q2'26 were measured against the two easiest quarters; Q4'26 is measured against +20%. Even on a two-year basis, growth slowed from 28% in Q1'26 to 25% in Q2'26 Der.
  3. The price transition is still running in clinical. Clinical work on the X rose from 76% to 78% of volume between Q1 and Q2'26 and is targeted at 80–85% by year-end D. Every point of conversion lowers revenue per gigabase. The research market, which finished converting a year earlier, shows the pattern: its revenue fell for most of 2025 while its gigabases kept growing.

Management also points to the 6–9 month lag before a newly placed X reaches normal consumption, saying "most of that benefit will come in 2027" MGMT. That is fair: placements of more than 80 and more than 95 X systems in Q1 and Q2'26, about 70% to clinical labs, have not yet shown up fully in consumables. Our rough estimate is that the 2026 placements could add about $300M a year of consumables once fully used, about 10% of the line, before price effects E: 03 B5d. That is the best argument that clinical revenue growth can hold up in 2027.

Two risks that management did not mention

First, reimbursement. On 21 September 2026 the US Medicare agency (CMS) proposed cutting 2027 payment rates for generic genomic-sequencing codes by about 23%, phased in at no more than 15% a year from 2027 to 2029 S. Branded tests such as Natera's Signatera are mostly on separate, protected codes, so the direct hit falls on hereditary and exome panels. The indirect effect is that clinical labs will push harder on their sequencing costs. Second, concentration. Roughly 10–20 large US labs drive most clinical volume X. Their own growth rates are high but will slow as they get bigger, and every one of them has a strong incentive to negotiate down the price of sequencing.

What would change our view. A Q3'26 clinical growth rate of 15% or more, against a tougher +12% comparison, would show the Middle East explanation was the whole story. A rate below 12% would show the deceleration is structural.

5.2 Is Illumina over-earning, and what happens to consumables pricing as the last 17% of volume leaves the NovaSeq 6000?

Answer

Yes, in a measurable sense. About $0.9B a year of revenue, roughly 28% of sequencing consumables, comes from NovaSeq 6000 work that earns about 3.4 times what the same gigabase earns on the X. If all of that work moved to the X tomorrow, high-throughput consumables revenue would fall about 29%. That will not happen quickly: perhaps a fifth of the work never moves, regulated tests are anchored to the 6000, and customers are still buying new 6000s. We expect the tail to shrink by about 4–6 points of high-throughput gigabase share in 2027, which costs roughly $90–130M of revenue that year before volume growth. The larger pricing risk in 2027–28 is no longer the 6000 tail. It is the price of the X itself: the 35B flow cell, contracts that only let prices fall, and competitive quotes from Roche, Element and Ultima.

Sizing the over-earning pool

Management discloses two numbers each quarter: the X's share of high-throughput gigabases and its share of high-throughput consumables revenue. In Q2'26 the X carried 83% of gigabases but only 59% of revenue. So the remaining 17% of gigabases, almost all on the NovaSeq 6000, earned 41% of revenue D. Dividing one ratio by the other, a gigabase on the 6000 earns about 3.4 times what it earns on the X Der. This is not a pure price comparison: it mixes list prices, flow-cell sizes (small flow cells cost more per gigabase) and discounts. The CFO's own 2024 estimate was that the move from 6000 to X cut the price per gigabase by "easily more than 60% to 70%" MGMT, which is consistent with a ratio near three.

The chart shows how the two shares have moved. The persistent gap of about 24 points between them is the over-earning. Note that the two lines rose by about the same number of points (+15 and +16). That is not a sign of stable pricing: if the X's price relative to the 6000 had held at its Q1'25 level, an 83% gigabase share would have produced about a 63% revenue share, not 59%. The X's implied price per gigabase relative to the 6000 fell from about 0.36 to about 0.30 over the period Der, consistent with Barclays' observation that the X's revenue share lagged its volume share.

The conversion is slow, and parts of it are blocked. The CEO's "rule of thumb" is that about 20% of clinical tests will stay on the 6000, and the last fifth is "taking substantially longer to transition" than the first four-fifths MGMT. More than ten new NovaSeq 6000s were sold in Q2'26 to customers that "plan to remain on that platform for years to come" MGMT. We found no FDA-cleared or CE-marked version of the NovaSeq X; the only high-throughput diagnostic instrument is the NovaSeq 6000Dx, so regulated tests cannot easily move S. A single pharmaceutical-grade test can cost up to $200M in clinical trials to move to a new platform X. And management guides 6000 pull-through (consumables bought per instrument per year) at $600–650K for 2026, down only modestly from about $670K in 2025 D.

On the CEO's own logic, clinical conversion should approach its ceiling at roughly 80–90% by 2027. That suggests the X's share of high-throughput gigabases rises from 83% to perhaps 87–89% by the end of 2027. At $45M a quarter per 5 points, the exit-rate drag is about $35–55M a quarter, or roughly $90–130M of 2027 revenue once the gradual timing is averaged Der. That is about 3–4% of sequencing consumables, and it is the main reason we sit below consensus on 2027 consumables growth (section 6).

The CEO was asked this question directly on the Q2'26 call. Jefferies' analyst asked whether Illumina is "over-earning on consumables" because customers are running both platforms in parallel. The CEO did not size the 6000 pool or the decommissioning schedule; he said the transition is "a standard part of the business", compared it to the HiSeq phase-out, and moved to clinical growth MGMT. We read that as a dodge rather than a rebuttal.

Forward pricing on the X itself

Once the 6000 tail is small, the price of the X becomes the variable that matters. Five forces push it down:

  • The 35B flow cell, due in the first half of 2027, should lower the cost per gigabase by about 20% for the largest users XE: UBS. Management has refused to discuss its price: "we are not ready at this point to go out and talk about pricing" MGMT.
  • Yield improvements on existing flow cells. Illumina's own roadmap quotes a customer saying usable output per X run rose about 30% since purchase; at an unchanged flow-cell price that is a 23% cut in price per gigabase that never appears as a list-price change S.
  • Contracts that can only move down. Natera's agreement freezes prices and caps the price per gigabase on new flow cells. Caris's agreement, written on the "open offer" Illumina made to win approval for the GRAIL deal, includes a most-favoured-customer clause: if Illumina gives a comparable customer a better price, it must refund Caris the difference on units already bought S.
  • Competitive quotes as leverage. Large academic centres negotiate "insane" discounts using rival quotes, and Illumina now gives away instruments, automation and software at threatened sites X.
  • Reagent rental and volume discounts. A small but rising share of X placements are leased or rented, with the instrument's cost recovered through higher consumables prices, and multi-unit orders get volume discounts MGMT.

One more data point argues the X's price is already slipping. Our analysis of the Q2'25 and Q2'26 share data shows the X's revenue per gigabase fell about 10% year over year if total gigabases grew 30%, and about 16% if they grew 40% S. Barclays made the same point in May: the X's revenue share stayed flat at 55% in Q1'26 while its gigabase share rose five points E: Barclays. Management called that comparison "a frivolous exercise" because flow-cell mix moves it MGMT.

Our forward view. Revenue per high-throughput gigabase fell about 19% in the last year. We expect about 12–18% in 2027: the mix effect from the 6000 shrinks, but within-X price declines from the 35B and contracts take its place Der. With clinical gigabases growing 25–35%, that supports clinical consumables revenue growth of roughly 8–16%, centred in the low teens.

What would change our view. In the Q3'26 deck, a derived 6000 revenue figure below $210M a quarter and an X revenue share above 60%, followed in February 2027 by a disclosed FY26 X pull-through of $1.35M or more, would mean the tail is converting without hurting revenue. The reverse would mean the drag is arriving faster than we assume.

5.3 Does multiomics uptake support the "1–2 points of growth" bridge, or is it a plug?

Answer

The 1–2 points look like a reasonable but unevidenced assumption, not a demonstrated number and not an outright plug. Our bottom-up estimate gives $21M, $59M or $134M of new-product revenue in 2027 (low, base, high), which is 0.45, 1.3 or 2.9 points of growth. Only about $31M (0.7 point) rests on disclosed evidence. The figure is "plug-like" in one specific sense: the high-single-digit 2027 target needs roughly 1–2 points on top of a core business growing 5–6%, and management has chosen not to disclose the pieces. Importantly, consensus does not need it.

What management has promised

"Multiomics" here means measuring other biological molecules (proteins, RNA in single cells, tissue location, DNA methylation) using Illumina's sequencers as the readout. The promise has evolved. In May 2025 the CEO said new products would add "probably another 1 point"; in July 2025 "at least 1% point… probably more '27 level"; in November 2025 "1% to 2%" in 2027; in February 2026 he added the data business (BioInsight) to the definition and said it would not be "meaningful here really in '26"; and in July 2026 the CFO said "new products will add 1 to 2 points of growth next year" MGMT. On a $4.62B base, 1–2 points is $46–92M of extra revenue in 2027 Der. Management has never said whether SomaLogic's growth after its first anniversary (February 2027) counts toward that figure.

Three points weigh against the claim. First, the expert panel found no customer buying SomaLogic, StrataMap or PIPseq, and TruPath intent ranged from 0% to 6% of a lab's volume X. Second, when the CEO said in July that the 1–2 points were "playing out as we're expecting" since 2024, the numeric target did not exist in 2024; it first appears in May 2025 MGMT. Third, 2026's guidance beat came from clinical consumables and X instruments, not from new products MGMT. Two points support it: SomaLogic is tracking at the top of its deal case, and the data business is real revenue that is "making money already from day 1" MGMT.

Why this matters less than it seems. Consensus 2027 revenue growth is 6.3% reported, about 6.6% after adjusting for the extra week in 2026 and SomaLogic's January. If the core business grows 5–6%, consensus implicitly includes about 0.5–1.5 points from new products, close to our base case. So the bridge is not a source of downside to consensus. It is a source of downside to management's high-single-digit target, which the stock's valuation arguably already assumes.

5.4 What is the buy side implying relative to the Street?

Answer

At $278 the market is paying for numbers well above consensus, a permanently higher multiple, or both. The stock sits 16–20% above the average sell-side target ($222–234). It trades at 52 times 2026 consensus earnings, against about 25 times at the end of April, while 2026 earnings estimates rose only about 5% in that time. A cash-flow model (reverse DCF) says the price needs revenue to grow about 16% a year from 2029 to 2035, more than twice the consensus rate of about 7%. Our reading is that the buy side has stopped valuing Illumina as a tools company and started valuing it as a clinical-genomics growth company, and that index buying and momentum amplified the move.

The multiple did the work. From 30 April to 9 October the price rose about 119% while FY26 consensus EPS rose from $5.12 to $5.36 E: BBG cons. Almost the entire move is re-rating. The price-to-earnings ratio (P/E: share price divided by earnings per share) went from about 25 times to about 52 times.

The likely buy-side "whisper" view is therefore something like this: 2027 revenue growth of 8–10% rather than 6.3%, reaching double digits in 2028, with operating margins at or above the 26% target, giving 2028 EPS of $7.50–8.00. Barclays captured the mood in July: investors, it wrote, treat high-single-digit growth as "in the bag", and new buyers must believe Illumina can deliver it consistently and eventually reach double digits E: Barclays. RBC's $310 target on 5 October cited "durable mid-teens clinical sequencing growth" and an "inflection in clinical sequencing demand" W.

Flows amplified it. Illumina joined the S&P 500 on 21 September. On 18 September, the day index funds bought, 32.6M shares traded, about 12 times normal volume and about 22% of shares outstanding W. Five brokers then raised targets above $300 within eight trading days, after the price had already moved. Meanwhile director Keith Meister sold about 3.06M shares for about $591M between May and early September, all discretionary sales D. Short interest is moderate (6.5% of float on one source, 10.8% on another) W. The stock reversed 11.6% from its $309 intraday record on 6 October.

Our view. The buy side's implied numbers are above ours. We think 2028 EPS of $6.50–6.80 is more likely than $7.50–8.00 (section 6), which would leave the stock dependent on keeping a multiple near 40 times. The Q3'26 print on 29 October and, more importantly, the first 2027 guide in February 2027 are the tests.
