2Segments: mix and margin, last six quarters

Illumina reports one segment, so it does not publish a gross or operating margin for any part of the business. What it does disclose is revenue by product line, growth by customer type, the NovaSeq X's share of high-throughput volume and revenue, and the gross margin of "product" versus "service" revenue. We use those disclosures to build five economic segments. Where a margin is our estimate rather than a disclosure, the label says so and the method is shown.

How the segment margins are built

Gross margin. The 10-Qs disclose the cost of product revenue (consumables plus instruments) and of service revenue. That gives a disclosed product gross margin and service gross margin each quarter Der. To split product into consumables and instruments we assume instruments earn about a 35% gross margin, consistent with management repeatedly calling instruments "lower-margin" and with industry norms; consumables gross margin then falls out of the arithmetic at about 75–77% every quarter Der. Moving the instrument assumption between 25% and 45% moves the consumables estimate by only about ±1.5 points.

EBIT margin. No segment EBIT exists. We show an illustrative figure that charges every line the same share of R&D plus SG&A (44–47% of revenue in these quarters). It is a rough allocation that tells you which lines carry the profit, not a precise number Der.

2.1 Sequencing consumables (67% of revenue): the engine

What it is. The flow cells and reagent kits consumed in every run on every Illumina sequencer, plus Illumina's own library-prep and assay kits (TSO 500, 5-base, TruPath, single-cell, Protein Prep). This is recurring, high-margin revenue that grows with the installed base and with how much customers sequence.

What the table shows. Revenue has grown only 1–8% a year for six quarters even though gigabases sequenced grew more than 30%. The gap is price: the NovaSeq X sells a gigabase for roughly a third of what the NovaSeq 6000 did. Inside that low growth sits a sharp split. Clinical consumables outside China accelerated from +8% to +20% before easing to +15%, while research and applied fell in five of six quarters. Clinical is now about two-thirds of the line.

What it is. Genotyping chips and scanners (used by consumer-genetics firms, agriculture and research) and, since 30 January 2026, most of SomaLogic's proteomics revenue, which management books "mostly in microarrays".

The read across segments. About 85–90% of the company's profit comes from consumables. Instruments roughly break even or lose money after an allocation of overhead, and service makes a modest profit. That is why the forecast debate is entirely about consumables growth and price per gigabase, and why record instrument placements matter only for what they imply about 2027 consumables.
