8Consensus in depth: what the Bloomberg estimates say and imply

The Bloomberg consensus of 10 October 2026 sits exactly on management's 2026 guidance midpoints and then assumes a clean acceleration: sequencing consumables go from +5.6% in 2026 to about +9% in 2027–28, while instruments shrink and margins expand by about 150 basis points a year. Consumables supply about 90% of the forecast revenue growth. In other words, consensus is one bet: that revenue per gigabase stops falling as fast as it has.

What the annual consensus implies

  1. 2026 is the guidance midpoint, with no cushion. Revenue $4,625M against a $4.60–4.64B guide; EPS $5.36 against $5.30–5.40; margin 23.5% against 23.4–23.6%; China $211M against $210–220M. Consensus has followed guidance up: 2026 EPS was $5.07 in early February.
  2. Consumables are the whole forecast. Sequencing consumables add $274M in 2027 and $316M in 2028, which is 91% and 86% of total revenue growth. Instruments shrink and service grows about 4.5%.
  3. 2027 sits at the low edge of management's target. Reported growth of 6.3% becomes about 7.1% excluding China and roughly 6.6% adjusting for the extra week in 2026 and SomaLogic's January. That is the bottom of "high single digit", with no visible room for the 1–2 points of new products management promises (section 5.3).
  4. Margins sit below the target. The 2027 operating margin of 25.2% is below management's ~25.5–26%. The margin expansion comes from operating expenses growing about 3.5% a year against revenue growing 6–7%: the incremental operating margin (extra profit per extra dollar of revenue) is about 50%.
  5. EPS growth is operating leverage, not buybacks. The share count barely moves (153.1M to 152.6M). At $278 a share, $313M of 2027 buybacks retires only about 0.7% of the shares.
  6. Gross margin rises every year. From 68.5% to 69.5% by 2028 and about 70.3% by 2030. Consensus therefore assumes no net price erosion at the gross-margin line, which is where we disagree most (section 6, item 6).

The widest disagreement is 2028 EPS: $6.02 at Barclays against $7.41 at JPM, a 23% spread. JPM is the only broker modelling double-digit growth in 2028. Barclays is the only one with gross margin falling in 2027–28; its whole bear case is about price, not volume. Because consensus 2028 EPS ($7.01) is above three of the four brokers in the folder, the contributors we cannot see must be well above $7. JPM's estimate path shows how the bull case formed: between 1 May and 31 July it raised 2028 revenue by $577M (11%) and 2028 EPS by 14%, while 2026 revenue rose only 1%. Its target rose from $125 to $230 over the same period, and about $44 of its June increase came purely from a lower discount rate and a higher long-term growth rate in its cash-flow model.

8.5 The price-per-gigabase test

The single most useful check on consensus is to ask how fast revenue per gigabase can fall while still delivering consensus consumables growth. The table answers it for three rates of gigabase growth.

Arithmetic: (1 + revenue growth) ÷ (1 + Gb growth) − 1 Der.

Over the last year, revenue per gigabase fell about 19% (gigabases +30%, consumables revenue about +5%). So consensus needs one of two things: gigabase growth staying near 30% with price declines easing slightly, or gigabase growth slowing toward management's 25% with price declines easing a lot. J.P. Morgan's customer survey implies only a 2–5% annual price decline, but its respondents also expect volume growth of only 11–19%, far below the 30% Illumina reports. The survey cannot support both a high volume figure and a low price figure at the same time.

8.6 Caveats on the consensus data

  • The printouts have no analyst count, range or revision history, and no operating metrics (installed base, placements, pull-through, gigabases, clinical growth). Those come only from broker notes.
  • Bloomberg's 2025 regional actuals use the old regions while 2026 uses the new ones, so only Greater China is comparable across years.
  • The 2028 quarterly consensus sums to $7.34 of EPS against the annual $7.01, a different set of contributors. We use the annual figure.
  • Several balance-sheet lines are stale: the $500M note that matured on 9 September 2026 is still shown as outstanding, and the 2026 buyback figure ($420M) disagrees with the quarterly sheet.
  • The 2032 column (share count jumping to 165M, product revenue +20%) is a thin-contributor artefact and should be ignored.
