# Element Biosciences' H2 2026 launch (VITARI): everything in the corpus

*2026-10-31. Sources: the UBS Fast Take (19 Feb 2026); Bloomberg Intelligence, Barclays and JPM notes; Illumina calls and conferences from Feb 2025 to Oct 2026; 23 expert calls and channel checks (Jul–Oct 2026); and our Oct 2026 web scrapes (`2026-10-11_clinical_consumables_scrape/`, with E-numbers in its evidence ledger; `2026-10-12_thesis_scrape_findings.csv`, with F- and D-numbers).*

## Answer

**Launch status.** VITARI is Element's first high-throughput sequencer, a step up from its benchtop AVITI. Element unveiled it on 19 Feb 2026 with a "ships in H2 2026" target.
- As of late October, nothing in the corpus confirms a shipment, a named customer or a delay.
- The latest scrape evidence (to about 12 Oct) shows pre-orders only, with "significant interest from existing AVITI customers".
- Element was at ASHG (20–24 Oct) with VITARI on its booth, but made no announced launch news.
- Our web searches mostly stop around 9 Sep, so a first shipment could have been missed.

**Specs and pricing.** On paper, VITARI is a mid-to-high-throughput box at about half Illumina's cost per Gb. It offers about a fifth of a NovaSeq X Plus's output per run, for about a tenth of the consumables cost.

**The evidence base is thin.**
- Every VITARI claim in the corpus is a company claim relayed by UBS, or expert hearsay.
- No expert has run one.
- Illumina management has never named it.

## 1. What Element announced (company claims, via UBS and the press release)

The figures come from the UBS Fast Take (19 Feb 2026) [E069, E191]:

| Item | VITARI | NovaSeq X Plus |
|---|---|---|
| Instrument list | ~$689K, about 40% below the X Plus | ~$1.15M implied |
| Consumables per run | ~$3K | ~$32K |
| Output per run | ~3 Tb (~10B reads) | ~16 Tb |
| Cost per Gb | ~$1.00 | ~$2.00 |
| Marketing claim | "$100 genome" | — |
| Timing | Ships H2 2026, pre-orders open | — |

The $1.00 vs $2.00 per Gb is our arithmetic from UBS's run figures. UBS itself says only "~50% lesser."

The $689K is ambiguous in the garbled UBS text: "~40% discount to NovaX+ (list is $689k)." We read it as VITARI's price, because the experts recall about $700K. Core Notes 08 and 09 read it as the X Plus list price. UBS rated ILMN Neutral (PT $135) and called the launch "keeps the pressure on."

## 2. What experts say (all hearsay on VITARI; no one has run one)

- **Natera associate director (22 Jul 2026).**
  - Called VITARI "almost equal with NovaSeq X". He recalled "$700,000, compared to more than $1 million" and "50X on genome with somewhere over $70".
  - Good for women's health, transplant, infectious disease and population genomes, "minus molecular oncology". "You cannot run Signatera or ctDNA with 50X." (Signatera is Natera's MRD test, a blood test for leftover cancer.)
  - He calls his figures "recollections."
- **Former Illumina field application scientist, now at SOPHiA GENETICS (24 Jul).**
  - VITARI costs "half of the NovaSeq X" and can "fit the same number of samples" as an X Plus.
  - "Element is now moving customers from the X Plus to VITARI."
  - He named no customers, and SOPHiA partners with Element, so weight this cautiously. It conflicts with the scrape's "pre-orders only."
- **Abbott senior scientist (29 Jul).** The interviewer, not the expert, cited Element's "$100 genome… later this year." The expert guessed Element "has a good opportunity to capture a little more share."
- **Arizona core manager, first-hand AVITI user (22 Jul).** Element's "next advance will be its higher-output sequencer," bringing cost per base "into a little bit more of a competitive space."
- **Skeptics.**
  - An Oxford Nanopore senior director (Tegus, ~Oct 2026) said Ultima and Element "have not succeeded at scale."
  - A PacBio segment lead (3 Aug) said there was "a lot of hype, and now we're not seeing as much adoption."
  - A Roche-employed ex-Illumina scientist (5 Aug) said labs would "add one" challenger box "versus maybe six NovaSeq Xes."
  - An ex-Illumina market-development manager (Tegus) said sub-$100 genomes matter little in oncology, which needs 120X or more.

## 3. What Illumina management has said

Management has never named VITARI. It answers competitor questions generically.

- **Q4'25 call (5 Feb 2026).** Dhingra said "little competition was seen in Q4."
  - Asked about a "$100 genome" competitor, Thaysen said AGBT (the industry conference) is where competitors "put their best foot forward."
  - He said Illumina can "compete on all parameters" and customers are "more sophisticated than just looking at one element."
- **TD Cowen (3 Mar).** Thaysen saw "nothing new" from Ultima and Element and said "we fight every day on the streets."
  - He is not discussing future instruments "so we don't freeze the market."
  - The host said these boxes "had not had the traction some expected."
- **Q1'26 call (30 Apr).** Asked about "competition launching this summer," Thaysen said expectations are in the guide. He said "we are looking forward to compete when they finally get to the market."
  - He would not price the 35B flow cell yet. The "$150 genome" in that question was almost certainly Roche's AXELIOS, not Element.
- **Q2 and Q3'26 calls (30 Jul, 29 Oct).** No Element or VITARI mention. On Q3, Thaysen answered the "$50 genome" question with "we love elasticity."

## 4. Element's business today (scrape data, mostly verified)

- **Scale.**
  - AVITI installed base: more than 450 systems in 40+ countries at end-2025, up more than 60% (about 170 net adds) [F082, F098]. AVITI24 was more than 35% of new bookings [F099].
  - 2025 revenue: $58M or about $85M depending on source, against a $100M target [F082, F101]. That is roughly $100K or less of pull-through per box a year [D007].
- **Funding.** Series E upsized on 9 Jun 2026 with $175M from Samsung Electronics, earmarked for VITARI, AVITI Dx and tissue profiling [E200]. No IPO filing.
- **Hiring.** An inside-sales posting reads "Scale without the premium. Introducing VITARI" [E166]. A 12 Oct snapshot showed only 3 of 18 openings in sales, none VITARI-specific [F562].
- **Placements, all research or AVITI so far.**
  - Minnesota's genomics center (UMGC) runs 5 AVITI24s and outsources X Plus work [F200, F1068].
  - Iowa moved routine work to AVITI24 [F305], and MSU replaced a NovaSeq 6000 with an AVITI [F139].
  - NCI bought an AVITI24 for $315K (23 Sep 2026) [F925].
  - There are tenders in Canada, Taiwan and Europe.
  - The South Carolina core is buying an AVITI24 and "hopes" 80% of in-house work moves to it.
- **Clinical.**
  - None of the 9 big US clinical labs has verified non-Illumina production use [E353].
  - Medicover runs AVITI for NIPT (non-invasive prenatal testing) [E353], and PathoNext runs it for comprehensive cancer genomic profiling [E363].
  - AVITI Dx got ISO 13485 on 17 Jun 2026. A self-declared Class A IVDR launch (the EU diagnostics regulation) is planned for late 2026; none is confirmed [F561].
- **Field pricing.**
  - AVITI is still 1.7–3x the X 25B per Gb at four cores [F286]. The median AVITI high-output price is about $8.29/Gb across 7 cores.
  - Cores are cutting: Northwestern −19% in Oct 2026, UMGC cut AVITI per read about two-thirds over two years [F1063].
  - Element's reagent prices stay flat through a product's life (Arizona core, first-hand).
- **Share.**
  - In the ENA archive, AVITI had about 2.4% of NovaSeq 6000 run counts (Q2'26) [F088] and only 0.6% of runs of 10 Gb or more, flat year on year [F648].
  - Its federal obligations were flat at about $0.75M [F924].
  - Rival share gains in public data are mostly MGI, not Element.

## 5. Litigation

- **Patents.** Illumina sued Element over AVITI (D. Del., May 2025), and Element countersued, naming the NovaSeq X [E225, E223].
- **Antitrust.** Element's antitrust case (N.D. Cal. 25-cv-08026) was dismissed with leave to amend on 28 May 2026. The court found no "specific provisions that locked customers" and found the 50%+ bundled-discount allegation unsupported. The amended complaint added no discount detail [E120, E202].
- **Munich.** A Munich court (14 Jul 2026) found Illumina's legacy MiSeq infringes an Element patent [E201].
- **10x Genomics** sued Element over AVITI24 in May 2026 [F635].

## 6. What this means for Illumina

- **Where it bites.** VITARI's pitch (half the cost per Gb, about 40% cheaper capital, mid-throughput scale) targets the same NovaSeq 6000 users and smaller X sites that sit at the heart of the 6000-tail and pricing thesis.
- **The FAS's pattern.** Element "wins" against a 6000 site and "loses" against an X Plus site. That makes the remaining 6000 base the exposed group.
- **Limits.** Element has no NIPT, oncology or infectious-disease panels, and 50X genomes don't serve MRD or tumor profiling. So the near-term risk is research and routine germline work, not Illumina's core clinical growth drivers.
- **No impact visible yet.** There are no shipments and no named customers. Public and federal data show AVITI share flat. Management sees "little competition."
- **What to watch.**
  - A first-shipment or first-customer press release (Element often announces these at ASHG or year-end).
  - VITARI on core rate cards.
  - AVITI Dx IVDR self-declaration.
  - Whether Illumina's 35B flow cell pricing (expected closer to launch, 2027) undercuts VITARI's ~$1/Gb.

## Contradictions and caveats

- The $689K could be VITARI's price or the X Plus list (see section 1).
- VITARI's genome cost: "over $70" at 50X (Natera expert) vs the marketed "$100 genome."
- VITARI's instrument price: ~$700K (Natera) vs "half the X" (FAS).
- The FAS's "moving X Plus customers to VITARI" conflicts with pre-orders only and no named customers. The FAS also has partner bias.
- Barclays (6 Aug) and one expert call (5 Aug) mislabel Roche's AXELIOS as Element. Don't cite either on Element.
- Element's 2025 revenue ($58M vs ~$85M) and clinical-research mix (20% vs more than 25%) are unresolved.
