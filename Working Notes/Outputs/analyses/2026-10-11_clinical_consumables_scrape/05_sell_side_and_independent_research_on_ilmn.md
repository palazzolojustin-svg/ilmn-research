## Key findings

1. **Sell-side models do not treat the 6000 conversion as a separate revenue cliff.** None of the notes I read quantifies revenue at risk from the remaining NovaSeq 6000 volume. JPM says transition pricing headwinds are "largely gone exiting this year" [JPM upgrade, 2026-06-10]. Barclays treats price per Gb as a "warrants watching" item and gives management "the benefit of the doubt" [BARC-1Q, 2026-04-30]. My own arithmetic (below, OPINION) says the HT consumables revenue that is still exposed to conversion is substantial.

2. **My derived conversion math (OPINION; it uses the 83%/59% shares you supplied).** If the 17% of HT volume still on the 6000 earned the X's revenue per Gb, HT revenue would be about 29% lower at constant volume. That is 17 Gb × 0.71 = 12.1 revenue points, against 41 today. HT Gb would need to grow about 41% just to hold HT revenue flat. At the >30% y/y Gb growth management reports, that is roughly 1.1–1.3 years of volume growth spent on the conversion alone. This ignores the 6000s that will persist (see finding 7).

3. **The price gap per Gb between X and 6000 has widened.** From the revenue-share and volume-share series you supplied, X revenue per Gb divided by 6000 revenue per Gb was about 0.45 in Q2'24 and 0.36 in Q4'24. It was about 0.29–0.31 in Q3–Q4'25, 0.27 in Q1'26 and 0.30 in Q2'26. So X is about 70% cheaper per Gb than the 6000, in line with management's "easily more than 60% to 70%". That gap was about 55% two years ago. The Q2'26 uptick (0.27→0.30) argues against Barclays' Q1 worry that X pricing was sliding. It does not show whether the 6000 or the X moved (OPINION, derived).

4. **Barclays and BI argue price-for-volume; JPM and Ameriprise argue stickiness.**
   - Barclays: Q1 saw "lower price/Gb on NSX volumes", with X revenue share flat at 55% while Gb share rose [BARC-1Q, 2026-04-30]. It suspects discounting ahead of Roche and Ultima.
   - BI: Illumina was "in the early stages of trading price for volume" [BI timeline, 2026-05-01].
   - Management called the revenue-share-versus-Gb reading "a frivolous exercise" and pointed to flow-cell and panel mix.
   - Q2 did not repeat the stall. Revenue share rose 55%→59% while volume share rose 82%→83%.

5. **JPM's survey implies a mild price decline, well below management's own model (OPINION, derived).** The survey covered 62 customers (30 research, 32 clinical) and was run in May 2026. Clinical respondents planned consumables volume growth of +14.6% in 2027 and +19.1% in 2028, against spend growth of +9.4% and +14.0%. That implies only about 4–5% price decline per unit. Research respondents plan volume +11.4%/+17.3% against spend +9.0%/+12.8%, an implied price decline of 2–4%. Management's long-term model is mid-20s% volume growth and mid-teens% price decline per year. JPM itself says the survey gap is smaller than the "-DD%" pricing pressure Illumina has seen recently [JPM survey, 2026-06-10].

6. **Survey volume growth is far below reported Gb growth.** Reported Gb on connected instruments is up more than 30% y/y, and clinical is "well above" that [JPM 2Q, 2026-07-31]. Customers plan clinical volume of only +15–19% for 2027–28. If volume decelerates toward the high teens while price falls at management's mid-teens, net consumables growth is roughly flat to low single digits. The survey's rosier answer rests on self-reported price falls of only about 4%.

7. **Many clinical labs keep 6000s alongside Xs.** In the JPM survey, about 75% of clinical respondents still own a NovaSeq 6000 (about 58% own X). The summary page says 16% for the 6000, which conflicts with the chart text [JPM survey, 2026-06-10]. Illumina sold more than 10 new 6000s in Q2 [JPM 2Q; Freedom, 2026-08-05]. Management called this "a normal transition dynamic rather than an identifiable consumables headwind" [Ameriprise, 2026-07-31]. Up to half of clinical X placements are "incremental" [JPM 2Q, 2026-07-31]. That cuts both ways: it supports volume growth, but it means the 6000 base will not convert cleanly.

8. **The analysts' growth forecasts differ widely.** For 2027 revenue growth:

   | Source | 2027 revenue growth |
   |---|---|
   | JPM (Aug) | +9.1% (2028: +12.0%) |
   | Freedom (Aug) | about +5.7% |
   | Barclays (Jul) | +5.5% |
   | UBS (Feb) | +4.1% |
   | FactSet consensus (Jul 31) | about +5.8% ($4,871M vs $4,603M) |
   | Management | "HSD" |

   Consensus sits well below management's own target.

9. **Clinical growth is already slowing.** ROW clinical consumables grew 20% in Q4'25 and Q1'26 but only 15% in Q2'26. Management guides full-year "mid-teens." If the first half averages about 17.5%, the second half implies roughly 12–14% (OPINION, my arithmetic). JPM blames "pockets of softness" in the Middle East and Latin America and says the US grew above +20% [JPM 2Q, 2026-07-31]. Barclays' arithmetic is "60% of the business grows more than 20%" [BARC-2Q, 2026-07-31]; Q2 already runs below that.

10. **Cash conversion lags earnings.** Free cash flow missed the Street three quarters running [JPM notes]. Q4'25 was $267M vs $412M. Q1'26 was $251M vs $502M. Q2'26 was $162M vs $321M. The first half was flat y/y on more than $100M of extra revenue. Management cited tax timing and inventory builds [Ameriprise, 2026-07-31].

11. **Margin quality is questioned by one analyst.** Seeking Alpha's Ayers attributes about $26M of the $31M y/y Q2 operating income gain to government incentives (+$18M, $22M vs $4M) and the absence of restructuring charges (+$8M). He sees only about $5M of underlying improvement [SA, 2026-09-08]. Adjusted gross margin fell 120 bps y/y to 68.2% despite a better consumables mix [JPM 2Q].

12. **Valuation has outrun every target in the corpus.** Barclays Underweight is at $160 against a $205 stock. JPM's Overweight target is $230. The FactSet consensus target is $175.72. Seeking Alpha's printout shows $278.17 on 2026-10-10 (a 27% gain from its $218.65 publication price). Brokers also moved fast: JPM went Neutral→Overweight, with its target at $120 in Feb, $185 in Jun and $230 in Jul. Freedom downgraded to Hold on valuation at $185 [Freedom, 2026-08-05].

## 1. Price versus volume: what each side says

**Bear and cautious side**
- **Barclays, Q1 (2026-04-30).**
  - FACT (as reported by Barclays): X share of HT consumables revenue flat q/q at 55% while its Gb share rose.
  - Barclays says the X Gb share rose "500 bps to 76%". That conflicts with the 82% Q1 figure in Freedom (2026-05-06) and Ameriprise (2026-05-01). 76% is the clinical figure; Barclays appears to have mislabeled it.
  - OPINION: Barclays concedes it has "imperfect data" and that this is one quarter.
- **Barclays, Q2 (2026-07-31).**
  - Competitors will force "continued price concessions, bundling, and favorable terms".
  - The 35B flow cell would need to be priced to compete, though "inherent technological barriers" could block that.
  - Barclays calls Illumina "a MSD business", and 35x P/E "the most expensive in core Tools".
  - OPINION: Axelios (as extracted, attributed to Element) is competitive in the near term "in counting applications".
- **Barclays, Feb (2026-02-05).** OPINION: "another round of pricing headwinds in 2027 and beyond"; research is more at risk than clinical.
- **BI.**
  - Q3'25 (2025-10-31): consumables looked to be at an "inflection point" where X volume offsets pricing.
  - Q1'26 (2026-05-01): "early stages of trading price for volume."
  - Q3'24 (2024-11-05): "healthy amount of skepticism" about elasticity of demand.
  - Q2'26 (2026-07-31): the "60 placements per quarter" goal is "increasingly less meaningful."
- **UBS (2026-02-26).**
  - Counting applications (RNAseq and similar) are about 40% of research usage and about 16% of total sequencing consumables sales (FACT as UBS estimates).
  - Roche quotes $0.06 per million reads against Illumina's $0.68 list on the 25B flow cell (the extract is garbled; read as 0.06 and 0.68). That is roughly 11 times cheaper.
  - UBS calls this where Illumina's pricing is "more vulnerable."
  - Roche's own genome price of $150 is benign for large accounts that are likely at similar or better pricing already.
- **UBS expert call (2026-02-26).**
  - An expert said they pay about $175 per genome after Illumina discounts, and larger users are at about $125.
  - The expert expects the 35B flow cell to bring Illumina to about $150.
  - JPM lists Illumina at about $200 per genome on the X, against Roche's $150 [JPM alert, 2026-02-26].
  - The distance between list and effective price suggests discounts are already large.
- **UBS (2026-02-19).** Element's announced system is about 40% below the X Plus ($689k list) on instrument price. It has about one-tenth the consumables cost per run (about $3k vs $32k) and about 50% lower $/Gb. Output is about one-fifth. The extract is partly garbled; ship timing reads "2H 2026."

**Bull and constructive side**
- **JPM (2026-06-10 and 2026-07-31).**
  - Transition pricing headwinds are "largely gone exiting this year."
  - Customers face "meaningful switching friction."
  - Consumables volume is at least +30%.
  - JPM models ROW clinical consumables at a +low/mid-teens CAGR through 2030, giving a total sequencing consumables CAGR of +LDD.
  - It also sees sequencing intensity rising: WGS needs about 15x the data of WES, and single-cell and spatial need 3–15x and 10–20x more than WGS.
- **Freedom (2026-02-10, 2026-05-06, 2026-08-05).** "Completion of the NovaSeq X transition... should help mitigate the impact of pricing erosion." Its consumables CAGR is 5.5% (2025–28). Freedom downgraded on valuation, not fundamentals.
- **Ameriprise (2026-07-31).** Buy rating, no price target. The case "increasingly depends on" whether placements convert to pull-through. Systems take at least six to nine months to normalize.
- **BI (2026-07-31).**
  - Clinical was 65% of sequencing consumables, up 10 points in two years, and adds "ballast."
  - HSD in 2027 "appears increasingly visible."
  - The weak point is the second-half cadence: Q3 guidance is "a touch softer than consensus."

  - Adjusted EPS growth in the mid/high teens.
  - Opex grows MSD/HSD, with gross margin in the high 60s.

## 5. Competition read-through

- **Axelios (Roche), JPM survey.** 40% of respondents were unfamiliar. Purchase intent is 6.2/10 for research and 4.7/10 for clinical.
  - Timing among considerers: 8% within 6–12 months, 35% within 12–24 months, about 32% in 24+ months.
  - 65% of would-be buyers expect to cut Illumina usage; only about 11% would decommission an Illumina instrument.
  - Data quality is the top factor both for and against.
  - Reagent rental would make 39% of academic and 37% of clinical respondents less likely to buy Axelios.
  - Share expectations (three years out): mid-size clinical labs go from 50% to 57% Illumina; large clinical labs stay at 54%; small clinical labs stay at 63%.
- **UBS experts (2026-02-26).**
  - Positive: yields of 40B reads and 16 genomes per chip, and no carryover across more than 20 reuses.
  - Negative: complicated library prep (duplex needs two adapters, which Roche will not share), different software, and shipping "nowhere near ready" (six to eight months).
  - Clinical reuse: 50% of one expert's clinical users were fine with it if validated; the other 50% (pathologists) said "a nonstarter."
  - The expert had expected Roche at $100 per genome.
- **Pricing.** JPM and BI both cite $150 per genome for Axelios against about $200 for the X, though BI's table lists the X at "$200 or less". Roche's price assumes 20 sensor reuses and excludes library prep (JPM alert).
- **BI (2026-06-26).** Disruption is "unlikely to be immediate, but Roche could still chip away where workflow or economic advantages are clear."
- **Customer concentration and bargaining power.** None of the notes quantifies top-customer share. JPM's argument that "all of the major CLIA labs" are on or moving to the X cuts two ways: it supports stickiness, but it also means a handful of large clinical buyers hold the leverage on price. The Q1 pattern of multi-unit orders fits this (OPINION).

## 6. Management behavior flagged in research

- **Preannounced range beaten.** Illumina preannounced Q4'25 adjusted EPS of $1.27–1.30 on 13 Jan 2026. It reported $1.35 on 5 Feb with the same $1.159B revenue [JPM, 2026-01-13 and 2026-02-06]. The extracts do not explain the extra $0.05–0.08.
- **Consistent beat pattern.** Reported against guidance or Street: Q4'25 EPS $1.35 vs $1.21 Street; Q1 $1.15 vs $1.05; Q2 $1.31 vs $1.23. The Q1 guide of $1.02–1.07 sat well below Barclays and consensus at $1.14. Barclays says Illumina "keeps up the trend" of a soft Q1 guide and a back-half-weighted ramp.
- **Guidance construction.** The initial 2026 guide took the average of 2H25 and used the Q3'25 growth rate as the low end [JPM, 2026-02-06]. By construction it should be conservative. Freedom now says "we no longer consider management's outlook conservative."
- **Disclosure gaps.**
  - Multiomics 1–2 point contribution unsized (Barclays, SA).
  - Barclays could only observe X share via a revenue-share metric that management dismissed as "frivolous."
  - Management gave a consistent 76%→78% clinical Gb conversion but, in the research, no clinical revenue share.
  - The "clinical" definition was called "very loose" by an ex-employee per your brief; no analyst in this set challenges it.
- **Buybacks.**
  - Q1: 2.0M shares for $242M at $120.85.
  - Q2: 0.94M shares for $122M at $129.07, with just 32K shares bought in May 25–Jun 28 at $145.22.
  - New $1.5B authorization (Apr 28): none used as of Q2.
  - Seeking Alpha links the pause to the $500M Sept 9 note maturity and $1.17B cash; a $300M 2029 note was issued in August. That inference is the author's, and the company has not said so.
- **Restated comparatives.** Barclays shows 2025 free cash flow as $654M in Feb and Apr, and $931M in Aug [BARC notes]. JPM's FY25 operating cash flow reads $1,129M in Feb and $1,079M in Jun; its Feb 2026 FY25 FCFF is $984M against $931M later. These are modeling inconsistencies, not company restatements.
- **Insider selling and compensation metrics.** Nothing in the research notes covers either topic.
- **Sell-side conflicts.** JPM discloses it owns 1% or more of Illumina, is an investment-banking client with compensation, and co-managed an offering in the past 12 months. This coincided with its upgrade (Jun 10) and a doubling of its target within two months. Barclays and UBS are market makers; UBS holds 0.5% or more.

## Contradictions and stale data

- **Gb share, Q1.** Barclays 76% overall vs Freedom/Ameriprise 82%; 76% is the clinical number.
- **NovaSeq X price.** UBS gives $689k list for the X Plus; BI gives $985k–$1.2M; Seeking Alpha says roughly $1M. Roche's $750k is described as 25% below the X's list price by SA; these cannot all be right.
- **Axelios attribution.** Barclays Aug (as extracted) names Element "Axelios"; Axelios is Roche's product. Probably an extraction or note error.
- **Q2 gross margin.** Freedom says "materially exceeding" its expectation; JPM and Barclays say in line with the Street at 68.2%.
- **Q1 service revenue.** BI shows $174M, down 20%. JPM shows sequencing service at $151M (+4% organic). The scopes differ (microarray included in BI's figure), but BI's "down 20%" conflicts with Freedom's +8.1% (the BI figure may be a typo).
- **Consumables totals.** Freedom: $852M in Q2. JPM and Ameriprise: sequencing consumables $775M, and $852M includes $77M of microarray consumables.
- **Revenue guidance.** Ameriprise and JPM show FY26 ROW organic ">5%"; BI shows "about 5%" and "above 5%" in different sections.
- **Q4'25 gross margin.** Reported 67.0%, vs Barclays' 68.9% estimate; tariffs cost 205 bps (JPM).
- **JPM FY26 adjusted EBITDA/EBIT.** $877M/$754M (Feb) vs $1,029M/$825M (Jun) on nearly the same revenue. Likely a definition change, not stated.
- **Buyback totals for 2025.** BI $740M; UBS $656M.
- **Stale.** All UBS notes are from February–early March 2026, before the Q1 and Q2 prints. The Bloomberg Intelligence 2025 sections inside the Jan 2026 and Feb 2026 BI files date from Oct–Nov 2025. The Seeking Alpha price ($278.17) is a print-date figure, not an analysis date.
