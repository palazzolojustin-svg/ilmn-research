## Quantified view (OPINION, my inference)

- **Research is small and shrinking.** Q2 research and applied consumables of $105M compare with $775M of sequencing consumables, about 14%. This is my arithmetic, assuming the two figures are on the same basis, which the sources do not confirm. Against the 7% decline, the drag is about 1 point on sequencing consumables growth, or about $7M a quarter.
- **Research is not the swing factor for transition risk.** About 92% of research volume is already on the X, so there is little 6000 revenue left there to convert. The remaining 6000 revenue at risk is mostly clinical and pharma.
- **Research and applied is a revenue drag, not a pricing-offset risk.** Its decline hides some clinical growth, but at about 14% of sequencing consumables it cannot offset it.
- **Watch FY27 NIH new-award counts.** Fewer new awards in FY26 would feed through to lab purchasing in 2027. The continuing resolution to 11 Dec means a budget fight in Q4 2026.
