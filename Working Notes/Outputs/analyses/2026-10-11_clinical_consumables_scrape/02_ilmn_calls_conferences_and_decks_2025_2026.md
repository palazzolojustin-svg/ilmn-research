1. **Jefferies' "over-earned" question was not answered (Q2'26).** Tycho Peterson asked whether consumables are being "over-earned" while customers run 6000s and Xs in parallel, with a headwind when the 6000s are decommissioned. He said customers will eventually move, that HiSeq systems were in the portfolio "only a few quarters ago," and that he does not see "a true headwind." He gave no 6000 revenue, no decommission pace and no sizing. Dhingra did not answer. FACT/MGMT [Q2'26 call, 2026-07-30]. Peterson's question is paraphrased in the extract, so check the Source for exact wording.
2. **Management's position on the remaining 6000 clinical volume changed over four quarters.**
   - Q2'25 (Thaysen): "no cliff." Some customers stay on the 6000 "until an assay's end of life (e.g. ~2 more years)."
   - Q1'26 (Thaysen): "laggards" with regulated or FDA-approved assays will stay longer. The business "should have fundamentally moved" by end-2026, "leaving a long tail."
   - Q2'26: the long tail is "a standard part of the business."
   - Illumina also sold more than 10 NovaSeq 6000s in Q2'26, after about 30 in FY25 [Q2'25 call; Q1'26 call; Q2'26 call; JPM slides 2026-01-13].
3. **X share of HT, from the Q2'26 call (volume, revenue):** 83% of volume and 59% of revenue. Clinical 78% of volume (Q1: 76%; Q4'25: "more than two-thirds"; Q3'25: ~64%; Q2'25: ~55%). Research 92% [Q2'26 call; Q1'26 call; Q4'25 call; Q3'25 call; Q2'25 call]. FACT.
   - **The clinical conversion curve is flattening:** 64% → ~67%+ → 76% → 78% across Q3'25 to Q2'26, and Q4'25 to Q2'26 added only ~11 points in two quarters.
   - **Remaining 6000 volume is overwhelmingly clinical (calc):** Backing out the clinical share of HT gigabytes from the Q2'26 numbers (0.83 = 0.78c + 0.92(1−c)) gives clinical ≈ 64% of HT Gb (≈ 55–65% across quarters, because of rounding). So ~80%+ of the remaining 6000 Gb is clinical. This matches the "not over-earned in research" logic: research is done, and the exposure is a clinical one.
4. **The clinical X-conversion target drifted.**
   - Q4'25: conversion "substantially complete by end of 2026."
   - Q1'26 (Dhingra): "over 80% to 85%."
   - Q1'26 (Thaysen, to Canaccord): "over 85%."
   - Q2'26: 80–85%.
   - Even the best case leaves 15–20% of clinical volume on 6000 at end-2026.
5. **Illustrative revenue at risk (calc, OPINION).**
   - FY25 6000 revenue: ~1,360 base × ~$670K ≈ $0.9B, against X ~890 × $1.35M ≈ $1.2B. The 6000 share of that sum (~43%) is close to the reported ~41% of HT revenue, so the pull-through disclosures are internally consistent.
   - The FY26 6000 guide is $600–650K, i.e. 3–10% lower per unit, with ~330 decommissions in FY25 [Q4'25 deck, 2026-02-05].
   - On management's own "60–70%+" price-per-Gb cut, 1 Gb moving from 6000 to X is revenue-neutral only if volume rises ~2.5–3.3x. The disclosed cohort (below) shows year-1 growth of roughly flat to low-single-digit. The remaining ~$0.8–0.9B 6000 revenue therefore converts at roughly neutral revenue only if each converting customer's Gb jumps ~3x, otherwise it is a revenue loss.
   - My arithmetic on the "equilibrium" claim is in item 6.
6. **Bernstein math error (May 2025).** Thaysen said the X cut the price point "by two-thirds" and that at that price cut "volume has to rise 100% … just to make up the revenue." A two-thirds cut needs volume to rise ~200% (3x) for neutral revenue. 100% would cover a one-half cut. FACT (quote) plus calc [Bernstein 2025-05-28]. It may be a slip, but it understates the volume hurdle.
7. **The "40 converted clinical customers" cohort is thin and was dropped.**
   - Introduced on the Q3'25 call (slide 9, "Transitioned Clinical Accounts Deliver Robust Revenue & Volume Growth"). Thaysen said at JPM (Jan 2026) that Illumina "does not intend to keep reporting individual cohorts."
   - Details are in the cohort section below.
8. **Transition metrics were "phased out," then partly kept.**
   - The Q4'25 deck says: "With transition milestones achieved, we will phase out transition-specific metrics after Q4 earnings" [Q4'25 deck, slide 10].
   - Yet the Q1'26 and Q2'26 calls and decks still gave the X volume and revenue shares (Q2'26 deck slide 8, with chart text garbled in extraction).
   - When asked why X revenue share stalled at 55% (Q4→Q1), Thaysen said it is "a little bit dangerous to read this figure precisely" [Q1'26 call]. It then jumped to 59% in Q2.
9. **Clinical consumables is guided "mid-teens" with H1 at ~17%.** Dhingra: H1 clinical was "about 17%" and Q2 was 15% (Q1: 20%). FY "mid-teens" implies H2 ~13–14% (calc). RoW organic guide is "~4.5%" for Q3 versus 8.1% in Q2, and Q4 is guided higher [Q2'26 call]. Management repeatedly said consumables are "steady" and that variability sits in instruments.
10. **Volume versus price evidence.**
    - Gb on connected HT/MT instruments grew ">30%" each quarter, with clinical "well above" that. Total sequencing consumables grew +5% and clinical +15% in Q2'26.
    - Implied price/mix drag is therefore large: roughly −20% to −25% on total consumables per Gb, and a smaller but still material drag on clinical (calc).
    - The CEO's long-run model is volume "mid-20s," price "mid-teens," giving HSD revenue growth [Bernstein 2025-05-28; JPM 2026-01-13].
11. **Placements are skewing to capacity, not conversion.**
    - Q1'26 (Thaysen): clinical is ">60% of placements and is mostly incremental placements rather than conversions of 6000s." Q2'26: mix about 70% clinical, 30% research.
    - Multi-unit orders are rising. Customers ordering 5, 10 or more get a volume discount (Thaysen: "reasonable"). Some units go on reagent-rental/lease (Dhingra, Q1'26), though only ~5 of 100+ in Q4'25.
    - Mgmt says clinical customers need "at least 6 to 9 months" to reach normal pull-through [Q2'26 call].
12. **35B flow cell pricing was withheld.** Stifel (Q1'26) asked for the 35B cost per Gb versus Roche's $150 genome. Thaysen: "we are not ready at this point to go out and talk about pricing"; it "will come closer to launch." He also said "aggressive pricing brings aggressive volume growth" in exome-heavy customer conversations [Q1'26 call]. On 2026-03-03 he repeated that Illumina will not price before launch [TD Cowen 2026-03-03].
13. **Memory/freight costs are being partly passed on.** Q2'26 (Thaysen): "we're sharing some of the increase with customers." Costs ran above plan: "we didn't anticipate to the level we have seen." Q1 had said mitigation would "fully offset" inflation during the year. Non-GAAP GM was 68.2% vs 69.4% a year earlier [Q2'26 call; Q1'26 call; Q2'26 deck].

## 3. The 40-customer cohort slide

- **Where it is:** Q3'25 deck slide 9, titled "Transitioned Clinical Accounts Deliver Robust Revenue & Volume Growth," subtitle "Fully Transitioned NovaSeq X Clinical Cohort." Two charts, "Consumables Revenue" and Gb volume, with x-axis 2023 / 2024 / 2025E (2025E is annualized year-to-date) [Q3'25 deck; Source file].
- **Labels (all that is shown):** "+DD", "+DD", "+LSD", "+DD" (DD = double digit, LSD = low single digit). There are no axis values and no dollars or Gb. Which label belongs to which year and chart is garbled in extraction.
- **Footnote:** "Analysis based on clinical customers (n=40) that have fully or largely transitioned to NovaSeq X, including a subset of top-tier accounts. 1. Customers that have transitioned all or vast majority of volume to NovaSeq X."
- **The call vs the footnote:** Dhingra said "fully transitioned" on the call; the footnote says "fully or largely."
- **Management's reading:** "volume offset price in year 1 and then revenue and volume both accelerated in year 2" (Dhingra, Q3'25 call). Schenkel read the chart as year-1 growth probably <5%, which Thaysen accepted as the takeaway [Wolfe 2025-11-19]. Thaysen: year one may be "a little bit flattish" [JPM 2026-01-13]. Year-1 volume and price are "almost in an equilibrium."
- **What it excludes** (Thaysen confirmed to Schenkel): brand-new customers to HT sequencing; it covers only former 6000 users.
- **Selection issue (OPINION):** Early converters, "including top-tier accounts," are the customers most motivated to expand assays. It is a survivor sample. Laggard cohorts (validated, FDA-approved, price-motivated, low-volume) are by construction absent, and Illumina has stopped updating it.
- **Cohort taxonomy** (Thaysen with Schenkel, Wolfe Nov 2025, `7th_Annual_Wolfe_Research_Healthcare_Conference.md`):
  1. Never-convert, low volume (smaller by volume).
  2. Old assays stay on the 6000, new larger assays go on the X.
  3. Exome → genome movers.
  4. Price-motivated movers (e.g. NIPT; "less than 20%", mostly already moved).
  - Cohorts 2 and 3 are "absolutely the largest of our clinical segments" and go to the 25B flow cell. Cohorts 1 and 4 likely stay on the 10B flow cell, "more like high-volume 6000 pricing."
  - Wolfe, Nov 2025: clinical is "less than 50%" of revenue on the X while total HT revenue was ~50%.

## 4. Parallel 6000 + X running

- **MGMT, repeated:** customers "keep the 6000 for validated, traditional assays" and build new assays on the X, so 6000 pull-through "stays up." Others shifted fully. "There will be fewer 6000 customers over time" [Q4'25 call, to Guggenheim, who said the 6000 pull-through was "holding up better than expected"].
- **Jan 2026:** the largest clinical cohort "either transitions fully to the X or layers incremental volume onto X alongside existing NovaSeq 6000 workflows" [JPM 2026-01-13]. This is the parallel-running behavior Jefferies asked about. It means 6000 revenue can persist while X volume is additive, so 6000 Gb will not fall only because customers "convert."
- **Counter-evidence (calc):** the 6000 FY26 pull-through is guided down 3–10%, and ~330 decommissions in FY25 against ~30 shipments show the base shrinking. Management still sold >10 6000s in Q2'26 because "some customers plan to stay on it for years."
- **Disclosure gap:** no breakdown of 6000 revenue by clinical vs research, no "fully on 6000," "mixed" or "converted" customer counts, and no decommission guide for FY26 in the extracts.

## 5. Pricing commentary

- **Price cut in transition:** "two-thirds" per Thaysen (Bernstein May 2025). The brief cites "easily more than 60% to 70%"; I did not find that exact phrase in my files (it may be elsewhere in the corpus).
- **Pricing actions (Q1'25):** Illumina raised prices on new orders to mitigate tariffs (~1 point of growth; "pricing applies only to new orders") [Q1'25 call]. Thaysen: "Nobody likes a price increase." Tariff cost was ~$85M gross in 2025 [Q1'25 call]. Q2'26 shows price actions in the other direction for customers: memory/freight cost-sharing.
- **Willingness to cut:** "in most circumstances, Illumina have the best cost structure," so it "can compete on price if it chooses" and "discusses differentiated pricing to open new applications" (Thaysen, Q2'25 call). Also: "If there are segments with significant elasticity where a different price point makes sense, 'we are all for it'" (Q4'25 call, to Nephron). That is explicit optionality on application-specific discounts.
- **Roche defense:** Counting applications (single cell, bulk RNA, protein) are "probably less than USD 100 million" of Illumina's space, and halving sequencing cost would give "maybe 20% more" experiments because library prep dominates the cost [TD Cowen 2026-03-03]. Barnard at AGBT 2025: sequencing is 10–20% of cost in counting applications. This is a management claim and excludes whole-genome and exome clinical work.
- **TruPath:** launched at $395 per genome, 16-plex now, "approximately likely around 96" over the next 18 months [TD Cowen 2026-03-03]. FACT/MGMT. Compare Roche's $150 genome list.
- **Reagent rental and volume discounts:** Reagent-rental/price-per-test is "often right" in the clinic and price "ties 100% to the reimbursement level" (Thaysen, AGBT 2025-02-25). Q1'26: some of ~83 units placed under reagent-rental or lease models "which have different revenue recognition" (Dhingra). Volume discounts for 5/10+ units. Q1'26 instrument revenue of $118M looked light against 80+ placements (BofA).
- **Customer-value framing:** management says customers now focus on "total cost of workflow," not $/Gb (Q4'25 call; JPM 2026). Claimed: ">30%" total sample-to-answer savings in "a few real-world examples" (MGMT, unverified) [JPM 2026-01-13].
