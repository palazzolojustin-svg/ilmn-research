**Assignment:** Research recent (2025 to Q3 2026) disclosures of Tempus AI, Caris Life Sciences, GeneDx, Myriad Genetics, NeoGenomics, Veracyte, Adaptive Biotechnologies (clonoSEQ), Labcorp and Quest genomics, Personalis and Foundation Medicine (via Roche): test volumes and growth, exome->genome shifts (GeneDx), MRD launches, sequencing COGS commentary, platform/supplier statements (Illumina dependence, NovaSeq X migrations and the cost savings they cite, e.g. Adaptive's NovaSeq X transition), validation of Ultima/Element/Roche, and any supply agreements. Summarize the implied 2026-2027 clinical sequencing volume growth across the group, and which customers have the most incentive and ability to switch.

## Key findings

1. **Caris is the clearest switching candidate.** FACT/MGMT: On its Q2 2026 call, Caris president David Spetzler said "There are two competitors out there now against the longstanding sequencing supplier, and their throughput and their cost is significantly lower Higher and lower, respectively, than what's available." He said this lets Caris "increase our capacity and decrease our cost of goods simultaneously." The "longstanding supplier" is Illumina (inferred; he didn't name it). [Caris Q2 2026 call, via earningswhispers.com/transcript/CAI/Q22026, fetched 2026-10-11]
2. **Caris's sequencing intensity is high, and it says it prefers depth over cost.** MGMT: "It's always deeper, not cheaper for us." It runs 114K+ whole-exome/whole-transcriptome (WES/WTS) tests per quarter, out of 345K+ oncology tests. Blood assays double the sequencing for buffy-coat subtraction. Management guides to "high 60s" gross margin and says "we're not trying to push gross margin as much as we could." [same source] Cost savings from a cheaper platform could therefore be spent on depth rather than banked. That favors Gb volume, but at a lower price per Gb.
3. **Caris's scale and size estimates (OPINION/unverified).** A search-result snippet says Caris runs about 50 Illumina NovaSeqs, "the largest installed base of any single customer." It also says analyst Jack Meehan estimated about $2,000 of sequencing per test, against about $3,500 for the MCED (multi-cancer early detection) test. It also cites a roughly 75% potential cost cut with Roche (COGS from $2,000 to $500). I could not open the primary source for this. Treat it as unverified. [search snippet, 2026-10-11; original article not retrieved]
4. **Adaptive is the best-documented NovaSeq X cost saver.** FACT: Q2 2026 MRD sequencing gross margin was 72%, against 64% a year earlier. MGMT (CEO Chad Robins): this was "helped by the NovaSeq X transition." The CFO attributed it to "lower assay costs following our NovaSeq X transition, along with continued operating leverage." The company targets above 70% for the full year. clonoSEQ volume was 36,111 tests, up 43% year on year. Clinical revenue rose 53%, with average selling price (ASP) up 7% to $1,382. [Adaptive Q2 2026 release (biospace.com/barchart.com) and call summary (vectorshift.ai), Aug 2026]
5. **The Adaptive case shows the X lowers customers' cost per test.** Volume is growing at +43%, so these customers keep buying consumables, but at a lower cost per test. The 8-point margin gain came from the X transition plus operating leverage, and the split was not disclosed. It is direct evidence that the X's lower $/Gb is being passed to the lab's COGS, which is the mechanism behind the revenue-at-risk question. Adaptive's clinical volume guide is 38-40% for FY26, up from 35%, against 43% in H1. [vectorshift.ai Q2 2026 summary]
6. **GeneDx volume is strong and its genome cost is falling.** FACT: Q2 2026 exome/genome volume was 30,785 (+32% y/y). Q3 guidance is 33,200. FY26 guide is at least 30% volume growth and at least 20% revenue growth. Revenue growth (+17% exome/genome) lagged volume growth (+32%), a gap the company has attributed to mix and pricing. MGMT: genome costs "nearly double" exome costs, mostly reagents. Cost per test is expected to be "relatively flat for the second half of 2026 with step improvements in 2027." [GeneDx Q2 2026 release (sec.gov 8-K, 2026-08-03) and call transcript (webull.com)]
7. **GeneDx's 2027 "step improvements" may be a platform price cut.** OPINION: Management said improvements come as "manufacturers advance their technology," which fits the 35B flow cell (about 20% lower $/Gb, expected around January 2027). The transcript doesn't name a platform. Reagent cost is the stated driver, so this is Illumina consumables price pass-through. [webull transcript]
8. **Tempus MRD volume is growing but near break-even.** FACT: MRD volume was about 9,000 tests in Q2 2026, up 38% sequentially from 6,500. Oncology volume was +31% y/y (up from +28% in Q1). Revenue was $382.5M (+22%). FY26 guide is $1.595-1.605B. MGMT: MRD economics are near break-even, so it will expand sales coverage gradually. [Tempus Q2 2026 release (businesswire, 2026-07-30); 360dx/GenomeWeb summaries]
9. **Tempus is buying Personalis for about $1.5B EV.** FACT: $16.25 per share, announced 2026-07-20, mostly stock, with up to 50% cash. Close is expected late 2026 or early 2027. Personalis's NeXT Personal is a whole-genome-based MRD test. This puts a WGS-based MRD test, which uses far more Gb per test, inside a large Illumina customer. It is a volume tailwind for Gb, with a cost-pressure incentive at break-even. [hitconsultant.net 2026-07-20; Personalis 8-K on sec.gov]
10. **Labcorp, Quest and Myriad all have Ultima relationships.** FACT: Labcorp expanded its collaboration with Ultima (UG 100 and ppmSeq) for WGS MRD in early-stage solid tumors. Quest struck its Ultima deal in January (the year isn't clear in the snippet) for Haystack MRD, and Haystack received New York State approval in Q2 2026. Myriad bought a UG 100 for its South San Francisco "Lab of the Future." These are exploratory and development deals. I found no disclosure of volume shifted off Illumina. [GenomeWeb/medtechdive/BioSpace snippets, dates 2024-2026, item dates unverified]
11. **Myriad is a volume headwind, not a tailwind.** FACT: Q2 2026 revenue was $190.7M (-11%), volume -1%, and revenue per test -9%. Prenatal volume fell 9%. FY26 revenue guide was cut to $770-790M (a $90M cut). Gross margin was 66.6% (-460 bps). Precise MRD expanded to colorectal and renal, with breast submitted to MolDX. [Myriad Q2 2026 release, sec.gov 8-K; 360dx]
12. **Roche's Foundation Medicine is already testing SBX prototypes.** FACT: Foundation is "using SBX prototypes within its genomic profiling laboratories." AXELIOS 1 launched RUO on 2026-06-29, with a clinical version later. [Roche, 2026-06-29; GenomeWeb snippet] Foundation is a captive, high-volume CGP user. If it moves, that is a large Illumina clinical volume loss with no commercial friction.
13. **Element's VITARI is coming.** FACT: $689K, a $100 genome, shipping H2 2026, with "early customer commitments" (none named). [Element release, 2026-02, biospace/businesswire]

## Group volume picture (2026-2027 implied)

The table below uses Q2 2026 reported volume growth and 2026 guidance. It is volume growth in tests, not in Gb. Gb per test varies widely.

| Company | Q2'26 volume | FY26 guide / direction | Gb intensity trend |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adaptive (clonoSEQ) | +43% (36,111) | 38-40% clinical volume | Stable. Margin gain from X |
| GeneDx (exome/genome) | +32% (30,785) | At least 30%; Q3 = 33,200 | Rising (genome mix, about 2x exome cost) |
| Tempus (oncology) | +31%; MRD 9,000 (+38% q/q) | Revenue +25% | Rising (xM/WGS MRD, Personalis) |
| Caris (clinical cases) | +18% (59,200) | Revenue +27-28% to $1.03-1.04B | High (WES+WTS, doubled for blood) |
| Veracyte | Decipher +17%, Afirma +10% | Revenue $590-596M | Afirma V2 transcriptome workflow; WGS TrueMRD (Medicare coverage obtained) |
| NeoGenomics | NGS revenue +26% | Raised FY guide | Not disclosed |
| Myriad | -1% | Cut to $770-790M | Falling in prenatal |

Sources: each company's Q2 2026 release (sec.gov / biospace / genomeweb, Jul-Aug 2026).

**OPINION (my inference):** The growers (Adaptive, GeneDx, Tempus, Caris, Veracyte, NeoGenomics) sit at roughly +18% to +43% test growth. Myriad is the exception. A simple weighted view points to mid-20s to low-30s % test growth for this group. That is consistent with the company's >30% Gb growth, and with its mid-20s volume and mid-teens price decline model. Gb growth should run a few points above test growth because of mix toward genomes, WGS MRD and deeper panels. The value at risk is the price per Gb: GeneDx and Adaptive both show falling cost per test while volume grows. Revenue per customer therefore grows well below volume (Caris is the possible exception, if it keeps going "deeper").

## Customer-by-customer (switch incentive vs. ability)

- **Caris:** Incentive is very high (about 50 NovaSeqs, COGS about 60% of price on MCED per the unverified analyst estimate, margin target in the high 60s). Ability is moderate: it is a large, sophisticated lab running clinical (CLIA/CAP) validated assays, and it is publicly evaluating alternatives. A switch means re-validating WES/WTS assays. [Caris Q2 call]
- **Foundation (Roche):** Incentive is the highest of the group because it is captive, and it is already running SBX prototypes. Ability is high. Switching is a matter of timing. [Roche 2026-06-29]
- **Labcorp, Quest, Myriad:** Incentive is moderate to high, and they have already purchased or contracted Ultima. They are development-stage for MRD, and I found no evidence of production-volume moves yet.
- **Tempus:** Incentive is high given break-even MRD and WGS MRD via Personalis. Ability is high given scale. No platform statements were found.
- **Adaptive:** Incentive is lower now. It just captured the X cost saving, and its sequencing margin is already 72%. A move would be a future decision.
- **GeneDx:** Incentive is high. Genomes cost nearly 2x exome, and it expects "step improvements in 2027." Ability is moderate, since a switch needs a genome re-validation and the supplier was not named.
- **Veracyte, NeoGenomics:** Moderate incentive. I found no platform disclosures.

## Contradictions and stale data

- **Mixed signals on ASP vs. volume.** GeneDx revenue (+17%) trails volume (+32%), consistent with the "price per test" pressure. Adaptive ASP rose 7% while its cost fell. Customers are keeping the savings, so the price decline does not fully pass through to their revenue.
- **Caris cost estimate ($2,000 per test, 75% cut) is an unverified secondary snippet** and mixes MCED pricing with sequencing. Do not rely on it.
- **Quest/Ultima deal date is ambiguous** ("January" in a snippet; the year was not confirmed). Labcorp/Ultima items are from 2024 and may have been expanded since.
- **Tempus's MRD volume (9,000 in Q2)** is a Tempus figure, not a platform split. Several summaries are third-party (MarketBeat, 360dx), not the transcript.
- The Caris "two competitors" quote is from a third-party transcript and contains an obvious typo ("Higher and lower, respectively"). It likely means higher throughput and lower cost.
