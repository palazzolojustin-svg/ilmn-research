## Key findings

1. **The over-earning is real in arithmetic but small in time-adjusted terms. It is a known drag of about 5 points a year, not a cliff.** The NovaSeq 6000 carries about 41% of high-throughput (HT) revenue on 17% of volume. That implies the X earns about 0.295x the 6000's revenue per Gb, a 70.5% cut (OPINION calc) [file 45; file 52]. If all of it converted at a 60–70% cut with +15% volume uplift, the run-rate loss is $378–574M, or 12–18.5% of FY26 consensus consumables of $3,104M. The central case is $473M (15.2%) [file 45].
2. **The 6000 revenue is likely already falling in guidance and consensus.** Guided 6000 pull-through of $600–650K on an average base of about 1,270 implies H2'26 6000 revenue of $138–200M a quarter, against $219M in Q2. That is about $40M a quarter lost in six months (OPINION calc, E-ledger via file 45). Conversion is already in the numbers.
3. **The profit pool is far more concentrated than the revenue pool.** The 6000 consumables are roughly 22–24% of core gross profit and 67–71% of core operating income. This assumes an 80–85% margin on $219M a quarter (OPINION calc) [file 108]. Full conversion at −60% to −70% $/Gb with +30% Gb costs about $1.75–2.63 of EPS a year, against an annualized Q2'26 EPS of $5.24 [file 108].
4. **The strongest bear point is the stack, not the tail.** Three price layers add up to about the mid-teens model with no spare room. They are tail conversion (about 6–7% a year), the 35B flow cell (about 4% a year) and residual repricing (about 4% a year). Any Roche or Ultima response is additive [file 45]. The 35B plus −15% base price needs about +47% Gb to hold clinical revenue flat. A half-weight 35B needs +30.7% [file 76].
5. **The strongest bull point is that clinical Gb growth is running well above the model.** Ex-China clinical grew +15% in Q2'26 and about +17% in H1. At a −15% price decline that implies Gb of +35–38% (OPINION calc) [file 76]. Management said "clinical sequencing volume growth above 30%" and Dhingra said "over 30% in clinical" [JPM deck, 2026-01-13, via file 52]. Agent 52 puts the defensible trailing clinical Gb range at +35–50%, central about +42%.
6. **Consensus appears to need very high Gb growth.** FY27 consumables consensus is $3,378M (+8.8%). Agent 140's model reaches only $2.86–2.97B at 20–30% clinical Gb, and says consensus embeds HT Gb growth of about 35% at a flat X price, or about 53% at −10% a year price (OPINION, assumption-heavy). The Q2 backtest cuts the other way: the model predicted +1% q/q HT and the actual was +6.8% [file 140]. The model may simply be too bearish.
7. **There is no named clinical lab in production on a non-Illumina HT platform.** Roche AXELIOS is still research-use-only (RUO). Foundation's early-access MRD data are vendor-sponsored. The nearest case is Medicover running AVITI inside IVDR kits (no volume or price) [file 100]. Lock-in from revalidation is real: $100–200K per switch, 5–7-year MRD trials, and "a month or two" of side-by-side work [file 04; file 87].
8. **Customer power is the under-discussed bear variable.** Experts say the top 10–20 labs dominate clinical volume. Large accounts pay "half or even a quarter" of what small accounts pay, and contracts "can very quickly become null and void" when a rival undercuts by 25–50% [file 04; file 128]. The Natera/Caris Open Offer template locks prices and promised a ≥43% cost-per-Gb cut by 2025 [file 47]. Those labs will take the 35B cut as a contractual right.
9. **The management-credibility issues that matter all concern the transition metrics.** The pull-through guide has not been reaffirmed since the Q4'25 deck. The Jefferies and Souda questions on parallel running were dodged. The "40 converted customers" cohort has selection bias. The cohort arithmetic implies converting Gb rose 2.4–3.3x, which only fits incremental volume [files 60, 76, 91].
10. **The insider and compensation items are mostly noise for this thesis.** Director Keith Meister (Corvex) sold about 2.06M shares (about $430M) from August to early September 2026 at roughly $200–227, and the stock later reached about $294 [file 15; file 104]. Thaysen bought 12,350 shares in May 2025 and has made no discretionary sales (FACT) [file 104].
11. **The 35B is not orderable and its terms are unknown.** The Illumina order page (2026-10-11) lists only 1.5B, 5B, 10B and 25B. The roadmap puts the 14B and 35B in H1 2027, not January. The cut size is hearsay, and Thaysen declined to price it [file 144; file 45].
12. **My net adjudication (OPINION):** over-earning exists but is slow-moving and mostly priced. The debate is whether clinical Gb stays at +35% or better after the 6000 tail is gone. Q3'26 (29 Oct) tests the near-term numbers, not the 2027–28 stack.

## Bear case (strongest evidence-based)

**Thesis Q1: over-earning on a lagged-conversion base**

- **Price gap (OPINION calc, FACT inputs):** 6000 revenue per Gb relative to the HT average rose from 1.33 in Q2'24 to 2.41 in Q2'26 [file 60]. The 6000 earns about 3.4x the X's revenue per Gb. The 6000 is about $219M a quarter, or $876M annualized [file 45].
- **Sensitivity:** uplift barely helps. Even +40% volume at a −70% cut loses 23.8% of HT revenue [file 76]. Break-even uplift on converting Gb is p/(1−p): +150% at a 60% cut and +233% at 70%.
- **Clinical concentration:** the clinical 6000 tail is about 34–36% of clinical HT revenue, or $680–785M a year at a 65–75% clinical share. That is 22–25% of total sequencing consumables [files 45, 52].
- **Dual owners:** in the JPM survey of 32 clinical respondents, 24 own a 6000 and 17 own an X. Pigeonhole logic puts 37.5–71% of clinical 6000 owners also on an X [file 45]. Those labs can shift volume onto idle X capacity with no new placements. A 20% utilization drop on the 6000 costs about $124M, or 4% of consumables.
- **Management does not answer the question.** On the Q2'26 call Peterson (Jefferies) asked about the clinical slowdown and over-earning. Thaysen cited "ins and outs" in the Middle East and Latin America and never addressed dual running [Q2'26 call, 2026-07-30, via file 76]. The Q1'26 Souda question on the initial 6000-to-X price hit was dodged on both sub-questions [file 60].
- **Parallel buying:** Thaysen said "some customers bought a few 6000s this quarter to continue operations", and Illumina sold more than 10 new 6000s. That is a sign of lock-in, but also of a base that won't convert cleanly [file 128; file 05].
- **Guide math:** the FY26 X pull-through guide of $1.35–1.40M needs H2 X revenue of about $1.46–1.55M annualized, against about $1.21M in H1'26 and a Q4'25 peak of about $1.35–1.37M. If Q3 is consensus-like, Q4 needs $1.55–1.67M. Either the guide is cut, or 6000 volume jumps onto existing X units fast (OPINION calc) [file 52]. The guide was not reaffirmed in the Q1'26 or Q2'26 decks [file 60].
- **Consensus tension:** the guided pull-throughs imply $2.2–2.3B of HT, 71–75% of consensus consumables, against the observed 69% share (OPINION calc) [file 45].

**Thesis Q2: volume may not outrun price**

- **Customers plan slower volume than Illumina reports.** The JPM survey (62 customers, May 2026) has clinical respondents planning +14.6% (2027) and +19.1% (2028) volume. That is far below ">30%" [file 05]. If volume decelerates to the high teens with price at mid-teens, net growth is roughly flat to low single digits.
- **Management's own model nets to little.** Gb +25–30% with price −15% gives only +6–10% before the 6000 tail. After about 5 points a year of tail drag, net is roughly 0–5% (OPINION calc) [file 140].
- **Gb per test is not obviously rising fast outside MRD.** Bottom-up from named labs covers only about 10–15% of the top-down clinical Gb (about 10 Pb versus about 106 Pb a quarter), so the named-customer story cannot validate the total [file 52].
- **Deceleration:** ex-China clinical growth slowed from 20% in Q1'26 to 15% in Q2'26. Two-year stacks were 28 (Q1) and 25 (Q2). A constant stack implies only +5–8% in Q4'26 against a 20% comp [file 76].
- **Competition:**
  - Roche AXELIOS lists at $150 a genome. Illumina's effective price is $125–175 and its list about $200. Parity at list needs the 35B cut [file 76].
  - Experts see the 6000's eventual obsolescence as an opening for rivals [file 04]. One ex-employee said Element could push Illumina consumable margins "to below 50% in some cases".
  - Novogene shut its 6000 service on a fixed timetable (stopped new quotes 2024-12-01, all service ended 2025-06-01) and moved to X Plus [file 100]. A provider can sunset the 6000 faster than customers like.
- **Contract mechanics force the cut:** Natera's §7(l) caps $/Gb on new products. The Open Offer price lock runs to about 2033 and bars price increases [files 45, 47]. This reduces Illumina's ability to recover price anywhere in the largest accounts.
- **Positioning:** the stock ran to about $294 into the S&P 500 inclusion (announced 4 Sep, effective 21 Sep) from about $205 post-Q2. Shares short rose from 7.5M (15 May) to 10.6M (15 Sep) and were 9.47M on 30 Sep [files 96, 104]. Optionslam shows a 16.9% monthly implied move through 20 Nov [file 104]. That is a high bar for a print.

## Bull case (strongest evidence-based)

**Thesis Q1: the tail is priced and not a cliff**

- **Guidance and consensus already embed 6000 decline** (finding 2). "No cliff" is partly a modelling fact, not a management assurance.
- **The margin evidence does not show erosion from the X mix.** At a 40% instrument margin, implied consumables margin was 78.8% in Q3'24 (X share 35%) and 78.4% in Q3'25 once the roughly $24M tariff cost is added back (X share 51%). Q4'24 was 76.6% against 76.8% in Q4'25 (X share 40% to 55%) (OPINION calc) [file 108]. The raw decline to 75.0% in Q2'26 is mostly tariffs, memory and freight.
- **Placements are mostly incremental.** Up to half of clinical X placements are incremental [JPM Q2 note, 2026-07-31, via file 05]. Thaysen: "much more additional incremental placement than necessarily conversion of the 6,000" [Q1'26 call, via file 60]. If so, the X base grows without cannibalizing the 6000 one for one.
- **Sell-side sees the pricing headwind fading.** JPM says transition pricing headwinds are "largely gone exiting this year" (2026-06-10). The Q2'26 X revenue share rose from 55% to 59% while volume share moved only 82% to 83%, which argues against Barclays' Q1 worry that X pricing was sliding [file 05]. The Q2 ratio uptick is within rounding (0.279–0.312 versus 0.254–0.283) [file 140].
- **Switching friction is substantial.** Revalidation costs of $100–200K, 5–7-year MRD trials ("you cannot switch an assay or a sequencer middle of a trial") and Adaptive's decade on a NextSeq 550 all support stickiness on the Illumina platform generally [file 04]. This protects the X base against Roche and Element even as it slows the 6000-to-X move.
- **The history is consoling.** In 2019, NovaSeq consumable shipments almost doubled (more than 110 petabases) and total consumables grew +14% on +50% total data, about −24% revenue per Gb. Consumables still grew. After three years only "a little over 1/3" of 850 HiSeq customers had begun transitioning [file 110]. The 6000 tail could take longer than 2028.
- **Federal and Chinese competitors face friction.** Complete Genomics was added to the DoD 1260H list on 8 Jun 2026. Its sale to Swiss Rockets (USD 50M, announced 23 Feb 2026) had not closed in any source found [file 144]. The effect is unquantified.

**Thesis Q2: volume has more room than the survey suggests**

- **Trailing clinical Gb is high** (finding 5). The upper-bound check also holds: JPM Jan 2026 gave clinical ">30%" forward and "approximately 25%" for the whole business.
- **Test volumes in the largest accounts are growing far faster than 25%.** Natera clinical MRD units were about 283,000 in Q2'26, +56% y/y, with total oncology tests +57.2% to about 296,700. It raised FY26 revenue guidance by $100M at the midpoint to $2.85–2.91B [file 125]. The Illumina link is inferred, since Natera's sequencing is mostly Illumina but the corpus doesn't confirm it.
- **Intensity per test is rising.** Signatera Genome uses about 22M reads per draw against about 1.6M for classic Signatera. That is roughly 2.2–3.4 Gb versus 0.16–0.24 Gb, about a 10–15x step-up (OPINION, derived from a search snippet) [file 135].
- **Guideline catalysts exist.** NCCN added ctDNA-MRD to the bladder guideline on 23 Jun 2026, tied to FDA approval of atezolizumab and the Signatera CDx (May 2026). Colon and rectal language is still prognostic only [file 125]. Reimbursement and serial testing cadence are the next unlocks.
- **A revenue-implied view favours the bulls.** Agent 140's 'consensus needs 35% HT Gb' framing will be met if the true clinical Gb is the +40–52% trailing range. Agent 45 notes that E205's +35–60% clears the worst-case +26% break-even easily; E083's +23% does not [file 45].
- **Management is not selling.** Thaysen's direct holdings rose from about 83K to about 108K shares, with 1,303 shares withheld for tax at $293.69 in October (FACT) [file 104]. The 2025 annual incentive paid out at 77% of target, below target, on revenue and non-GAAP operating income dollars [file 133].

## Adjudication

**Which evidence is strongest and most recent**
- *Strongest (hard numbers, recent):* the X/6000 share series through Q2'26, the pull-through guide, tariff-adjusted margin analysis, Natera's Q2'26 volumes, and the order page showing no 35B kit.
- *Weakest:* the 35B size (hearsay), the clinical share of HT Gb (64% is an assumption), and the dual-owner share (a count, not revenue).
- **The clinical Gb dispute (E083 +23% versus E205 +35–60%) is mostly a time-basis difference.** E205 is trailing and E083 forward. E083 also weights by revenue instead of Gb. Swapping in Q2'26 prints lifts E083 to about +31% [file 52]. The agent-45 worst-case break-even (+26% a year for 2027–28) sits between. I side with the trailing +35–50% range as a fact about 2025–H1'26, and treat 2027–28 as open.
- **Biased or stale sources:**
  - Ex-employee experts are the main bear sources. The "cannibalized the business" and "half or even a quarter" quotes come from people who left in Sep 2024 and Jul 2025 (EXPERT-DIRECT only to those dates). The X's cost advantage they describe may be stale.
  - Vendor-sponsored data (Roche workshops, Element and Medicover releases) are bull-for-competitor but non-clinical.
  - The JPM survey's 4–5% implied price decline is self-reported and conflicts with management's mid-teens. It also has a count conflict (75% of clinical respondents own a 6000 versus a "16%" summary figure) [file 05].
  - The "320 customers yet to transition" HiSeq figure was verified only through a fetch summary, not the SEC exhibit [file 110].
- **Where consensus may be wrong:**
  - *Too bullish:* FY27 $3,378M needs roughly 35%+ HT Gb at flat X price (OPINION, assumption-heavy), and the 35B date may land inside the forecast year.
  - *Too bearish:* any model treating 6000 revenue as static. It is already falling.
  - Sell-side treats the 6000 as a non-event. Nobody in the notes quantifies revenue at risk [file 05].
