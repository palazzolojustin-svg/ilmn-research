The table below does.

- **2019 is the one year with both a volume and a revenue figure.** FACT: "total data generated by Illumina sequencers increased 50%" and sequencing consumables grew "14% or over $250 million" ([Q4'19 call, 2020-01-29](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2020/01/29/illumina-inc-ilmn-q4-2019-earnings-call-transcript.aspx)). OPINION: that implies roughly a 24% fall in revenue per Gb (1.14/1.50 − 1). This is a blended figure that includes NextSeq and MiSeq. It is a rough match for the CFO's "~25%" in E-series items, and it gives the 2019 elasticity of revenue to Gb as about 0.3 (revenue growth ÷ volume growth). Volume did not offset price during the HiSeq-to-NovaSeq 6000 conversion.
- **Revenue growth fell steadily through the 6000 transition.** FACT: sequencing consumables grew 15% in FY17, 23% (about $1.8B) in FY18, 14% in FY19 and fell 2% (about $2.0B) in FY20. FY18 benefited from stocking orders: Q4'18 growth was 16% after normalizing, and 31% in Q4'17 ([Q4'18 call, 2019-01-30](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2019/01/30/illumina-ilmn-q4-2018-earnings-conference-call-tra.aspx); [Q4'17 release, 2018-01-30](https://www.illumina.com/company/news-center/press-releases/2018/2329227.html); [Q4'20 call, 2021-02-11](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2021/02/11/illumina-inc-ilmn-q4-2020-earnings-call-transcript/)).
- **The analog says conversion takes six years or more.** FACT: HiSeq "active installed base of approximately 1,300" at end-2019, about 600 inactive, about 200 decommissions in 2019, and HiSeq consumables "below $100 million" in Q4'19 for the first time since the 6000 launched. About 1,000 active HiSeqs remained at end-2020, with 320 customers still pending upgrade ([Q4'20 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2021/02/11/illumina-inc-ilmn-q4-2020-earnings-call-transcript/)). This updates E181, where Goswami said some assays were still on HiSeq six years on.
- **The "last 20%" tail was long, and it was held back by v1.5 reagents.** MGMT, per the Q4'20 summary: the v1.5 reagent launch "lowered entry barriers" for smaller labs, so Illumina cut the cost of conversion for the stragglers. That is an analog for the 35B flow cell and for any cheaper chemistry aimed at the 6000 tail.
- **Pull-through per NovaSeq rose as the base ramped.** FACT: about $1.2M per system in 2019 (highest in Q4'19, helped by UK Biobank), and guided to $1.1–1.2M for 2020. Q4'20 was "over $1.2 million", with 2021 guided to $1.1–1.2M. Q3'19 was "over $1 million" on an annualized basis. These figures are not in the ledger. The 6000 earned about $1.2M per system when the base was mostly early adopters running at scale. The ledger shows the 6000 at about $670K per system in FY25 (old, lightly used machines). The analog implies the 6000's per-system figure decays as the base ages.
- **The 6000 base grew fast.** FACT: about 285 NovaSeqs installed at end-2017 and about 600 at end-2018. Shipments were about 285 in FY17 ("high 80s" in Q4'17), a little under 320 in FY18 and about 320 in FY19, with more than 100 in Q4'19. About 75% of HiSeq customers had not yet taken a first NovaSeq at end-2018 ([Q4'18 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2019/01/30/illumina-ilmn-q4-2018-earnings-conference-call-tra.aspx); [Q4'17 summary](https://www.illumina.com/company/news-center/press-releases/2018/2329227.html)). The 2018 shipment figure is inferred from the 2019 comparison ("slightly higher than 2018") and I did not confirm it.
- **Launch price per Gb and the claimed cost.** FACT: the 6000 and 5000 launched at $985K and $850K, with "usage costs 20% lower than the HiSeq X and 45% lower than the HiSeq 4000" ([GenomeWeb/Bio-IT World, 2017-01-10](https://www.bio-itworld.com/news/2017/01/10/illumina-launches-novaseq-sequencers-aiming-to-replace-1900-sequencers)). A blog (not primary) says S4 reagents were to be about $1.7/Gb versus about $7/Gb on the HiSeq X, but S4 was not ready until late 2017 ([Genohub, 2017-01-10](https://blog.genohub.com/2017/01/10/illumina-unveils-novaseq-5000-and-6000/)). OPINION: the first-year 6000 step was only about 20% below the HiSeq X per Gb at launch. The large cut came later with S4 (S4 vs HiSeq X is about 75% by that blog's numbers). That is smaller up front than the "more than 60–70%" cited for 6000 to X.
- **Clinical was resilient through the 2020 downturn.** FACT: clinical consumables grew about 20% (about $130M) to about $830M in 2019, then about 8% to about $890M in 2020, with Q4'20 above 20%. Total sequencing consumables fell 2% in 2020 (COVID, with research hit hardest). This supports the idea that clinical dominates the retained base (E181, Goswami's "more muted than HiSeq" comment).
- **Guidance misses and cuts.** FACT: Q3'19 guidance was cut to about 6% revenue growth, citing lower near-term DTC, "a more conservative assumption about the speed with which certain population genomics initiatives ramp", and lower non-high-throughput systems and consumables. Q3'19 high-throughput shipments were almost $300M, of which just under $200M was NovaSeq. NovaSeq consumables grew just over 20% sequentially ([Q3'19 call, 2019-10-25](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2019/10/25/illumina-inc-ilmn-q3-2019-earnings-call-transcript.aspx); [8-K Q3'19](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1110803/000111080319000054/a3q19earningsrelease.htm)). FY20 guidance was for sequencing consumables up about 17%, and the actual outcome was −2%. The 2018 guidance of "above 20%" for 2019 ended at 14%. Both are misses against initial guidance.

## OPINION: did volume offset price?

In revenue terms, volume did not fully offset price during 2017–2019, though the NovaSeq cohort alone grew strongly. The offset came from share shift. NovaSeq consumables grew more than 5x in 2018 while HiSeq consumables fell, so total revenue rose 23% in 2018 and 14% in 2019. By late 2019 HiSeq consumables had shrunk below $100M a quarter. The overall result was revenue growth of 14–23% against 50% volume growth. For 2026–27, the analog suggests two things:

1. The conversion tail is long (three or more years until under half the HiSeq customers had moved).
2. Revenue per Gb fell about 24% a year even without a step-change in price. The 6000-to-X step cuts per-Gb price by more than 60–70%, so volume growth would have to exceed about 3x just to hold revenue flat.
