# Agent 09: Clinical customers I: Natera, Guardant, BillionToOne, Exact/Abbott

*Model: sonnet. Run 2026-10-11 02:01:08. Cost $1.07 (API-equivalent).*

## Key findings

1. **Guardant's NovaSeq X move cut Guardant360 Liquid cost per test by about $200.** The CFO said "we completed the Guardant360 Liquid transition to NovaSeq X in May, reducing cost per test by approximately $200." Q1'26 coverage describes the same ~$200 as measured versus Q1'25. FACT/MGMT. [Guardant Q2'26 call, 2026-07-30, via MarketBeat https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/reports/2026-7-30-guardant-health-inc-stock/; Q1'26 summary via Yahoo/247WallSt]
   - Guardant's non-GAAP gross margin was 67% in Q2'26 against 66% a year earlier. FY26 guidance is 64-65%. FACT. [Guardant Q2'26 call, 2026-07-30]
   - The $200 saving did not show up as a big margin jump. Savings are being reinvested or diluted by mix (Shield, Reveal ramp). OPINION.
   - The $200 is a per-test cost cut, so it is a per-test price decline Illumina absorbed.
   - Guardant did not state what share of that $200 came from X versus other workflow changes.

2. **Natera is still single-source Illumina.** Its FY25 10-K says its tests are "only validated to perform on Illumina's sequencing platform." Illumina is the sole supplier of sequencers and reagents for Panorama, Horizon, Signatera and Prospera, under a supply agreement that expires in August 2033. FACT. [Natera 10-K FY2025, filed 2026 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1604821/000110465926020881/ntra-20251231x10k.htm), via search summary]
   - Natera's licensees under its Constellation distribution model also have no alternative to Illumina. FACT.
   - A separate fetch of the 10-K excerpt said Natera's technology is "compatible with… a range of next generation sequencing, or NGS, platforms." This is boilerplate, not validation of any other platform.

3. **Guardant's Illumina supply agreement runs to January 2033, with an exit option.** It auto-renews yearly. Guardant "may also terminate the supply agreement for convenience upon 90 days' prior written notice." Pricing, minimums and exclusivity are not disclosed. FACT. [Guardant 10-K FY2025 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1576280/000157628026000013/gh-20251231.htm)]
   - The 90-day convenience exit is real bargaining leverage for Guardant. OPINION.
   - The 10-K names Ultima, PacBio, Thermo and Oxford Nanopore only as competitors of Illumina, not as Guardant platforms.

4. **BillionToOne is a litigation risk to Illumina's customer base.** On May 7, 2026, Illumina sued BillionToOne in Delaware, alleging that UNITY infringes US Patents 10,612,096, 12,139,760 and 12,435,373. The patents cover fetal-fraction determination and identifying variants in DNA mixtures. Illumina seeks an injunction or royalties plus damages. FACT. [BLLN 10-Q for Q2'26 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002070849/000207084926000050/blln-20260630.htm); Bloomberg Government, May 2026 (https://news.bgov.com/delaware-brief/illumina-sues-billiontoone-says-prenatal-tests-infringe-patents)]
   - BillionToOne says the claims are "without merit."
   - The 10-Q does not name Illumina as a supplier or name its sequencers. BillionToOne's smNGS (single-molecule counting) approach is reportedly less dependent on sequencing hardware. That claim comes from the S-1 summary and is unverified.
   - Suing a fast-growing customer or competitor in the NIPT (non-invasive prenatal testing) market is a notable move. See the credibility section.

5. **Natera's oncology volume is growing about 56% a year, so sequencing demand is growing fast.** FACT. [Natera Q2'26 release, 2026-08-06 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001604821/000160482126000015/ntra-20260630xex991.htm)]

   | Measure | Q2'26 | Prior | Growth |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Clinical MRD (minimal residual disease) units | ~283K | ~181K (Q2'25) | +56% |
   | Total oncology tests | ~296.7K | | +57% |
   | Total tests | ~1.04M | ~853K (Q2'25) | +22% |

   - Q4'25 had ~225.3K clinical MRD tests against 144.5K in Q4'24, up about 56%. FACT. [Natera prelim Q4'25 PR, Jan 2026]
   - Sequential adds were 34K MRD units in Q2'26. Natera said over 50% of US oncologists ordered Signatera in Q4'25. MGMT.
   - Natera's Q1'26 total of ~1,013.6K tests was up 18.5%. FACT. Total-test growth is far below oncology growth, so women's health volume is growing only about 10-15%. This is my inference.

6. **Natera's gross margin is flat to slightly up and is not driven by a visible sequencing price cut.** FACT. [Natera Q2'26 release]
   - GAAP gross margin was 64.5% in Q2'26 against 63.4% in Q2'25. Q1'26 was 64.7% against 63.1%. FY26 guidance is 64-66%, with a long-term target of "70%+."
   - Natera cites "continued progress in reducing cost of revenues associated with tests processed" but gives no per-test COGS.
   - New launches (Latitude, Fetal Focus, Signatera Genome) are "not yet optimized for cost efficiency" and "leave a lot of room to achieve COGS improvements over time as volume scales" (CFO Brophy). MGMT. The Signatera Genome launch is a margin headwind today.
   - Management expects step-function gains from MolDX coverage and from cost-reduction programs over "the next 12-18 months." MGMT.

7. **Signatera price (ASP, average selling price) is rising, not falling.** That gives Natera room to absorb sequencing cost without cutting prices. FACT. [Natera Q2'26 call, via GuruFocus/MarketBeat]
   - ASP was ~$1,275 in Q2'26 against ~$1,225 in Q4'25.
   - Pending MolDX submissions could add roughly $150-200. The long-term target is $2,000.
   - Implication for Illumina: Natera's customers are earning more per test even as volume rises ~56%. They can afford more Gb per test (genome-based tests) without a price squeeze. OPINION.

8. **Guardant Shield is an early-stage cost story, and Shield's cost-per-test target implies large Gb-per-dollar pressure.** MGMT. [Guardant Q2'26 call, 2026-07-30]
   - Cost per Shield test is ~$410 today. A new FDA-approved "higher-throughput, lower-COGS" workflow went live in August 2026 and should cut cost about 15% by year-end. The 2028 target is $200 per test.
   - Shield volume was ~66K tests in Q2'26 against ~16K a year ago (about 4x). Q1'26 was ~44K. FY26 guidance is 270-285K tests and $218-230M revenue.
   - FY25 Shield was ~87K tests and $79.7M revenue. FACT.
   - Halving Shield cost from $410 to $200 would largely come from sequencing and lab automation. Part of it will land on Illumina's pricing or on a cheaper platform. OPINION. Guardant did not say which.

9. **Guardant's other volumes are strong.** FACT. [Guardant Q2'26 call and release]
   - Total revenue was $335M, up 44%. Oncology revenue was $219M, up 38%.
   - Oncology tests were ~104K, up 63% (Q1'26: ~86K).
   - Guardant360 Liquid volume grew more than 30% y/y.
   - Reveal (MRD) volume grew more than 100% y/y.
   - The FY26 oncology volume growth outlook was raised to about 50% from 35%. FY26 revenue guidance is $1.34-1.36B.
   - Screening non-GAAP gross margin rose from 18% (Q1'25) to 56% (Q1'26). FACT (Yahoo summary). Screening margin was fixed by scale and workflow, with sequencing the largest COGS line. OPINION.

10. **BillionToOne's growth is a mix of volume and price, and margin is expanding.** FACT. [BLLN Q2'26 release, 2026-08-05]
    - Q2'26 tests were 196K against 145K (+35%).
    - Revenue was $109.4M against $66.6M (+64%), with a 21% rise in overall ASP.
    - Gross margin was 70% against 65%. The H1'26 margin was 71.7% on $217.8M revenue.
    - Prenatal revenue was $190.8M for H1; oncology was $24.4M.
    - Over 90% of revenue comes from third-party payers. About $12M of H1 revenue came from prior-period performance obligations, which is catch-up, not run-rate. [Panabee analysis]
    - Note that revenue per test is rising about 20%+ while cost per test falls. Customers' per-test economics are improving, not squeezed.

11. **The Natera expert call is the best qualitative read on switching.** OPINION/MGMT-adjacent (current Natera employee, not an official statement). [Expert call, 2026-07-22, Extracts/ILMN/expert_call/Expert_2026-07-22_Associate_Director_at_Natera...]
    - Established MRD assays will not switch sequencers: "you cannot switch an assay or a sequencer middle of a trial." Trials take five to seven years. "The big four, plus Natera, they're just using Illumina."
    - Element at ~50X depth cannot run Signatera. Tissue-informed MRD wants more than 80-120X.
    - The expert says Natera "also uses aspects of Element Biosciences for ctDNA", but then says the big players use only Illumina. This is internally inconsistent.
    - Ultima is the likely switch for new, shallow-depth applications (oncology screening, women's health, transplant) and for small or local labs.

12. **Other labs are the ones validating Ultima.** FACT. Labcorp has expanded its collaboration with Ultima (UG 100 and ppmSeq) for WGS MRD (Plasma Detect), and Myriad is exploring the UG 100 for Precise MRD. [Labcorp PR via BioSpace/Labcorp.com; Myriad PR]
    - I found no Natera, Guardant, BillionToOne or Exact announcement of Ultima, Element or Roche adoption. NIPT, Signatera and Guardant360 stay Illumina-only as far as disclosed.

## Test volume to Gb estimate

These are my own estimates. They assume rough and unverified inputs, so treat the structure as the point.

- **Natera oncology.** Clinical MRD units grew 56% y/y. Revenue per unit is rising (ASP +4% in two quarters), so units are the better proxy for Gb. If the mix shifts to Signatera Genome (WGS) and deeper phased-variant assays, Gb per test rises further. Gb per test could be 2-10x higher for WGS-based MRD than for a roughly 50-plex tumor-informed panel. OPINION. That figure is not in the sources.
- **Natera total.** Total tests grew 18-22%. Oncology is about 28% of tests (283K of 1.04M) but likely a much larger share of Gb. OPINION.
- **Guardant.**
  - 360 Liquid is up >30% in volume. Its move to X lowers $/Gb.
  - Reveal is up >100%.
  - Shield is up ~4x. Shield is a low-pass or methylation workflow, so its Gb per test is probably well below Reveal's.
  - Aggregate oncology volume is +63% in Q2'26.
- **BillionToOne.** Tests are up 35% y/y. NIPT is shallow, so the Gb weight is low.
- **Net read.** Test volumes for the three are growing 35-60% y/y, well above the 30%+ Gb growth management cites. But the per-test cost-down programs (Guardant -$200, Shield -15% now and to $200 by 2028) show customers are demanding steep price declines. Both pull in the same direction: volume up, price per Gb down, and revenue growth for Illumina below volume growth. OPINION.

## Exact Sciences / Abbott

- Abbott closed the Exact deal in March 2026 for about $21B equity and ~$23B including debt. Exact was expected to contribute ~$3.2B of 2026 sales. FACT. [S&P Global, Apr 2026; Nasdaq; 360Dx]
- Exact's Q4'25 revenue was $878M (+23%), with Screening $695M and Precision Oncology $183M. FACT. [Exact Q4'25 8-K, 2026-02-13]
- Abbott says cancer diagnostics grew 13% in H1'26, and Cologuard grew "mid-teens." H2 should be stronger, helped by Cologuard Plus pricing. Cancerguard (MCED, multi-cancer early detection) is being invested in, and new Oncodetect (MRD) data are coming. MGMT. [Abbott Q2'26 via GenomeWeb/Chartmill, 2026-07-16]
- Cologuard is a stool-based, non-sequencing-heavy test, so it is not a significant Illumina Gb driver. Cancerguard and Oncodetect are the sequencing-relevant lines. Abbott does not disclose volumes for either. I found no Illumina supplier language for Exact post-deal.
- Abbott's ownership means no separate Exact disclosure of sequencing cost. This is a data gap for the read-through.

## Management credibility and behavior flags (customers, not Illumina)

- Illumina is suing a customer-class company (BillionToOne) over NIPT patents. It was filed May 2026, after BillionToOne's IPO. This is potentially at odds with the "customer-first" tone and could push the largest NIPT labs to diversify. OPINION.
- Natera's definitions are tweaked repeatedly. It reports "clinical MRD units" and "true-ups" separately, and revenue "ex true-ups" (CEO/CFO at Q2'26: 40% growth ex-true-ups against 38% reported). That is Natera, not Illumina, but it complicates volume-to-revenue links.
- Guardant's gross margin guide of 64-65% sits below the Q2 print of 67%. Either guidance is conservative or H2 mix is worse. The guidance was kept unchanged while revenue guidance was raised. OPINION.

## Contradictions and stale data

- **Natera and Element.** The expert says Natera uses "aspects of Element Biosciences for ctDNA" but also that the big MRD players "are just using Illumina." The Natera 10-K says tests are "only validated" on Illumina. Treat 10-K language as authoritative.
- **Guardant's $200.** One source says the $200 saving is measured "versus Q1 2025" and that the transition completed in May. The CFO line says it was completed in May "reducing cost per test by approximately $200." Different sources, so timing of the benefit in reported margin is unclear. The Q1'26 summary says margin was already helped before May, which suggests a phased transition.
- **Panabee on BillionToOne.** It describes the 35% test growth as "quarter-over-quarter"; the company release says year over year. I used the release. It also says "no explicit 2026 guidance" while the company release title says it "reiterates 2026 revenue guidance." I did not resolve this.
- **Natera oncology versus clinical MRD.** ~296.7K oncology tests (+57%) versus ~283K clinical MRD (+56%). These are different bases.
- **GuruFocus and others on Natera.** Two aggregator pages differ on whether the call mentioned Illumina or sequencing. The call apparently did not discuss it. This silence is itself a data point: management did not volunteer Illumina pricing commentary.
- **Several search snippets were summary-level,** and some figures (Guardant Q1 86K/44K tests, screening margin 18%→56%) rest on a single aggregator. I could not verify them against primary transcripts.

- **No Natera 10-K COGS bridge** (the fetched excerpt omitted MD&A). Natera 10-K FY25 total tests: ~3.5M. Product revenues $2,295.8M (2025), $1,685.1M (2024), $1,068.5M (2023). FACT.
- **No disclosure of X versus 6000 mix at Natera, Guardant or BillionToOne** (apart from Guardant's completed G360 Liquid move). Natera and BillionToOne are unknown. This matters directly for the Q1 question about parallel runs on 6000 and X.
