- **The Ultima clinical-services preprint is now identified, but I could not read its text.** [FACT] "Evaluation of the Ultima Genomics UG 100™ sequencing platform for clinical services" was posted to bioRxiv on 2025-12-12 (v1). The authors are Santuari, Kolpakov, Blin, Xenarios and Howald of the Health 2030 Genome Center. The comparator is **NovaSeq 6000**, not the X. (bioRxiv API, https://api.biorxiv.org/details/biorxiv/10.64898/2025.12.11.693628; Europe PMC record PPR1231843.) This updates open gap 6 and E114/E128.
- **The preprint's main finding is a clinical caveat for Ultima.** [FACT, per abstract and search snippet] SNV precision and recall were tested on GIAB reference samples, with a focus on long homopolymer regions (runs of one repeated base). The search snippet says homopolymer calling accuracy "drops below 90% after a length of 8 bases." (https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.11.693628.full.pdf, via search snippet only.) The same snippet says accreditation of sequencing centers requires rigorous evaluation of the platform. [OPINION] This supports the view that Ultima wins new shallow uses first (E116). The full PDF and the JATS XML both returned HTTP 429, so I have no depth, cost-per-sample or sample-count numbers.
- **The same preprint calls the 6000 and the X the clinical standard.** [FACT, snippet] It says "NovaSeq 6000 System and NovaSeq X Series are currently the machines of choice for clinical applications in terms of cost and quality." This is weak evidence that a Swiss lab benchmarked the challenger against the 6000 as of late 2025.
- **A clinical exome lab validated the 6000 in late 2025.** [FACT] AOUS Siena (Italy), published Nov 2025, validated a NovaSeq 6000 RUO with Hamilton automated library prep against the CE-IVD NovaSeq 6000Dx. The study used 96 clinical exomes at a mean depth of 139.65× and about 76.6–79.2M aligned reads per run. SNV concordance was 100%, and diagnostic yield was 32.4%. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12639258) [OPINION] This is a small, anecdotal case, but it is evidence that regulated clinical labs were still validating 6000 workflows in late 2025. That fits the "sticky tail" in E012 and E016. The X is not mentioned.
- **No NovaSeq X Dx was found.** [FACT, absence] I found no FDA or CE record of a NovaSeq X-based diagnostic (Dx) instrument. The only IVD high-throughput sequencer I found is the NovaSeq 6000Dx, announced in 2022. Illumina's EU TruSight Oncology Comprehensive page lists only the NextSeq 550Dx, with up to 7 patients plus 2 controls per run and a 4–5 day assay (https://www.illumina.com/products/by-type/ivd-products/trusight-oncology-comprehensive.html, fetched 2026-10-11). [OPINION] If so, FDA-cleared or CE-marked assays cannot yet run on the X, which would help explain the regulated 6000 tail in E016. Treat this as unconfirmed, because I did not search the FDA databases directly.
- **AXELIOS clinical-lab names now have detail.** [FACT] At ESHG (13–16 Jun 2026, Gothenburg), Roche ran a 14 Jun satellite session on AXELIOS 1 for rare-disease research (https://diagnostics.roche.com/eshg).
  - Broad Clinical Labs (Sean Hofherr) presented "Rapid amplification-free SBX-D whole genome sequencing for scale", a duplex, no-amplification workflow needing **500 ng input DNA**.
  - Centogene (Jorge Pinto Basto, VP Medical Genetics) presented "preliminary results" on variant calling in rare disease **research**.
  - Roche's stated workflow is under 24 hours for 16 human 30× genomes, including a 4-hour sequencing run.
  - This adds Centogene to the named users in E124. It also qualifies E126's "150 ng" caveat: Broad's amplification-free route needs 500 ng, which is more than an average real patient sample provides.
- **CNAG is reported as a first commercial buyer.** [FACT, search snippet] Spain's CNAG reportedly bought an AXELIOS 1 and received it a few weeks after April test runs. Foundation Medicine and the Wellcome Sanger Institute also showed prototype data. A GenomeWeb piece titled "Roche sets sight on clinic following commercial launch of Axelios 1" exists, but it returned 403, so I have no detail on regulatory timelines (https://www.genomeweb.com/sequencing/roche-sets-sight-clinic-following-commercial-launch-axelios-1-sequencer). This confirms E124 and adds CNAG and Centogene as named sites. The title suggests IVD plans, but no timeline is verified.
- **A small Ultima-vs-NovaSeq FFPE benchmark exists.** [FACT] Owkin's March 2026 work compares the UG100 and a NovaSeq (model unspecified) on clinically relevant FFPE tissue across snRNA-seq, whole-transcriptome, exome and genome. It found "highly comparable results", with a cleaner but more conservative variant-calling profile and insertion-biased indels on the UG100 (https://www.owkin.com/publications/multi-modal-benchmarking-of-the-ultima-ug100-and-illumina-novaseq-sequencing-platforms-using-clinically-relevant-ffpe-tissues).
