# Does Illumina sell library prep, and who controls that market?

Prepared 11 Oct 2026. Sources: ILMN 10-Ks FY24 and FY25; ILMN calls 2023–26 (TSO 500); JPM conference deck 13 Jan 2026; Bernstein May 2025; Core Notes 06, 07, 09 and 12; the clinical-consumables scrape (GeneDx 10-K, Twist 8-K, Qiagen). Items marked [general knowledge] are not in the folder.

## Short answer

Yes, Illumina sells library-prep and assay kits, but it does not control that market. A library is the prepared sample a sequencer reads: the DNA is cut up, tagged with adapters and, for panels and exomes, the target genes are fished out. Illumina's sequencers accept libraries made with anyone's reagents, and the big clinical labs build their own tests from multi-vendor parts. Prep pricing is set competitively across many suppliers. The folder holds no market-share data and no evidence of capacity constraints.

## What Illumina sells

- **Products:** library-prep kits (DNA Prep, PCR-Free, TruSeq, RNA Prep), exome and target panels, and assays. TSO 500, a ~500-gene cancer panel, was ">$100M" in 2023 and is now also FDA-approved as TSO Comprehensive.
- **New kits:** single-cell (PIPseq), 5-base methylation, Protein Prep and StrataMap spatial.
- **Booking:** all of these sit inside sequencing consumables ($2,939M in FY25). Illumina does not split prep out [10-K FY25].
- **TruPath** ($395 genome) skips "traditional library prep", so the price is essentially sequencing.

## Who else is in the market

- **Folder evidence:**
  - **GeneDx** lists Illumina, Twist, Agilent and Life Technologies (Thermo) as limited-source suppliers [GeneDx 10-K FY25].
  - **Twist:** NGS revenue was $61.8M in its fiscal Q3'26, up 12%.
  - **Qiagen:** universal panels for third-party sequencers grew more than 20%.
  - **Roche:** AXELIOS uses conventional prep on standard robots, with partners including Watchmaker and 10x.
- **[General knowledge]:** the main third-party brands are Roche's KAPA, NEB's NEBNext, IDT's xGen (Danaher), Twist and Agilent's SureSelect for capture, plus 10x for single cell.

## Share of spend

- **Single cell:** one ex-Illumina expert says Illumina captured only about 25% of spend in a typical 10x Genomics single-cell workflow (single recollection).
- **Counting applications:** management says sequencing is only 10–20% of cost in RNA and single-cell work, so prep and other steps take most of the spend.
- **Clinical genomes:** prep costs under $100 a genome against about $250 of sequencing [expert]. Interpretation is the largest cost.

## Why it matters

1. **Prep is where the cost is moving.** As sequencing gets cheaper, Roche's CEO says "costs shift to library prep and sample collection." That is why Illumina now sells "total cost of workflow" and is pushing its own kits and prep-free products. It is also why the 1–2 points of new-product growth sit in this space.
2. **Roche owns a large prep franchise** (KAPA) [general knowledge]. That gives AXELIOS a workflow advantage Illumina's earlier rivals lacked.
3. **The exome-to-genome shift hurts capture-panel suppliers** (Twist, IDT, Agilent, and Illumina's own exome panels). It helps Illumina's sequencing revenue more than it costs Illumina in kit sales.
4. **Illumina's openness has limits.** One expert says Illumina tells TSO users it won't support validation on Element, and raised prices on users of third-party panels. That is single-source opinion.

## Gaps

- No market-share data and no Illumina prep revenue beyond the 2023 TSO 500 figure.
- No evidence on KAPA, IDT, NEB or Watchmaker pricing, or on enzyme or oligo supply capacity.
