# Why ILMN Fell ~12–16% After Hours on Q3 2026 Results (29 Oct 2026)

*Prepared 2026-10-30 from web sources: Illumina's Q3 2026 press release (8-K, EX-99.1), Investing.com's call transcript and slide summary, MarketBeat's call highlights, StockStory and GuruFocus. Updated the same day after a full read of the Seeking Alpha transcript (`Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/ILMN_2026Q3_Earnings_Call.md`). The section "What the full transcript adds" supersedes anything above it that conflicts.*

## Short answer

The quarter was fine on paper. The stock fell because it was priced for acceleration, and Q3 showed growth stalling at about 5% with no sign of a step-up into Q4 or 2027. The shares closed at $290.06, near the 52-week high of $309, at about 52x 2026 EPS, then traded at about $248–256 after hours.

## Guidance

- **FY2026:**
  - Revenue $4.64–4.66B, up $30M at the midpoint, which is roughly the Q3 beat.
  - EPS $5.40–5.45, up $0.08.
  - Organic growth (>4%) and ROW organic growth (>5%) **unchanged**.
- **Q4 2026:**
  - Revenue $1.22–1.24B, about in line with the ~$1.22B consensus.
  - EPS $1.51–1.56.
  - **ROW organic about 4.5%, "similar to Q3."** Evercore's Vijay Kumar read the guide as implying close to 6% (call Q&A).
  - Consumables growth 5–6%.
  - No meaningful budget flush (year-end spending of leftover budgets) assumed.
- **2027:**
  - Detailed guidance deferred to the Q4 call.
  - Thaysen: "still on the track to deliver on the 2027 of high single digit."
  - He called >5% consumables growth "a good starting point."
  - New products and BioInsight should add "one to two points."
  - Asked about double-digit growth, he said "absolutely not impossible," but committed to nothing.

## Why the stock fell (our reading)

1. **Headline miss.** Reported revenue missed only because of the $37M tariff credit, but screens and headlines printed "revenue miss."
2. **Growth stalled.**
   - ROW organic growth fell from 8.1% to 4.7%.
   - Instruments swung from +31% to about −2%.
   - Clinical growth stayed around 14%, well below the 20% of late 2025 and early 2026.
   - The Q2 surge now looks partly instrument-driven and lumpy.
3. **No acceleration in the guide.**
   - Q4 is guided at about 4.5% ROW organic, flat with Q3.
   - The full-year raise only passed through the beat, and organic growth guides were unchanged.
   - Getting to high-single-digit growth in 2027 now needs a step-up of about 300–400 bps from the current ~4.5–5%.
4. **The bar was far above that.**
   - The stock sat at about 52x 2026 EPS, near its highs.
   - The buy-side was underwriting low-double-digit 2027 growth.
   - Management reiterated high-single digits and pushed specifics to February.
5. **Pricing tone.**
   - Thaysen declined to detail discounts.
   - He said Illumina is "very willing to also talk about what the right price point is" with large customers.
   - He said interest in operating leases and reagent rentals is rising among research customers and mid-sized clinical labs.
   - Both point to more price-for-volume trading.
6. **Background:**
   - On 28 Oct the US blacklisted BGI, which raises the risk of Chinese retaliation against Illumina. Thaysen said Illumina was "assessing" the situation.
   - Stephens downgraded the stock to Equal Weight ($275 price target) before the print.

Management gave no reason for the drop. One article called the selloff "disconnected from fundamentals."

## Read-through for the short pitch

- **Supports the thesis:**
  - Growth is converging toward about 5%, not accelerating.
  - The gap between the buy-side bar (low double digits) and management (high single digits) is now visible.
  - Large-customer pricing flexibility and the rise of leasing back the discounting argument.
  - The X/6000 revenue-per-Gb gap is still wide, at about 3.5x.
- **Cuts against it:**
  - Management says the X-transition price headwind is smaller in 2027 than in 2026.
  - Consumables have grown more than 5% every quarter this year.
  - Margins and EPS keep beating.
- **Update needed in the writeup:** the Q2 figures (17%/41% and 3.4x) can roll to Q3 (16%/40% and about 3.5x). Note that the stock is no longer at $290.

## What the full transcript adds (checked 2026-10-30)

**Corrections to the sections above:**

- **The 84%/60% X share is confirmed.** CFO Dhingra said about 84% of high-throughput volume and 60% of revenue are now on the X. The "92%" in the slide summary most likely refers to research and applied customers, where he said "90% or greater" of volume has been on the X for five quarters.
- **Evercore's Vijay Kumar.** He did not say his own model was at 6%. He said the Q4 guide "implies something like close to like 6% for Rest of World," which is his reading of the guide. Management's stated Q4 figure is about 4.5% ROW organic.
- **BGI.** Thaysen said Illumina "learned about this situation as many of you did yesterday morning" and is "trying to better understand what the impact is." Earlier notes paraphrased this as "assessing."

**New for the short thesis:**

1. **The X-transition slide goes away.** Illumina will stop showing the X share of volume and revenue after Q4 2026, saying the charts are "becoming less relevant." That removes the disclosure behind the 6000-vs-X revenue-per-Gb ratio (about 3.5x in Q3). After Q4 the pitch must rely on core-lab and expert pricing data.
2. **Volume grows about 30%, revenue about 5%.** Gb output on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments grew "above 30%." Sequencing consumables grew 5% ROW organic. Together that implies revenue per Gb fell roughly 19–20% year on year. This is our calculation, and it is rough: the two measures cover different instrument sets and regions.
3. **Clinical conversion is slowing.** 78% of clinical volume is on the X, and conversion has slowed because labs keep existing tests on the 6000 while adding X capacity for new ones. Management expects 80–85% "in the coming quarters." It therefore expects the transition price headwind to be lower in 2027 than in 2026. This is the main bull rebuttal to the discounting thesis.
4. **Pricing language** (in answer to Leerink): customers willing to "sign up for a large volume" will find Illumina "very willing to also talk about what the right price point is."
5. **The $50 genome.** Stifel's Dan Arias raised competitor genomes "pushing down to $50." He asked whether Illumina must revisit price elasticity, or rely on the 35B, to win large AI projects. Thaysen did not answer on price. He said "We love elasticity. We can address any opportunity that we need to," and "I'm not super concerned about what others are doing right now."
6. **Discounts.** Piper's David Westenberg asked about discounts to the largest clinical customers. Thaysen called it "very specific," and said there "might always be some certain difference" between customers' growth and Illumina's.
7. **Leasing.** Over 90% of X placements are still outright sales. Demand for operating leases and reagent rental is growing in research and in clinical, mainly at mid-sized clinical labs worldwide, not at the largest clinical customers.

**Other figures from the call:**

- **Growth:**
  - Clinical consumables ROW +14% ("14%, 15%," like Q2). US clinical is "well above" 14%.
  - Research and applied fell 6%. Its revenue has grown sequentially for two quarters, with "signs research activity is stabilizing."
- **Revenue lines:**
  - Services and other: $158M, +8%, helped by Billion Cell Atlas revenue.
  - Microarrays and other: $110M, +2%. SomaLogic beat the deal model.
- **Margins and cash:**
  - Gross margin 69.4%, up 20 bps.
  - Operating cash flow $419M; free cash flow $378M.
  - Buybacks only $9M, with $1.8B of authorization left.
  - Cash $1.34B, debt $1.79B, gross leverage 1.4x.
- **FY2026 guide:**
  - Operating margin about 23.6%; EPS growth 12–13%.
  - Sequencing consumables mid-single digits; instruments low-single digits (ROW organic).
- **Q4:**
  - Consumables +5–6% against an 11% compare.
  - The extra 14th week has "minimal" revenue impact, adds payroll cost, and was already in the guide.
  - No budget flush assumed.
- **2027:**
  - "Still on the track to deliver on the '27 of high single digit."
  - Above that is "absolutely not impossible," but "we want to also show we can do high single digit before we go beyond that."
  - New products and BioInsight should add 1–2 points of growth.
  - More margin expansion is expected.
- **X demand:**
  - Thaysen saw no pull-forward.
  - Several multi-unit capacity orders came from the largest clinical customers.
  - X placements rose year on year in both clinical and research.
- **China:** back to year-on-year growth in Q3.
- **Unanswered:** Tycho Peterson's question about clinical stocking orders. Nobody addressed it directly.
