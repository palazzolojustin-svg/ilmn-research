# Research & applied: why assume it stabilises?

Prepared 11 Oct 2026, in answer to a comment on primer section 3.2. The comment asked: if research volumes won't ramp, why assume the segment stabilises, why won't prices keep falling, and isn't this business perpetually exposed to the price gap between vendors?

Sources: ILMN calls Q3'25–Q2'26 and the JPM deck of Jan 2026; JPM survey of 10 Jun 2026; Barclays, UBS and BI notes; expert calls of Jul–Aug 2026; clinical-consumables ledger (E008, E054, E070, E206, E259, E313, E423–E425).

## Answer

The objection is largely right. Research & applied (R&A, about 35% of sequencing consumables, ~$255M a quarter) is a structural price-taker. Its price per gigabase will keep falling, and nothing shows volume ramping. The primer's base case of about +2% R&A growth in 2027 was too generous. Our revised base is about −2%, with a range of −6% to +2%.

## What "stabilise" can and can't mean

R&A revenue fell 7–12% a year mainly because of a one-time step: research moved from the NovaSeq 6000 to the X, which cuts the price per gigabase by 60–70%. In H1'26 about 16 points of the per-gigabase decline came from that mix shift, and only about 4 points from within-platform price [ledger E008]. Research is now 90–92% on the X, so the cliff is behind it. Revenue now depends on volume growth minus continuing price erosion on the X. Prices do not stop falling; the steep part is over.

## Why the steady-state is flat to down, not up

**Volume shows no ramp.**
- **Q2'26 rebound unexplained.** The +9% q/q was attributed to late-quarter US funding releases. Management says it is "too early to call a recovery" and is "not assuming any meaningful improvement".
- **The guide already assumes slowing declines.** The FY26 guide (down mid to high single digits) implies H2 of about 0% to −6%.
- **Experts point down.** One genome centre expects 2026 down ~5% and is more pessimistic on 2027. The Georgia core fell ~20% in 2025.
- **NIH funding is weak.** New awards are about 12% of the total, against 18–22% normally.

**Price erosion continues and may speed up in 2027.**
- **New flow cell.** The 35B cuts price per gigabase about 20% for big users in early 2027.
- **Service-provider pricing.** Novogene ran a ~$2.60/Gb promotion; its 2025 volume rose 28% on revenue up 4.7%.
- **Rivals:** Ultima is taking a rising share of work academic cores lose, and Roche prices counting work at $0.06 per million reads against Illumina's $0.68.
- **Contracts break easily.** Academic contracts "become null and void" when rivals undercut by 25–50% [expert].

**Partial offsets.**
- **Outsourced work often stays on Illumina, at a lower price.** Work leaving academic cores goes to providers that largely run the NovaSeq X.
- **Workflow stickiness:** 10x kits, trained staff, "I will pay more, but I will not switch" [expert].
- **The JPM survey is more upbeat.** Academic consumables spend is +9% in 2027, but the respondents are self-selected Illumina customers, and only ~5% bought an instrument in 2025–26.

**Verdict.** R&A is an ex-growth to slowly shrinking pool: volume up perhaps 5–10% a year, revenue per gigabase down 8–12%. It is permanently exposed to the gap between Illumina and rival prices because research has no validated tests to lock it in.

## Implications for the numbers

- **Our 2027 base falls.** Sequencing consumables growth falls from ~+7% to ~+5.5%, and FY27 EPS from ~$5.90 to ~$5.75. Each 5 points of R&A growth is about $55M of revenue or ~$0.20 of EPS.
- **Clinical must carry the whole forecast.** With R&A at −2%, consensus FY27 sequencing consumables of +8.8% needs clinical consumables growth of roughly 15–17%. That is above the "mid-teens" 2026 guide and the ~13% implied for H2'26. This widens our below-consensus view.
- **Not yet in the primer.** These changes have not been carried into the primer's tables and valuation (section 6 table, item 7, section 10.5).
