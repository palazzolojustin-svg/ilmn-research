# Is the Street anchoring on the JPM customer survey? (2026-11-09)

*Sources: a full-text search of every file in `Working Notes/Sources/` (broker notes, earnings calls, conferences, expert calls, consensus) for "survey", the survey's distinctive numbers (18.6%, 6.2/10, 62 respondents) and its "sticky" framing; the Bloomberg consensus files (stamped 10 Oct 2026); and `2026-11-01_ILMN_how_the_street_models_ILMN.md` for each broker's estimates.*

## Short answer

**The research folder does not show the Street broadly anchoring on the JPM survey. What evidence there is points the other way.**

- **No other broker cites it.** Outside JPM's own notes, no note, call or conference in the folder mentions the survey or any of its numbers. JPM's only follow-up reference is its own 2Q recap (31 Jul, L187), which links back to "ILMN Customer Survey deck".
- **Consensus sits below JPM, not on it.** The 10 Oct Bloomberg consensus has 2027 revenue at $4,918M (+6.3%). JPM has $5,047M (+9.1%) in its June model and $5,000M (+9.1%) in its July model update. Barclays is at +5.5% and Freedom's consumables line at +6.1%. Consensus is closer to the bears than to JPM.
- **On research, the other brokers follow management, not JPM.** Every other broker repeats Illumina's guide that research and applied sequencing consumables will fall by a mid-to-high single-digit percentage, and calls any recovery upside. None adopts JPM's view that research grows at a mid-single-digit rate each year through 2030, or the survey's +9% customer spending plans for 2027.
- **No analyst on the Q2 or Q3 2026 calls refers to a survey.**

## What could look like anchoring, and why it probably isn't

- **Price targets rose after JPM's upgrade.** Barclays went from $122 to $160 (31 Jul) and Freedom from $155 to $185 (5 Aug). Both notes tie the change to the Q2 beat-and-raise and to the stock's move, not to customer survey data. Barclays still rates the stock Underweight.
- **"Sticky" clinical workflows appear in other notes.** Bloomberg Intelligence (31 Jul) writes of "the sticky nature of clinical workflows". Barclays used "how sticky the customer base is" in February, four months before the survey. The idea predates the survey.
- **Investors are said to be assuming high-single-digit growth.** Barclays (31 Jul) writes that "investors are assuming the +HSD% top line is in the bag." That refers to management's 2027 target, which management reaffirmed, not to JPM's survey.

## Limits of this check

- **Coverage.** The folder holds notes from only five brokers (JPM, Barclays, UBS to March, Freedom, Ameriprise) plus Bloomberg Intelligence and one Seeking Alpha article. Leerink, Evercore, TD Cowen, Citi, Guggenheim, Piper, BofA, Jefferies, Canaccord and Stephens are missing, though their analysts appear on the calls. There are no notes after Q3 (29 Oct).
- **Consensus is a single snapshot (10 Oct).** We cannot see whether estimates moved up after the 10 Jun survey, which is the most direct test of anchoring.
- **Direct versus indirect influence.** JPM is a large, widely read shop, so its survey could shape buy-side thinking without other brokers citing it. Nothing in the folder can show that either way.

## Web cross-check (added 9 Nov 2026)

A web search for public references to the survey, run the same day, found that the survey traveled through the financial news wires but not into other brokers' work.

**Where the survey was repeated.**
- **Investing.com, 10 Jun 2026**, republished on Yahoo Finance. It says the brokerage's concerns over migration to rival platforms "have eased after recent surveys and industry checks", with the survey cited as showing that "Illumina's large clinical customers remain highly committed to its ecosystem". It also says spending "is expected to accelerate through 2027 and 2028", with "respondents also forecasting gains in Illumina's share". Note that the survey's large-clinical tier is about 3 labs.
- **Other upgrade write-ups.** The upgrade was also covered by GenomeWeb, Seeking Alpha news, MarketScreener and MarketBeat (10 Jun).

**Where the framing resurfaced without attribution.** A TIKR blog post (updated 19 Jul 2026) repeats JPM's customer finding ("JPMorgan, after speaking with customers, reported that management teams already transitioning to the NovaSeq X…") as its only customer evidence. It also lists July price-target raises:
- Piper Sandler to $200
- Evercore ISI to $200
- Leerink to $210
- Canaccord, TD Cowen and Guggenheim, figures not given

None of those raises is tied to the survey.

**Not found.** No other broker cites the JPM survey's numbers or runs a comparable survey. No public source repeats the +9% / +18.6% spending figures or the 6.2/10 purchase intent.

**Reading.** The survey's narrative, that clinical customers are sticky and spending will accelerate, was spread widely through the news wires on the day of the upgrade and reappears in retail commentary. Its numbers were not taken up by other analysts or by consensus. The July wave of target raises followed the Q2 beat and the stock's move, and the write-ups do not attribute those raises to the survey.

**The stock was already moving before JPM's note.** It was at $126.74 on 30 Apr, $160.03 on 10 Jun (up ~26% before the upgrade), about $175.75 around 24 Jun, $187.15 on 16 Jul and $205.09 on 31 Jul. Target raises lagged the price, the usual pattern when targets catch up to a re-rating.

**Estimates show no dated evidence of a June revision.**
- A Yahoo consensus snapshot from about Mar–Apr 2026 has FY27 EPS at $5.83 (19 analysts) and FY26 at $5.13.
- The Bloomberg consensus of 10 Oct has $6.11 and $5.36.
- That is about +5% for FY27 over roughly six months. It fits the guidance raises of 30 Apr (EPS to $5.15–5.30) and 30 Jul (to $5.30–5.40).
- No dated June snapshot was found, so a 10 June step cannot be isolated.

**Reading.** Price targets did rise around and after 10 Jun. But the stock had already run, the one other June raise (Barclays) gives no survey rationale, and estimate drift matches the company's guidance raises. Nothing here shows revisions caused by the survey.
