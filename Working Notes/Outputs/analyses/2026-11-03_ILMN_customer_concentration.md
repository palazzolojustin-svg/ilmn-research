# Illumina customer concentration (3 Nov 2026)

## Answer

On disclosure, Illumina looks unconcentrated: no customer exceeded 10% of revenue in any year from 2020 to 2025, which caps every customer below about $434m on FY2025 revenue of $4,343m. Illumina publishes no top-customer table.

Economically, concentration is higher and probably rising, especially in clinical. Clinical is over 65% of sequencing consumables, and experts say roughly 10-20 labs drive most of it. Our rough estimate is that six large labs are about 5-10% of total revenue and 12-23% of clinical consumables. That estimate rests on an assumed cost share, not on disclosure.

## What is disclosed

- **10% test.** No customer above 10% of revenue in 2020-25 (10-Ks). The sources do not say whether distributors, or labs under one parent such as Foundation Medicine under Roche, are aggregated for this test.
- **New risk factor.** The FY2025 10-K says revenue is "increasingly derived from a small number of large, centralized laboratory customers," and warns of greater pricing pressure and reduced negotiating leverage. Our notes found that "centralized" language in no earlier 10-K.
- **Customer base.** About 1,000 high-throughput customers in Sep 2023: about 750 with one NovaSeq 6000 and about 250 with several (Morgan Stanley conference). Dhingra tracks the top ~30 customers per region (Evercore, Dec 2024).
- **Largest customers are growing faster.** Management projected in the Q3 2025 call that business with the largest customers would grow faster than the company average. Evidence since:
  - US/Canada clinical consumables grew above 20% in Q2 2026, against 15% for ex-China clinical overall.
  - Reported Q2 2026 test growth: Natera +22% overall, Guardant oncology +63%, Tempus oncology +31%, GeneDx exome/genome +32%.
- **Geography.** The US was 52% of FY2025 revenue. Greater China fell from $502m (11%) in 2021 to $243m (6%) in 2025. It is the closest thing to a 10% block, but it is a region spread across many customers.

## Sizing (our calculation)

No filing states what any named lab spends with Illumina. The only available proxy is each lab's cost of revenue, which also includes labor and other costs, annualized from Q2 2026. Tempus and Foundation Medicine are excluded because their cost of revenue is not in our files.

| Lab | Annualized cost of revenue |
|---|---|
| Natera | $1,066m |
| Guardant | $464m |
| Caris | $336m |
| GeneDx | $145m |
| BillionToOne | $126m |
| GRAIL | $93m |
| **Total** | **about $2,230m** |

Assuming a share of that cost goes to Illumina:

| Illumina share of lab cost | Implied spend | % of FY2025 revenue | % of clinical consumables (~$1.9bn) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10% | $223m | 5.1% | 11.7% |
| 20% | $446m | 10.3% | 23.3% |
| 30% | $669m | 15.4% | 35.0% |

The 10-20% rows are the most defensible. Illumina's Barnard put sequencing at 10-20% of cost in counting applications (AGBT 2025), but that is a workflow-cost figure, not a lab cost-of-revenue figure. Natera alone would need to spend over half of its FY2025 product cost ($811m) with Illumina to reach the $434m line, which is consistent with the 10-K.

Other data points:

- GeneDx is about 0.5% of Illumina's high-throughput output, on our earlier estimate.
- One unnamed Middle East population-genomics account implies about 1.2-1.4% of revenue, from an ex-employee's recollection.
- GRAIL is at most about 1.7% of revenue.

## Why it matters

- **Bargaining power.** Former employees say large accounts pay "half or even a quarter" of small accounts' prices, so their share of gigabases exceeds their share of revenue.
- **Weak contracts.** The supply contracts we could read (Natera, Caris, Guardant, Tempus) allow exit on 90 days' notice with no dollar minimums. The Natera and Caris contracts contain clauses under which price per Gb can only fall.
- **Offsetting stickiness.** Revalidating a clinical test on a new platform can cost $100,000 to tens of millions of dollars and take years.

## Conflicts and gaps

- Experts say the top 10-20 customers are "a very large proportion" of the business but give no percentage. A partial gigabase sizing (Caris, GeneDx and Natera MRD) found "a few percent" of the clinical base, but it omits several large labs. The two views do not reconcile.
- Concentration statements come from at most three ex-employees.
- The Tempus and GeneDx supplier figures (33% and 20% of purchases or payments to a single supplier) do not verifiably name Illumina.
- Natera may use some Element capacity; one expert said both things.
- Trend is inferred from growth rates. We have no direct measure. A customer at 10% of consumables growing 20%, against 6% for the company, gains about 1.3 points of share a year.
- A next step is the GRAIL FY2025 10-K related-party note, which may give an actual Illumina purchases figure.
