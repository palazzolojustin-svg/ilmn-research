# Customer channel checks: is Illumina's discounting accelerating? Full quotes (2026-11-09)

*Source: all 101 expert calls in `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/` (Jan–Oct 2026). Every quote below is copied in full from the original transcript; only page headers and bold markup were removed. Most calls are AlphaSense AI-interviewer "channel checks", which ask the customer what changed in the past 90 days, or in the October round, in Q3 versus Q2. Where the interviewer's question sets the timing, it is shown first. The rest are moderated academic-expert (KOL) interviews and buy-side-led calls.*

*Cross-references: core-lab rate cards (`2026-10-17_10B_core_discounting_tracker.md`, `2026-10-14_core_lab_dataset_2027_discounting_signals.md`) and Core Notes 17 (Open Offer contracts).*

## The short answer

The customer calls do not show broad, accelerating discounting. They show **targeted** price moves in two waves:

- **After Roche launched AXELIOS at AGBT (late Feb 2026).** Research cores describe X Plus price cuts, "steeper discounts", and kit pricing aimed at Ultima.
- **In Q3 2026.** Customers describe four things:
  - bigger trade-in credits for swapping a NovaSeq 6000 for a NovaSeq X;
  - 5B and 10B flow-cell kits repriced close to the 25B;
  - 10–15% incentives on the 25B;
  - free reagents inside collaborations.

Most customers still describe flat or rising net prices. February list increases run 3–10%, and tariff surcharges are on top. About 17 of 20 October checks said Q3 discounts were the same as Q2. Clinical labs show the least movement.

**The strongest customer quotes, in order:**
1. 1.6, academic lab manager: "steeper discounts".
2. 1.1, UCSD: X Plus price cuts.
3. 1.7, biotech: 30–40% on a NextSeq single-cell flow cell.
4. 2.3, academic medical center: 50% trade-in.
5. 1.11, core facility: Q3 trade-in "discount war".
6. 1.12, research core: 5B/10B kits cut toward the 25B.
7. 1.8, Vanderbilt: a bigger discount after threatening to switch.
8. 1.14, academic core: 10–15% on the 25B.

The strongest non-customer quotes are 3.1 (Complete Genomics: "Lately…") and 3.3 (ex-Illumina: price-matching, and a 30–40% fall in the blended price since 2022).

**Terms.**
- A **flow cell** is the consumable cartridge a run uses. NovaSeq X flow cells are named by billions of reads (1.5B, 5B, 10B, 25B, 35B), and the bigger ones are cheaper per read.
- **List price** is the published price; **net price** is what the customer pays after discounts.
- A **trade-in** is credit for returning an old instrument when buying a new one.
- A **core lab** is a university facility that sequences for many research groups.

## 5. How to read this

- **The timing does not show a clean acceleration.** Both waves are real but narrow: post-AGBT in Mar–Apr, then products and trade-ins in Q3. The October question that asks directly whether Q3 discounts changed from Q2 came back mostly "same". The rate-card tracker reached the same conclusion: a large 10B cut in the latest annual cycle, but no robust speed-up in the last two months.
- **Where the discounting sits matters for the model.** It clusters in two places:
  - mid-tier flow cells (5B, 10B and NextSeq single-cell), where Element and Roche compete;
  - 6000-to-X trade-ins.

  The 25B mostly held its list price, with 10–15% incentives at some cores. The trade-ins speed up the conversion, which is itself a large cut in revenue per gigabase, because on the core rate cards the 6000 S4 costs about twice the 25B per GB.
- **Most competitive pressure shows up as share loss, not Illumina price cuts.** Customers cite Ultima at about one-third of Illumina's list cost per run, Element below the NextSeq per GB, and Roche AXELIOS at about 50% savings. They more often move volume than win a concession.
- **What the calls cannot tell us.**
  - No customer had 35B pricing.
  - The large clinical labs (Natera, Guardant, GeneDx and others) are not interviewed directly; their pricing sits in contracts such as the Open Offer, which freezes big oncology labs' real prices.
  - Each AI check is a single snapshot, and several experts contradict themselves within a call. Both sides are shown above.
  - One expert first said discounts fell 10–15%, then retracted it; that answer is excluded.
