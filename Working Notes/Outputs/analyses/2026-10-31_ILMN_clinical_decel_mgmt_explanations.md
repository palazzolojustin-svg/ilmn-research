# How management explained the clinical slowdown (20% → 15% → 14%)

*2026-10-31. Sources: Q1, Q2 and Q3 2026 earnings call transcripts in `Sources/ILMN/earnings_call/`; sell-side recaps of Q2'26 (JPM 5 Aug, Ameriprise 31 Jul). No Illumina conference after March 2026 is in the folder.*

Clinical consumables ex-China grew 20% in Q4'25 and Q1'26, then 15% in Q2'26 and 14% in Q3'26.

Management has given only one specific reason, and only for Q2: Middle East and Latin America. For Q3 it offered no new reason. It called Q3 "very much like Q2" and pointed to strong US growth and new X placements.

Analysts asked twice about stocking orders, in Q2 (Ryskin) and Q3 (Peterson). Neither question got a direct answer.

## Q1'26 (30 Apr 2026): 20%, guide "mid-teens"

**Thaysen, Q&A (Subbu Nambi, Guggenheim), L464, L474:**

> "we're very pleased with the momentum we've seen in clinical, clearly over the last 2 quarters, but actually also through '25. And since we are, of course, transitioning more and more of the revenue on to the consumables business over to the X platform, the price headwind is getting behind us, and we continue to see that being strong growth."

> "So yes, at this point, we still feel like actually a mid-teens growth is a strong growth performance for the clinical market. Should it be even higher than that, that could be upside to our guide forecast."

## Q2'26 (30 Jul 2026): 20% → 15%

**Thaysen, prepared remarks, L76:**

> "Clinical markets, which represent approximately 65% of sequencing consumables revenue remained our primary growth driver. Rest of World clinical growth was broad-based across regions and applications with particularly strength in our U.S./Canada region. Strong instrument placements over the past 3 quarters are expanding customer capacity and will support consumable growth for many quarters to come."

**Dhingra, prepared remarks, L136:**

> "Sequencing consumables revenue in clinical markets grew 15% ex China, with the U.S./Canada region continuing to grow above 20%. We saw slower growth in Europe, Middle East and Latin America region, largely due to ongoing near-term dynamics in the region."

**Thaysen, Q&A (Tycho Peterson, Jefferies, asked about "the sequential slowdown in clinical consumables"), L256–260:**

> "And from the clinical performance, the momentum continues to be high. And as Ankur was mentioning, we continue to have strong confidence, and that's why we are raising our guide for the clinical consumable growth up to mid-teens, which is improvement from what we looked at in the beginning of the year.
>
> If you look into the details of the 20% growth we had last quarter down to 15% now. I mean, first of all, it's still very strong. But we still continue to see U.S. running faster than 20%. So that momentum is really strong. There's been some in and outs in -- especially in the Middle East and Latin America. I think we all know the situation in the Middle East, and that is impacting at least short term, the growth rate.
>
> So I wouldn't put more into it than that. But I think the headline here is that mid-teens growth is strong and is still beyond what we started the year with and is still very much driving high single-digit growth for us for next year towards that."

**Thaysen, Q&A (earlier answer), L228:**

> "So while we do see some moderations back up and down from each quarter, we still believe that the elevation will continue into second half of the year. So we feel good about that. But there will, of course, be a little bit ups and downs in that."

**Thaysen, Q&A (Dan Leonard, RBC, on breadth), L358:**

> "But the performance we have continues to be broad-based. Obviously, there are some ins and outs in this. But if you look at the regional level, as you mentioned, U.S. continues to be the main driver of the growth here. But all the other regions, except China, is still growing very nicely."

**Thaysen and Dhingra, Q&A (Mike Ryskin, BofA, asked about stocking and the slowdown), L322, L330.** Neither answered the stocking question.

> Thaysen: "And so instead of looking at a step down, I actually think that Q2 was very, very strong. And Q3 always is a little bit lower than Q4. So I think that's a normal phasing for us. So I'm not too worried about that."

> Dhingra: "Now going back to a slowdown comment. Overall, from our perspective, the business is holding very well. The consumables growth story is the one that's remained steady through the year. And the variability is almost always around the instrument placements."

**Sell-side readback (JPM 2Q26 recap, 5 Aug 2026, L22):**

> "+15% ROW, albeit down from the +20% trend seen the past two quarters, which was attributable to pockets of softness in the Middle East and Latin America in the quarter though U.S. clinical consumables grew >+20%."

## Q3'26 (29 Oct 2026): 15% → 14%

**Dhingra, prepared remarks, L57:**

> "End market growth rates for Rest of World sequencing consumables were similar in Q3 and Q2, with clinical growth of 14% and research and applied declines of 6%. We continue to expect mid-teens clinical growth and mid- to high single-digit declines in research and applied sequencing consumables for Rest of World for 2026."

**Thaysen, Q&A (Tycho Peterson, Jefferies: "any stocking orders to call out? You had that last quarter, obviously. And then 4Q implies, I think, low double-digit clinical growth"), L117.** Stocking orders were not addressed.

> "Thanks, Tycho. And first and foremost, we continue to be pleased with the overall clinical performance. We see ourselves for the full year in the mid-teens. We had here Q3 looked very much like Q2 with a 14%, 15% growth in clinical. And we continue this to -- expect that to continue. We will serve our customers, continue to serve them. And there are always puts and takes in the quarter, but we feel good about how we see also Q4 coming along."

**Dhingra, same exchange, L133:**

> "Tycho, to your question on the Q4. So overall, let's say, I'm raising the guidance for Q4 consumables. So our expectations there are going up given the strong placements that we continue to have. In terms of your growth rate question, I'll recall last year Q4 was a growth -- 11% growth rate quarter on the sequencing consumables. So we're still expecting a pretty good 5%, 6% growth in Q4 overall for the consumables. So that's still a good growth on top of one of the more unique compares throughout last year. So that would be one of the factors there. But our expectations are improving for Q4."

**Thaysen, Q&A (Luke Sergott, Barclays, on the exit rate), L219:**

> "As I mentioned before, the clinical market is very healthy. I know many of you want to sit and have a conversation about whether we grow 14%, 15% or 16% in clinical. What the takeaway is that this is a strong market. There is still a lot of application that is just coming online. So we feel good about that, and we see that, that has legs. If you just look at a great point here is the U.S./Can business that has continued to grow very, very strongly over the last 4 quarters in the clinical, and we don't think that will stop."

**Thaysen, Q&A (David Westenberg, Piper, on pricing and discounts to large clinical customers), L267–269:**

> "No. Thanks, Dave. That's very specific into where we could go pricing-wise… We will continue to make sure that both we can grow in alignment with our customers. There might always be some certain difference between the different growth rates. But what we are taking away is that when our customers are growing, we were also growing very healthy."

**Dhingra, same exchange, L275:**

> "within our U.S. business, the growth rate within that clinical is well above the 14% that you're talking about. So we're reasonably well indexed that way. And always remember, the big customers make up a reasonable part of our business, but Illumina's business is global… Within our U.S. business, that growth rate on the clinical is well above that 14%."

**Dhingra, prepared remarks, L79** (the X transition, not presented as an explanation for the slowdown):

> "In clinical, 78% of volume is currently on the X, and we have seen the pace of conversion slow in recent quarters… Certain clinical customers are keeping current on-market assays on the 6000 while adding X capacity for new tests."

## What to take from it

1. **Q2 has one stated cause: Europe, Middle East and Latin America.** The CFO said "near-term dynamics", and the CEO added "the situation in the Middle East". Neither gave a size. US/Canada was said to be above 20%.
2. **Q3 has no stated cause.** Management called it "very much like Q2". It did not repeat the Middle East explanation, and it did not say the region had recovered.
3. **Stocking orders were asked about twice and never answered.** Peterson's "You had that last quarter, obviously" implies Q2 had some. Management neither confirmed nor denied it.
4. **The defense is US growth plus X placements.** The US is "well above" 14%, and every X placement is described as a future revenue generator. Neither speaker quantified the US rate in Q3, and Q2's ">20%" was not repeated.
5. **Pricing is not on management's list of causes.** Asked about price concessions to large clinical customers, Thaysen declined ("very specific"). He conceded there "might always be some certain difference" between customers' growth and Illumina's.
