# Does the last ~15–20% of NovaSeq 6000 volume ever transition?

*Prepared 11 Oct 2026. Sources: ILMN earnings calls, conferences, decks and filings (2023–2026); the 23 expert calls; Core Notes 16 (clinical consumables addendum) and its evidence ledger; the 2026-10-11 clinical consumables scrape files 87, 95, 103, 107, 110, 113, 114 and 124. Labels: Fact = reported number or document; Mgmt = management claim; Opinion = analyst, expert or our judgement; Our calc = our arithmetic.*

## Short answer

There is a meaningful rebuttal, but only to one version of the claim. "The last 15–20% is permanent and keeps earning 6000 prices" is weak: the tail is already shrinking, by roughly 20–35% a year in units and volume. "15–20% will still be on the 6000 at end-2026 or end-2027" is strong, and nobody credibly rebuts it. Management's own target says so. Illumina still sells new 6000s. The last platform tail (HiSeq) took 6–8 years to disappear.

The "15–20%" figure is also partly an illusion of the share metric. At the clinical X growth rates Illumina reports, its 80–85% end-2026 target can be reached with almost no further conversion of 6000 volume, so the target itself says nothing about whether the tail is permanent.

## 1. Why the share figure misleads

- **The metric.** Illumina's conversion metric is the X's share of clinical HT Gb shipped. HT means high-throughput; Gb (gigabase) is a billion DNA letters, the unit of sequencing output. The share was 78% at Q2 2026, against a target of 80–85% by end-2026 (Q2 2026 call).
- **Dilution alone gets there.** *Our calc:* if X clinical volume grows ~14% in H2 2026 (about 30% a year) and 6000 volume stays flat, the X share rises to ~80%. Over a full year at +30% it reaches ~82%. So the low end of the target needs no conversion at all.
- **The recent past was real conversion.** *Our calc:* the X share rose from 64% (Q3 2025) to 78% (Q2 2026). Assume clinical Gb grew ~30–40% a year. Then implied clinical 6000 volume fell about 20–25% in three quarters, roughly 25–35% annualized. Some of that slowed recently: Q2 2026 added only 2 points of share.
- **Denominators get mixed up.** The famous "20%" came from Jefferies' Tycho Peterson on the Q4 2024 call, framed as "20% of tests will never move over to X". CEO Thaysen replied: "rule of thumb is probably around 20% will stay and they will, of course, become smaller, small over time." That figure is not a share of Gb, and it carries a built-in decay.

## 2. Evidence the tail is shrinking

**The fleet is being retired fast.**
- *Fact:* the active 6000 base fell from ~1,770 (end-2023) to ~1,700 (Q3 2024) to ~1,360 (end-FY25), with about 330 decommissions against ~30 placements in FY25. That is roughly 20% of the fleet in one year (Q4 2023 call; Evercore, Dec 2024; Q4 2025 deck).
- *Fact:* in Q1 2023 management had guided only "about 10 to 12 decommissions".

**6000 revenue is falling.**
- *Our calc, Addendum 16:* derived 6000 revenue went $252M, $234M, $225M and $219M per quarter (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026), down about 13% over three quarters.
- Per-unit pull-through (annual consumables revenue per installed machine) is flat at about $670–700K. The decline therefore comes from boxes being switched off, not from remaining boxes running less.
- *Our calc, E245:* the FY26 6000 pull-through guide of $600–650K implies H2 2026 6000 revenue of $138–200M a quarter, which already embeds a further unwind.

**Management's own framing is time-limited.**
- *Mgmt, Thaysen, Q2 2025 call:* customers stay "until the end of life, so to say, for the assay, where they feel like they maybe have 2 years more."
- *Mgmt, Thaysen, Q4 2025 call:* "there will be fewer and fewer 6000 customers over time."
- *Mgmt, Dhingra, Q4 2025 call:* "by the time we get to end of 2026, the 6000 transition should be mostly substantially done from a volume perspective."
- These describe assays ageing out, not a permanent installed tail.

**The regulated lock is smaller than implied.**
- *Opinion:* a former Illumina senior manager (Tegus, est. Jan 2026) said Illumina classifies clinical "very generous[ly]" and most volume runs "as LDTs". An LDT (lab-developed test) can be revalidated without FDA review.
- *Mgmt (customer):* Guardant moved its Guardant360 Liquid LDT to the X in May 2026, saving about $200 a test (Guardant Q2 2026 call).
- *Mgmt (customer):* Adaptive's FDA-cleared clonoSEQ MRD test credits "the NovaSeq X transition" for lifting sequencing gross margin to 72% from 64% (Adaptive Q2 2026). One expert disputed this, but Adaptive's own disclosure is the better source.
- *Fact:* FDA-approved tests do move. FoundationOne CDx went from the HiSeq 4000 to the 6000 through an approved supplement in about 11 months (openFDA, approved Apr 2024).

**The economics push labs to move.**
- *Mgmt:* the X is "easily more than 60% to 70%" cheaper per Gb.
- *Opinion (expert):* revalidation costs about $100–200K per test.
- *Our calc:* for a lab spending around $1M a year on 6000 reagents, the payback is months. That is a far stronger pull than the HiSeq-to-6000 step, where usage cost fell only ~20–45% (Bio-IT World, Jan 2017).

**Outside the clinic, the 6000 is already being retired.**
- *Fact:* Novogene ended all 6000 support in June 2025.
- *Fact:* Dana-Farber's core retired its 6000 in Oct 2024. A Georgia core director (Jul 2026) said "we are going to sunset" theirs.

**Illumina retires platforms on a set template.**
- *Fact:* MiSeq, MiniSeq and iSeq got an end-of-sale date of 30 Sep 2025 and end of service on 31 Dec 2029, announced Mar 2025.
- *Our calc:* a 6000 notice in 2027 would imply service ending around 2031–32. That is an eventual forcing event, but not soon.

## 3. Evidence the tail persists

**Illumina is still selling 6000s.**
- *Mgmt, Dhingra, Q2 2026 call:* "We also placed over 10 NovaSeq 6000 units as some consumers plan to remain on that platform for years to come."
- *Mgmt, Thaysen, same call:* it is "a standard part of the business" and not "any true headwinds".
- *Fact:* no 6000 end-of-sale notice or trade-in program exists.

**Some tests are locked to the 6000.**
- *Fact:* Caris MI Cancer Seek (an FDA-approved PMA, Nov 2024) names the NovaSeq 6000. It has had 10 supplements since, none moving it to another sequencer.
- *Fact:* we found no FDA-cleared or CE-marked NovaSeq X, and no FDA-approved sequencing test that names the X.
- *Opinion (expert):* moving an approved test is "incredibly hard."

**Management has said some customers will never move.**
- *Mgmt, Dhingra, Wolfe conference, Nov 2024:* "some of them, I believe, personally, will never move… We have customers who still use MiSeq for their clinical test. They haven't even moved to NextSeq."
- *Mgmt, Thaysen, Q1 2026 call:* "laggards… then there will be a long tail of transition going forward."

**The HiSeq precedent shows a long tail.**
- *Fact:* of ~850 HiSeq customers when the NovaSeq 6000 launched (Jan 2017), about 74% had not bought a 6000 by end-2018. Only "a little over 1/3" had started by end-2019.
- *Fact:* about 1,000 HiSeqs were still active at end-2020.
- *Mgmt:* Goswami (Nov 2023) said assays were still on HiSeq "six plus years" after the launch. Thaysen (Q2 2026) said HiSeqs were in "our portfolio" until "a few quarters ago", about 8–9 years after launch.
- *Our calc:* applying the same lag to the X (first shipped Q1 2023) puts the end of the 6000 tail around 2029–31.

**Remaining machines are not running less.** Implied 6000 pull-through has been flat for four quarters. Guggenheim's Subbu Nambi noted on the Q4 2025 call that it is "holding up a lot better than expected."

## 4. What it adds up to

*Our opinion.*

**What the evidence settles.** The tail is not static. Fleet retirements (~20% a year), implied clinical 6000 volume (down ~25–35% a year) and derived revenue (down ~13% in three quarters) all point the same way. This matches HiSeq's history of steady unit decay (~13–23% a year in 2019–20).

**What it does not support.** Neither the HiSeq precedent nor the FDA record supports the tail reaching zero by 2027. The realistic profile is a decaying tail: perhaps 15–20% of clinical volume at end-2026, falling to a high-single-digit or low-double-digit share by 2028, and largely gone around 2029–31. Regulated tests such as Caris's and an end-of-service date set the floor.

**Who is right.**
- Bears who say the remaining premium will unwind are right about the direction. The Addendum's base case is a ~$150–190M a year revenue unwind in 2027–28.
- The bull view that the tail stays and keeps earning 6000 prices holds only for the regulated core.
- Management's "no true headwind" is defensible only because the unwind is slow and partly already in guidance.

## 5. What would settle it

- **Q3 2026 print (29 Oct):**
  - derived 6000 revenue below ~$210M (decay on track) or at/above $219M (sticky);
  - the clinical X share rising past 79–80%;
  - whether the FY26 6000 pull-through guide is repeated.
- **Guardant360 Liquid CDx:** whether the FDA-approved version (May 2026) names the X or the 6000. If it names the X, a regulatory route exists and the regulated tail can move.
- **A 6000 end-of-sale notice.** This would start a roughly four-year clock to end of service.
- **35B pricing (H1 2027).** A deeper X price cut raises the payoff from revalidating the remaining tests.

## 6. Modeling note: is "5% of Gb converting a year" the right rate? (added 11 Oct 2026)

*Our calc throughout, on Q2 2026 shares: total HT 83% X / 17% 6000; clinical 78% / 22%.*

The answer depends entirely on the denominator.

| Reading of "5%" | What it implies for the 6000 base | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 5% of total HT Gb moves each year (absolute, at constant volume) | 5/17 = ~29% of the remaining 6000 base a year (clinical-only version: 5/22 = ~23%) | In line with the evidence |
| X share rises 5 points a year (e.g. clinical 78% to 83%) | With clinical Gb +30%, 6000 volume is flat in absolute terms (17% of 130 = 22) | Too slow: assumes almost no real conversion |
| 5% of the 6000's own volume a year | ~1 point of share | Far too slow |
| 5 points a quarter (the 2024 pace) | Tail would be gone by about 2027 | Too fast for a regulated tail |

**The evidence brackets a 20–35% annual decay of the remaining 6000 base:**
- fleet retirements of ~20% in FY25;
- implied clinical 6000 volume down ~25–35% annualized over the last three quarters;
- derived 6000 revenue down ~13% over three quarters;
- HiSeq units decaying ~13–23% a year in 2019–20;
- the midpoint of the 80–85% end-2026 guide, which implies ~25% a year.

**Recommendation.** Model the decay as a percentage of the remaining 6000 base, not as a constant number of points. A constant 5 points cannot run for more than 3–4 years, and the tail should slow as it narrows to regulated tests.
- **Base:** 25% a year.
- **Bull (sticky tail):** 15% a year.
- **Bear:** 35% a year.

At 25%, year one moves about 5.5 points of clinical volume (22 × 0.25), so it starts near the 5% figure and fades.

**Revenue mapping (Addendum E006/E413, constant Gb):**
- Each point of clinical Gb moving costs ~$24M a year; each point of total HT costs ~$36M.
- Five points of total HT a year is therefore ~$180M a year before any volume offset. That is consistent with the Addendum's ~$150–190M base-case unwind in 2027–28.
- Keep X volume growth as a separate line from conversion, so dilution is not counted as conversion.

## 7. Who holds the clinical 6000 tail, and why the sell side says the headwind "abates" (added 11 Oct 2026)

**Who holds it.** No source names the holders or splits the tail by lab size. The weight of evidence points to large accounts running legacy, validated or FDA-approved assays alongside the X, not mainly small labs.
- **Management's cohort description** *(Mgmt, Thaysen, Wolfe, 19 Nov 2025)*:
  - On the cohort that keeps current assays on the 6000 and puts new assays on the X, and the exome-to-genome cohort: "those 2 segments are absolutely the largest of our clinical segments."
  - On the low-volume labs staying on the 6000: "a smaller cohort of customers, at least volume-wise."
- **FDA-approved tests anchored to the 6000** *(Fact)*:
  - Caris MI Cancer Seek (approved Nov 2024) names the 6000.
  - FoundationOne CDx moved to the 6000 only in April 2024.
  - Neither has a supplement naming the X.
- **Signs of large labs running both machines:**
  - *Fact (anecdotal):* a Tempus job posting lists "Novaseq6000 and NovaX".
  - *Fact (anecdotal):* Natera postings name the NovaSeq and the X Plus.
  - *Fact (survey):* in J.P. Morgan's survey, 75% of 32 clinical respondents own a 6000 and 53% own an X, so 37–71% of 6000 owners also own an X.
  - *Opinion (competitor employee, hearsay):* a Roche lead said NHS-embedded labs typically run "a second Illumina sequencer."
- **J.P. Morgan's own wording concedes the point.** The note says the major CLIA labs "have transitioned (or are in the process of transitioning)". "In the process of" means they are still dual-running.
- **The tail is clinical.** *Our calc:* about 80–83% of the Gb still on the 6000 is clinical.
- **Our judgement of the split (low confidence):**
  - large-account legacy and regulated work: ~60–70% of clinical 6000 dollars;
  - small, low-volume labs: ~15–25%, with a bigger share of dollars than of Gb because they likely run small, pricier flow cells;
  - mid-size, international and government: the rest.
- **Price-inelastic either way.** Old validated assays generate no extra volume when they move. Converted tail volume therefore takes the full ~70% per-Gb cut, with no same-assay volume offset.

**Why the sell side says the headwind abates.** J.P. Morgan says transition pricing headwinds are "largely gone exiting this year." That statement is about the rate of change, not the level. The year-over-year drag equals the pace of conversion times the price gap.
- **Research has largely finished.** About 90% of research volume has converted, so research comparisons are clean.
- **Clinical is decelerating.** It needs only +2 to +7 points by end-2026, and the remaining tail is sticky, so fewer points convert each year and the annual drag shrinks. The stickiness is what makes the rate fall.
- **Offsetting volume is company-wide.** It does not have to come from the converting labs. *Our calc:* conversion alone needs only ~15% clinical Gb growth to hold 2027 clinical HT revenue flat, against ≥30% reported.
- **J.P. Morgan's survey** implies only ~4–5% annual price declines.

**Where that framing breaks.**
- **The stock remains.** *Our calc, Addendum:* about $500M a year of clinical revenue still earns 6000 prices.
- **If the tail decays as the evidence suggests (~25% a year):**
  - about 5.5 points of clinical Gb move in year one, at ~$24M a point, or ~$130M a year net at constant Gb;
  - adding the research residual gives the Addendum's ~$150–190M a year for 2027–28, about 4–6% of sequencing consumables.
- **"Headwind gone" and "tail converts" cannot both hold.** The headwind fades only if the tail stays put.
- **Regulated assays move in steps.** FDA supplements take about 11–15 months. The first FDA-approved test on the X, or a 6000 end-of-sale notice, could therefore re-accelerate conversion in 2027–29 rather than let it fade.
- **The 35B is a separate new price layer.** It is roughly a 20% per-Gb cut on the X in H1 2027, not counted as "transition", and no broker models it.
- **Offset depends on growth.** *Our calc:* the Addendum base case needs ~+27% clinical Gb in 2027 with a partial 35B cut and assumes +30%. If clinical Gb growth slows toward the mid-20s, the leftover conversion bites.

## 8. Remaining HT revenue on the 6000 by segment, Q2 2026 (added 11 Oct 2026)

**Disclosed:** X share of HT Gb is 83% total, 78% clinical and 92% research. X share of HT revenue is 59% total, so 41% of HT revenue is still on the 6000. Illumina gives no split of revenue by platform within clinical or research.

**Our calc.** We assume the same 6000-to-X price ratio in both segments (3.39x per Gb) and clinical at ~64% of HT Gb. Both are derived assumptions.

| Segment | 6000 share of segment's HT Gb | 6000 share of segment's HT revenue | Share of all 6000 revenue | ~$/quarter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical | 22% | ~49% | ~83% | ~$180M |
| Research and applied | 8% | ~23% | ~17% | ~$40M |
| Total HT | 17% | 41% (disclosed) | 100% | ~$219M |

These figures reconcile to the disclosed 41%. If clinical labs pay less per Gb than research labs (contract pricing), the clinical revenue share would be somewhat lower.

## 9. How big is the remaining headwind against what has already been absorbed? (added 11 Oct 2026)

*Our calc, from Addendum ledger E040: derived quarterly platform revenue, assuming HT is 69% of sequencing consumables.*

| $M per quarter | Q2'24 | Q3'24 | Q4'24 | Q1'25 | Q2'25 | Q3'25 | Q4'25 | Q1'26 | Q2'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NovaSeq X | 136 | 184 | 193 | 206 | 225 | 263 | 287 | 276 | 316 |
| NovaSeq 6000 | 367 | 327 | 289 | 274 | 286 | 252 | 234 | 225 | 219 |

Ledger E040's line that the "6000 y/y decline slowed to −$7M in Q2'26" does not match its own series. The series gives −$67M year on year in Q2'26 and −$81M in Q2'25.

**The 10 new 6000s are noise.**
- *Fact:* FY25 had ~30 placements against ~330 decommissions, on a base of ~1,360.
- Ten units is under 1% of the base.
- Management's own FY26 6000 pull-through guide ($600–650K) implies 6000 revenue falls to ~$138–200M a quarter in H2 2026, from $219M (E245). So guidance itself assumes the decline.

**The stock still exposed.**
- The 6000 brings in ~$219M a quarter, ~$880M annualized.
- That is ~41% of HT and ~28% of sequencing consumables.

**What has already been absorbed.**
- From Q2'24 to Q2'26, 6000 revenue fell $148M a quarter (~$590M annualized) while X revenue rose $180M a quarter. HT grew ~6%.
- In Q2'26 alone, the 6000 was down $67M year on year (~$270M annualized gross) and the X up $91M.

**What lies ahead.**
- 25% a year decay: a gross 6000 loss of ~$220M in year one; ~$150–190M net of volume re-landing on the X (Addendum base case).
- 35% a year: gross ~$310M.
- Either is about 5–8% of sequencing consumables a year. That is the same order of magnitude as the hit absorbed over the past year, not larger.

**Recent run-rate versus guide.**
- Sequential 6000 declines have narrowed: −$34M, −$18M, −$9M, −$6M a quarter. That signals stickiness, about −11% a year at the Q2 pace.
- The H2 2026 guide implies re-acceleration to about −$40M a quarter. The Q3 print tests which is right.

**Correction: the tail is denser, so each unit that converts hurts more (added after user challenge).**
- *Our calc:* index HT Gb at 100 in Q2'24 and ~180 in Q2'26 (~35% a year growth).
- Revenue per Gb:
  - Q2'24: 6000 $6.67, X $3.02 (2.2x). Each converted Gb lost ~$3.65.
  - Q2'26: 6000 $7.16, X $2.12 (3.4x). Each converted Gb loses ~$5.04, about 40% more.
- Per point of HT share, the cost is ~$9M a quarter (~$36M a year), about 2.5x the Q2'24 figure (~$3.6M a quarter). The gap is wider and the Gb base is bigger.
- **Stock at risk:** $219M a quarter × (1 − 1/3.4) ≈ $154M a quarter, about $620M a year at full conversion. That is ~20% of sequencing consumables.
- **What still holds:** at a 25–35% annual decay of the remaining base, the gross yearly 6000 loss (~$220–310M) is similar to the yearly pace absorbed in 2024–26 (~$295M a year average; ~$270M year on year in Q2'26).
- **What breaks it:** the comparison is only that benign because the tail is assumed to convert slowly. Revenue per point converted is ~2.5x higher. A faster or lumpier conversion (regulated tests moving, a 6000 end-of-sale notice, a broad 35B cut) would produce a larger yearly hit than anything absorbed so far.
- **The offset is larger too:** each 1% of X revenue growth is now ~$3.2M a quarter, against ~$1.4M in Q2'24.

**What decides it.**
- The question is not whether the 6000 declines but whether X volume keeps offsetting it.
- *Our calc (Addendum sensitivity 1, clinical HT revenue in 2027 at a −14.5% X price path):* clinical Gb +25% gives −1.4%; +30% gives +2.5%; +40% gives +10.4%.
- FY27 consensus (+8.8% sequencing consumables) needs the trailing ~40% clinical Gb growth to persist, or a narrow 35B cut.

## Caveats

- Shares are Gb shipped, not Gb sequenced, and are rounded to whole points.
- 6000 revenue and clinical 6000 volume are our derivations from rounded share data and assumed HT and clinical shares.
- HiSeq history comes from fetch summaries of 2018–2021 call transcripts (SEC and fool.com links in ledger E377 and E386). Sources disagree on whether "under half" or "a little over 1/3" of customers had started by end-2019.
- No source counts labs that own both a 6000 and an X. J.P. Morgan's survey suggests 37–71% of clinical 6000 owners also own an X.
