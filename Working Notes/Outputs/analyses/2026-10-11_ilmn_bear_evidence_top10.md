# Illumina: the 10 most compelling pieces of evidence for over-earning and a volume plateau

*As of 2026-10-11. Drawn from Core Notes file 16 (`16_Addendum_Clinical_Consumables_Thesis.md`); E-tags point to the evidence ledger in `Working Notes/Outputs/analyses/2026-10-11_clinical_consumables_scrape/00_evidence_ledger.md`. This is the bear side on purpose; each item carries its caveat, and the strongest counter-evidence is summarized at the end.*

## 1. The old machine earns 41% of the revenue on 17% of the volume (E001, E447, E002, E046)

**Context:** Illumina sells two high-throughput (HT) sequencers: the older NovaSeq 6000 and the newer NovaSeq X, which reads DNA far more cheaply per gigabase (Gb, a billion DNA letters). Illumina reports what share of HT Gb and of HT consumables revenue runs on the X, so the 6000's share is the remainder. Comparing the two shares shows how much more a 6000 Gb earns than an X Gb.

**What we found:**

- In Q2 2026 the X carried 83% of HT Gb shipped but only 59% of HT consumables revenue. The 6000 carried 17% of the Gb and 41% of the revenue.
- So a 6000 Gb earned about 3.4 times what an X Gb did (rounding band 3.2–3.6x). Put the other way, the X is about 70% cheaper per Gb.
- The premium widened from about 2.2x in 2024 to about 3.4x since Q3 2025, while the X's own revenue per Gb stayed stable. What is left on the 6000 is the high-priced tail.

**Why it's compelling:**

- These are Illumina's own disclosed numbers, not estimates.
- Every 6000 Gb that converts now loses about 70% of its revenue, against about 55% in 2024. The next conversions are the most expensive ones Illumina has faced.

**Caveat:** the shares measure Gb shipped, not Gb used, and are rounded to whole percentages. University price lists show a smaller gap (the X 34–60% cheaper per Gb), probably because the 6000 tail leans to small, pricier flow cells.

## 2. The tail that is left is clinical, and it is worth about $0.73B a year (E009, E186, E187, E104, E367)

**Context:** Illumina splits customers into clinical labs (patient testing) and research labs. It reports the X's share of volume for each group, but not its share of revenue, so the clinical dollars have to be derived.

**What we found:**

- Clinical labs still had 22% of their HT Gb on the 6000, against 8% for research labs, so about 80% of the remaining 6000 volume is clinical.
- At the same 3.4x gap, clinical 6000 revenue is about $182M a quarter, or about $0.73B a year. That is about 49% of clinical HT revenue earned from only 22% of clinical HT Gb. The X has only about 51% of clinical HT revenue, against about 77% in research.
- Priced at X rates, about $0.5B a year of that would vanish (about 23% of FY26 clinical consumables). Our base case unwinds about $190M of it in 2027 and a further $145M in 2028.
- If 6000 consumables earn the 80–85% gross margin we assume, the 6000 supplies roughly two-thirds of core operating income (operating profit excluding one-off items).

**Why it's compelling:**

- The over-earning sits in exactly the segment the bull case rests on.
- The profit at stake is far larger than the revenue share suggests.

**Caveat:** an equal price gap in clinical and research is our assumption, and Illumina does not disclose margins by machine. Tariff-adjusted consumables margin shows no erosion so far, so the profit figures are low confidence.

## 3. Guidance already needs the 6000 decline to speed up (E245, E040, E268, E038, E276)

**Context:** pull-through is Illumina's term for annual consumables revenue per installed machine. In February 2026 Illumina guided FY26 pull-through for each machine.

**What we found:**

- The 6000 guide ($600–650K a unit on about 1,200–1,300 average units) implies FY26 6000 revenue of $0.72–0.83B. With H1 at about $444M, H2 must run at $138–200M a quarter, against $219M in Q2. That is a decline of about $40M a quarter.
- But in Q2 2026 6000 revenue fell only $7M year on year, against $67M a year earlier.
- The X guide ($1.35–1.40M) needs H2 pull-through of about $1.46–1.55M annualized, 20–25% above the H1 run-rate of $1.19–1.24M.
- Neither pull-through guide has been repeated since February 2026.

**Why it's compelling:**

- Either the 6000 decline accelerates sharply in H2 (a revenue hit), or the X guide is quietly cut.
- The Q3 print on 29 October tests it directly.

**Caveat:** part of this decline is already in consensus. A slower 6000 decline would help near-term numbers, but it would also mean the over-earning lasts longer.

## 4. Three price cuts land in 2027 and already fill management's model (E247, E214, E058, E084, E305, E267, E047)

**Context:** management's long-term model has volume (Gb) growing in the mid-20s percent a year and price per Gb falling in the mid-teens, netting high-single-digit revenue growth. The 35B is the next X flow cell, the glass slide DNA is read on, with about 40% more output than today's largest.

**What we found:**

- Illumina's roadmap lists the 35B for H1 2027 with no price. A former Illumina associate director expects it to cut price per Gb about 20% (about $2.50 to $2.00).
- From 2027, tail conversion takes about 6–7% a year off sequencing consumables, the 35B about 4% and normal repricing about 4%. Together that already equals management's "mid-teens" decline, leaving no room for a price response to Roche or Ultima.
- To hold 2027 clinical HT revenue flat, clinical Gb must grow about 15% (conversion only), about 27% (our base case) or about 47% (a full 35B cut plus a mid-teens decline).
- The 35B alone is worth $63–253M a year of revenue, depending on how much of the X base it reaches.

**Why it's compelling:**

- The hurdle for volume rises just as the volume evidence (items 7–9) points down.

**Caveat:** the 20% cut is one former employee's estimate. If management's mid-teens already includes the 35B, our stack double-counts it.

## 5. The biggest customers' contracts only let price fall (E212, E192, E194, E232, E249, E256, E279, E286)

**Context:** large clinical labs buy under multi-year supply agreements, several filed as exhibits to their SEC filings. Many date from the "Open Offer", a standard contract Illumina offered cancer-test customers to win approval for its GRAIL deal.

**What we found:**

- Natera's March 2026 amendment says new product versions must keep a comparable inflation-adjusted "price per gigabase" (section 7(l)) and bars further inflation-adjusted price increases (7(k)). So the 35B cannot raise Natera's price per Gb.
- Natera's and Caris's terms include a most-favored-customer clause: prices no worse than any "Equivalent customer" or GRAIL gets. A better price elsewhere becomes a cash refund on units already bought.
- Caris's Open Offer required the highest-throughput flow cell to be at least 43% cheaper per Gb than the 2021 S4 price by 2025.
- Guardant, Caris and Tempus can leave on 90 days' notice with no purchase minimums. Hard commitments that could be Illumina purchases total at most about $53M across all customers checked, under 5% of clinical consumables.

**Why it's compelling:**

- Price cuts flow automatically to the largest accounts.
- Nothing in these contracts protects Illumina's revenue per Gb.

**Caveat:** several clauses are redacted, Natera's most-favored clause may have been replaced in 2026, and some exhibits are old (Guardant 2020, Tempus 2021).

## 6. Customers are concentrated, have leverage, and are shopping (E107, E113, E199, E400, E108, E114)

**Context:** clinical volume comes from roughly 10–20 large labs such as Natera, Guardant, Tempus and Caris. Concentrated buyers negotiate harder.

**What we found:**

- Illumina's FY25 10-K adds a new warning: revenue is "increasingly derived from a small number of large, centralized laboratory customers", which "may result in greater pricing pressure, reduced negotiating leverage". The word "centralized" appears in no earlier 10-K.
- Former employees say large accounts pay "half or even a quarter" of small accounts' prices, and that contracts "can quickly become null and void" when a rival undercuts by 25–50%.
- Caris, which runs more than 50 NovaSeqs, says "two competitors… create the opportunity to increase our capacity and decrease our cost of goods simultaneously", while it prepares to triple capacity.
- Labcorp, Quest, Myriad and NeoGenomics all work with Ultima on cancer-relapse (MRD) testing.

**Why it's compelling:**

- The new capacity that is supposed to deliver volume growth may be dual-sourced, or used to extract price.

**Caveat:** none of the nine largest US clinical labs we checked runs a rival sequencer in production, and moving an approved test takes years.

## 7. Test growth overstates data growth, because the fastest-growing tests use the least sequencing (E432, E081, E099, E271, E088, E096)

**Context:** Illumina sells Gb, not tests. A test's data use (Gb per test) decides how much test growth turns into Illumina revenue.

**What we found:**

- Natera's MRD tests (repeated blood tests that look for traces of cancer after treatment) grew 56% in Q2 2026. But a classic Signatera draw uses about 0.16 Gb, against more than 100 Gb for a whole genome. Across a patient-year, the one-off tumor exome carries 80–90% of the Gb.
- Roche's MRD design monitors patients with digital PCR, a cheaper test that needs no sequencing.
- Test volumes at the named labs (Natera, Guardant, GeneDx, Caris, Adaptive and BillionToOne) explain only about 7–13% of top-down clinical Gb.
- Experts say prenatal screening (NIPT) already reaches about 70–80% of pregnancies and tumor profiling (CGP) is "quite saturated".

**Why it's compelling:**

- Headline volume growth at customers does not translate one-for-one into Illumina Gb.

**Caveat:** genome-based MRD uses 10–15 times more data per draw (about 2.2–3.4 Gb), and labs are spending savings on deeper tests, so intensity could rise.

## 8. Forward-looking volume evidence is far below the trailing 40% (E067, E083, E263, E402, E403, E414, E416)

**Context:** backing volume out of reported revenue implies clinical Gb grew about 42% a year over 2025 to mid-2026. The question is whether that continues.

**What we found:**

- In JPM's survey, 32 clinical labs planned volume growth of +14.6% in 2027 and +19.1% in 2028.
- Our test-by-test model first gave +23% a year (about +31% re-run on Q2 2026 prints).
- Adjacent suppliers grew much slower: Twist's sequencing products +12%, Azenta's multiomics +8% organically, and Qiagen's panels for other companies' sequencers just over 20%.
- Management itself models the whole business at about 25% volume growth long-term, and clinical at "above 30%".

**Why it's compelling:**

- Every forward-looking signal sits below the trailing rate.
- Our base case (+30%) clears its own +27% break-even hurdle by only about 3 points.

**Caveat:** the survey is small and self-reported, and a similar model under-predicted Q2 2026 (+1% predicted against +6.8% actual).

## 9. Growth is already slowing, and history says volume does not fully turn into revenue (E073, E074, E306, E189, E375, E351)

**Context:** in past transitions, cheaper sequencing brought more volume but less revenue per Gb.

**What we found:**

- Clinical consumables growth outside China slowed from +20% (Q4 2025 and Q1 2026) to +15% (Q2 2026).
- The Q3 guide is about 4.5% organic growth outside China, against 8.1% in Q2.
- Two-year stacks (each quarter's growth plus the prior year's) imply Q4 clinical growth of only about 5–8% against a tough +20% comparison.
- In the last transition (HiSeq to NovaSeq), data rose 50% in 2019 while sequencing consumables grew 14% (about 24% lower revenue per Gb), against guidance of "above 20%".
- Novogene, a large sequencing service company, grew output 33% in FY25 while its sequencing revenue grew 0.6%.

**Why it's compelling:**

- Both the current trend and the precedent point to decelerating volume, with price absorbing much of what remains.

**Caveat:** the Q3 guide covers all products, Q4 has an extra week, and clinical consumables grew right through the heaviest conversion.

## 10. Management stopped giving the numbers that would settle the question (E204, E207, E208, E276, E277, E014, E209, E338, E392, E036)

**Context:** a company confident in a transition usually keeps disclosing the metrics that prove it.

**What we found:**

- The volume share is Gb shipped (flow-cell capacity sold), not Gb sequenced, and is never reconciled with the separate ">30%" growth metric.
- The ">30%" metric widened from high- to high-and-mid-throughput machines in Q3 2025, excludes China, and is given only as a floor.
- The clinical-versus-research activity split was last given in Q2 2023, and the FY26 pull-through guide has not been repeated since February 2026. Q2 2026 said pull-through "increased" with no number.
- Asked directly about over-earning on the 6000 (Jefferies, Q2 2026), the CEO gave no 6000 revenue, decommission pace or customer count. Asked about the price hit at converting labs (Leerink, Q1 2026), the CFO said it was "built into the guide", with no number.
- The CFO's "91% of HT volume has transitioned" was the research figure. The total was 78%.
- Only the Q1 2025 10-Q blamed "reduced pricing". The FY24 10-K sentence saying other HT consumables decrease "as customers transition to NovaSeq X" is gone from the FY25 10-K.

**Why it's compelling:**

- Disclosure fades exactly where the tail begins.
- The two questions closest to this thesis got no numbers.

**Caveat:** the audit opinion is clean, nothing has been restated, and the SEC has never questioned Illumina's metrics. The "91%" looks like a slip, not deception.

## Why it matters now, and the other side

**The Street does not model any of this.** Bloomberg consensus has sequencing consumables up 8.8% in 2027 and 9.4% in 2028, which implies clinical growth of about 13–14%, against our base case of about +4%. No broker note has a 6000 revenue line, a price-per-Gb path or a 35B assumption, and the stock is about 3.5 times its May 2025 price (E080, E411, E171).

**The strongest counter-evidence:** clinical Gb has grown about 42% a year, comfortably beating price so far. The regulated tail is slow (no FDA-approved test has moved to the X), so the hit is spread out. Tariff-adjusted consumables margin has not eroded. And our models under-predicted Q2 2026, so they may lean bearish (E205, E287, E366, E446).
