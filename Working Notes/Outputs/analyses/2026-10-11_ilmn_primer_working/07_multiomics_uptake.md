# 07. Multiomics uptake: does the evidence support "1-2 points" of 2027 growth?

Prepared 2026-10-11 for the ILMN buy-side primer. Tags: **[D]** disclosed by Illumina (filings, slides, calls); **[MGMT]** management statement; **[E: source]** Core Notes/Extracts/sell-side; **[X]** expert-call claim (unverified); **[W]** web, with URL; **[Der]** my calculation, shown; **[A]** my assumption (not evidence). Dollar figures are $M unless stated.

## 0. Bottom line

The bridge is **neither proven nor obviously a plug**. Only two pieces can be sized from disclosures: SomaLogic's own growth and the BioInsight/Billion Cell Atlas services line. Together they support about **0.7 point** (about $31M) of 2027 growth in my base case. The other pieces (Protein Prep kits, single cell, spatial, TruPath, 5-base, software) are not disclosed anywhere; they sit inside consumables lines that Illumina does not break out, so the other ~0.6 point in my base case is assumption. My three scenarios give **$21M / $59M / $134M** of incremental 2027 revenue, or **0.45 / 1.3 / 2.9 points** on a $4.62B 2026 base, against management's $46-92M (1-2 points). Reaching even 1 point requires the base case, and reaching 2 points requires everything to go right at once.

Consensus does not need the full claim. Bloomberg 2027 revenue growth is 6.3% ($4,918M vs $4,625M) [E: Core Notes 03], which is below the "high single digit" target. If the core business grows about 5% organically, consensus needs only about 1-1.5 points from new products. The full 1-2 points matters mainly for the claim that 2027 reaches 7-9%.

### 2.3 Run-rate

- Full-quarter Q1'26 on an as-if basis: $17.7M + $8M = about **$25.7M**; Q2'26: **$22.2M**. [Der]
- H1'26 about **$48M** vs H1'25 **$36M**: about **+33%** like-for-like [Der]. But H1'25 was an easy comparison (Q1'25 was down 14%), and H2'25 is a hard one ($60.5M gross in Q3+Q4 on the Standard BioTools basis; about $53M after an assumed 12% elimination haircut) [Der/A].
- Annualized Q2: about **$89M**; H1 doubled: about $96M.
- Check against the guide: FY26 acquisition contribution was guided at 1.5-2.0 points [D: Q4'25 and Q1'26 guidance], on FY25 revenue of $4,343M, which is $65-87M [Der]. H1 delivered $39.9M, so H2 needs $25-47M. At the Q2 run-rate (about $22M) H2 gives about $44M and FY26 about **$84M (1.9 points)**, at the top of the range. Management says SomaLogic is "tracking toward the high end of deal expectations" (Q2 call) and Barclays says "trending towards the high end" [E: Barclays 2026-08-06]. Consistent.
- Implication for growth: the guide implies full-year Soma (Jan 30 to year-end) of $73-95M including the stub, versus about $89M for FY25 on an eliminated basis. So FY26 Soma growth is guided near flat to mid-single digits on a full-year basis, even though H1 looked like +33%. The H1 optics flatter growth; H2 comparisons are tough.

### 2.4 Milestones: what thresholds do they imply?

- The 2025 milestone ($25M) was paid in full, April 2026 [D: Q1'26 10-Q]. The thresholds are **not public**: the merger agreement exhibit is redacted and the 8-K says only "specified targets for net revenue" from SomaScan assay services, SOMAmer array kits and SOMAmer NGS library prep kits [W: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1162194/000110465925061438/tm2518613d1_8k.htm]. FY25 Business revenue of about $97.5M (before eliminations) at least met the 2025 threshold, but that sets only a floor.
- Contingent liabilities [D: 10-Qs]: $81M at the deal date (max $75M earn-outs plus the royalty); $84M at 29 Mar 2026, which is $59M after the $25M 2025 payment; $37M at 28 Jun 2026; then **$30M paid 27 Jul 2026** to buy out the 2026 milestone (max $50M) and the 2% NGS-kit royalty, and to terminate the single-SOMAmer licence and royalty [D; W: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001162194/000114036126029841/ef20078901_8k.htm].
- [Der] reading, low confidence: the March valuation of about $59M for the 2026 milestone plus royalties suggests Illumina's Monte Carlo then expected the 2026 target to be hit or nearly hit. The fall to $37M reflects the negotiated buyout scenario (Standard BioTools wanted cash for its pending Treeline merger), not necessarily a miss. I cannot back out a revenue threshold from these numbers; the buyout is a $30M one-time cost that removes the 10-year 2% royalty (a drag of about 2% on NGS-kit revenue), a small positive for margin.

### 2.5 Is SomaLogic inside the "1-2 points"?

- In 2026, **no**. The guide lists "SomaLogic +1.5 to 2 points" as an acquisition line, separate from organic growth, which "excludes currency and SomaLogic" [D: Q4'25 call]. The 2024-vintage 1-2 points predates the deal.
- In 2027, **it is ambiguous, and it matters**. SomaLogic enters the organic base from Feb 2027 (anniversary of 30 Jan 2027). Jan 4-29 2027 (about $7M) will be reported as acquired [Der/A]. From Feb 2027, SomaLogic's growth is organic, and any growth it produces counts toward the 7-9% target regardless of how management labels it.
- Size of that effect [Der]: Q2'26 pro forma growth was 1,159/1,076 = +7.7%; less about 0.9 points of currency gives about 6.8% like-for-like, versus 6.5% organic excluding SomaLogic. So SomaLogic's own growth added only about 0.3 point to Q2 growth even at roughly +30%. At FY26 post-close revenue of about $88M, growth of +12% adds about $11M (0.2 point); +25% adds $22M (0.5 point).

Conclusion: SomaLogic's organic growth is worth **0.1-0.5 point** of 2027 growth, i.e. at most a quarter of the 1-2 point claim. The "1-2 points" therefore depends mostly on products that are not SomaLogic.

- [Der] Isolating BioInsight: management said in Aug 2024 that services "already grow mid-single digits" [E: Extracts]. At 5% on $136M, baseline growth is about $7M in Q2, so the excess is about **$11M in Q2** and about $3M in Q1 (about $14M in H1). So **$11M is an upper bound for BioInsight in Q2**, and the lower bound is perhaps $4-5M [A].
- Statements: Q1'26 call says BioInsight service revenue "can be lumpy"; Q2'26 says revenue booking has started, 300M+ cells delivered (from "hundreds of millions generated" in April), six pharma partners (three added after quarter-end; AstraZeneca, Merck and Lilly were the original three) and "making money from day 1 on the top line and bottom line" [MGMT, E: Core Notes 05]. No revenue figure or AI revenue assumption was given. The target is one billion cells, so about 700M remain to be delivered.
- Sizing 2027 [A]: Q2 run-rate excess of up to $11M a quarter implies $20-45M a year if sustained. Three new partners add a full year of revenue in 2027. But deliveries are project-based and lumpy, and the atlas is a defined-size program. I use incremental 2027 BioInsight revenue of **$8M low / $20M base / $40M high** over 2026.
- Sell-side: Freedom Broker says revenue "will likely stay uneven because partnership agreements are project-based" and makes only "modest" revisions [E: Freedom 2026-08-05].

## 5. Expert evidence (the dollar value of uptake is essentially unobserved)

From my reads of 02a/02b (21 calls; BioInsight and the Billion Cell Atlas are not mentioned by name in any) and Core Notes 07:

- **No call reports an actual SomaScan or SomaSeq purchase.** Interest only: academic biotech head "potentially interested" while on Olink; small core says price per sample is too high and it "does not make us use more Illumina"; ex-insider: "Illumina is not able to win in that proteomics domain" vs Olink ("a mountain and a sea level" on data quality) [X: 02a calls 7, 8; 02b calls 2, 5, 6, 9]. A clinical genomics user said it is "absolutely the right strategy" but NGS protein detection in the clinic is "quite some time" away [X: 02b call 9].
- **TruPath:** adoption guesses of 1-2% of volume (largest academic genome center), 5-6% (one core, by end 2026, "very likely"), and "not at all likely" (two cores); described as "limited because of price" and "an expensive, different version of Illumina sequencing" [X: 02b calls 2, 4, 5]. An Australian clinical lab is "super-excited" but notes it needs long DNA and not FFPE [X: 02a].
- **Single cell:** demand "exploded," driven by cheaper competitors (10x Flex, Singleron "almost 5X cheaper than 10x") rather than Illumina's kit; PIPseq is "under evaluation" at one large center with "potential competitive advantage in some scenarios" [X]. One core reports its Illumina-instrument output up about 40% year over year in Q2'26, "mainly due to single-cell applications": a sequencing pull-through signal, not an Illumina kit signal [X: 02b call 2].
- **Spatial:** "Not the Illumina one" (choose 10x or BGI); Illumina "not great" so far; the ex-insider puts Illumina's spatial-driven sequencing revenue at "not more than maybe low eight figures," i.e. under about $30M, in total, today, and true spatial as "probably not even a $500 million market" [X: 02a call 4/6]. He also recalls Illumina telling investors these products add "hundreds of millions of dollars over about five years" [X, recollection].
- **Strategy read:** multiomics is "a short-term strategy? 100%": exists because genomics hit sample saturation [X: 02b call 8]; purpose is lock-in (products run only on Illumina sequencers) rather than standalone revenue [X: 02a call 4].
- Caveat: these are mostly academic cores, the weakest end market (research consumables -7%), and several are AI-interviewer channel checks. They are not a sample of pharma, which is where BioInsight sells.

Notes on the assumption rows:

- **(a) Protein Prep pull-through [Der/A].** A 25B flow cell yields about 25B reads over 8 lanes (about 3.1B per lane). Core pricing is under about $2,000 a lane [X: 02b call 2], about $0.64 per million reads. At an assumed 10-20M reads per protein sample, sequencing is only about $6-13 a sample. So Protein Prep "lifting X consumables" is real but immaterial; the revenue is the kit and the assay.
- **(b) Single cell pull-through.** 10,000 cells at 50,000 reads is about 500M reads, about $320 of sequencing at core pricing [Der/A]. Pull-through is meaningful per experiment but most single-cell sequencing is run for 10x, Parse and Singleron customers, so Illumina earns it with or without its own kit; it is not incremental to the new product.
- **(c) TruPath [A].** List $395 per genome includes the flow cell; the X cut cost per sample from about $600 to about $200 [E: Core Notes 06], so a conventional X genome plus library prep is likely roughly $300. If 30-40% of TruPath revenue is genuinely incremental and the rest replaces standard WGS flow cells and prep, then 50k / 150k / 300k genomes ($20M / $59M / $119M gross) give $3-8M / $8M / $22M net (assumes about 15-25%, 25-30% and 35-40% incremental respectively). The genome counts are my assumptions; early-access names (GeneDx, Rady, Baylor) suggest rare-disease clinical volume, but experts see 1-6% of volume at the cores they know.

### Reading the result

1. **Visible evidence supports about 0.7 point at base** (Soma organic plus BioInsight, $31M), and that evidence itself is extrapolated from one quarter (services) and a rebased acquisition (Soma).
2. **The 2026 test is the better check.** If the 1-2 points were already happening in 2026 ("playing out as expected"), H1'26 should show about $21-42M per half. The only visible new-product signal is the services excess of about $14M in H1 (about 0.7% of H1 revenue). Everything else would sit in consumables, where research and applied consumables fell 7% and clinical rose 15% on test volume. So consumables cannot confirm or refute the claim, which is why Barclays "cannot verify" it.
3. **RoW organic growth in 2026 beat guidance (Q2 8.1%, FY now above 5%) because of clinical consumables and X instruments**, not new products [E: Q2'26 call]. Guidance for research consumables was unchanged.
4. **Cannibalization.** TruPath and 5-base partly replace standard WGS flow cells and library prep, and methylation arrays; net numbers above reflect that. In contrast SomaSeq/Protein Prep and spatial/single-cell are additive to sequencing consumption but their sequencing pull-through is small (Notes a, b). Experts also note some single-cell demand is migrating to imaging spatial that "does not use sequencing" [X: 02b call 11], a headwind for sequencing pull-through.
5. **Margin point:** SomaLogic revenue is lower margin (a 100 bp operating margin drag and $0.18 EPS dilution in 2026 [D]), so even where the revenue arrives it does not help margins; the 26% operating margin goal is described by BI as "an open question."

### What consensus needs [Der]

- 2027E revenue $4,918M vs $4,625M: **+6.3% reported**.
- Adjustments to get to organic: FY26 includes a 53rd week worth roughly 0.5 point (CFO: "perhaps 0.5 point") [E: Core Notes 05], so FY27 faces about -0.5 point; Soma's pre-anniversary January adds about +0.15 point (about $7M). Organic-equivalent growth is therefore about **6.6%**.
- If core Illumina (clinical, X and research ex-new products) grows about 5% (H2'26 guide is about 4.5% RoW organic in Q3; FY26 is above 5%), consensus needs about **1.5 points** from new products. If the X cohort ramp lifts core to 6-6.5% ("most of the benefit in 2027" per CFO), consensus needs only 0.1-0.6 point.
- To reach management's "high single digit" (7-9%), the sum of core plus new products must be 7-9%. With core at 5.5-6.5% and new products 1-2 points, the range is 6.5-8.5%. So the 1-2 points is not purely a plug, but it fills the gap between a roughly 6% core and the 7-9% target. On my base (1.3 points) and a core of about 6%, the answer is **about 7.3%, at the low end of "high single digit"**. On my low case it is about 6.5%, which is consensus.

## 8. Verdict and watch list

**Verdict.** The new-products figure looks like a **reasonable but unevidenced assumption, not a demonstrated number**. About half of what I can justify rests on two disclosed pieces (SomaLogic growth, BioInsight services); the rest is invisible inside consumables. It is "plug-like" only in the sense that the high-single-digit target needs roughly 1-2 points on top of a core running 5-6%, and management has chosen not to break the number out. Consensus (6.3%) does not rely on the high end.

**Evidence gaps (data a buyer should ask for):**

1. Whether SomaLogic's post-anniversary growth is inside the "1-2 points."
2. A quarterly BioInsight revenue figure, or sequencing service growth excluding BioInsight and AGD.
3. Any revenue, unit or sample-count disclosure for SomaSeq, TruPath (genomes), StrataMap and the single-cell kits.
4. Whether pull-through on new-product sequencing is counted in the 1-2 points.

**Watch list:** Q3'26 results (late Oct/early Nov 2026): services growth ex-BioInsight, microarray line (Soma run-rate vs the $22M), any new Billion Cell Atlas delivery count; ASHG 2026; the FY27 guide in Feb 2027, which will be the first time Illumina sets a number for new products as part of formal guidance.
