# 02a. Expert calls, Jul 2026 (12 AlphaSense transcripts): findings for the ILMN primer

Prepared 2026-10-11. Source folder: `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/expert_call/` (extracts in `Working Notes/Extracts/ILMN/expert_call/`). All 12 originals were read in full.

Convention: every claim below is **[X]**, meaning an expert's claim, not a verified fact. Lines tagged **[Note]** are my own reading of how a claim fits the primer. Question tags: Q1 clinical volume (Gb) growth, Q2 over-earning and pricing, Q3 multiomics uptake, Q4 competition/switching/China, Q5 variance to Street.

## Read this first: what the 12 calls can and cannot tell you

- **None of the 12 experts gives a clinical gigabase growth rate.** The closest are one small biotech (+25-50% Gb, not clinical), one academic core (flat to +5-10% on an Element machine), and the ex-Illumina strategy manager's reasoning that price per Gb has fallen as fast as volume has risen since 2022. Q1 therefore rests on indirect evidence.
- **Calls 4 and 6 are very likely the same person.** Both are "Former Associate Director, Product Management, Multi-omic and Multi-modal Assays", both left Illumina July 2025, the bio text is near-identical, and the interviews are one day apart (23 and 24 Jul). Treat as one source with two sessions, not two independent votes. Their statements are consistent with each other.
- **Only three experts are close to Illumina's clinical customers**: Natera (call 1), Exact Sciences/ex-Illumina bioinformatician (call 5), and the Roche UK lead (call 12, NHS view). The rest are academic, research, agricultural, or software voices.
- **Experts mean different things by "market share"** (instrument installs, Gb, revenue, new placements). Share numbers below are not comparable until the metric is pinned down.
- Calls 4/6/7 and 1/12 are the most decision-relevant. Calls 10 and 11 (AI) have almost nothing on pricing, volume or share.

## (a) Call-by-call

### Call 1. Associate Director, Oncology Lifecycle Management, Natera (22 Jul 2026; published 24 Jul)
**Credibility.** Current employee of Natera, a large Illumina clinical customer (MRD, "the leader in ctDNA"). Physician by training, 4.5 years at Natera. Direct knowledge: MRD economics, Natera's use of Illumina, a 6-month stint on Element's benchtop at a Boston start-up. Reading/conference knowledge only: Ultima, Roche. Admits he is "biased for MRD" on deep-sequencing needs. Weak on Illumina diversification (had to be prompted on Fluent; cited the "Q1 call" loosely). Wrong or loose on some facts (see section c).

- (Q4, Q1) [X] For 20+ years there was no serious short-read rival; "This recently has changed. It has changed a lot." He names Ultima (cost), Element VITARI ("almost equal with NovaSeq X") and Roche AXELIOS ("the main disruptor") as the challengers.
- (Q4, Q5) [X] Competitors "haven't infiltrated so much" yet. He expects Illumina to lose share gradually: "It's not going to be in six months, they will lose 30%." The barriers to bypass Illumina "are very, very high".
- (Q2, Q4) [X] Ultima pricing: "$80-$110" per sample for low-depth uses (oncology screening, fetal, women's health), Solaris chemistry about 50% less DNA input and 30% lower cost, cost per million reads "below $0.40", "somewhere $50 genome". The UG200 is priced "comparable with Illumina", so no reason to use it. These are recollections of a review, not quotes from price lists.
- (Q2, Q4) [X] Element: VITARI instrument about $700,000 against "more than $1 million" for NovaSeq X; 50X genome "somewhere over $70". AVITI benchtop "somewhere over $100 per genome". Element cannot do CGP (needs 150X or more plus RNA) or tissue-informed MRD (80-120X or more); "you cannot run Signatera or ctDNA with 50X".
- (Q4, Q1) [X] Why MRD players stay on Illumina: MRD-guided trials last 5-7 years and "you cannot switch an assay or a sequencer middle of a trial. It's a no-no." In the US "the big four, plus Natera, they're just using Illumina". Established assays rarely switch; new labs, start-ups, diversified portfolios and Europe (no reimbursement, local labs "can do whatever they would like") are where switching happens.
- (Q4, Q2) [X] Stickiness extras: used Illumina machines are plentiful in the aftermarket and some buyers still prefer them after "five, seven years" of use. Running one commercial test on two sequencers is unattractive and "FDA is not going to love it".
- (Q1) [X] MRD market about $5B now, expected $30B by 2030 (consultant projections $30-50B); NIPT, transplant, infectious disease and regional labs together "more than $40 billion". This is testing revenue, not sequencing Gb. He notes the industry is moving MRD from whole-exome to whole-genome ("the future"), which raises Gb per test.
- (Q4) [X] Roche SBX: RUO only at launch (end June 2026), priced about $750,000, consumables claimed reusable "up to 20 times or up to three months", four-hour deep run claimed on 150 ng input that is "nearly impossible from a real patient". He is "not fully convinced that it will radically change the market"; a company running a "multi-billion-dollar business on sequencing" will not switch on a brand name alone.
- (Q4) [X] Long read is niche in commercial terms (repeat expansions, HLA, fusions, structural variants where short reads miss "almost 1/3"); nobody in big MRD is "brave enough" to use it yet.
- (Q3) [X] Illumina is "not great" in spatial and single cell so far, but "will definitely have to" be there.

### Call 2. Manager, Genomic Research Services, University of Arizona Genetics Core (22 Jul 2026; published 24 Jul)
**Credibility.** Current academic core facility manager, an Element AVITI user (primary short-read) with one Illumina NextSeq 550 for methylation arrays; a sister facility on campus has NovaSeq 6000/X. First-hand Q2 2026 operating data, but a single small academic site, no clinical exposure, and an AI interviewer ("channel check"). Illumina views are mostly second-hand except pricing and support.

- (Q1) [X] Q2 2026 Element output "about the same", then "probably about a 5% or 10% increase" versus Q2 2025; full-year 2026 roughly flat; his own growth target is 10-20%. Spare capacity remains.
- (Q1, Q5) [X] Volume headwinds are research funding stress (researchers "much more cost sensitive", postponing studies) and for-profit CROs taking standard workflows ("greatly reduced our sequencing volume").
- (Q2, Q4) [X] "The Element consumables do not change in cost" through the product life cycle; he sees that as a competitive advantage over Illumina because he passes kit costs to users and Illumina's "change frequently and go up exclusively". He sees price increases on library prep, array kits and sequencing reagents from other suppliers, and little discounting for a facility like his.
- (Q4, Q2) [X] Share view: Illumina's highest-output platforms (NovaSeq X) are gaining; lower-output Illumina platforms lose because "cost per base is no longer competitive". Element and Ultima awareness rising. He expects Roche AXELIOS to pressure Illumina "in the next six months", but "minor at first"; Illumina "has quite a stable share".
- (Q3) [X] TruPath Genome: "Not at all likely" to use ("most of the genome work has migrated away from facilities like ours"). SomaLogic "improves Illumina's value proposition", but would not make him use more Illumina (the sister facility would handle it).
- (Q5) [X] Capital is "as low as it has ever been": a machine over $500,000 is "much less feasible" for small cores; upgrade paths and creative access would be valued. No equipment purchase planned in 12 months.
- (Q5) [X] Illumina support is "great"; the only complaint is it was "very hard to keep up with their tech cycle from a capital equipment standpoint".

**Credibility.** Customer-side, but left the University of Auckland core in **January 2021**, so his operating detail is 5+ years old even though he speaks in the present tense. New Zealand, research and agriculture, no diagnostics exposure. Hands-on with Illumina, Nanopore and MGI. Pre-revenue company now.

- (Q4) [X] Illumina is "the gold standard"; "Almost it's becoming like Kleenex". Absent a disruptor, "for the next five years maybe... Illumina will continue to dominate", but "the market, they'll be cut away a little bit".
- (Q4) [X] MGI is the competitor he knows: small-to-XL scales, "very competitive" price, kits "fairly compatible with Illumina kits" so switching is "pretty easy"; "same data and at lower cost". Example: a large New Zealand agricultural sequencing provider is "ditching their very large Illumina sequencers for MGI... That's a major shift" because of equipment cost. Brand is MGI's main obstacle; US access may be harder.
- (Q4, Q1) [X] Switching barrier is high for validated diagnostics: he would see "very little reason" to move unless the alternative were "dramatically cheaper... several times maybe". Academic institutes, public research institutes and cores are "much more flexible".
- (Q2) [X] Illumina small-scale pricing is "a little bit wild": price per base at small scale "100X more expensive than at the larger scale, something like that" (a rough guess stated twice). Large instruments cost NZD 1.5-2 million (about "$1 million"); Nanopore devices $10,000-20,000. Capex approval for a big sequencer "can take several years".
- (Q1) [X] The whole market is "definitely growing" as cost per base falls: WGS now default for unexplained genetic disorders, microbial work moved from 16S to metagenomics. No numbers given.
- (Q3) [X] Single cell has "exploded" and is "now flooded"; Illumina is "trying to do what everyone else does" and "trying to do too many things". Advice: even out pricing, or focus on cutting the cost of core sequencing.
- (Q4, Q3) [X] DRAGEN is a "good advantage" and Illumina service is "head and shoulders above" (hedged: "at least in the past"). He believes DRAGEN requires data from an Illumina machine (hedged understanding).
- (Q4) [X] Long read at his facility is "closer to 20% or 30%" of revenue (Nanopore), growing. This is one facility, not the market.
- (Q4) [X] Roche SBX "remains to be seen"; if the price is low enough it "could be a major market shift", but if it is a "$2 million machine" only places like the Broad would buy it.

### Call 4. Former Associate Director, Product Management, Multi-omic and Multi-modal Assays, Illumina (23 Jul 2026; published 6 Aug)
**Credibility.** Left Illumina **July 2025** (about 12 months old). Four Illumina years across market development, corporate strategy (competitive intelligence) and spatial product management; earlier Roche Tissue Diagnostics. Pricing-negotiation and cycle-history observations are first-hand to mid-2025. Anything after July 2025 (35B flow cell timing, StrataMap launch, reagent rental, AXELIOS) is second-hand. He built numbers on the fly and says so. Likely the same person as call 6.

- (Q1, Q2) [X] **Core thesis: price per Gb now falls as fast as volume rises.** In the NextSeq-to-NovaSeq era price fell about 4X ($20/Gb to about $5/Gb) and customers sequenced 5-10X more. Now "maybe the price would drop by half, but they would only increase their sequencing by about 25%". He blames competition (BGI/MGI, then Singular, Element, Ultima "in 2021, 2022"), and says this is "one of the big factors" behind Illumina's "real flat growth starting in about 2022 and to the present day".
- (Q1, Q2, Q5) [X] Blended consumables price for high-throughput fell from about $5/Gb (NovaSeq 6000) toward about $2.50/Gb (NovaSeq X). Rough math on the spot: a "30%-40% decrease" in blended price from 2022 to now "would then offset that on the demand side". "Unfortunately for Illumina, the price decrease is keeping up with demand."
- (Q1, Q2) [X] Why further cuts matter less: sequencing is now a small slice of customer cost. A 30X genome is about 100 Gb; NovaSeq X sequencing on the biggest flow cell at list is about $250 per genome (about $2.50/Gb); genome sample prep under $100; clinical data analysis $500-1,000 per sample (research $50-200). Cutting sequencing $100 to $80 changes total sample cost by a single-digit percent. For a clinical lab he agreed the new flow cell lowers total effective cost by only about 5%. A real step change would need $10-50 per genome, which Illumina "does not have the appetite" to do ("speedrunning sequencing's path to commoditization").
- (Q1, Q5) [X] "I would be surprised if it grew. I think flat would be a huge win" for core sequencing consumables (the part he called "the 80%"). Growth must come from pre-sequencing consumables (differentiated sample prep, single cell, spatial), which report inside sequencing consumables.
- (Q2, Q5) [X] 35B NovaSeq X flow cell (up from 25B; also a 5B): he believes officially announced June 2026. Cost maybe "down to like $2.50 per gigabase, and maybe that drops to $2 flat", about 20%, and "is going to be a pricing decrease. They're definitely going to do that" as the response to Roche. A 20% cut "probably not" spurs big demand; it may stop customers leaving.
- (Q2, Q4) [X] Contracts: the Broad (10+ high-throughput Illumina sequencers) gets "insane" discounts on annual or biannual contracts "heavily guided by competition"; Ultima gives the Broad "crazy pricing" and the Broad returns to Illumina. Core-lab contracts "can very quickly become null and void if another company comes in and says, 'we can undercut Illumina by 25%, 50%.'" Illumina either price-matches or not depending on precedent; matching everyone is "a pure race to the bottom... all of a sudden, Illumina's at a 20% margin and the wheels are falling off" (illustrative, not a disclosed number).
- (Q1, Q2, Q4) [X] Clinical (Natera, Foundation, Roche, Guardant, Exact; he put clinical at about 50% of the business as the interviewer's premise): "very lucrative", "growing like crazy" as of his tenure, but "quite a bit of price erosion" because of Element, Ultima and now Roche; "the sequencer just isn't really all that important to the output of the assay anymore". Switching has "a lot of friction" but is feasible for a big, cash-rich customer. Top 10-20 customers are a very large share of Illumina.
- (Q4, Q5) [X] Reagent rental: he says Illumina never did it while he was there (see section c, other experts disagree) and that it has "become a necessity" because private rivals "are giving instruments away" and Illumina "would never discount a NovaSeq to 90%" while "Element and Ultima... will absolutely do that".
- (Q4) [X] Roche AXELIOS: "not ready", "isn't a very good sequencer by modern sequencing standards", not much cheaper per base, suited to single cell, spatial, RNA; "a slow burn"; "I would be absolutely shocked if they were losing accounts to it". Best case 10-15 of 100 instruments bought in 2027, "maybe 10" (client's framing: 10-15% of new purchases in year one). The scary case is a clinically viable Roche instrument.
- (Q3) [X] Strategy since about 2021 patent cliff is to invest in differentiated sample prep: five-base methylation assay ("highly differentiated"), spatial (StrataMap, "just launched" June 2026), Fluent (single cell, deal closed early 2025), and a "pseudo-long-read" product. The aim is lock-in: "if you go with another sequencing technology, you can't get any of this". He recalls Illumina telling investors of "hundreds of millions of dollars added to the top line over the next five years or so" (recollection of company messaging, not his estimate).
- (Q3) [X] Sequencing-based spatial is small: 10x Visium consumables "maybe eight figures annually"; sequencing is under half of that experiment, so Illumina's current spatial-driven sequencing revenue is "not going to be more than maybe low eight figures" (second-hand, from analyst reports). He is "cautiously pessimistic" on 10x Atera but notes ~$500,000 instrument fatigue favors Illumina's consumable approach (no capex, run as few as four samples).
- (Q4) [X] Long read is "probably a little too niche to support an independent company"; the promised discoveries "haven't happened".

### Call 5. Sr. Staff Machine Learning Scientist (Exact Sciences, per the call; header says "Genece Health") (23 Jul 2026; published 27 Jul)
**Credibility.** Customer. Four to five years as an Illumina bioinformatician (DRAGEN, TSO 500), but **five to seven years ago**; now at Exact Sciences (described as recently bought by Abbott), specialty MCED/MRD/early detection. The Ultima decision was at a prior start-up "a few years ago", before Element's platform existed. Roche SBX details are second-hand (colleagues). The bio contains an unrelated Illumina VP profile; ignore it. Employer and years-of-experience are inconsistent in the document.

- (Q4, Q1) [X] "Illumina is definitely losing grip in this market", but it "still has a lot of stickiness, especially in established clinical settings, oncology and diagnosis". Switching an FDA-approved or LDT product could mean, "worst-case scenario", "another $200 million of clinical trials to justify this change". "That's where most of the money is in the market."
- (Q4) [X] Exposed segments: big pharma building new products ("it's anybody's game now") and agile universities/start-ups. His start-up chose Ultima for an LDT after Ultima "very aggressively" pursued the business and gave "really good discount".
- (Q2, Q4) [X] Ultima was "about 2X, 3X cheaper" per megabase even before discount; BGI was "a little bit cheaper" than Illumina but not as cheap as Ultima after discount. Element was passed over because it was not out yet. Ultima's Q60 base quality with ppmSeq was decisive for early cancer detection and MRD, where the signal is one in a million or one in a billion: base quality "spell[s] out your ceiling for the final detection accuracy". The company later considered renting a UG 200 for ppmSeq.
- [Note] This conflicts with Natera's claim that deep ctDNA work needs Illumina-class depth and with the Natera view that only Illumina is used in MRD. It shows that quality-sensitive early-detection start-ups are Ultima's entry point in clinical.
- (Q4) [X] Long read (Nanopore Q20-ish, topping at Q30-35) is "very, very unlikely" for MRD; niche uses are rare/neurodegenerative repeats, "not like a trillion-dollar market like oncology" (his figure, unverified). No tipping point: "It's not like Apple one day just replaced Nokia."
- (Q4) [X] Roche SBX is meant mainly for Roche and big-pharma in-house use to cut sequencing COGS ("an enabler... not a replacer"). Duplex quality about 45-52 versus Ultima Q60; underlying nanopore-style noise; reported bench workflow concerns. He "highly doubt[s]" people will abandon pipelines; uptake would come via Roche-driven collaborations ("a good PR stunt").
- (Q2, Q5) [X] NovaSeq X is "like doubling" the NovaSeq, "the benchmark" but less dramatic than earlier generations. Start-ups validating an LDT use third-party services and often pay up to double for a faster small flow cell ("200 versus 100") to hit a deadline. The main support complaint he heard was whether reps "give more discount, or they take away the discount after a year".
- (Q3, Q4) [X] DRAGEN is state of the art but "definitely not a decisive differentiator": "Most of the people just don't use it", even when free; start-ups and diagnostic firms run their own pipelines (BCL to FASTQ, GATK).
- (Q4) [X] Ultima's sales team was proactive and its small team "closer circle, not as siloed" ("pretty desperate at the time"). Illumina support was fine five to seven years ago.
- (Q2) [X] Sequencer makers earn most revenue from kits and buffers; forcing exclusive reagents raises revenue per instrument but could cost instrument sales (general reasoning, no figures).

### Call 6. Former Associate Director, Product Management, Multi-omic and Multi-modal Assays, Illumina (24 Jul 2026; published 28 Jul)
**Credibility.** Very likely the same person as call 4: left July 2025, now a consultant, background Roche tissue diagnostics then Adaptive Biotechnologies (about 2020) then four years at Illumina. He explicitly separates first-hand (history, spatial, strategy) from outside observation (Chinese vendors, SBX, Ultima gen-2, Element newest features) and says the talent/management views are "just going to be the opinion of whoever is answering".

- (Q4, Q5) [X] **Share path:** Illumina's share "fell from probably around 80% down now into the mid-60s percent and just continuously eroding"; erosion "like three pps a year" (citing DeciBio data the strategy team used). He expects it to level out near **50%**: "around 50% is going to be a reasonable number to flatline at", because "everyone who wants to leave or has a good reason to leave will have left". This assumes Illumina keeps adding multiomic offerings and iterating. The metric is not defined.
- (Q2, Q4) [X] Why customers leave: better instrument prices/promotions; better consumables pricing that "Illumina had a really hard time price matching" (Element "would drop Illumina's consumable margins to below 50% in some cases", which Illumina had "no appetite" to do); and in some cases better technology (AVITI quality "simply better" at launch, because sequencing-by-binding avoids Illumina's unnatural-base problem). Ultima ppmSeq gives "Q70" but "has some other issues".
- (Q4, Q5) [X] Commoditization is "out of their hands": "There just is no reason to choose Illumina sequencers in most cases anymore, especially if you are a new customer starting out"; "a race to the bottom right now in terms of pricing". Illumina is "a premium company with a premium product" and cannot switch to being the cost leader.
- (Q1, Q5) [X] **NovaSeq X was disappointing.** Historically a new platform drove instrument purchases and then volume ("a price decrease of 25%, but then there might be 2X or 3X more sequencing"). With X "that really wasn't the case. The placements were slower than expected" and sequencing intensity "didn't really get ahead of the price decrease", which is why growth has been "mostly flat" since.
- (Q1, Q4) [X] Clinical lock-in example: Adaptive's clonoSEQ MRD assay was built on an older mid-throughput Illumina machine (550) that costs "probably close to 10X as much per read as the current generation of NovaSeq", and Adaptive had still not updated the fleet after roughly 10 years because internal friction is so high. FDA-cleared products make platform changes "incredibly hard". Illumina's clinical first-mover advantage is "a huge advantage that other companies, I honestly do not know how they're going to ever address".
- (Q2, Q5) [X] Tiering: big customers (Broad; Natera, Guardant, Foundation) "could be paying half or even a quarter of what a relatively small CRO or core lab was paying per base, even with the same instrument"; add a lower-throughput instrument and it "can go up easily five or tenfold". The MiSeq tier is low revenue, "dying because sequencing has gotten so cheap", though removal could open developing-market doors to Element.
- (Q3, Q4) [X] Strategy is now "ecosystem": StrataMap (spatial, four years to build), "the five-day solution", Fluent (single cell), Constellation long reads now TruPath ("pseudo-long read"). But product cycles are too slow; by launch competitors have improved (10x Atera, Element spatial). Fluent "really struggled to gain broader acceptance". Single cell about $1-1.5B market; true spatial "probably not even a $500 million market today".
- (Q4) [X] 10x relationship is adversarial: 10x collaborated with every sequencing rival because cheaper sequencing means bigger 10x experiments (e.g. sequencing falling from 50% to 20% of a $1,000 experiment); "I do not think Illumina has any bargaining or leverage over 10x at all".
- (Q4) [X] China/Asia: GeneMind and Salus BioMed copy Illumina mid-throughput boxes at "insanely low pricing" but sell in China and Southeast Asia, not seen in Europe; "going cheaper just isn't really affecting your bottom line" once you are at "$100 genome or $1 per gigabase" because sequencing is "5%-10% of the total cost of some of those workflows". MGI rarely appears in competitive deals; hard to sell in US, some European traction.
- (Q4) [X] Roche AXELIOS 1: "either become a big bunch of nothing, or... a serious disruptor". Needs duplex reads to reach Q38; single-end 200-300 bp; versus Ultima Q70, Element Q40-50, "Illumina now announcing a Q60 product". Clinical angle: sample-to-answer in four to six hours versus 24-36 hours on NovaSeq, reusable flow cell, Roche's clinical standing; a Foundation Medicine adoption "seems like it could happen".
- (Q4) [X] Ultima gen-2 instrument: gen-1 was "terrible" (heavy, hot, unreliable); gen-2 reportedly fixes most issues and lists below a $100 genome; he wonders if Natera, Guardant, Exact would take a fleet.
- (Q5) [X] People: Illumina leaks strategy and R&D talent; "lifers" with too much influence; the "Danish management team" lacks sequencing experience (he names two people; see section c). He thinks much of Illumina's success was "just luck".

### Call 7. Former Field Application Scientist, Illumina; now SOPHiA GENETICS (24 Jul 2026; published 28 Jul)
**Credibility.** Left Illumina **October 2024** (about 21 months stale). Supported population-genome and clinical NGS in the Gulf (Emirati and Bahrain genome projects; one account of 21 NovaSeq 6000 + 15 X Plus). Now at SOPHiA GENETICS, a software vendor that **partners with Element**, plus a consultancy, so there is a commercial interest in Element and in non-Illumina bundles. Geographically MENA-heavy. Gives a share view and rankings with no data. Offered "shocking" price-per-Gb numbers on Illumina's coming strategy but was not asked; a follow-up lead.

- (Q4, Q5) [X] "Illumina already lost 10% of the market. They used to be 80%. Now they're at 70%... I think Illumina is going to lose... at least 30%, if not even more sometimes" over four to five years (to 2029/2030), "divided by others". It is unclear whether the 30% includes the 10% already lost. He says earnings "are achieved by layoffs and other things" and that "there is one [layoff wave] coming soon".
- (Q4) [X] Ranking of challengers: Element (2), Ultima (3), Roche (4), GeneMind (5), MGI (6, "lost its touch"). On installed base he says Element exceeds Roche, Ultima, GeneMind and MGI. Scenarios: Roche could reach number two by 2029-30 via acquisitions; MGI number three if it fixes technical problems.
- (Q2, Q4) [X] **The NovaSeq 6000 versus X Plus mechanics.** "The consumables for the NovaSeq X is actually cheaper than the consumables for a NextSeq 550 and the NovaSeq 6000". A 10B flow cell gives up to 13B reads and about 24 whole genomes; AVITI flow cell tops out near 1B reads, instrument about half a million dollars. At a site with a NovaSeq 6000, "AVITI is going to win" on price; at an X Plus site "Element will lose" (cheaper consumables, more samples per flow cell, no revalidation). VITARI (about half the NovaSeq X price, same sample count as X Plus) changes that and "Element is moving customers from the X Plus to VITARI".
- [Note] This is the most direct statement on the question of what happens as the 6000 tail converts. The tail is where competitors win on price today, which supports Illumina converting it onto the X (at lower $/Gb) to defend it.
- (Q1, Q4, Q5) [X] Buyers' ranking of reasons to switch: (1) validation ("number one headache", competitors "have to offer free sequencing reagents to win"); (2) samples per flow cell; (3) end-to-end solution (automation scripts, panels, software). Element lacks oncology/infectious-disease panels and NIPT, so NIPT sites on NextSeq 550 "will never shift".
- (Q2, Q5) [X] Illumina's defence is now bundling and giveaways: for a site like MSK with an X Plus where Element offers assay + analysis + automation, Illumina offers "a Hamilton for free... a NextSeq 2000 for free... full training, and... a free ICI for one year". He says Illumina "never gave reagent rental deals" before ("too late"). Element's counter-bundle includes five years of free Microsoft cloud storage and automation script support for existing Hamilton/Tecan.
- (Q4, Q2) [X] Pressure tactics: when a customer considers Element for TSO, Illumina says TSO "is not validated to Element" and will not support validation; customers "think four times". Illumina has raised prices "out of nowhere" on customers using third-party panels and blamed their panels in troubleshooting; some of those customers quietly placed Roche instruments.
- (Q4) [X] Roche: "too early", Illumina does not see it as a threat; a few placements "secretly" with reagent deals (e.g. "500 samples a year", discounts, free items) that customers use to push Illumina's prices down; weak panel breadth (AVENIO), no RNA, weak NIPT and infectious disease, weak software (uses Franklin, good in germline, weak in somatic).
- (Q4) [X] Ultima: "super cheap compared to the accuracy", population-genome projects "secretly validating Ultima instruments without telling Illumina"; bottleneck is lack of a routine-workflow pipeline; has more instruments placed than Roche.
- (Q4, China) [X] GeneMind is "the exact mimic of Illumina", no conversion kit, "very much cheaper", very competitive in the Middle East and Europe, but cannot do 500-gene CGP; MGI is cheap but complicated and more labor, "not at clinical-level accuracy" for oncology/MRD; M42 (UAE) "kicked out [MGI] in less than 48 hours".
- (Q3) [X] Proteomics: SomaLogic versus Olink (now Thermo) data quality is "a mountain and a sea level"; "Illumina is not able to win in that proteomics domain". Multiomics is "an add-on"; customers start with germline, exome, rare disease, somatic, MRD, and only later go to proteomics/single cell. Single cell: 10x is losing to cheaper rivals such as Singleron ("almost 5X cheaper than 10x", sequenced on Illumina). Illumina Complete Long Reads "failed drastically".
- (Q5) [X] Legacy phase-out: old MiSeq, NextSeq 550 (slower, Dx certification), and iSeq are being discontinued in favor of MiSeq i100 and NovaSeq X Plus; software (ICI, ICA) is "really, really expensive" and pricing per sample/storage is unresolved; big customers dislike DRAGEN licensing contracts.

### Call 8. Head of synthetic biology at a ~50-person biotech (24 Jul 2026; published 29 Jul)
**Credibility.** Current customer; research/synthetic biology, not clinical; MiniSeq, MiSeq, NovaSeq plus Nanopore and some PacBio. AI-run channel check; growth is a company-specific effect (platform just matured). Not a market read.

## (b) Cross-call synthesis by question

### Q1. True go-forward clinical Gb growth, and are the stated reasons for deceleration right?

**Best numbers given (all [X]):**
- No clinical Gb growth rate exists in these calls.
- Academic Element core: Gb flat to +5-10% (Q2 2026), full-year flat (call 2).
- Small research biotech on Illumina: Gb +25-50%, but project-driven (call 8).
- Price versus volume since 2022: blended high-throughput price down 30-40% (about $5/Gb on the 6000 toward about $2.50/Gb on X) and "the price decrease is keeping up with demand" (calls 4/6, one person).
- Elasticity: historically price -4X, volume +5-10X; now price -50%, volume only about +25% (call 4); earlier-era example price -25%, volume 2-3X (call 6).
- 35B flow cell: about 20% lower $/Gb ($2.50 to $2.00) (call 4). Effective clinical total cost drops only about 5% (call 4).
- Per-genome economics: about 100 Gb per 30X genome; $250 list sequencing; sample prep under $100; clinical data analysis $500-1,000 (call 4).
- Market-value growth (not Gb): MRD about $5B to $30B by 2030 (call 1); clinical sequencing, liquid biopsy and MRD are "where all the growth is"; CGP "quite saturated" with small annual growth (call 12).

**Scorecard on the five stated reasons:**
1. *Price elasticity fading.* **Supported** by the ex-Illumina strategist (calls 4/6): sequencing is now a small slice of per-sample cost, so another 20% cut moves little ("5%" for a clinical lab); a step change needs $10-50 per genome. **Partly contradicted** at the application edge: call 3 says WGS is now default for unexplained genetic disease because cost fell, and call 5 and 9 say cheaper X flow cells improve business cases for small clinical labs. Elasticity looks weak for existing assays and alive only where a new use becomes affordable.
2. *NovaSeq X conversion done.* **Not supported.** Call 4 says "a bunch of outstanding NovaSeq 6000s" are "slowly changing over". Call 7 says customers are still moving 6000 to X Plus. Call 12 notes NHS labs run second Illumina sequencers for CGP. The conversion is ongoing, and every converted Gb brings roughly a halving of $/Gb (call 4).
3. *Reimbursement.* **Mixed and thin.** Natera says MRD reimbursement is expanding (more tests and indications) but Medicare is "a very small component", private is limited, and no company has European reimbursement. Call 12 adds that NHS CGP is mature (seven labs) while MRD adoption is still being decided. No expert quantifies reimbursement as a volume cap.
4. *Test-volume growth.* **Supported as the main driver of the clinical pie.** Liquid biopsy and MRD growth (calls 1, 12); CGP saturated (call 12); NIPT is large and stable (call 1). But the experts talk about test revenue, not Gb.
5. *Sequencing intensity per test.* **Cuts both ways, and is under-discussed.** Higher: MRD moving from exome to whole-genome (call 1); tissue-informed MRD at 80-120X or more; CGP at 150X or more plus RNA. Lower or flat: NIPT, transplant, infectious disease "overkill" above 30X (call 1); Element/Ultima appeal for 30-50X work; call 4 says intensity growth no longer keeps up with price cuts.
6. *Competitors.* **Experts say they hit price, not Gb.** Clinical Gb is fought over by vendors, but the total Gb is set by tests run; the effect on Illumina is lower $/Gb now (call 4: "quite a bit of price erosion" in clinical) and possible share loss later (call 12: validation on Ultima/Roche "still up in the air").

**Agreement:** clinical is the growth segment and is sticky (calls 1, 4, 5, 6, 9, 12). **Disagreement:** whether elasticity is dead (calls 4/6 versus 3/9) and how soon clinical validation moves off Illumina (call 12 "five to ten years" versus call 1 "very, very high" barriers).
