# Customer surveys and customer evidence cited in equity research (2026-11-09)

*Scope: all 75 broker and independent notes in the folder. That is 31 on Illumina plus 1 Barclays sector note (`Sources/ILMN/research/`, `Sources/SECTOR/research/`), and 43 on peers: Oxford Nanopore 30, PacBio 6, Roche 3, MGI 4. Two Sonnet agents swept them for surveys, channel checks, expert/KOL calls and customer feedback. Key quotes were re-checked against the notes. Quotes are full sentences, with line numbers.*

## Short answer

**Only J.P. Morgan ran a customer survey, and only JPM cites it.**
- No other Illumina broker (Barclays, UBS, Freedom, Ameriprise, Bloomberg Intelligence, Seeking Alpha) and no peer note (including JPM's own Oxford Nanopore and Roche analysts) mentions JPM's survey or runs a survey of its own. JPM's only follow-up references are to itself, in its 2Q26 recap.

**The rest of the customer evidence is thin and unquantified:**
- UBS: a handful of researcher and expert calls around AGBT (n = 4).
- Deutsche Bank: channel checks with no sample size, in its ONT notes.
- Barclays: PacBio lab checks.
- Roche's claims of positive customer feedback, relayed by JPM and Barclays.

**Positive customer-survey results for Illumina come only from JPM.** The second-most supportive evidence is UBS's small post-AGBT sample, which found researchers "unclear why someone would switch".

## 1. J.P. Morgan: the survey and how the upgrade uses it

**"Upgrading to Overweight on a Sticky Clinical User Base and Test Volume Runway Following Customer Survey; PT to $185"** (Casey Woodring, 10 Jun 2026; Neutral → Overweight, PT $125 → $185, price $160.03). File `20260615_JP_Morgan_ILMN_Illumina-_Inc.-_Upgrading_to_Overweight_on_a_Sticky_Clin.md`.

*L33, opening paragraph [survey, positive]:*
> "Our survey (see slide deck here) of 62 respondents (30 research and 32 clinical) leans toward a favorable outlook for ILMN, showing that both research and clinical customer groups expect instrument and consumables spending in 2027 and 2028 to be above ILMN's current organic growth trends and long-term target of +HSD organic growth. These customers also indicated that ILMN's overall NGS share is expected to increase over the next two years."

*L33, same paragraph [expert, positive]:*
> "Beyond test volume growth, we have consistently seen and heard from experts (see our AMP and ASCO takeaways) that sequencing intensity is growing as diagnostics companies shift to WGS and transcriptome sequencing, which should serve as an added tailwind for ILMN over time."

*L165 [checks with diagnostics-company management teams, positive]:*
> "Based on what we've heard from some of our Spec. Dx management teams, there is general intrigue, but they aren't urgently planning to switch and are either actively transitioning to the NovaSeq X or have just transitioned."

*L167 [expert evidence relayed from AlphaSense; negative for Roche, so positive for Illumina]:*
> "Operationally, the reusable cartridge/flow-cell concept is a potential point of hesitation for clinical customers as industry participants (per AlphaSense Expert Insights) have flagged elevated contamination and cross-sample carryover risk and raised the question of whether performance can remain consistent across reuse cycles, which is an especially high bar for regulated, reproducible clinical workflows. Additional workflow concerns, including reportedly higher DNA input needs (limiting compatibility with low-yield specimens) and a fragmented analysis stack that depends on multiple third-party tools, may complicate integration and add "hidden" costs."

*L169 [survey, positive]:*
> "Survey results support a more loyal customer base than appreciated (see below for full survey takeaways). Based on our survey results, clinical customers' open responses point to meaningful switching friction and limited urgency to add a new sequencing vendor, with respondents emphasizing satisfaction with incumbent platforms on core purchase criteria and the operational burden of changing. One customer noted, "it is difficult to suddenly switch as we are using almost every product of Illumina and we do not need to add any more vendors," while another said, "we are currently receiving good services and do not have any reasons to evaluate any other vendor.""

*L171 [survey, positive]:*
> "…our survey suggests that research customers plan to increase consumables and instrument spending in 2027 (average increase of +9.0% y/y and +18.6% y/y, respectively) and in 2028 (average increase of +12.8% y/y and +19.0% y/y, respectively), and we think there is the most upside potential in this end market in 2H26 into 2027 as grant disbursements reset and A&G customers regain confidence in more normalized funding patterns."

*L211, "Key takeaways from our customer survey" [survey; positive on spend and share, mixed on AXELIOS]:*
> "We conducted a survey of 62 ILMN customers, including 30 research and 32 clinical customers, primarily in North America (65%) with the remainder in Europe. Overall, the survey suggests a positive spending outlook, with respondents expecting instrument and consumables spending to increase notably in 2027 and 2028. For research customers, instrument spending is expected to grow +18.6% y/y in 2027 vs. 2026 and +19.0% y/y in 2028 vs. 2027, while consumables spending is expected to grow +9.0% y/y and +12.8% y/y, respectively. Meanwhile, clinical customers are expected to see even stronger growth, with instrument spend growing +22.2% y/y in 2027 vs. 2026 and +31.0% y/y in 2028 vs. 2027, while consumables are expected to grow +9.4% y/y and +14.0% y/y, respectively. Notably, consumable volume growth exceeded customer spending expectations for both research (spread between volume and spend is +2.4% in 2027 and +4.5% in 2028) and clinical (spread between volume and spend is +5.2% in 2027 and +5.1% in 2028), indicating favorable pricing for the customers, albeit below the -DD% pricing pressure we've seen for ILMN. In terms of expected ILMN share of NGS activity, research customers across all size cohorts generally expect a modest increase in ILMN share ranging from ~+1-3% increase, while among clinical customers, the increase is more concentrated with mid-sized customers expecting a ~+7% increase in share. When asked about Axelios 1, of the customers who were familiar with the instrument, research customers noted that they were more likely to purchase the instrument (with an average likelihood of purchasing at 6.2/10) vs. clinical customers (4.7/10). Among those considering a purchase, 8% expect to purchase within 6-12 months (skewed to clinical), 35% within 12-24 months (skewed to research), 32% in 24+ months (skewed to clinical), and 24% are not sure (skewed to clinical)."

**The survey deck: the negative finding the note leaves out.** `20260615_JP_Morgan_ILMN_Illumina-_Inc.md`, key takeaways:
> "And if these customers were to purchase an Axelios 1, the majority (65%) of respondents expect to reduce their ILMN footprint."

**2Q26 recap** (31 Jul 2026; Overweight, PT $230). `20260805_JP_Morgan_...Consuma.md`. This only points back to the survey.
- L22: "…along with the NGS customer survey we also ran in May for more detail."

## 2. UBS: post-AGBT researcher and expert feedback (the only other first-hand customer evidence on Illumina)

**"First Read: Roche Updated As Expected; Competitive Overhang Could Soften"** (Dan Leonard, 26 Feb / 3 Mar 2026; Neutral, PT $135). `20260303_UBS_...Com.md`, L24–28 [checks; positive for Illumina]:
> "Early feedback following the Roche pricing announcement so far is skeptical, as the first couple researchers we've spoken to are unclear why someone would switch from Illumina to Roche, even following this pricing announcement. They continue to consider speed the most compelling feature of the Axelios, which has limited relevance in research applications. On the counting side, there are questions around quality needed for cell barcodes and UMI's on 10x single-cell RNAseq, given that the quality for the simplex mode is lesser than the duplex mode on the Roche instrument."

**"First Read: Roche Feedback Rolling In, and It's Mixed"** (4 Mar 2026; Neutral, PT $135). `20260304_UBS_...md` [expert calls; mixed, leaning positive for Illumina]:
- *L19–24:* "We've been continuing to get feedback on the Roche Axelios updates (n = 4, including the Doc Debates expert call we hosted this evening), and our initial take still holds. Bottom-line, we believe the update is relatively benign for Neutral-rated Illumina (ILMN) and should soften the competitive overhang. The range of feedback is wide, with one very negative, one very positive, and two muted/indifferent. While only a small sample, we imagine this distribution is reasonably representative of what we'll find in the research community."
- *L38–39:* "$150 / genome is not compelling. Already at $175 / genome with his discounts, and Illumina's new 35B flow cell likely brings Illumina pricing to $150 anyway. Larger users already at $125 / genome, our expert believes."
- *L59:* "Reusability not fully addressed, and thinks this will be a big problem in the clinic. Queried his clinical users, 50% had no problem with reusability if properly validated, while for 50% -- the pathologists -- reusability was a big no, a nonstarter."

## How to read it

The bull case's customer evidence rests on one source: JPM's 62-person survey, plus JPM's conversations with the diagnostics managements it covers. The only other first-hand evidence is UBS's four calls, which were mixed but leaned toward Illumina, and Deutsche Bank's unquantified checks, which were balanced. The evidence against Illumina in the folder is mostly Roche's own claims and the survey finding JPM did not repeat: 65% of potential AXELIOS buyers would cut their Illumina footprint. See `2026-11-09_ILMN_JPM_customer_survey_issues.md` for the survey's weaknesses and `2026-11-09_ILMN_customer_channel_checks_discounting_quotes.md` for the expert-network channel checks, which are a far larger sample and lean toward flat-to-rising prices with targeted discounts.
