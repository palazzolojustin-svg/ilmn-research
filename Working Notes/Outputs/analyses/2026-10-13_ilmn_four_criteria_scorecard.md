# Would Illumina make a three-stock portfolio? A four-criteria scorecard

*Prepared 13 Oct 2026. Draws on all Core Notes and four agent sweeps of the Working Notes, one per criterion. Labels: Fact (reported), Mgmt (management claim), Opinion (broker, expert or our arithmetic). The stock price is the 9 Oct 2026 close of $278.*

## The answer

**No. Illumina does not pass this test.** It scores well on only half of one criterion, the stickiness of its clinical customers. It fails the other three, and the reason is structural.

Illumina's moat protects **volume, not price**. Clinical labs rarely leave, because moving a validated test is slow and expensive. But to keep them, and to fend off Roche, Ultima and Element, Illumina keeps cutting the price per Gb. That means:

- defending against competition (criterion 4) is paid for with margin (criterion 3);
- growth depends on a price variable that customers and rivals set (criterion 1);
- the business throws off about $1B of cash a year, but the main use for it is buybacks at about 35x earnings, not reinvestment at high returns (criterion 2).

That pattern describes a high-quality incumbent harvesting a maturing franchise. It does not describe a compounder you could own forever.

| Criterion | Grade | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Predictable growth levers | C | About 90% recurring revenue, but growth depends on volume growth outrunning price cuts, and the revenue guide was cut in 2023, 2024 and 2025 |
| 2. Recurring high-return redeployment | D | Cash comes in, but there is no large internal or M&A outlet. The record includes GRAIL. Buybacks now run at a ~2.4% FCF yield |
| 3. Control of incremental margins | D+ | The margin gains came from cost cuts. Price is a one-way ratchet down, and organic operating leverage has not appeared |
| 4. Fend off competition | B- in clinical, C- elsewhere | No large clinical lab has switched, but share is held by giving up price. Low/mid-throughput, counting applications, single-cell and China are eroding |

## 1. Predictable levers for growth: C

**What is predictable.**
- Consumables were 74% of FY25 revenue, up from 68% in FY23. Management puts recurring revenue (consumables plus service) at about 90% (Fact; 10-K; JPM, Jan 2026).
- Clinical consumables outside China grew 12% in 2025 and about 17% in H1 2026. Clinical is now about 65% of sequencing consumables (Mgmt).
- More than 270 NovaSeq X systems were placed in 2025 and more than 175 in H1 2026, about 70% of them clinical. Each X takes 6–9 months to reach full use, so 2027 consumables are partly "pre-loaded" (Mgmt, Q2'26 call).

**What is not.**
- **Growth equals volume growth minus price decline, and Illumina controls neither.**
  - Gb (billions of DNA letters read) grew more than 30% in Q1–Q2 2026, while sequencing consumables grew only 4–5%. That implies revenue per Gb fell about 20% (Opinion, our arithmetic).
  - Management's own long-term model is volume growth in the mid-20s and price down mid-teens a year. That nets about 6–10% revenue growth. A few points' error on either input swings revenue growth from mid-single to double digits.
- **The guidance record is poor.**
  - The 2023 growth guide went from +7–10% to −2/−3%.
  - The 2024 guide went from flat to −3%.
  - In 2025 the guide went from low-single-digit growth to a decline in May.
  - The 2027 targets themselves replaced abandoned mid-teens growth and 25% margin goals (Fact).
  - 2026 has been beat-and-raise, but against a deliberately low bar.
- **The positive drivers are outside management's control.** MRD, CGP and rare-disease volumes depend on payers, NCCN guidelines and the FDA.
  - CMS's preliminary 2027 lab fee schedule (21 Sep 2026) cuts generic genomic-sequencing codes by 23% on average.
  - The cuts mostly spare branded tests, and any one test's cut is capped at 15% a year (Fact).
  - NIH grants are down about 9,700 versus FY24, and Illumina remains on China's unreliable-entity list (Fact).
- **Known price headwinds stack up in 2027.** They come in three layers:
  - the NovaSeq 6000 tail: 17% of HT Gb but 41% of HT revenue at Q2 2026;
  - the 35B flow cell, due H1 2027 and still unpriced;
  - routine repricing.

  Together these roughly use up management's mid-teens price model before any competitive response. Our base case has clinical consumables up about 4% in 2027, against about 13–14% implied by consensus. That model under-predicted Q2 2026, so hold it loosely (Opinion; Core Notes 16).

**Brokers split exactly on this point.** J.P. Morgan calls high-single-digit growth "highly achievable" (31 Jul 2026). Barclays calls it "a MSD business" that will need "continued price concessions, bundling, and favorable terms" (31 Jul 2026).

## 2. Access to recurring high-return redeployment: D

**The cash is real.**
- Free cash flow was $931M in FY25, about 21% of revenue (Fact). Consensus has about $1.0B for FY26 and $1.1B for FY27.
- At $278 and about 151M shares, the market value is about $42B. That gives a free-cash-flow yield of about 2.4% (Opinion).

**The outlets are not.**
- **The M&A record.**
  - GRAIL cost about $9.7B of accounting consideration and about $6.6B of impairments (FY22–24, our sum). Spinning it off took another $974M of cash.
  - What is left is a stake worth about $88M plus about $104M already sold (Fact).
  - Solexa ($480M, 2007) was a great deal, but it was 19 years ago. Helix failed, and the PacBio deal was blocked.
  - Management has ruled out large deals: "For sure, we're not looking at any larger acquisition" (Thaysen, Nov 2023). As the market leader, Illumina also faces antitrust limits on what it can buy.
- **Recent deals are small and so far dilutive.**
  - SomaLogic cost about $418M in cash all-in. It costs about $0.18 of FY26 EPS and about 100 bp of operating margin, and management expects it to turn profitable in 2027 (Mgmt).
  - Fluent, Partek and Onso together cost well under $200M and do not show in the numbers.
- **The R&D record.**
  - Illumina spent about $6.0B of GAAP R&D over FY21–25, including GRAIL.
  - Over the same years core revenue went from $4,519M to $4,343M, and sequencing consumables from $2,911M to $2,939M (Fact).
  - In revenue terms that R&D bought a defensive platform change, from the 6000 to the X, not growth.
  - R&D is planned to fall from about 22% of revenue toward about 19.5% by 2030–31 in consensus. That is harvesting, not stepping up.
- **The new growth bets are small.** Management says multiomics and BioInsight add 1–2 points of growth in 2027, about $45–90M a year. None of it is broken out. Barclays says "we have no way to confirm these numbers."
- **Buybacks are the main outlet, and the price is now high.**
  - Illumina bought $742M of stock in FY25 at about $95 and $366M in H1'26 at about $124. Those purchases were well timed.
  - But the diluted share count has fallen only from 159M to 153M, because stock-based pay was $275M in FY25.
  - Buying slowed to 32K shares above $140 in Q2 2026. The new $1.5B authorization was unused at June 28.
  - At $278 the same dollars retire about half the shares they would have at $140.
- **The ">20% ROIC" claim is mostly a function of write-downs** (Opinion). It holds on today's written-down capital base of about $3.7B. Adding back the GRAIL write-offs gives an ROIC of about 8%. Management's claimed ">140%" cash conversion was about 109% in H1 2026.

This is the criterion where Illumina is weakest. A three-stock holding needs to reinvest its earnings at high returns again and again. Illumina can mostly only return its cash.

## 3. Ability to control incremental margins: D+

**The margin gains so far came from cost cuts, not operating leverage.**
- From FY24 to FY25, non-GAAP operating income rose about $80M on only $11M of extra revenue (Opinion, our arithmetic).
  - Operating expenses (R&D plus SG&A) fell about $90M.
  - Gross margin slipped from 68.6% to 68.2%.
  - All of the gain came from cost programs: about $175M (2023), $200M of run-rate savings (2024) and another $100M (2025).
- **H1 2026 shows no organic leverage.**
  - Revenue rose $151M and operating income rose $35M, a 23% incremental margin, roughly equal to the average margin.
  - Operating expenses rose 6.4% and absorbed about two-thirds of the extra gross profit.
  - Government incentives rose from $6M to $28M year to date and flow straight to profit. Without them, H1 margin fell from about 21.8% to about 20.9% (Opinion; Q2'26 10-Q).
- **The guide relies on a steep second half.** Q4 2026 must reach about 25.5% operating margin. That implies a Q4 incremental margin of about 60%, against about 15% in Q3 (Opinion).
- **The 2027 target needs costs nearly flat.** Hitting 26% on consensus revenue requires a 66% incremental margin. Holding gross margin at about 68.4%, operating expenses could rise only about 1% (Opinion).
  - Consensus has 25.2%, reaching 26% only in 2028. Barclays has 24.3%.
  - Management's own 2024 breakdown expected about two-thirds of the 500 bp to come from operating leverage. So far more than all of it came from cost cuts.

**Price only moves one way at the accounts that matter.**
- The Open Offer and Natera contracts freeze inflation-adjusted prices through about August 2033. They allow only pass-through of material cost increases that are outside Illumina's control (Fact; Core Notes 17).
- Natera's contract says a new flow cell cannot raise its real price per Gb. For Illumina's largest oncology accounts, the 35B can therefore only be a price cut.
- Most-favoured-customer clauses spread any concession one lab wins to comparable labs.
- Revenue per Gb is still falling by double digits. J.P. Morgan cites "-DD% pricing pressure seen for Illumina recently" (15 Jun 2026).
- Roche lists $150 per genome against Illumina's roughly $200 (J.P. Morgan estimate, Feb 2026).

**Costs Illumina does not control.**
- Tariffs were about $85M gross in 2025. Management stopped quantifying their margin impact after Q4 2025.
- Memory and freight costs were "a headwind we didn't anticipate" (Q2'26 call). Illumina is now "sharing some of the increase with customers", the opposite of what its competitors are doing.

**In its favour.**
- Tariff-adjusted consumables margin held at about 77–79% while the X took HT revenue share from 35% to 55% (Opinion).
- Its scale cost base is credible, and Barclays cites "inherent technological barriers" to rivals matching it.

**The risk.** The remaining 6000 consumables, about $219M a quarter, are probably a very high-margin profit pool. Under our assumption of an 80–85% margin, they generate a large share of operating income. That pool shrinks as the tail converts (Opinion, low confidence; Core Notes 16).

## 4. Ability to fend off incremental competition: B- in clinical, C- elsewhere

**Where the moat holds.**
- **No large lab has switched.** None of the nine largest US clinical customers checked has moved production volume to a rival short-read sequencer as of October 2026. The nine are Natera, Guardant, Tempus, Caris, GeneDx, Foundation, Labcorp, Quest and Myriad (Fact; this is a "not found" result).
- **Switching costs are real.**
  - Revalidating a lab-developed test takes months and costs about $100–200K.
  - Changing the platform for an FDA-approved test has taken 11–17 months per change in the openFDA record.
  - Five-to-seven-year MRD trials "cannot switch a sequencer middle of a trial" (expert).
  - Foundation's FDA-approved test moved to the 6000 about 5.5 years after the 6000 launched.
- **A new, freshly validated X base is building.** Placements beat the 50–60 a quarter framework every quarter from Q4 2025.
- **Customers expect Illumina to hold share.** In J.P. Morgan's 62-customer survey, mid-sized clinical labs expect Illumina's share of their work to rise from 50% to 57%. Of buyers considering Roche, only about 11% would retire an Illumina instrument (J.P. Morgan, Jun 2026).

**Where it is eroding.**
- **Share is slipping.** Expert share estimates fall from about 80% to "mid-60s", or to 70%. None is audited.
- **Every rival attacks a different segment.** "They're trying to compete with many companies, while these competitors are only competing with Illumina" (former Illumina associate director, Aug 2026). Illumina is weakest in:
  - low/mid-throughput, where Element and MGI compete;
  - research "counting" applications, where Roche charges $0.06 per million reads against $0.68 on Illumina's 25B list (UBS);
  - single-cell and spatial, where 10x is moving to imaging systems that need no sequencer;
  - China, where MGI has about 70% of public tenders.
- **Patents are expiring.** The 10-K concedes "as issued patents expire, including those related to our sequencing-by-synthesis technology, we may lose some competitive advantage." A Munich court has also ruled that Illumina's MiSeq infringes an Element patent.
- **Customers have leverage and can leave quickly.**
  - Contracts have no dollar minimums and allow exit on 90 days' notice.
  - The 10-K newly warns of "a small number of large, centralized laboratory customers."
  - Caris says rival platforms' "throughput and cost is significantly higher and lower, respectively" and is about to triple capacity.
  - Foundation, which Roche owns, is the most likely first big switch.
- **Illumina's defense is mostly price.** It uses reagent rental, volume discounts on multi-unit orders, free instruments at threatened sites, and the coming 35B. That is why the moat holds share but does not hold margin.

**The range on Roche** runs from "will just simply die out" to 10–20% of Illumina's base within three years if Roche prices aggressively. Year one is about 100 placements, about a third done by September. Most experts expect gradual erosion, mainly in new projects.

## What would change the verdict

- **Toward "yes":**
  - clinical Gb growth stays near the trailing ~40% through the 35B launch;
  - the 35B is priced narrowly rather than across the board;
  - multiomics and BioInsight show disclosed, profitable revenue;
  - opex stays flat while revenue grows high-single digits, so operating leverage finally appears.
- **Toward a firmer "no":**
  - the first large clinical lab, most likely Foundation or Caris, puts production volume on Roche or Ultima;
  - a broad 35B cut;
  - Q4 2026 misses the ~25.5% margin it needs;
  - buybacks continue at today's price.
- **Next data points:**
  - the Q3 2026 print on 29 Oct, covering derived 6000 revenue, the X revenue share, clinical growth and buyback pace;
  - ASHG (20–24 Oct) for any 35B news;
  - customer Q3 prints in early November.

## Caveats

- Several key figures are our derivations, not disclosures. These include 6000 revenue, the 3.4x price gap, platform margins and our ROIC estimate.
- Many expert views come from former Illumina staff who left in 2024–25, or from competitors' employees.
- Broker targets and notes predate the stock's run to about $278.
