# Therapy selection vs NIPT vs MRD: what the CEO meant and how volumes are trending

Prepared 12 Oct 2026. Sources: Illumina Q2'26 call, TD Cowen Mar 2026 and JPM Jan 2026 deck; company releases (Guardant, Tempus, Caris, Natera, BillionToOne, Myriad); clinical-consumables ledger (E078, E081, E082, E096, E099, E431, E452–E453); `2026-10-11_big_clinical_customers_test_volume_trends.md`.

## What "therapy selection" is

**Definition.** Sequencing a cancer patient's tumour, from tissue or a blood draw, to find mutations that point to the right drug. It is also called comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP).
- **Products** (our mapping; Illumina names none): FoundationOne CDx and Liquid CDx (Roche), Guardant360, Tempus xT/xF, Caris MI Profile / MI Cancer Seek, Illumina's own TSO 500 / TSO Comprehensive.
- **Intensity:** deep and large panels, ~15 Gb per tissue test and up to ~120 Gb for liquid panels (estimates).

**Illumina's split of clinical consumables** (JPM deck, Jan 2026): oncology ~51%, genetic disease ~25%, reproductive health ~21%, other ~3%. Within oncology there is no dollar split. Management says therapy selection is "most of the business" for "at least a year or 2" (TD Cowen, Mar 2026) and "still larger in dollars" than MRD (Q2'26).

## Reading

1. **Ranking.** MRD grows fastest (+55% to >100%), then therapy selection (~+18–31%: steady to accelerating at Tempus and Guardant360, flat at Caris), then NIPT (high single digit to low teens).
   - **BillionToOne is an outlier:** share gains and price (revenue per test +21%).
2. **The dollars still sit in therapy selection.** It is still the largest dollar driver, and its test growth of ~20–30% is healthy. An expert calls CGP "quite saturated"; a research-agent forecast (not an expert) has growth easing to ~15–25% by 2028. Volume prints do not yet show a slowdown (see 2026-10-12_therapy_selection_maturity_and_nipt_decel.md).
3. **NIPT is the slowest and shallowest pool.** ~1–2 Gb a test and ~70–80% of US pregnancies already screened, yet ~21% of clinical consumables.
   - **Upside:** the ACMG's Dec 2025 recommendation of cfDNA screening for all pregnancies, Natera's enhanced Panorama and Fetal Focus.
   - **Weak spots:** Illumina is suing BillionToOne over NIPT patents; Myriad's prenatal volume is falling.
5. **Is MRD a weak volume driver because the tests are shallow?** Only partly.
   - **Serial draws are shallow.** Classic Signatera uses ~0.2 Gb per draw.
   - **Each new patient needs a one-time tumour-plus-normal profile.** That is ~25 Gb for an exome design, and ~200 Gb for Signatera Genome.
   - **Genome-based MRD raises per-draw intensity about 10x** (~2.2–3.4 Gb): Signatera Genome, Personalis/Tempus, Illumina's own kit due 2027.
   - **Folder estimate:** gigabases per new MRD patient rise from ~36 to ~45 (10% genome mix) or ~72 (25% mix). That implies MRD gigabase growth of roughly 35–80% a year [our calculation].
   - **So:** MRD is a real volume driver through new-patient onboarding and the genome mix shift, not through repeat draws.
   - **Dollar caveats:** Natera's contract caps price per Gb on new flow cells, and WGS-MRD labs such as Personalis earn ~$250 a test, so Illumina captures less per gigabase.
6. **Mix drag for Illumina.** Volume growth is skewed toward MRD, which today is mostly shallow amplicon tests (Signatera is under 1 Gb a draw), and toward slow NIPT. That partly explains why customer test growth of 20–60% turns into ~15% Illumina clinical revenue growth.
