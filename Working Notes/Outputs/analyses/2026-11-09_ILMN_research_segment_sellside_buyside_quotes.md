# Illumina's research segment: what the sell-side expects, and what it says the buy-side expects (full quotes, 2026-11-09)

*Sources: every ILMN broker note in `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/research/` (Nov 2025 – Sep 2026), the Barclays sector note, and the Q4'25–Q3'26 earnings calls plus the JPM (Jan) and TD Cowen (Mar) conferences. Quotes are copied in full from the originals, with file and line number; only page headers and bold markup were removed. There are no broker notes in the folder after Q3 results (29 Oct 2026).*

*"Research" here means Illumina's **research and applied** customers: academic and government labs, pharma and biotech R&D, and applied markets. That is roughly 35–40% of revenue (Barclays, Jul 2026; JPM, Jun 2026). The other customer group is **clinical**: diagnostic labs running cancer, prenatal and genetic-disease tests.*

## The short answer

**Management set a low bar and never moved it.** In February 2026, Illumina guided research and applied sequencing consumables (the reagents and flow cells customers buy for each run) to fall by a mid-to-high single-digit percentage in 2026. It repeated that guide at every quarter, while raising the company-wide guide three times. Reported declines eased from −12% in Q1 to −7% in Q2 and −6% in Q3. In October management said "we are seeing signs research activity is stabilizing". It still did not call a recovery.

**The sell-side mostly mirrors management and treats research as upside, not as a driver.**
- No broker in the folder publishes its own quarterly research forecast.
- **JPM** is the only firm with a stated research model: research consumables grow at a +mid-single-digit compound annual growth rate (CAGR) from 2026 to 2030. Its June customer survey had academic customers planning +9% consumables spend in 2027.
- **Freedom** called its research base case "moderately negative" in February.
- **Barclays** is the outlier on the bear side. It argues that research "is more at risk" from Roche's new AXELIOS sequencer, and "we do not envision the market stabilizing anytime soon."

**What brokers say the buy-side expects:**
- **February.** Investors read the 2026 guide as "some type of market freeze" and were "hitting pause" (Barclays). The 2026 consensus bar was low, essentially asking Illumina to keep "research deterioration at ba[y]" (Bloomberg Intelligence).
- **May.** "The market is likely to look through" research weakness because clinical was strong (Freedom).
- **July.** By then, "investors are assuming the +HSD% top line is in the bag". Getting to double-digit growth requires "the research market stabiliz[ing]" (Barclays).
- **August–September.** Freedom and an independent Seeking Alpha author said the stock had priced in an acceleration, including "research normalization".

No broker quotes a buy-side number for research itself. The buy-side view only appears as positioning and narrative. Analysts' questions on the calls show the same arc. In February they asked whether academics would "actually believe" the NIH budget. By April they were asking whether the research guide was too low. By October research only came up as possible 2027 upside, "maybe a research inflection".

## Part 2. The sell-side's own expectations for research, in date order

### Barclays, Luke Sergott: "Getting Ready for the Main Event – Hulk Hogan vs. The Big Show This Year At AGBT" (5 Feb 2026; Underweight, PT $110)
`research/20260212_Barclays_...Hulk_H.md`, L11 continuing at L49

> "ILMN keeps up the trend with a 1Q guide coming in much softer than expected and resulting in a 2H weighted ramp in the business. On the one hand, the macro is stable. Clinical seq cons continued to ramp w/ LB and NGS-based assays across oncology testing, leading to ~20% growth ex-China and research has improved as the year progressed. So with what is expected to be a better year for research budgets, the thinking is that we should see at least MSD+ growth in sequencing consumables. However, mgmt's guide is for a modest improvement in sequencing consumables for the year and instruments to be slightly down to flat. With this setup, we would expect investors to assume a healthy dose of conservatism to be baked into the guide. However, with plenty of competitive launches coming this year, investors are reading into the guide as portraying some type of market freeze and/or some decel in Clinical. And with a steep drop-off in HT placements in 1Q off of a really strong 4Q, the thinking is that there was some pullforwards / backlog flush ahead of these competitive launches. Management was clear that this is not the reason for their guide and that they do not see a fully recovered market dynamic throughout the year at the moment. There are still funding issues on the research side and some of their biggest accounts hail from institutions that are in the cross-hairs of funding cuts w/in the current administration, so everything is not back to normal. Outside of these dynamics, mgmt. continues to invest across the portfolio to diversify into multi-omics, workflow simplification/automation, and building out a comprehensive analytical software ecosystem around DRAGEN. Given the guide, the macro, and the setup into what is expected to be some meaningful announcements at AGBT, investors are likely hitting pause and taking a wait and see approach as to the year shakes out. As we have seen with the rest of the group, investors have little risk appetite for a 2H-weighted guide, especially with a guide baking in some improvement across sequencing consumables and a normal instrument demand cadence throughout the year (bigger 4Q placement seasonality) with what is expected to be significant competitive pressure either on a market freeze and/or an upcoming pricing war within the sequencing space (we read of reports that Roche is looking to price a $100 genome). How meaningful Roche's launch may be remains to be seen, but at these competitive price points, it will certainly put ILMN in a position in which they have to fend off a well capitalized competitor that can aggressively launch in both research and clinical markets. We think that the research market is more at risk in the NT than the clinical market given how sticky the customer base is. So in the end, we think that ILMN is likely to achieve the guide, but we struggle to see upside, which is what is needed at these valuations, especially considering how well ILMN has held in vs. the group over the past 2 weeks."

## How to read this

- **The sell-side's research expectation is management's expectation.** Every broker except Barclays and JPM simply relays the "mid-to-high single-digit decline" and calls a funding recovery upside. JPM is the only firm that quantifies a recovery (+MSD CAGR), and it rests on a 30-person customer survey. Barclays is the only firm that argues the opposite, citing competitive pricing and AXELIOS in counting applications.
- **The buy-side bar moved from "research is a risk" to "research is upside that may already be priced".** In February investors were "hitting pause". By July they assumed high-single-digit growth was "in the bag", and the path to double digits ran through research stabilizing. The bull case for 2027 therefore leans on research turning from about −6% to positive, which no broker models in detail.
- **The consensus gap that matters.** Bloomberg consensus for 2027 sequencing consumables (+8.8%) only works if research grows low single digits or clinical stays near 15% (our back-solve). Management's own words through October ("signs research activity is stabilizing", "too early to call a recovery") support stabilization, not growth.

**Gaps.**
- There are no broker notes after the Q3 results (29 Oct 2026) in the folder.
- No broker gives a quantified buy-side ("whisper") number for research.
- Barclays' "Research and Applied consumables: flat to slightly down" bullet (Feb) conflicts with management's stated guide.
- The JPM survey sample is small (30 research customers, self-selected Illumina users).
