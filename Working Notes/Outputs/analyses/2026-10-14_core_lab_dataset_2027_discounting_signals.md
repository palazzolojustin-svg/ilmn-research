# Core-lab price dataset: ten signals that Illumina will have to discount more in 2027

*Prepared 14 Oct 2026 from `Outputs/datasets/2026-10-13_core_lab_price_per_gb.xlsx`. Figures are internal (own-institution) rates in USD per Gb at 2x150 unless noted, and "latest" means October 2026.*

**Same-core** comparisons use only cores that price both items, which removes differences between cores. University of Illinois is excluded from the change counts, because its series jumps both ways (+61%, −43%, +39%) in a way that looks like extraction noise.

## Read this first

Core prices are not Illumina's net prices. Cores add labour and overhead, but external rates sit only about 13% above internal rates (median, 16 cores). That suggests markups are thin and reagent cost moves straight through, so a core's price change is a reasonable, lagged read on what it pays Illumina.

The samples are small: about 10 cores per table have more than one observed price. Treat the findings as directional.

## The ten findings

1. **Cores started cutting NovaSeq X prices in mid-to-late 2026, after a year of increases.**
   - Every NovaSeq X 10B price change in 2025 was an increase, for example Utrecht +37% in June 2025.
   - From March 2026 the changes are cuts at:
     - Kansas Medical Center (−40%);
     - Case Western (−29%, then −22%);
     - Florida (−4%);
     - EPFL (−33%);
     - UC San Diego (−18%);
     - Northwestern (−13%).
   - On the 25B, UConn (−50%), Northwestern (−14%) and UCSD (−9%) cut in August–October 2026.
   - Discounting appears to be starting before the 35B flow cell arrives, not after.

2. **The cuts are deepest on the 10B, the flow cell most exposed to mid-size rivals.**
   - Among cores whose 10B price changed, the median move was −13%, against +3% on the 25B.
   - The 10B's same-core premium over the 25B is now a median of only 1.51x, and as low as 1.02x.
   - This is the volume tier where Element's VITARI and Roche's flexible-loading pitch compete.

3. **Ultima undercuts the 25B by four to six times at the cores that offer both.**
   - Ultima's UG100 runs $0.75–1.03/Gb across three cores, against a 25B median of $2.81.
   - At Cornell and Minnesota, which price both, the 25B costs 4.2x and 6.2x the Ultima price.
   - Even the cheapest 25B core ($1.51, UConn) is about twice Ultima's price.
   - That gap is the price umbrella the 35B and any rebates will have to close.

4. **Six cores already sell the 25B below Illumina's own $2.00/Gb list claim.**
   - The six:
     - UConn $1.51;
     - NCI Frederick $1.64;
     - Virginia Tech $1.86;
     - UChicago $1.88;
     - Dana-Farber $1.90;
     - UCSD $1.94.
   - Including labour, these imply reagent net prices well below list, so bespoke discounting is already common at strategic accounts.
   - The Open Offer's most-favoured-customer terms let concessions like these spread to comparable labs.

5. **The 25B price spread is threefold, a sign of negotiated discounts.**
   - Internal 25B prices run from $1.51 to $4.67/Gb (interquartile range $2.27–3.49) for the same consumable.
   - A spread that wide means price is negotiated account by account.
   - As rivals quote more labs, the high end of the range is the most exposed.

6. **The remaining NovaSeq 6000 work sits on small, expensive flow cells, so converting it is a large per-Gb cut.**
   - Same-core, the 6000 S4 costs 2.06x the 25B per Gb (13 cores).
   - The S2's median is $10.57/Gb and the SP's $19.20/Gb; same-core, the SP costs 6.96x the 25B (12 cores).
   - That is consistent with the 6000's 17% of HT Gb carrying 41% of HT revenue at Q2 2026.
   - Each tail customer that converts gives up a large step in price per Gb, which mechanically lowers Illumina's revenue per Gb in 2027.

7. **Cores are pricing the 6000 out.**
   - All five 6000 S4 price changes were increases (median +8%; MIT +40%).
   - 26 of the 43 cores that list NovaSeq X no longer list any 6000 option.
   - Rising 6000 prices and disappearing 6000 lines push the tail to convert, and the conversion itself is a price cut (finding 6).

8. **Element is cheaper than Illumina's mid-throughput NextSeq at the cores that offer it.**
   - AVITI High Output's median is $8.29/Gb, with five of seven cores at $5.22–8.73.
   - That is below the NextSeq P4 ($10.80) and P3 ($14.37) medians. At Cornell, AVITI costs 0.43x the P4.
   - Illumina's small 1.5B flow cell costs 2.9x the 25B same-core, and the P4 4.6x.
   - Defending the mid tier, where Illumina's per-Gb prices are highest, will take price.

9. **Rivals sit on the same price list at many cores, and they are cutting too.**
   - 14 of the 68 cores with current prices list AVITI or Ultima, and 7 of them list a rival alongside NovaSeq X.
   - That lets customers compare prices line by line.
   - Northwestern cut AVITI 19% in October 2026.
   - Minnesota, which already runs almost all of its in-house work on Element or Ultima, prices Ultima at $0.75/Gb.

10. **The newest prices are the lowest.** The cuts above landed in August–October 2026, just ahead of the 35B (Illumina roadmap: H1 2027). If Illumina prices the 35B to close even part of the gap to Ultima and Roche, the current cut pattern widens rather than reverses.

## What argues against it

- **Most cores held the 25B flat.**
  - The cross-core 25B median barely moved: $2.89 in Oct 2024 (8 cores) against $2.81 in Oct 2026 (38 cores).
  - Among cores whose 25B changed, more raised than cut (6 against 3).
  - Harvard and others raised 25B prices.
- **The data is thin.** It covers about 10 cores per table, mostly North American and European academic sites.
- **Clinical labs are missing.** The large clinical labs, where most of Illumina's volume sits, publish no price lists.
- **The cuts may be a lagged pass-through, not a new discounting cycle.** They could reflect Illumina repricing to cores that happened earlier, and four data points per table cannot separate the two.
