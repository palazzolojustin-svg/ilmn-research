# Competitor Sequencing Launches in H2 2026, with Price per Gb

*Prepared 2026-10-28 from the Core Notes (09–15), peer transcripts (PacBio, Oxford Nanopore, Roche, 10x), sell-side notes (UBS, JPM, Barclays, RBC) and expert calls. "Calc" means converted from a per-genome price at about 100 Gb per 30X human genome, or about 62 Gb per 20X PacBio HiFi genome. Price per Gb is the customer's price, which is the competitor's revenue per Gb.*

## Answer

Only one competitor is bringing a genuinely new short-read sequencer to market in the second half of 2026, and it is the one that matters for Illumina:

- **Element Biosciences VITARI.** It is due to ship in H2 2026, at roughly **$1.0 per Gb**. That is about half the roughly $2.0/Gb UBS calculates for Illumina's NovaSeq X Plus on the same basis.
- **Roche AXELIOS 1.** It launched on 29 Jun 2026 at about **$1.5/Gb**. It is technically an H1 launch, but its placements are ramping through H2.

Ultima plans a chemistry upgrade, Solaris 2.0 Max, for later in 2026, but it is unpriced and not confirmed shipped. The long-read companies' H2 items are upgrades without a new published price per Gb. MGI and Thermo have no H2 2026 launch in the folder.

## What slipped to 2027 or later

- **PacBio's** new high-throughput platform: "later next year" (CEO, 16 Sep 2026).
- **Oxford Nanopore's** PromethION Q-Line: late 2027.
- **Oxford Nanopore's** four-genomes-per-flow-cell workflow, aimed at below $200 per genome (about $2/Gb): no date.

## Caveats

- **VITARI's numbers are not settled.** The garbled UBS line ("~40% discount to NovaX+ (list is $689k)") can be read either way. The evidence ledger's correction treats $689k as VITARI's own price, with X Plus at about $1.15M, and that fits expert quotes (VITARI about $700k, X over $1M). One expert's "over $70 for a 50X genome" would mean about $0.45/Gb and looks mis-recalled.
- **Attribution.** In the 29 Jul expert call it was the interviewer who said "$100 genome later this year," not the Abbott scientist. The claim comes from Element's own materials.
- **Not all prices are like for like.** Roche's $150 excludes library prep and assumes the sensor module is reused 20 times. PacBio's price is for a 20X long-read genome, which is not comparable with short-read per-Gb prices.
- **None of these is an audited street price.** They are list prices or company claims.
