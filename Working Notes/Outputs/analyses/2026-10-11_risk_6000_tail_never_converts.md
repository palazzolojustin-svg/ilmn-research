# Risk to the thesis: the NovaSeq 6000 tail never converts

Prepared 11 Oct 2026. Sources: ILMN calls Nov 2024–Q2'26 (tail statements); Q4'25 deck (installed base, pull-through); Illumina retirement page (fetched 11 Oct 2026); the clinical-consumables ledger E015, E019, E053, E116, E130, E250, E359, E393 and file 103; primer working files 01 and 04.

## Short answer

Yes, it is a real risk, but to one leg of the thesis: the 2027 conversion drag. It does not threaten the whole bear case. Part of the tail never converting is already management's base case. If the tail froze completely from today, about $90–130M of revenue drag would disappear from our 2027 estimate. That is roughly 3–4 points of sequencing consumables growth and ~$0.35–0.50 of EPS, enough to lift our FY27 EPS (~$5.75) to roughly consensus ($6.11). Even a frozen tail keeps shrinking in dollars, though. And the other legs (35B pricing, research with no growth, clinical deceleration, gross margin, valuation) are unaffected.

## Why "never converts" is partly already the base case

- **Management expects a permanent tail.**
  - The CEO's rule of thumb is that ~20% of clinical tests "will stay" on the 6000 (Feb 2025).
  - The CFO said some "will never move" (Nov 2024).
  - The CEO calls the rest "laggards" with FDA-approved assays and a "long tail" after 2026 (Q1'26).
- **Clinical X share is near the floor.** It was 78% in Q2'26, against a target of 80–85% by end-2026. That leaves only ~2–7 points of clinical volume to convert, not 22.
- **Our base assumed modest further conversion.** The X's share of high-throughput gigabases goes from 83% to 87–89% by end-2027, which is the $90–130M drag. The risk is that the last few points also stall.

## What keeps the tail in place

- **No end-of-life date.** Illumina has announced no end of sale, service or reagent supply for the 6000. Its retirement page lists only the iSeq, MiniSeq and MiSeq research versions. The MiSeq precedent gave ~4 years of service after its notice, so even a 2027 notice would mean support into ~2031.
- **Regulated tests are anchored.** The only high-throughput diagnostic-grade sequencer is the NovaSeq 6000Dx. FDA records show no approved X version for Foundation, Guardant, Tempus or Caris tests. Revalidation costs $100–200K at a small lab and up to $200M for an FDA test, and mid-trial switches are "a no-no".
- **Customers are still buying 6000s.** Q2'26 saw ">10" sold, against ~30 in all of FY25.

## Why 6000 revenue shrinks anyway

Derived 6000 high-throughput consumables fell from ~$286M (Q2'25) to ~$219M (Q2'26), −23%. These are our figures, not disclosed. Three forces work even without conversion:
- **Decommissions.** About 330 machines were retired in FY25, and the base fell from ~1,770 (end-2023) to ~1,360 (end-2025).
- **List price cuts.** S4 flow-cell lists fell 16–24% by July 2025 [Salk price lists].
- **Lower pull-through.** Annual consumables per machine went from $700–800K (FY24 guide) to $670K (FY25) to a $600–650K FY26 guide.

So a "frozen" tail still declines, perhaps 5–10% a year in dollars, against the ~20% our base assumes with conversion.

## Longer-term twist

A tail that doesn't move to the X is also the pool most exposed to rivals. When a 6000 end-of-life is eventually announced, labs must revalidate anyway.
- **Experts on that moment:** "AVITI is going to win" at 6000 sites, and the 6000's end of life "is going to create an opportunity for a lot of other companies".
- **The trade-off.** The risk to the bear case is a 2027–28 timing risk. Beyond that, the tail turns from an over-earning cushion into a share-loss risk.

## Follow-up: translating clinical volume conversion into revenue

**Question:** "If ~5 points of clinical volume convert, can I assume ~15% of revenue converts per year?"

**Answer:** No. The mapping runs like this.

**Inputs** [our calculation]:
- Clinical 6000 consumables are about $175M a quarter (~80% of the ~$219M 6000 pool).
- At a 3.4x price ratio, the 22% of clinical volume still on the 6000 earns about 49% of clinical high-throughput (HT) dollars (~$358M a quarter).

**Each point of clinical volume that converts:**
- moves ~$8M a quarter of 6000 revenue;
- reprices it at the X rate (~29% of the old price);
- costs ~$5.6M a quarter net, or ~$22M a year.

| Points converted | Share of the clinical 6000 pool that moves | Net annual revenue loss | % of clinical HT revenue | % of clinical consumables |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 9% | ~$45M | ~3% | ~2% |
| 5 | 23% | ~$112M | ~8% | ~6% |
| 7 | 32% | ~$157M | ~11% | ~8% |

**Timing.** The 80–85% target is for end-2026, so most of the remaining points move in H2'26. They then hit 2027 through the year-on-year comparison. After that, the CEO's ~20% "will stay" rule implies only ~1 point a year.

**Illustrative path:** 76/78/80/82% through 2026 (average 79%) and 83/84/84.5/85% through 2027 (average ~84%). That gives ~$115M of 2027 drag, consistent with our $90–130M base. Do not model 5 points a year in perpetuity.

## Follow-up: the "1:1" chart and the "why now"

**Question:** the X's gigabase share and revenue share have moved roughly 1:1 (+15 and +16 points). Does that mean pricing hasn't been hit yet, so the coming cohort is the pricing catalyst?

**Answer:** The data say the opposite. Pricing has already been hit; the catalyst is real but comes from a different source.

**Parallel lines mean the X's relative price fell.** At a constant X-to-6000 price ratio, revenue share should rise faster than gigabase share as gigabase share climbs. The curve is convex, with a slope of about 1.1 at 68% and about 1.6 at 83%. Holding the Q1'25 ratio (0.355) constant, an 83% gigabase share implies about 63% revenue share, against the actual 59%.

| Quarter | X Gb share | X revenue share | Implied X/6000 price ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1'25 | 68% | 43% | 0.355 |
| Q2'25 | 69% | 44% | 0.353 |
| Q3'25 | 78% | 51% | 0.294 |
| Q4'25 | 80% | 55% | 0.306 |
| Q1'26 | 82% | 55% | 0.268 |
| Q2'26 | 83% | 59% | 0.295 |

The implied ratio fell about 17%. Rounding and flow-cell mix add noise, but the decline holds in every rounding [ledger E265, E270].

**Conversions have also been the main price hit all along.** Each converted gigabase loses 60–70% of its price. That is why high-throughput revenue grew ~4.5% while gigabases grew >30%. The remaining tail is smaller and stickier, so conversion drag shrinks from here: ~$22M a year per clinical point, and ~$90–130M in 2027.

**The real "why now" is a shift to like-for-like price cuts on the X.** As conversions end, the price driver becomes direct cuts on X volume (83% of gigabases, ~60% of HT revenue):
- the 35B flow cell in H1 2027, about −20% per gigabase for big users;
- contracts that only let prices fall;
- Roche, Element and Ultima quotes.

Illustratively, if 30–50% of X gigabases move to the 35B during 2027, the 2027 hit is about $40–80M, growing in 2028 [our calculation]. Price erosion moves from the shrinking 6000 tail onto Illumina's core product.

The primer chart caption (section 5.2) was corrected on 11 Oct 2026; it had wrongly said gigabases moved "much faster than dollars".

## Follow-up: is Q1→Q2'26 (+1 point of gigabases, +4 of revenue) the start of a "1:3" cohort?

**Question:** gigabase share rose 1 point while revenue share rose 4 in the quarter clinical growth slowed. Does that mark a 1:3 cohort that drives the slowdown?

**Answer:** Probably not. Three reasons:

1. **The curve gets steeper, but not to 1:3 yet.** At today's price ratio the slope of revenue share against gigabase share is about 1.7 at 83%. It reaches about 2.0 at 88% [our calculation]. A one-quarter 1:4 reading is mostly noise.
   - Shares are rounded to whole points, so the true moves could be 0–2 points of gigabases and 3–5 of revenue.
   - Q2 also reversed Q1's anomaly, when revenue share was flat while gigabase share rose 2 points.
   - Over two quarters (Q4'25 to Q2'26) the ratio is +3 to +4, about 1.3, close to the curve.
2. **The share ratio says nothing about dollars lost per point.**
   - Each point of gigabases that converts costs the same dollars wherever it sits on the curve: the 6000 price minus the X price. That is about $22M a year per clinical point.
   - The steeper share slope is arithmetic: the 6000 sells each gigabase for ~3.4x. Later cohorts are not more damaging per point.
3. **Q2's revenue-share jump came from X growth, not tail conversion.**
   - Derived X consumables rose ~14% q/q against ~9.5% growth in the average installed base, so per-box pull-through rose from $1.19M to $1.24M.
   - Derived 6000 revenue fell only ~3% q/q.
   - Clinical conversion was slow (76% → 78%). Faster conversion therefore did not cause the 20% → 15% clinical slowdown. Comparisons, the Middle East and price within the X are better explanations.

**Test in the Q3'26 deck.** At a price ratio of 0.28–0.31, an 84–85% gigabase share should give a 61–64% revenue share [ledger E270].
- **On the curve:** nothing new.
- **Below 60%:** further price slippage on the X.
- **Above 64% with gigabases ≤84%:** a genuinely steeper relationship, worth revisiting.

**Directional summary.** The user's direction is half right.
- **Right:** the remaining tail is revenue-dense (about 17% of HT gigabases but about 41% of HT revenue). Revenue share will rise faster than gigabase share from here, and each remaining point matters more to revenue than its volume suggests.
- **Wrong:** the dollar cost per converted point is not rising, and total remaining conversion damage is shrinking because fewer and stickier points remain.
- **Wrong:** Q2's clinical slowdown was not caused by faster conversion.
- **The accelerating price risk** is cuts on the X itself (35B, contracts, rivals).

## Follow-up: is the qualitative story right?

The user's story: shares moved ~1:1 because each conversion's extra volume offset its price cut. Now the ratio is steepening because the remaining converters are the highest-volume 6000 users, so upcoming conversions carry the most pricing headwind.

**Verdict:** the conclusion is plausible, but for a different reason than the one given.

1. **Volume offset: right in spirit.**
   - The CFO said 40 converted clinical customers saw volume offset price in year one.
   - Those uplift gigabases sit on the cheap X. They push the X's gigabase share up without lifting its revenue share as much.
   - That is why the average ~1:1 sits below the >1 slope a constant price ratio implies.
2. **The steepening ratio: not evidence about the cohort.** A rising revenue-to-gigabase share slope is mechanical: the 6000 sells at ~3.4x per gigabase, so the curve is convex. It would steepen with any cohort, and Q2'26 alone is noise.
3. **"Highest-volume customers remain": not supported.**
   - The big labs moved first. JPM says all large CLIA labs have moved or are moving, and the CEO says new-assay and exome-to-genome movers are the largest cohorts [Wolfe Nov 2025; E028].
   - The remaining tail has two parts. One is small, low-volume labs (on 10B-type flow cells, which management says are priced "more like high-volume 6000 pricing"). The other is regulated or FDA-approved assays, some at large labs (Foundation's F1CDx and Caris's MI Cancer Seek on the 6000/6000Dx).
   - The tail is defined by switching cost, not size.
4. **Why later conversions could still hurt more per gigabase.**
   - Early converters often moved alongside new or bigger tests (exome to genome, new assays on the X), so volume rose to offset price.
   - Late converters will mostly be fixed legacy or regulated assays moved for cost, with little reason to sequence more. They get the full 60–70% price cut with little volume offset.
   - So conversion drag per converted gigabase likely rises, even though the number of points left falls.
   - The timing is lumpy and slow. Regulated assays need FDA supplements (F1CDx took ~11 months on the 6000), and some labs are still buying 6000s.

**Net.** The remaining conversions are probably the least volume-accretive, so the most net price-dilutive per unit. Total dollars remain bounded (~$90–130M in 2027) because the tail is small and sticky. The larger 2027–28 price risk is still the X itself.

**Does the remaining tail have offsetting volume?** Largely no, with two qualifiers [our inference from E028, E250 and Q1'26 call]:
- **Small, low-volume labs.** Demand is fixed by their test orders, so conversion adds little volume. But they mostly won't convert to an X at all: they can't fill one, and it costs ~$1M. They more likely stay, outsource to providers running the X, or move to Element. The CEO's "never-convert, low volume" cohort.
- **Regulated / FDA-approved tests.**
  - The approved assay fixes the panel and depth, so gigabases per test don't rise on a new platform. Test counts still grow with patient orders, but that happens on either platform, so it is not an offset from conversion.
  - These tests can't move until a diagnostic-grade NovaSeq X exists (none found) and the FDA approves each test on it.
  - Illumina could price diagnostic-grade X consumables above research X pricing, which would soften the cut.
- **Net:** less volume offset per converted gigabase than earlier cohorts, but slow, lumpy timing and some pricing control for Illumina on any Dx version.

## Evidence for the composition of the tail, and a correction

**What the tail looks like.** The tail has at least four parts. The earlier "two groups" description missed the third, which is likely the largest.
1. **Small, low-volume labs.** The CEO's cohort taxonomy names a "never-convert, low volume" group that stays on smaller flow cells at "more like high-volume 6000 pricing" [Wolfe, 19 Nov 2025; ledger E028; management claim]. Support also comes from the economics: an X costs ~$1M and needs volume to fill it [expert calls]. No count or dollar size is disclosed.
2. **Regulated and FDA-approved tests.**
   - The CEO calls them "laggards" with regulated or FDA-approved assays on the 6000 (Q1'26).
   - **Regulatory record:** F1CDx (Foundation) was approved on the 6000 in Apr 2024, and Caris's MI Cancer Seek PMA is specified on the 6000. openFDA showed no PMA supplement naming the X for F1CDx, F1 Liquid CDx, G360 CDx, xT CDx or Shield, and no NovaSeq X Dx was found [ledger E250, E287, E296].
   - **Anecdote:** a regulated Italian exome lab was still validating the 6000 in Nov 2025 [E251].
   - **Gaps:** the Guardant SSED was unreadable, and test volumes are not disclosed.
3. **Legacy lab-developed tests at large labs (omitted earlier).**
   - The CEO's "cohort 2": old assays stay on the 6000 while new, larger assays go to the X. It is one of the two "absolutely the largest" cohorts (Wolfe, Nov 2025).
   - In Feb 2026 he said customers keep the 6000 for "traditional assays", which is why 6000 pull-through held up. The CFO (Nov 2024) said customers prefer building new tests to revalidating old ones.
   - JPM (15 Jun 2026): all major CLIA labs have transitioned or are transitioning, which is consistent with partial moves.
4. **The research remainder.** About 8–10% of research high-throughput volume is still on the 6000.

**What is not known.** Illumina does not disclose how the 6000 base or its gigabases split by customer size, test type or regulatory status. The shares of each group are unknown, and the >10 new 6000s sold in Q2'26 are not attributed. The groups are inferred from management descriptions and regulatory records, not measured.

**Bearing on offsetting volume.** Groups 2 and 3 are mature, fixed assays, so they are unlikely to add volume when moved. The conclusion that later conversions bring little offsetting volume holds, with lower confidence on how big each group is.

**Is group 3 (legacy LDTs at large labs) volume-inelastic?** Largely yes.
- **The mechanism.** Gigabases equal tests × Gb per test. The validated assay fixes Gb per test, and test counts depend on orders and reimbursement, not on sequencing price. Porting the same assay to the X adds almost no volume, so the full ~70% price cut on those gigabases lands.
- **Qualifier: conversions often coincide with an assay upgrade.** Labs that must revalidate anyway may move to a bigger panel, deeper coverage or a genome. That is the CEO's "new, bigger assays on the X" pattern, and it is where earlier offsetting volume came from. A pure port gets no offset; an upgrade does.
- **Qualifier: test growth continues on either platform** (e.g. tissue profiling up ~18–31% at Caris and Tempus). That supports revenue but is not an offset created by conversion.
- **Corollary: inelastic means a weak incentive to switch.** Savings are small relative to total test cost (~$63 a sample on TSO 500 against $600–1,100 all-in). So these labs likely convert slowly, which keeps the over-earning longer, but each conversion is close to a pure price cut.
- **Modelling rule:** treat group 3 conversions as 1:1 gigabases with ~70% revenue loss on the moved dollars, unless paired with an assay upgrade.

**Evidence on volume-inelasticity of legacy clinical assays.** The evidence is indirect: management hedges and expert opinion, with no before/after data.
- **For:**
  - **Management's cohort taxonomy.** Old validated assays stay on the 6000; growth comes from new or bigger assays on the X; price-motivated movers are under 20% and mostly done [Wolfe, 19 Nov 2025]. Customers "keep the 6000 for validated, traditional assays" [Q4'25 call].
  - **The 40-customer cohort's flat year one.** Flat revenue after a 60–70% price cut needs ~2.5–3.3x the gigabases, which only new assays or genomes can supply [E303].
  - **Elasticity estimates.** Post-2022, halving price yields ~25% more volume [expert]. Management says ~20% more for counting applications (TD Cowen, Mar 2026). The 2019 data imply revenue-to-volume elasticity of ~0.3 [E375].
  - **Demand limits.** Reimbursement, samples ("sequenced everything in the freezer"), saturation of NIPT and tissue profiling, and MRD cadence [experts].
  - **Lock-in.** Validation cost "sometimes" outweighs the saving (CEO, Q3'24). Revalidation costs $100–200K. Adaptive's clonoSEQ stayed on the NextSeq 550 for about a decade at ~10x the per-read cost.
- **Against:**
  - **Depth, not test count, responds to price.** Management says labs offer "substantially larger assays" within reimbursement (Wolfe, Nov 2025). Customers are adding methylation markers needing deeper sequencing (Q3'24). Caris: "always deeper, not cheaper" [E092].
  - **The cohort accelerated in year two,** though the sample is self-selected and the split between old and new assays is unknown.
  - **"Aggressive pricing brings aggressive volume growth"** (Q1'26), though that was in the exome-to-genome context.
- **Missing:**
  - before/after gigabases for any single assay moved to the X (Guardant360 Liquid moved in May 2026; volume not disclosed);
  - NIPT movers' revenue versus volume;
  - the old- versus new-assay split in the cohort.
- **Caveat:** the TSO 500 "$113 vs $50 per sample" figure comes from the earlier scrape and was not re-located in this pass.
- **Verdict:** test count is inelastic, but depth per test is partly elastic, mostly when a lab revalidates and upgrades.

## The 40-customer converted cohort: what is actually disclosed

**The disclosure.**
- **Q3'25 call** (Sources line 154): the CFO cited "the 40 customers that have fully transitioned to X". Volume offset price in year one, then revenue and volume both accelerated in year two. He called it "elasticity of demand".
- **Q3'25 deck** (Sources lines 176–190). The slide shows clinical consumables revenue and Gb, split X and 6000, for 2023, 2024 and 2025E, with no axis values; the only labels are "+DD" and "+LSD".
  - The footnote says n=40 "fully or largely transitioned", including a subset of top-tier accounts, with 2025E annualised from YTD at 28 Sep 2025.
  - "Fully" on the call versus "fully or largely" in the deck is a small inconsistency.
- **Wolfe, 19 Nov 2025:**
  - Schenkel read year one off the chart as "probably under 5% growth", and the CEO accepted "almost in an equilibrium".
  - The cohort contains customers already on the 6000.
  - Price-motivated movers (e.g. NIPT) are not excluded.
- **JPM conference, 13 Jan 2026:** year one "might be a little flattish". Illumina will not continue to share the cohort.
- **Brokers:** no broker commentary found.

**The size of the price cut differs by flow-cell route** [core price lists, scrape file 11; management].

| Route | Cut in price per Gb | Gb multiple for flat to +5% revenue |
|---|---|---|
| CFO's blended claim | 60–70% | 2.5–3.5x |
| S4 → 25B (median of core lists) | ~53% | ~2.1–2.2x |
| 10B vs S4 | ~35% | ~1.5–1.6x |
| S1/S2 → 25B | 78–82% | ~4.5–5.8x |

The earlier "2.5–3.3x" applies only at the CFO's 60–70%. On the most common S4-to-25B route it is ~2.1x.

**Test growth alone does not close the gap.**
- **Test growth supplies only ~1.2–1.6x.** Named customers' Q2'26 test growth was +18% to +63%.
- **So per-test gigabases must have risen.** On the 25B route, flat revenue needs ~2.1x total Gb, so Gb per test must have risen ~33–86%.
- **Which route the big movers used.** The CEO said the two largest cohorts use the 25B (new or bigger assays; exome to genome), while low-volume and price-motivated movers stay on the 10B [E028]. On the 10B, test growth alone could explain flat revenue.
- **Named examples of bigger or new tests on the X:** Natera Signatera Genome (WGS-MRD tiers in its Jan 2025 amendment), Tempus–Personalis (WGS MRD), GeneDx genomes, Caris ("always deeper"), Guardant Reveal and Shield. Adaptive is the counter-example: it pocketed savings as margin.

**Gaps.**
- no axis values;
- no test-count versus Gb-per-test split;
- selection bias (Illumina chose the 40);
- the cohort ended in Sep 2025 and will not be updated;
- some of the offset could be price lags, discounts or under-filled flow cells [E045].

**Verdict.** The support is moderate. Disclosed shape, management attribution and named launches, plus arithmetic that rules out test-count growth alone on the 25B route. But the cohort's volume sources are not measured.

## Extra evidence search (three agents: folder, customer filings, case studies)

**Result: the evidence for "converters sequenced more per test" got weaker, not stronger.**
- **Customer filings point to banked savings, not reinvestment** [web, 2023–26; several items from search snippets only].
  - **Guardant:** Guardant360 Liquid moved to the X in May 2026, saving ~$200 per test. Management said it would let part of the gross profit drop through and fund the Shield commercial build-out.
  - **Adaptive:** sequencing gross margin rose from 53% (2024) to 71% (Q4'25), partly credited to the X ("more samples per run").
  - **GeneDx:** gross margin rose from 56% to 70% on lower cost per test.
  - **Natera, Tempus, Caris:** no X-specific disclosure.
  - No customer said the X let it deepen coverage or enlarge panels.
- **Test-count growth alone can roughly explain the volume** [folder calc]. Weighting named labs' Q2'26 test growth by assumed Gb per test gives ~+27% (excluding Guardant) to ~+51% (including it) at unchanged intensity. That is near management's "above 30%" without any per-test increase. The result is very sensitive to the assumed Guardant Gb per test.
- **Only qualitative case studies were found.**
  - Uppsala: +40% samples, genome and RNA tests added.
  - UMC Utrecht: exome to genome analysis.
  - MedGenome: a new genome prenatal test.
  - Fulgent: coverage 40x → 60–70x, but via TruPath chemistry, not the X.
  - No lab published before/after depth or cost per sample.
- **Unit costs are flat to up.** Natera's cost per test rose 9%; Caris's cost per case rose 5–6%. That is consistent with mix and non-sequencing costs, not proof of more depth.

**Implication.** The "volume offset price" story rests on Illumina's own 40-account cohort and management statements. Independent customer evidence points to labs banking X savings as margin. If that is typical, conversions are closer to pure price cuts than the cohort suggests, which supports the price-headwind view.

**Open tension.** The cohort's reported flat year-one revenue at a ~53% price cut needs ~2.1x Gb. Test growth of 20–60% alone gives at most ~1.6x. The cohort is selected, the cut on its route may have been smaller, or discounts and price lags softened it. This is unresolved without the cohort's numbers.

## Signals to watch

**Tail freezing (bad for the thesis):**
- clinical X share stuck at 78–80% in the Q3 and Q4 decks;
- derived 6000 revenue holding at $210M or more a quarter;
- FY26 6000 pull-through at $650K or more (Feb 2027);
- more 6000 sales and no end-of-life notice.

**Tail converting (consistent with the thesis):**
- clinical X share above 80–82%;
- derived 6000 revenue below $210M;
- an end-of-sale notice.
