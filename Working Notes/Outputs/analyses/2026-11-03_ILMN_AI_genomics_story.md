# Illumina's "AI genomics" story: sequencing volume or data sales? (3 Nov 2026)

## Answer

Management's pitch is mainly about **selling data and AI tools to pharma**, with extra sequencing volume as a secondary benefit ("it will be a blend", CEO Thaysen, Q3 2026 call). In 2026-27 both effects are small and undisclosed. AI is not visible in Illumina's reported demand today: research & applied consumables, where AI-driven data generation would show up, fell 6% outside China in Q3 2026, and growth is coming from clinical customers.

## What Illumina is actually selling

**BioInsight** (launched Oct 2025) is the data and AI unit. Its first product is the **Billion Cell Atlas**:

- **What it is.** Illumina runs Perturb-seq experiments: it switches off genes one at a time in cells and sequences the result. The output is a large, standardized map of how genes drive cell behaviour, which can be used to train AI "foundation models" of biology (large models adaptable to many tasks) and to validate drug targets.
- **Who pays.** Pharma consortium members: AstraZeneca, Merck and Eli Lilly (JPM, Jan 2026), three unnamed partners added in Aug 2026 for six in total, and GSK named as "the latest" (Q3 2026 call). It is unclear whether GSK is one of the six or a seventh.
- **How they pay.** Management has described the model as data-set creation fees, later subscriptions, and custom model building (CFO, Q4 2025 call; CEO, TD Cowen Mar 2026). The revenue booked so far looks like project or service revenue.
- **Where the revenue is booked.** "Sequencing service and other" revenue was $151m, $154m and $158m in Q1-Q3 2026. Q2 was up 14%, "helped by" Atlas revenue, and Q3 was up 8% RoW organic. The Atlas is an unquantified slice of that line.
- **Progress.**
  - More than 300m cells had been delivered by Q2 2026.
  - The Atlas was "over halfway complete" by Q3 2026.
  - A 5-billion-cell second generation is planned.
  - Revenue booking began in Q2 2026, with "visible color" promised for 2027 (CFO).
- **The 2027 growth claim.** Management says "new products and BioInsight" will add 1-2 points of growth in 2027. That figure bundles multiomics assays (single-cell, spatial, SomaLogic proteomics) with the data business, so it is not an AI-only number. On a 2026 base of about $4.6bn it means roughly $45-95m. Thaysen says AI could make it "substantially bigger." Barclays says the claim cannot be verified without more disclosure (Aug 2026).
- **Software.** SpliceAI2 (Q3 2026) is the latest of these tools. It predicts how mutations disrupt RNA splicing, the step where a gene's message is cut and joined. It sits on the BioInsight platform alongside DRAGEN, the GPU-ported analysis software from the NVIDIA partnership. None of these tools has its own revenue disclosure.

## Does AI drive more sequencing volume?

Management's argument:

- Data sets built by individual labs are small and noisy. Models trained on them learn which lab the data came from rather than the biology, so pharma needs data generated at very large scale.
- Customers want "data sets… rather than the sequencing itself" (Q3 2026 call).

That argument supports selling data first and selling instruments and reagents second.

Evidence of actual volume today is thin:

- **Illumina.** No AI-attributed volume disclosure exists. Output in gigabases (Gb, a unit of DNA data) is up more than 30% year on year, but management credits clinical customers.
- **Channel checks.** Five interviews in Jul-Aug 2026 with US genomics cores and a biotech found no AI-driven demand. Growth comes from single-cell work and cheaper runs, and some of it is going to Element and Ultima.
- **Peers: AI demand is real but small.**
  - 10x Genomics calls AI "a significant and structural tailwind" and "unquestionably" incremental, but still "very much early days" and a low share of revenue (May 2026).
  - PacBio's Basecamp foundation-model deal is about $10-20m over 18 months (Barclays estimate).
  - Oxford Nanopore's UK Biobank work is about $20m in 2026 (RBC estimate).
- **Where AI volume does appear.** It shows up in single-cell and perturbation work rather than whole genomes. JPM, citing the company, says single-cell needs 3-15 times more sequencing per sample than a whole genome, and spatial 10-20 times.

## Risks to the story

1. **Price deflation.** AI data generation is cost-sensitive. 10x credits cheaper reads from Ultima and Roche with making mega-projects possible. Ultima is reportedly part of an AI data consortium with PacBio (Barclays). Volume growth may therefore come with falling price per Gb.
2. **Customers may own the data.** Most peer AI deals are customer-commissioned: the customer owns the model and buys sequencing as an input. Illumina's Atlas, by contrast, keeps the data proprietary and sells access to it.
3. **Data type.** Barclays argues that long reads and methylation, the strengths of Oxford Nanopore and PacBio, are better AI training data than short reads. This is an opinion with no data behind it.
4. **Commoditization.** Experts, including a former Illumina architect and a Roche precision-medicine lead, expect value to move from sequencing to analytics and AI layers over 5-10 years.

## What to watch

- The 2027 disclosure of BioInsight and Atlas revenue, and the growth of the services line.
- The Atlas partner count and pricing.
- Any inflection in research & applied consumables, the line where AI-driven research volume would show up.
- Whether the 1-2 point 2027 contribution is broken out between multiomics products and data.
