# Are other big labs echoing GeneDx's genome problem?

*12 Oct 2026. The question: GeneDx (Q2 2026 call, 3 Aug 2026) collects only about 32% of what it bills for outpatient genomes, down from 43% a year earlier. Its CFO says a genome costs nearly twice an exome, "almost entirely higher reagent costs", and it has moved to exome-first testing with a genome only if the exome finds nothing (a "reflex" order). Are other large labs saying the same things, and do these headwinds hit them?*

*Sources: one corpus agent (scrape findings CSV, clinical-consumables scrape files 09, 10, 12, 105 and 111_112, and Core Notes 16 and 18) and two web agents (Q2 2026 calls, releases, 10-Qs and payer documents). Quotes marked "summary" came from search summaries or transcript extractions and should be checked against full transcripts.*

## Answer

Mostly no.

**The pain is concentrated in germline testing.** The reimbursement problem sits with the labs that sell inherited-disease, hereditary-cancer and rare-disease tests: GeneDx, Myriad and Fulgent. All three cite payer friction, denials or collection problems.

**No other lab cites reagent cost as a reason to put less data into a test.** The large oncology and MRD labs are doing the opposite: their selling prices are rising, and they are adding data per test:
- Natera is building tumor-informed MRD from a whole genome.
- Tempus is buying Personalis and launched a whole-genome product.
- Caris runs a whole exome plus a whole transcriptome on every case. Its management: "It's always deeper, not cheaper for us."

The one exception is Guardant's Shield colon-cancer screen, which is moving to a lower-cost workflow that reads only methylation.

**The policy headwind lands on the same germline codes.** CMS's proposed 2027 Medicare lab fee schedule cuts genomic-sequencing codes by about 23% and molecular-pathology codes by about 22%. Proprietary tests are cut only about 2.4%, and any single year's cut is capped at 15% (proposed 21 Sep 2026; final rates due Nov 2026). That falls on generic exome, genome and hereditary codes. Branded oncology tests with special Medicare pricing (ADLT status) are in line for increases.

## Lab by lab

**Labs that echo GeneDx on payers:**

- **Myriad.**
  - **Revenue:** Q2 2026 revenue fell 11% to $190.7M. Hereditary-cancer revenue per test fell 15%, and prenatal revenue fell 16% (about −9% volume and −8% price).
  - **Management's explanation:** it blamed "payer-initiated revenue cycle friction", "higher denial rates" and "changing prior authorization requirements". It took an $11M prior-period collections hit and cut FY26 guidance to $770–790M.
  - **Sequencing cost:** no comment.
- **Fulgent.**
  - **Collections:** the collection rate fell, but it blames an operational billing-system change, not payers, and expects to recover.
  - **Guidance:** it cut FY26 revenue guidance to $330–340M from $350M.
  - **Data per test:** it raised genome coverage (40x to 60–70x) and moved whole genomes onto Illumina's TruPath.
- **BillionToOne.** More than $10M of claims are held up while national payers set up in-network codes, and receivables rose 80% in six months. Even so, average selling price (ASP) rose 21% to $551, and gross profit per test rose 30% on price alone.

**Labs that do not echo GeneDx:**

- **Natera.**
  - **Price:** Signatera ASP rose to about $1,275, guided stable for the rest of 2026. Pending Medicare approvals could add $150–200, against a long-term target of about $2,000. Days to collect fell by 4.
  - **Cost:** cost per test rose from about $216 to $255 because of mix and newer, not-yet-optimized products. Management sees "a lot of room" to bring COGS down.
  - **Data per test:** Signatera Genome uses roughly 10–15x the data of classic Signatera.
- **Tempus.**
  - **Price:** oncology average reimbursement is about $1,720. An FDA approval should move its tumor tests to ADLT pricing, worth about +$200 per test and about $85M a year from 2027.
  - **Margin:** diagnostics gross margin rose to 63.9% from 59.4%.
  - **Data per test:** it is adding whole-genome and MRD products.
- **Guardant.**
  - **Price:** oncology ASPs are stable. It expects a $8,455 ADLT rate for Guardant360 Liquid CDx, against about $5,000 today.
  - **Cost:** moving G360 Liquid to the NovaSeq X cut cost per test by about $200. Shield's cost per test falls from about $410 by about 15% by end-2026, toward $200 by 2028.
  - **Data per test:** Shield is moving to a methylation-only workflow, the only lower-data design found.
- **Caris.**
  - **Price:** record ASP above $3,850 (summary: clinical ASP up about 30%). It expects a price increase from the Medicare repricing cycle.
  - **Margin:** gross margin rose to 68% from 63%.
  - **Supplier:** it says "two competitors" now challenge "the longstanding sequencing supplier" and it is "looking at additional sequencing capabilities".
- **Adaptive (clonoSEQ).** Volume rose 43% and ASP rose 7% to $1,382. MRD gross margin rose to 72% from 64%, which a summary credits to the NovaSeq X transition.
- **Others.**
  - NeoGenomics' gross margin rose 260 bp, and Veracyte's to 72%.
  - Personalis has a gross margin of about 21%, citing "higher diagnostic test costs and lower reimbursement".
  - Labcorp, Quest, Exact (now inside Abbott), Baylor, Ambry and Revvity gave no relevant disclosure.

## Do the headwinds hit them?

- **Payer friction (denials, prior authorization, collections) does hit them, but unevenly.** It lands on germline, hereditary and rare-disease testing. Sector-wide, XiFin data show denials on lab, molecular and genomic claims rising from 8.0% (2024) to 11.2% (2025). Branded oncology and MRD tests are gaining price through ADLT and Medicare coverage decisions instead.
- **Reagent cost does not bind the high-priced oncology labs.** At $1,275–3,850 a test, sequencing is a manageable cost. These labs are banking sequencing savings as margin (Adaptive +8 points, Guardant −$200 a test) rather than complaining. GeneDx is different because its outpatient genome is poorly paid, so the reagent cost bites.

## What it means for the thesis

- **1C (volume).**
  - **Against:** oncology is adding data per test (Caris, Natera Genome, Tempus WGS), which works against the thesis.
  - **For:** MRD is still small in Illumina's total Gb (0.02–0.5% for Natera), and the germline exome-to-genome shift, the biggest potential Gb driver, is the part payers are squeezing. The proposed 2027 cuts to generic genomic codes would deepen that.
- **1B (discounting).**
  - **Labs are cutting costs.** Every large lab is pushing cost per test down, and the high-priced oncology labs keep the savings as margin rather than buying more sequencing. That means less volume elasticity at these accounts, so price cuts to them are mostly lost revenue for Illumina.
  - **Two labs have leverage over price.** Caris is openly weighing other sequencing suppliers. GeneDx is budgeting reagent "step improvements in 2027".

## Caveats

- Several quotes come from transcript extractions or search summaries, and some fetches were blocked. The Tempus figures come from its CEO letter and press release, without transcript text.
- Labcorp and Quest disclose almost nothing on genetics volumes.
- One agent could not locate GeneDx's "32% genome mix" in the transcript. It coincides with the 32% collection rate, so it may be confused with it. Last night's verifier did confirm both figures on the same transcript.
