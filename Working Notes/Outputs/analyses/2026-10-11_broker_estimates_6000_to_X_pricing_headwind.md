# Broker estimates of the NovaSeq 6000-to-X pricing headwind

*Prepared 11 Oct 2026. Sources: all 14 ILMN earnings calls (Q1 2023 to Q2 2026), all 16 conference transcripts, all 31 ILMN research notes, the Barclays sector note, the Barclays PacBio notes, the 3 Bloomberg consensus sheets, 5 expert calls, and the Addendum 16 evidence ledger. Quotes are verbatim from the files in `Working Notes/Sources/ILMN/`. The key quotes were re-checked against the source text.*

## The short answer

Very few brokers ever put their own number on this. In written research, no broker in the folder models a 6000 revenue line, a price-per-gigabase path or a dollar headwind from the conversion. Nearly every number comes from analysts' questions on earnings calls and at conferences, and most of those either do back-of-envelope arithmetic or repeat management's framework.

**What is being measured.** The NovaSeq X sequences much more cheaply per gigabase (Gb, a billion DNA letters) than the NovaSeq 6000. When a customer moves work to the X, Illumina earns less per Gb. The question is how big that cut is.

The estimates fall into three groups:

1. **Size of the per-Gb price cut from conversion:**
   - RBC: about 40%, from simple arithmetic.
   - Wolfe: a "30%-ish" hit in one quarter.
   - Evercore's description of the investor debate: $600 to $200 per sample, about two-thirds.
   - Management's own figure, for comparison: "easily more than 60% to 70%" on a Gb-to-Gb basis.
2. **Annual blended price decline:**
   - Evercore: 20–25%, citing the Aug 2024 Analyst Day.
   - Stifel and Canaccord: "mid-teens", citing management's framework.
   - J.P. Morgan: a "-DD%" (double-digit) decline historically, but only about 2–5% a year implied by its customer survey for 2027–28.
3. **When the headwind ends:**
   - J.P. Morgan: "largely gone exiting this year" (2026).
   - Barclays: per-Gb pricing on the X still "warrants watching", with a possible new round of price pressure in 2027 and beyond.
   - Wolfe: converted clinical customers grew "under 5%" in their first year.

## Each estimate in full

### Evercore ISI, Vijay Kumar: "600 to 200" (28 Nov 2023)

*Evercore ISI HealthCONx conference. File: `conference/ILMN_2023-11-28_Evercore_ISI_6th_Annual_HealthCONx_Conference.md`*

> "I think one of the questions has been, look, Illumina launched a new system, which lowered price per sample from 600 to 200, and there is no price elasticity of demand. We're not seeing volumes of 3 times, and that's why we're seeing some of these numbers, right? So when you think about that price elasticity of demand, how would you respond to that, you know, the question?"

Joydeep Goswami (then CFO) did not dispute the figure:

> "elasticity will happen over multiple years as well, right? So this is a -- we see this as a five or six year cycle while that price decline, most of that price decline is, it hits you right up front, but the benefits of that elasticity continue over a longer period of time as well."

**Reading:** a cut of about two-thirds in price per sample, which needs roughly 3x volume to break even. Kumar framed it as the investor debate rather than his own estimate. In the same session he read the 6000 pull-through guide (annual consumables revenue per installed machine) as "the million going down to 700k to 900k". Goswami corrected this to $800–900K, tracking toward the low end.

### TD Cowen, Dan Brennan: X pull-through math (8 Feb 2024)

*Q4 2023 call. File: `earnings_call/ILMN_2023Q4_Earnings_Call.md`*

> "On the X pull-through, we were coming up around $1 million in the fourth quarter and some quick math. We arrived at something like maybe $850,000 in 2024. So any comment on those numbers that they seem reasonable? And if so, could you just speak to like what would drive the assumption for that kind of sequential step down?"

Goswami: "I will disagree with them that we haven't done the math, but the math is not relevant, and we don't want to confuse you."

**Reading:** this measures revenue per instrument rather than the per-Gb price. It turned out too low: X pull-through averaged $1.3M in 2024.

### Canaccord, Kyle Mikson: 6000 pull-through guide "possibly aggressive" (8 Feb 2024)

*Q4 2023 call. Same file.*

> "On 6000 pull-through. The $700,000, $800,000 seems like possibly aggressive compared to how HiSeq kind of decline in its years, post the 2000 launch. So just kind of walk through what you're thinking about in terms of the decline, I guess, or deterioration in 6000 pull-through?"

Goswami: "about 50% of our Conn's [consumables] revenue is coming from clinical customers who have these validated assays… So the overall reduction in pull-through will be more mitigated compared to what you saw with HiSeq."

### RBC, Conor McNamara: "roughly a 40% headwind on pricing" (6 Aug 2024)

*Q2 2024 call. File: `earnings_call/ILMN_2024Q2_Earnings_Call.md`*

> "And just a follow-up to that. So if you -- I mean, simple math, if you say volumes were up 40% and sequencing consumables were flat, roughly a 40% headwind on pricing, is that the kind of pricing headwind we should think as the 5% conversion continues? Or was it -- is it higher early on in the NovaSeq X launch? Just trying to figure out what the pricing headwind will be?"

CFO Ankur Dhingra:

> "So it's higher early on and more of the volume moves to X, the impact of pricing continues to reduce… If you go back and think about holistically, there was a transition from 6k to X would have resulted overall, all-inclusive, easily more than 60% to 70% price decline on a Gb-to-Gb basis."

**Reading:** this is the clearest broker number. It is simple arithmetic: Gb up 40% against flat consumables revenue. Management did not confirm 40%. It answered with a larger like-for-like figure, 60–70%+, and said the blended drag shrinks as the mix shifts (about 5 points of HT revenue moving to the X each quarter at that time).

### RBC, Conor McNamara: the "25% volume, 15% pricing" scenario (13 Aug 2024)

*Illumina 2024 Strategy Update. File: `conference/ILMN_2024-08-13_Illumina_2024_Strategy_Update_Special_Call.md`. Question read aloud by Illumina IR.*

> "So from Conor McNamara at RBC. We showed a scenario of 25% volume growth with 15% pricing conversion headwinds. So does that imply double-digit revenue growth in high-throughput sequencing consumables?"

Dhingra: "that would relate to more like mid-to-high single-digit growth in the consumables… as X transitions, as the impact of price comes down, we will get a much larger share of that volume growth flowing into our revenue."

**Reading:** the 15% figure is Illumina's own slide scenario, not RBC's estimate. It became the "mid-teens" price assumption that analysts repeated through 2025.

### Evercore ISI, Vijay Kumar: "minus 20, minus 25" (4 Nov 2024)

*Q3 2024 call. File: `earnings_call/ILMN_2024Q3_Earnings_Call.md`*

> "The activity levels were about 40%, but I didn't hear a price for GB commentary. Was that in line with the minus 20, minus 25 that you expected at the Analyst Day?"

Dhingra:

> "Yes, it was in that 25-percentage range… since it accelerated, this quarter we did see the price impact towards the higher end of that range, closer towards 25 rather than 20, but it was still within that model range."

**Reading:** a 20–25% decline in blended price per Gb. Kumar attributes the range to the August 2024 Analyst Day. The Strategy Update transcript does not contain that figure (it shows 15%), so the 20–25% may have been on a slide. Management confirmed that Q3 2024 ran near 25%.

### Wolfe Research, Doug Schenkel: a "30%-ish pricing hit" (20 Nov 2024)

*Wolfe Research Healthcare Conference. File: `conference/ILMN_2024-11-20_CFO_Ankur_Dhingra_Hosts_Wolfe_Research_2024_Healthcare_Conference.md`*

> "I think on the Q3 call, you said high throughput and mid throughput sequencing activity, I think, was the term you used, grew 40% year-over-year… what we have in the model is we have high throughput, mid throughput consumable revenue growing 5% in the third quarter, high throughput being higher than mid throughput, but regardless, even if I'm off by half, like let's say it's 10% growth, there's a big delta between 40% and 5% or 10% revenue growth. What's the delta?"

Dhingra: "Largely price… the price decline during the quarter was towards the north -- higher end of the range that we've provided, that gets it closer to about, say, 25% decline on the price."

Schenkel's follow-up:

> "The concern or the focus or the controversy, whatever you want to call it, is where are you in getting to the other side of the pricing headwind. When we hear 40% or 30%-ish pricing hit in the quarter, should we be freaked out, or should we be encouraged that you're further along in the curve than maybe we would think?"

Dhingra said a little over 10% of HT volume moved from the 6000 to the X in the quarter. Once more than 75% of volume was on the X in H2 2025, "the price transition is getting largely annualized."

**Reading:** Wolfe's model implied a 30–40% price hit in the quarter. Management put it at about 25%. The 40% in the question is the volume growth figure, so the "30%-ish" is the more meaningful number.

### Stifel, Dan Arias, and Canaccord, Kyle Mikson: "mid-teens" (31 Jul 2025)

*Q2 2025 call. File: `earnings_call/ILMN_2025Q2_Earnings_Call.md`*

Stifel (Dan Arias):

> "Do you think you can stay in that 30% range in 3Q and 4Q? And if so, does that mean that we can still feel okay about that framework for 2026 that you've laid out, sort of mid-20s Gb growth and then a mid-teens pricing headwind as an offset?"

Dhingra said Gb growth could stay above 30%. He did not address the price figure.

Canaccord (Kyle Mikson):

> "On the growth framework, Jacob, you talked about in the past, I think, like volume growth in the 20% to 30% range, price decreases as well in the mid-teens… do you have the cushion to push price lower if you have to, if and when new competitive threats enter the fold, i.e. that Roche kind of unveils its pricing?"

CEO Jacob Thaysen: "on the pricing perspective, we are still very stable and we are within the expected range of the pricing decline we are seeing."

**Reading:** both analysts quote management's framework. CEO Thaysen repeated it at Bernstein in May 2025: "we are modeling that in the mid-teens".

### Wolfe Research, Doug Schenkel: clinical growth net of pricing (31 Jul 2025 and 30 Oct 2025)

*Q2 2025 call:*

> "a clear good guy is clinical will be over 60% of sales growing double digits with much higher volume offset by some pricing. So on a weighted basis, that could grow 6 points. You got maybe a 2- to 3-point growth headwind when it comes to the research end market."

*Q3 2025 call (`earnings_call/ILMN_2025Q3_Earnings_Call.md`):*

> "One, it seems plausible that research revenue could be flat to down low singles next year in a somewhat stable funding environment given the state of the transition to the X. Two, clinical revenue should grow, but maybe mid-single digits to high single digits as volumes grow and customers continue to transition to the X… And then third, China could be a 50 to 100 basis point headwind to growth."

**Reading:** net sizing rather than a standalone price figure. As it turned out, the FY26 clinical guide (double digits to mid-teens) came in well above Wolfe's 2026 clinical frame.

### Wolfe Research, Doug Schenkel: "under 5%" in year one (19 Nov 2025)

*Wolfe Research Healthcare Conference. File: `conference/ILMN_2025-11-19_7th_Annual_Wolfe_Research_Healthcare_Conference.md`*

> "a case study of the first 40 clinical customers that converted… The slide didn't have percentages for growth. I don't want to admit, but we might have got a ruler out to try to look at the -- But it looks like kind of the point of that slide is in the first full year of transition, you still grow. It's probably under 5% growth, if my measuring is about right. But then as you move past that, you start to grow with volume and the growth accelerates."

Thaysen: "customers are seeing a very strong price reduction when they go through that… the first year, you have actually the volume price is almost in an equilibrium and then you start to see the volume come back."

**Reading:** this is the only estimate of what conversion does to a single customer's revenue. Year one is roughly flat (below 5% growth), because the price cut absorbs almost all the extra volume. The cohort covers early converters, who are the most growth-minded, so it may flatter the laggards still on the 6000.

### UBS, Dan Leonard: "a path to ~$150/genome" (26 Feb–3 Mar 2026)

*File: `research/20260303_UBS_ILMN_First_Read-_Illumina_Inc_-Roche_Updated_As_Expected-_Com.md`*

> "…Illumina's roadmap announced earlier in the week suggests a path to ~$150/genome on the NovaSeq X+ assuming the forthcoming 35B flow cell is priced consistent with the 25B flow cell."

**Reading:** this is the next price step on the X itself, not the 6000-to-X cut. It is about 25% below the ~$200 list genome J.P. Morgan cites. UBS's expert call (4 Mar 2026) said one customer already pays "$175 / genome with his discounts" and expects the 35B to reach $150.

### Barclays, Luke Sergott: "lower price/Gb on NSX volumes" (30 Apr 2026)

*File: `research/20260507_Barclays_ILMN_Illumina_Inc.-_If_You_Seed_it-_The_Consumable_Revs_Will.md`*

> "Bears are going to point to the in-line consumables revs and what appears to be lower price/Gb on NSX volumes."

> "We think that part of the issue is around price/Gb on NSX where we saw an increase in NSX Gb output as a % of total output but % of NSX consumable revs remained flat at 55%."

> "…the cynical view is that ILMN is discounting ahead of Roche's launch and in response to Ultima."

As relayed by Barclays, management called reading quarter-to-quarter shifts "a frivolous exercise… due to mix", because new X volume goes to the 25B flow cell while remaining 6000 volume can sit on smaller, pricier flow cells. Barclays gave management "the benefit of the doubt" but said the issue "warrants watching".

**Reading:** no figure. Barclays' "+500bps to 76%" is the clinical share, not the total (82%). The X revenue share then rose to 59% in Q2 2026, which weakens the "discounting" reading.

### J.P. Morgan, Casey Woodring: "-DD%" and "largely gone" (10 Jun 2026)

*Upgrade to Overweight. File: `research/20260615_JP_Morgan_ILMN_Illumina-_Inc.-_Upgrading_to_Overweight_on_a_Sticky_Clin.md`*

> "Notably, consumable volume growth exceeded customer spending expectations for both research (spread between volume and spend is +2.4% in 2027 and +4.5% in 2028) and clinical (spread between volume and spend is +5.2% in 2027 and +5.1% in 2028), indicating favorable pricing for the customers, albeit below the -DD% pricing pressure we've seen for ILMN."

> "All of the major CLIA labs are ILMN users and have transitioned (or are in the process of transitioning) testing to the NovaSeq X (76% of clinical HT Gb was shipped on NovaSeq X as of 1Q26) with the associated pricing headwinds largely gone exiting this year…"

**Reading:** JPM characterizes past pricing pressure as double-digit-percent negative, without a period or per-Gb figure. Its 62-customer survey implies only about 2–5% annual price declines for 2027–28. JPM's view is that the conversion headwind ends in 2026. This is the most bullish published view, and it directly contradicts Barclays.

## Context: management's own numbers

These are not broker estimates. They are the benchmarks the brokers were testing.

- **Like-for-like cut:** "easily more than 60% to 70% price decline on a Gb-to-Gb basis" (Dhingra, Q2 2024 call). Thaysen at Bernstein (May 2025): "we reduced two-third of the price point… you actually have to have 100% more volume in order actually to just make up top line." Strictly, a two-thirds cut needs volume to triple, not double.
- **Blended annual decline:** 20–25% (Aug 2024 model, Q3 2024 "closer towards 25"). Since 2025, management models mid-teens against mid-20s volume growth.
- **Pull-through (FY25 actual / FY26 guide):** X about $1.35M / $1.35–1.40M; 6000 about $670K / $600–650K (Q4 2025 deck).
- **Revenue-implied gap (our arithmetic, not a broker's):** in Q2 2026 the X carried 83% of HT Gb but 59% of HT revenue. Each 6000 Gb therefore earned about 3.4x an X Gb, which makes the X about 70% cheaper. This agrees with management's 60–70%+ (Addendum 16).

## Where sources disagree, and what is stale

- **Size of the like-for-like cut:** RBC's 40% (Aug 2024) was below management's 60–70%+. At that point the revenue-implied gap was only about 54–55%; it reached 65–71% from Q3 2025.
- **Blended annual decline:** Evercore and management put it at 20–25% (2024); management, Stifel and Canaccord at mid-teens (2025 onward); J.P. Morgan's survey at 2–5% (2027–28). The Addendum's revenue bridge implies 20–28% a year for clinical over 2025 to mid-2026.
- **When it ends:** J.P. Morgan says 2026. Barclays sees a new competitive round in 2027 and beyond, and says the 35B "will need to be priced to compete". Bloomberg Intelligence's January 2025 expectation of a "2H inflection" (2025) slipped to "2H26" by February 2026.
- **Coverage gaps:**
  - No broker sizes the remaining clinical 6000 tail in dollars.
  - Most brokers appear in the folder only as call questioners or price targets: Evercore, Bernstein, Wolfe, TD Cowen, Guggenheim, RBC, Deutsche Bank, Canaccord and Jefferies.
  - The folder has no UBS note after March 2026.
- **Missing source:** the Aug 2024 Analyst Day slide behind the "minus 20, minus 25" and "15%" figures is not in the transcript text.
