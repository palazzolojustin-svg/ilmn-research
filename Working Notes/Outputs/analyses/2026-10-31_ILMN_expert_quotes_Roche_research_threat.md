# Expert quotes: Roche AXELIOS as a share threat to Illumina on the research side (2026-10-31)

*These are full expert answers, verbatim from the transcripts in `Sources/ILMN/expert_call/`, with line numbers. Only expert speech is quoted; interviewer prompts are noted where they shaped the answer. The strongest come first: research-side customers who run or buy sequencers. Bias flags: experts 11 and 15 are Roche employees, and 4 and 14 work for competitors (Oxford Nanopore, PacBio).*

## Research customers (core labs and genome centers)

**1. Vice President, Harvard/MIT-affiliated academic genome center (31 Jul 2026).** He runs NovaSeq X Plus, NovaSeq 6000, Ultima, Element, PacBio and a beta AXELIOS. File: `Expert_2026-07-31_Vice_President_at_Research_Center…`
- L263: "It's a new platform. It has been shown to have good data quality. They have come out with some aggressive pricing basically on par, if not a little bit cheaper than Illumina sequencing. I think a really big feature of AXELIOS is the flexible run setup. The run times are very fast, and so you can generate a lot of data in a short amount of time, which I think some labs that have issues with batching and efficiently filling up NovaSeq X Plus, like the larger flow cells, will like to have this level of flexibility in terms of being able to run smaller number of samples more frequently."
- L283: "I think AXELIOS will start to have some share. That's new because it is just launching this summer. I think Illumina probably does not have too much exposure. It's too much vulnerability to Roche. Probably in the next year, I think it will be minimal because I think there is time for people to actually adopt it and deploy it towards projects. I think there won't be major shifts in the coming year, but looking out further, there could be some shift away from Illumina since there are other options now in the market."
- L293: "We are likely to acquire a couple AXELIOS boxes. We have had a beta version of the box that we will upgrade to the commercial version. We could buy more Illumina NovaSeq X Pluses if more volume appears, but right now, no plans on that. That would be the extent of our plans right now."
- L361: "I think it's going to create pressure, probably not a tremendous amount of pressure in the first year, because as I talked about earlier, I think that it does take time for people to make these evaluations and to switch and to evaluate a new platform. Over time, I think that it could, in some settings, exert more pressure than perhaps the other smaller companies with their launches."
- L373: "I think it has more distinguishing features that allow you to do very rapid sequencing for prices that are very competitive with Illumina sequencing. I also think that there is a factor with Roche being a very large company with large reach in terms of sales and support. I think that it has the potential to become even cheaper. It should be very cheap given the sequencing chemistry that it's based on. I think that it will take time for them to be testing the market to figure out what the market will bear in terms of pricing."

**2. Single-Cell Genomic Technology Specialist, Brigham and Women's Hospital profiling core (Tegus, ~Oct 2026).** Runs NovaSeq X Plus and has analyzed AXELIOS data. File: `Expert_Tegus_Single_Cell_…Brigham…`
- L93–99: "It's scalable, so you can load a lot of samples. I am not sure what is their biggest flow cell, but I think it is comparable to Illumina, so you can maybe do 25 billion reads or more in one run. You can run four different experiments at the same time in parallel. This is attractive for large diagnostics, for large projects, especially in biopharma and biotech. This comes with somehow better cost per sample or cost per gigabyte of data generated, which again is very important. Especially for pilot studies, people look for cost, especially when they are starting a new project. They all want to get a better cost, and Roche is offering more competitive prices compared to Illumina in the market. I feel it does offer a lot of advantages."
- L125: "In my hands, I have worked with single-cell data libraries sequenced on Roche AXELIOS platform, and I would say it was great. I did not see any major or substantial differences from other platforms such as Illumina. I wouldn't personally see anything different, but for new users, it would be nice to see a good comparison between different platforms of the same kind of sample, same kind of data."
- L129–137: "Another major hurdle or caveat is that a lot of labs have already developed this ecosystem around Illumina sequencers… It is hard to break that ecosystem. If they purchase Roche, it is hard to build or replace all that ecosystem. However, Roche could be like a second platform at first. And cores like us can offer it and say, 'You have this new machine. It's a better price, high speed. Are you willing to try it?' and my assumption is that a lot of people would say yes."
- L423: "For large biotech or biopharma, we see that they are willing to invest in newer platforms. If AXELIOS is now just came out, it's exciting, it's interesting, they are more willing to try it. Budget and availability of money is a very important factor here. With a small lab, a researcher who has a couple of grants, the risks are high. You don't want to put your money in something that's new for you. It's just higher risk for them."
- L445–447 (asked how likely labs are to adopt): "I would say for a large biotech or pharma using Roche is maybe four or five out of five. It's very highly likely. I wouldn't see why not. It offers a lot of advantages, and you can build your own ecosystem around it, you're not connected to other smaller labs, versus if you're looking at smaller labs where there's the PI or the principal investigator with researchers and they have their own projects, I would say it's maybe, I don't know, two or three out of five."
- L847: "I think my overall view of AXELIOS is that I'm optimistic. I think it is important because it adds another major player into the market. It is good. It is competitive. It could also mean that Illumina will lower their prices to compete with AXELIOS in the existing sequencing market."
- L875–885 (the interviewer asked what happens if Illumina matches Roche on price): "If they match Roche, then it will be very hard for researchers to choose between Roche and Illumina, especially that Illumina has been in the market for a long time. The presence of Roche would drive the sequencing prices lower."

**4. Senior Director, Strategic Product Management, Oxford Nanopore (Tegus, ~Oct 2026).** A competitor employee who left Illumina in 2014. File: `Expert_Tegus_Senior_Director_…Oxford_Nanopore…`
- L131–135: "Roche arguably has an interesting offering when it comes to being able to count shorter oligonucleotides, particularly for tag counting applications like RNA sequencing and liquid biopsy types of applications. We're looking at circulating tumor or cell-free DNA. You don't care about read length. It's commonly shorter than longer. In those applications, again, it's the density of reads that matters more than the read length, and that's where I think something like a Roche system has quite a disruptive potential."
- L459: "Hard to say with perfect certainty because there's always shoes left to drop and everything, but I think they could probably pick away 10%-20% relatively easily. It depends on how close to 80/20, the 80/20 rule Illumina is now, again, with their clinical diagnostics mega providers."
- L501–503: "…to see if they could do something with that that either displaces one of their current systems or adds a third that they can do something with. There are going to be opportunities out there. I don't think of Roche in the next three years as displacing more than that 10% or 20% that I lied with, but thinking out over 10 or longer, I think the potential is there for them to be quite threatening."
- L525–527: "If the whole industry is around $5 billion probably in revenue in 10 years, it'd be interesting to see if Roche could pull off $1 billion of that. That's a complete dart at the wall kind of number, but that's, what, 25% of the current market valuation, which should be growing still."
- L323: "You can be opportunistic and find places where a service contract is about to lapse for an Illumina system, for example, and just offer that entity, that customer, a good price point, and maybe take a loss on your margin to get your foot in the door, and then you might at least make good revenue, if not better revenue, on AXELIOS than Illumina at that point. That's a slow but sure strategy."

From what I understand, their pricing structure, it's still quite expensive to even get into that technology, so that might hinder things."

**6. Laboratory Director, LifeStrands Genomics, Australia (25 Jul 2026).** File: `Expert_2026-07-25_Laboratory_Director…`
- L157: "Not really. They've just gotten bigger and faster… There are companies, for example, Roche, with that AXELIOS sequencer that they've introduced. They have a way of making it really fast, which is quite attractive."

## Quotes that cut against the research-share case (to know before you pitch)

- **Georgia academic core director (31 Jul, L477):** "They made a fairly persuasive pitch for an instrument at the campus. I don't currently believe that it will compete for basic sciences. I think the main advantage is speed, where it may make an impact… for laboratories looking at doing clinical genomics, particularly for newborns."
- **PacBio segment lead (3 Aug):** Roche is "really focused on acute testing and NICU sequencing… like Illumina, they don't want to focus on research." Also, L159: "I don't see people switching completely just yet. I think some of the bigger customers are buying instruments in multiple competitor instruments as well."
- **Roche scientific affairs manager (5 Aug, L177; Roche employee):** "I actually don't even think it is displacing… I don't think any core lab is going to be like, 'Okay, we no longer need NovaSeq X, but we will try this AXELIOS instrument'… but they'll only have one versus maybe six NovaSeq Xes."
- **South Carolina academic core director (30 Jul):** "my prediction, it will just simply die out because I don't see any competitive advantages on this platform."
- **Oxford Nanopore director (Tegus, L49):** "Research-use market has fallen off a cliff in the Trump administration's second turnaround." This shrinks the pool Roche would take share from.
