## Why Thermo Fisher Matters to Illumina, and What These Sources Do and Don't Cover

Thermo Fisher Scientific (TMO) is the largest life-science tools company. It sells lab instruments, consumables (supplies and reagents customers keep re-buying) and services to drug makers, universities, hospitals and industrial customers. It touches Illumina in three ways. It owns Ion Torrent, a rival DNA sequencing platform. It owns Olink, a proteomics business (measuring proteins rather than DNA) that competes with Illumina's SomaLogic. And it serves the same biotech, pharma, academic and government customers.

**The key caveat is that neither source here discusses Illumina, DNA sequencing, Ion Torrent, clinical NGS (next-generation sequencing used in medical testing) or any sequencing competitor.** The two sources are the Q2 2026 earnings call [Q2 2026 call, 2026-07-23] and CEO Marc Casper's appearance at the Morgan Stanley Global Healthcare Conference [MS conf, 2026-09-15]. The Morgan Stanley talk also skipped diagnostics, pricing and competitors by name. Nothing here shows how Ion Torrent is doing or how Thermo views Illumina, so this is a coverage gap.

The section's value is as read-across: what Thermo's experience implies about conditions Illumina also faces. Thermo is the industry's broadest bellwether. Its view of biotech funding, U.S. academic and government spending, China and AI-driven lab demand bears directly on Illumina's customers.

## Read-Across for Illumina

### Biotech funding is recovering, and Thermo explains how it reaches tool suppliers

Casper said biotech is "in a much better part of the cycle" because funding and confidence are improving [MS conf]. Emerging biotechs sounded more upbeat in 2025, before funding was strong. Funding then improved through 2025 and stepped up further in 2026, helped by industry M&A (large companies buying biotechs, which returns cash to venture investors) and IPOs. Funding typically takes 2 to 3 quarters to turn into spending.

He described the order in which new funding reaches Thermo's businesses [MS conf]:

1. **Clinical research comes first**, because trials get a company to its next milestone and funding round. This already shows as very strong growth in authorizations (newly contracted work) and revenue.
2. **Lab supplies come next.** These flow through Fisher Scientific, Thermo's research and safety distribution channel, as companies refill research pipelines. Casper said this was "starting now" in September.
3. **High-tech reagents come last.** He expected these specialized research consumables to strengthen "over the next couple of quarters."

In July, Casper added a second lag: biotech authorizations had been strong "for a few quarters," and they take roughly six months to become revenue [Q2 2026 call]. He called the pickup "not surprising." It fit the framework CFO Jim Meyer set out at the May 2026 Investor Day, which named biotech recovery and academic and government recovery as the two big drivers of faster growth.

**Why this matters for Illumina.** Research sequencing consumables probably sit closer to "high-tech reagents" than to trial services in this sequence. If Casper's order of events holds, the recovery should reach sequencing customers from roughly late 2026 into 2027. That timing is an inference from Thermo's framework, not a statement Thermo made about sequencing.

### U.S. academic and government spending is stabilizing, but nobody is calling a trend yet

Academic and government customers returned to low-single-digit growth in Q2 2026 [Q2 2026 call; MS conf]. Europe was very strong. The U.S. was "slightly positive," a return to growth. China remained "quite muted," neither worse nor better, though the Morgan Stanley talk called it "a better quarter." Growth came from chromatography and mass spectrometry and from adoption of high-end instruments, including new launches.

Casper's tone firmed between the two events. In July he said "we're not calling a new trend based on Q2" and wanted broader-based activity first. With the market "going through a stabilization period," Thermo left its outlook unchanged and treated sustained improvement as upside [Q2 2026 call]. He added that "it's less about the headlines than it is actually about funding flow and the funding flow is improving," which supported "slow stabilization." By September he said: "Funding has been okay, right, but anxiety has been high" [MS conf]. Policy-change announcements in 2025 made customers cautious. He called 2026 "stabilizing but still muted" and expects a return to growth in 2027. He described the anxiety as "literally only a U.S. dynamic": Europe has fewer policy issues, and China is muted because its government restrains academic spending.

**Why this matters for Illumina.** U.S. academic and government labs are a large customer group for research sequencing. Thermo sees money flowing again despite noisy headlines, but neither firm's evidence yet shows a durable recovery. One nuance applies to any Illumina launch cycle: "irrespective of funding environments, if you have really relevant innovation, customers get money" [Q2 2026 call, on academic adoption of mass spectrometry]. Clearly better instruments can win budget even when overall budgets are tight.

### China: a gradual recovery, but academic demand stays weak

China is about 7.5% of Thermo's revenue [Q2 2026 call]. It returned to low-single-digit growth in Q2 2026, driven by pharma/biotech and industrial customers. Casper said China had dragged on industry growth for several years; Thermo had generally beaten peers but was still "flattish to down" [MS conf]. In July he said China was "not accretive to our organic growth as a company yet," meaning it was not yet growing faster than the company overall [Q2 2026 call].

After his August 2026 trip, his second of the year, Casper said: "I actually came away more bullish on China" [MS conf]. Innovative Chinese biotechs are investing heavily in R&D and want Western partners to derisk licensing deals, because Thermo's name behind a product or trial data reassures buyers. He believes China can grow at least at the company average over time. Academic and government spending will stay weak until Beijing changes fiscal policy, but "I don't think that matters all that much." Thermo has been in China for over 40 years, does not need it to hit targets, and sees it as upside given modest assumptions.

**Why this matters for Illumina.** Illumina's China position differs sharply from Thermo's, and its China academic exposure has been especially hurt. Thermo's experience suggests commercial biotech is the improving part of China, while government-funded science stays weak.

### AI is driving demand for reagents rather than displacing it

Asked whether pharma AI spending might crowd out traditional tools, Casper argued the opposite [MS conf]. Investment follows drug-development returns, and better returns from AI will fund bigger pipelines. More drug uses will be tested in parallel, better understanding of biology will guide research spending, and large-scale experiments are generating data to train foundational biological models (large AI models of how biology works). These high-throughput labs "consume many reagents." He is "quite bullish that AI will actually be a facilitator of industry growth," calling it "another tailwind" that strengthens Thermo because it has the most data and customer insight. Internally, AI is accelerating the PPI Business System, Thermo's continuous-improvement method [Q2 2026 call].

**Why this matters for Illumina.** Data generation to train biology models is central to the case for sequencing and proteomics demand, and a major peer independently reports seeing it.

### Proteomics and multiomics: Olink and mass spectrometry

The only Olink item is a strategic collaboration with Precision Health Research, Singapore, for a population health study [Q2 2026 call]. It pairs Olink proteomics with Thermo's Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometer (an instrument that identifies molecules by their mass). Population-scale proteomics is where SomaLogic competes, but Thermo pairs Olink with its own mass spectrometry rather than with sequencing. Neither source mentions SomaLogic or compares platforms.

At the American Society for Mass Spectrometry (ASMS) conference in June 2026, Thermo launched two mass spectrometers with AI-driven software, including proteomics workflows [Q2 2026 call]. The **Orbitrap Tribrid Apex** targets multiomics (combining several molecular data types), structural biology, biopharma characterization and small molecules. The **Orbitrap Excedion** is pitched as reducing drug-development risk and speeding time to market.

### Other product launches relevant to genomics labs

Thermo launched the **Applied Biosystems PowerFlex Thermal Cycler**, a next-generation PCR platform [Q2 2026 call]. PCR is the technique for copying DNA, a standard step in many sequencing workflows, and a thermal cycler runs it. It also launched the **Vanquish Amplify UHPLC**, a liquid chromatography system (which separates mixtures into components) with less sample loss and better reproducibility. Casper called it "an important addition to our liquid chromatography offering" that "bodes well for the future."

Casper called the quarter "outstanding" and said customer activity "continued to strengthen" across end markets [Q2 2026 call]. Morgan Stanley's interviewer noted it was Thermo's strongest organic growth since 2021 [MS conf].

Two "beat" comparisons should not be confused. **Versus Thermo's own prior guidance**, revenue was about $300M ahead, from organic growth 2 percentage points stronger than planned, a larger acquisition contribution and favorable currency [Q2 2026 call, Meyer]. Adjusted EPS was $0.30 ahead, from the extra revenue, strong cost productivity and excellent acquisition performance, notably Clario. Meyer said productivity and volume leverage (fixed costs spread over more sales) drove the margin gain, offsetting unfavorable product mix and funding strategic investments.

### Growth by geography and customer type

By geography, organic growth was low single digits in North America, high single digits in Europe, and high single digits in Asia Pacific, with China at low single digits [Q2 2026 call, Meyer].

By end market, Casper reported "both sequential improvement and strong revenue growth across each of our end markets" [Q2 2026 call]:

- **Pharma and biotech** (about 60% of revenue) grew mid-single digits, led by bioproduction (manufacturing biologic drugs), clinical research and the research and safety channel. Each of the last several quarters was a little stronger than the one before, and the segment is "really progressing in a nice direction" [Q2 2026 call; MS conf].
- **Academic and government** grew low-single digits, as covered above.
- **Industrial and applied** (about 15% of revenue [MS conf]) grew mid-single digits, led by electron microscopy, chemical analysis and the research and safety channel.
- **Diagnostics and health care** grew mid-single digits, driven by the health care market channel and immunodiagnostics (blood tests that detect antibodies or antigens).

Meyer explained the drivers [Q2 2026 call]. In **Life Sciences Solutions**, bioproduction had "another quarter of excellent organic growth," while productivity gains were partly offset by the newly acquired filtration and separation business and unfavorable mix. **Analytical Instruments** grew well in all three businesses, led by electron microscopy (Casper called it high-single-digit growth); its margin gain came from productivity, volume leverage, currency and favorable mix. **Specialty Diagnostics** was led by the health care channel, immunodiagnostics and transplant diagnostics, with volume leverage and productivity partly offset by mix. **Laboratory Products & Biopharma Services** was led by the research and safety channel and clinical research; productivity and strong Clario results were partly offset by mix and strategic investments.

### Cash flow and balance sheet

Meyer gave these figures [Q2 2026 call]. In the first half of 2026, operating cash flow was $3.3B and free cash flow (cash left after capital spending) was $2.5B, after $800M of net capital expenditures. Q2 shareholder returns totaled $1.2B: $1B of buybacks and about $175M of dividends. At quarter-end, cash was $4.1B and total debt $42.5B. Leverage was 3.6x gross debt to adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a rough proxy for operating cash earnings), or 3.3x on a net basis. Adjusted ROIC (return on invested capital) was 10.9%.

Meyer bridged the $0.25 midpoint increase: +$0.30 from Q2 performance and +$0.05 from a higher second-half revenue outlook, offset by −$0.05 from the microbiology divestiture and −$0.05 from a second-half currency headwind [Q2 2026 call].

Organic growth was 3% in the first half and is guided to 4% in the second half, with Q3 and Q4 similar. Q3 adjusted EPS was guided $0.35–$0.40 above Q2. In September, Casper reaffirmed that full-year organic growth is "likely to be 4%," with the second half at about 4% split evenly between Q3 and Q4 [MS conf]. He feels "very good" about it and noted Thermo thinks in annual or six-month periods, not quarters. Orders are running ahead of revenue. The swing factors, working through the backlog and continued end-market strength, "are playing out as we would expect," and he expects to enter 2027 with "very solid momentum."

### Medium- and long-term targets (Investor Day, May 2026)

Casper restated the Investor Day targets [MS conf]. For 2026–2027, Thermo targets 3% to 6% organic growth per year and is "progressing higher in the range," with orders suggesting strengthening into 2027. From 2028, it targets growth "in the 7% range," which he said "doesn't feel like very far from today's date." The drivers are academic and government stabilization and improving biotech funding, both under way in his view, plus structural forces: an aging population, scientific breakthroughs and demand for tools and pharma services. He called the industry "an outstanding neighborhood to live in" after a "painful last few years." China is upside given modest assumptions. The main risk is "something that really throws the economy off track," a general macro risk rather than one specific to tools.

## Other Business Drivers

### Pharma and biotech mood

Casper said large-pharma executives at a breakfast the day before the July call were upbeat about their pipelines and focused on speeding innovation productively [Q2 2026 call]. Companies facing large patent cliffs (when key drugs lose exclusivity) are "really working their pipeline." He said customers "have their arms around the macro," having absorbed the main policy risks: the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA, the 2022 U.S. law letting Medicare negotiate drug prices), most-favored-nation (MFN) pricing (proposals tying U.S. drug prices to the lowest paid abroad) and tariffs. Asked by Operon's Jack Meehan, he did not say whether guidance leaves room for upside, but called Q2 a "clean quarter" (no unusual one-offs) and expected the pharma and biotech second half to be "a little better."

On personalized mRNA cancer vaccines (therapies tailored to an individual's tumor), Casper said the opportunity is "less about the Moderna and Merck specific spending in two important customers but more about the interest in the area, which then spurs a reinvestment cycle" [MS conf]. He likened it to capital flowing into antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs, antibodies that deliver a toxic drug to cancer cells), calling it "yet another leg to drive growth in the industry."

### Reshoring of drug manufacturing to the U.S.

Casper described three one-time waves of benefit from U.S. policy rewarding domestic production [MS conf]:

1. **CDMO contracts** (contract development and manufacturing organizations make drugs for other companies). Customers can meet U.S. requirements by contracting U.S. production instead of building plants. Thermo has won several large contracts, and President Trump visited its Cincinnati oral solid dose (pill) facility in March 2026. This is the first phase.
2. **Expansions of existing facilities**, where customers buy equipment and stock labs. Activity is picking up and probably steps up in 2027.
3. **Greenfield (brand-new) plants**, which account for most announcements and dollars and bring one-time equipment and lab fit-out purchases, with Fisher Scientific supplying labs. Expected in 2027–2028.

Because total drug volume is unchanged, this is a one-time boost "additive to how we've thought about our growth outlook" that will later normalize; "we'll take it." Many U.S. medicines are now made in Western Europe (innovative drugs) or Asia Pacific, with generics mostly made in India.

### Bioproduction

Bioproduction (supplying the tools to make biologic drugs) is growing faster than the market, per the interviewer's data [MS conf]. Casper credits long-run share gains and customers buying best-in-class technology to lower production costs [Q2 2026 call; MS conf]. Thermo leads in cell culture media (the nutrient broth cells grow in) and single-use technologies (disposable bioprocessing equipment). Its DynaDrive single-use bioreactor is becoming standard across contract manufacturers and adopted by innovative pharma, "quickly becoming the favorite technology," which bodes well for follow-on consumables. Its fast-growing purification resin business is converting drug-molecule wins from 5 to 7 years ago into revenue. When TD Cowen noted a large peer's customer delays, Casper reported none and said there are "a number of fine players" [Q2 2026 call].

The filtration and separation business, acquired from Solventum (formerly part of 3M), progressed between the two events. In July, demand was strong and capacity was being added but not yet visible in organic growth; integration was going well, and the respected legacy 3M technology plus Thermo's reach and supply reliability were generating many customer trials [Q2 2026 call]. By September, past its one-year anniversary, it counted in organic growth. Casper said its technology had been strong but its commercial reach weak. He expects mid- to high-single-digit growth, slightly dilutive to bioproduction's growth rate but accretive to the company's and "highly accretive" to EPS, reaching the bioproduction rate as capacity expands [MS conf].

Casper expects long-term bioproduction growth at high single to low double digits, as through 2019, after the pandemic surge and an industry "hangover" [MS conf].

### Pharma Services (Patheon) and clinical research (PPD, Clario)

**Pharma Services** grew at low single digits in the first half, as signaled in January, with a "nice step-up" expected in the second half based on contracts and shipment schedules; it has "the best visibility of any of our businesses" [Q2 2026 call; MS conf]. Meyer cautioned this "doesn't necessarily imply that the fourth quarter grows over the third quarter." Reshoring wins, visible in drug product and somewhat in drug substance, were already in the outlook. The business spans clinical trial packaging and logistics (Thermo physically touches about 40% of all experimental medicines), drug product (mainly sterile fill-finish, putting drugs into final vials or syringes, where capacity has expanded) and drug substance (Thermo is one of the largest producers of biologics using single-use technology). Casper expects it to be a "nice growth business" in 2027 and 2028 [MS conf].

**Clinical research** (Thermo is a CRO, a contract research organization that runs trials for drug companies) had an excellent Q2, with authorizations growing "well in excess of our revenue" [Q2 2026 call; MS conf]. The combined Patheon/PPD offering, called "Accelerated drug development" in the July transcript and "Accelerator Drug Development" in September, cuts time to market, which matters because failing fast is cheaper and earlier launches extend patent exclusivity. Demand is "incredibly strong," especially from biotech, and AI is being used "quite aggressively." Early-stage pipelines show heavy interest in neurology, continued oncology strength and adoption of new modalities (new drug types) [MS conf].

**Clario**, a $9B acquisition closed in March 2026, generates and manages trial endpoints (the measurements that decide whether a trial succeeds). It leads in three of the four major endpoint types, with Thermo's central lab covering the fourth, and aims to be the standard AI-enabled platform whoever runs the trial. Q2, its first full quarter, brought very strong authorizations, revenue and earnings, smooth integration and interest from customers outside Thermo's CRO. Casper said the first six months "have gone great" [Q2 2026 call; MS conf].

### Industrial and channels

**Electron microscopy** was very strong on semiconductor demand [Q2 2026 call; MS conf]. These microscopes show chip structures at the atomic level and are increasingly used for yield ramp (raising the share of good chips in a new fab), helped by automated near-line testing. "Orders are far outstripping revenue," and new fab construction should sustain demand.

**Chemical analysis**, the smallest instrument business, returned to growth as high commodity prices boosted spectroscopy demand and global conflict lifted safety and security spending (radiation and explosive detectors); "market conditions are getting better" [Q2 2026 call].

In **channels**, the health care channel had specific Q1 headwinds and an "incredibly strong" Q2; Casper said to "take the average of the 2 quarters" as the run-rate. The research and safety channel grew on share gains and pharma and biotech account wins plus broad market improvement; better academic demand helped but was not the main driver [Q2 2026 call].

## Strategy and Capital Deployment

Casper's three strategic pillars are high-impact innovation, being a trusted partner to customers and an "unparalleled commercial engine." In April 2026 Thermo opened a flagship U.S. Bioprocess Design Center in Massachusetts for collaborative customer work [Q2 2026 call]. Updated sustainability reporting cited a net-zero roadmap, more zero-waste sites and STEM programs reaching over 185,000 students a year.

M&A remains the top use of capital alongside shareholder returns. Casper cited "an active pipeline of M&A opportunities in our highly fragmented industry" while keeping a strong investment-grade credit rating [Q2 2026 call; MS conf]. Through September 2026, Thermo had done $9B of M&A, $4B of buybacks and a 10% dividend increase.

**Microbiology divestiture.** Announced in late April 2026 and expected in July to close in Q3, the sale was confirmed in September as closed in August 2026. Casper said it "reflects our active management of the company" and frees capital for higher-value areas. The business had $645M of 2025 revenue, about a quarter sold through Thermo's own channels, which Thermo keeps. The sale cuts 2026 revenue by about $200M (net of retained sales) and 2026 adjusted EPS by $0.05, and is about $0.15 dilutive in the first full year after close. Thermo spent the expected proceeds on the $1B of Q2 buybacks ahead of closing, "based on an assessment of our valuation at that time" [Q2 2026 call, Meyer].

Casper closed the July call saying Thermo is "on track to deliver a strong year" and "well positioned to deliver a great 2026 and build an even brighter future for our company." In September he said 2026 "is setting up to be really an outstanding year."
