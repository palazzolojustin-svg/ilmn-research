# Peer: MGI Tech (688114, Shanghai)

*Core Notes section 15. Synthesized from 4 source extracts as of 2026-10-10. Bracketed tags cite the source; full detail is in Working Notes (see INDEX.md).*

## What this section rests on, and what it lacks

MGI Tech is a Shenzhen-headquartered company listed on Shanghai's STAR Market (ticker 688114.SS; 688114 CH on Bloomberg). It is China's main maker of DNA sequencers (machines that read genetic code) and Illumina's most direct competitor there.

**There are no MGI earnings-call or conference transcripts in this folder.** Everything below comes from four short notes by one broker, UBS. They were written by Henry Liu, CFA (lead analyst) and Chen Chen, PhD of UBS Securities Co. Limited in its China "Advanced Medical Devices" coverage. Judy Xie (associate) joined from April 2026, and Jun Deng is thanked for contributions. The notes are:

- a "Fast Take" (brief reaction note) on preliminary 2025 results [UBS, 2026-01-26];
- a Fast Take on an acquisition [UBS, 2026-02-02];
- a full review of 2025 and Q1 2026 results [UBS, 2026-04-29];
- a "First Read" (quick post-results note) on H1 2026 [UBS, 2026-08-24].

Two limits follow. Management statements appear only second-hand and are tagged "[MGI mgmt via UBS]". More importantly, **none of the notes names Illumina or any other competitor, or discusses China's trade restrictions on Illumina.** The China-competition picture must be read through indirect evidence: MGI's share of public tenders, "import substitution" (Chinese buyers replacing foreign products with domestic ones), and MGI's moves to cut its own US geopolitical exposure. For Illumina's side, see the Illumina sections of the Core Notes.

## Business overview

### What MGI does

UBS calls MGI "China and the world's leading genome sequencer and lab automation equipment producer," focused on developing, making and selling life-science instruments and reagents [UBS, all four notes]. It reports four revenue lines; 2025 total revenue was Rmb2,780m:

- **"Seq-all" (genome sequencing):** sequencers plus reagents, the consumable chemical kits needed for every run. This is the main business, which UBS says "showcases the company's competitive edge": Rmb2,203m, about 79% of revenue.
- **Generative Lab Intelligence (GLI):** lab automation (robotic sample handling) that complements sequencing: Rmb254m.
- **Omics, or the "new business":** new products and services exploring "digitalisation opportunities in life sciences," including spatial omics: Rmb279m.
- **Other:** Rmb43–44m.

### Technology: short reads, long reads and spatial

MGI's core platform is short-read sequencing, or NGS (next-generation sequencing), which reads DNA in many small fragments, the same approach Illumina uses. The notes name two MGI chemistries, CoolMPS and StandardMPS, in connection with licensing deals (below).

On 1 February 2026, MGI agreed to buy nanopore-sequencing and spatial-omics platforms from BGI Tech for Rmb365.7m. **Nanopore sequencing** threads DNA through a tiny pore and produces "long reads" (long continuous stretches of sequence). **Spatial omics** measures genes or proteins while preserving their location in tissue. The notes do not describe BGI Tech's relationship to MGI or give the assets' revenue or profit. The seller committed to earnings targets over 2028–30 for nanopore and 2026–28 for spatial [UBS, 2026-04-29].

UBS expects "limited direct financial impact near term" but calls the deal "key for strengthening MGI's product (NGS+nanopore-sequencing) and tech barrier (short +long-read)," a tech barrier being an edge rivals find hard to copy. It adds that "MGI is the only supplier in China and one of two globally with such capability," without naming the other [UBS, 2026-02-02]. By H1 2026, management credited "synergies between short-read and long-read sequencing and spatial omics" for part of omics growth [MGI mgmt via UBS, 2026-08-24]. Longer term, UBS believes high-throughput short-read sequencing "will remain the mainstream technology for the foreseeable future" on cost, stability and breadth of applications [UBS, 2026-04-29].

### Position in China: dominance through import substitution

MGI's domestic strength shows mainly in management claims relayed by UBS:

- Its share of public bids (government and hospital tenders) in China's sequencing market rose to about 70% in 2025 [MGI mgmt via UBS, 2026-04-29].
- By end-2025 it had shipped about 60% of the prior year's orders tied to import-substitution demand [MGI mgmt via UBS, 2026-04-29].
- As import substitution rises, competition has shifted "from product pricing to broader competitive capabilities" [MGI mgmt via UBS, 2026-08-24].

The numbers are weaker. China seq-all revenue fell 11% in 2025, and UBS repeatedly cites "weak market demand and intensified competition," pricing pressure and gross-margin erosion [UBS, 2026-04-29]. Domestic lab-automation demand "remained under pressure" in H1 2026 [UBS, 2026-08-24].

*Interpretation, not stated in the sources:* the import-substitution orders and 70% tender share are consistent with Chinese public buyers turning away from foreign sequencers such as Illumina's.

### Position abroad: growth in the Americas while exiting US ownership

Overseas seq-all revenue is mixed. In 2025, Asia-Pacific (APAC) fell 29%, Europe, Middle East and Africa (EMEA) rose 4% and the Americas rose 21% [UBS, 2026-04-29]. In H1 2026 (year on year), the Americas jumped 126%, APAC rose 8.5% and EMEA was flat [UBS, 2026-08-24]. UBS argues MGI has gained global share "even amid rising geopolitical tensions" through product competitiveness and its overseas sales network, but with "lacklustre demand and geopolitical uncertainties, the process could take some time" [UBS, 2026-04-29].

Meanwhile, MGI is converting its Western business into licensing, an important geopolitical signal:

1. **CoolMPS licence (13 October 2025).** MGI out-licensed (let another firm use, for fees) CoolMPS outside APAC and Greater China to Swiss Rockets for US$20m upfront, a US$20m milestone and tiered royalties on future sales. As of H1 2026 the upfront payment had not been recognized as revenue, and management estimated the technology transfer was about 50% complete [UBS, 2026-08-24].
2. **Complete Genomics sale and StandardMPS licence (24 February 2026).** MGI agreed to sell its US subsidiary, Complete Genomics Inc, for US$50m and out-licensed StandardMPS in the US and Canada to Swiss Rockets for royalties. UBS assumes completion in early 2027, with a milestone payment and asset-disposal gain booked that year [UBS, 2026-04-29].

UBS expects these deals plus the nanopore acquisition to raise competitiveness, reduce geopolitical risk and improve long-term profitability, partly by shedding a loss-making US subsidiary [UBS, 2026-04-29]. In effect, MGI is moving from selling directly in North America to collecting royalties there through a partner.

## Results

All figures are in Chinese yuan (Rmb). Revenue peaked at Rmb4,231m in 2022, when MGI earned Rmb239m, then fell about 31% to Rmb2,911m in 2023 and has stayed near Rmb3bn, with large losses in 2023–24. Net cash (cash minus debt) shrank from Rmb6,348m at end-2022 to Rmb4,417m at end-2023 and Rmb1,601m at end-2024 as the company burned cash, then recovered to Rmb1,974m at end-2025.

### Full-year 2025: preliminary range, then final

In January, MGI guided to a 2025 net loss attributable to the parent (the loss belonging to listed shareholders, excluding minority interests) of Rmb221–273m. That was far smaller than 2024's Rmb601m loss but worse than UBS's Rmb126m estimate. UBS's explanation: "Cost savings, FX gain and tax reversal helped narrow losses YoY, though partially offset by impairment loss in our view." It sized none of these currency gains, tax items or asset write-downs [UBS, 2026-01-26].

Final results were "largely in line with the preliminary results" [UBS, 2026-04-29]:

- **Revenue** was Rmb2.78bn, down 7.73% year on year and below UBS's pre-results Rmb2,998m.
- **Net loss to parent** was Rmb222m (2024: Rmb601m). The **recurring net loss**, which strips out one-offs, was Rmb390m (2024: Rmb653m); the gap shows how much one-offs flattered 2025.
- **Gross margin** (revenue left after direct production costs) fell to 52.9% from 55.5% in 2024 and 59.5% in 2023.
- **EBIT** (operating profit before interest and tax) was a Rmb237m loss (–8.5% margin) versus a Rmb460m loss in 2024. **EBITDA** (EBIT plus depreciation and amortization) turned positive at Rmb123m. Operating cash flow was Rmb199m.
- **EPS** (earnings per share) was –Rmb0.51 versus UBS's pre-results –Rmb0.30.

Seq-all revenue fell 8.04% to Rmb2.20bn, with instruments down 17% and reagents down 6%. Yet sequencer units rose to more than 1,470 from more than 1,260 in 2024. UBS attributes the gap to a lower average selling price (ASP), from pricing pressure and a shift toward cheaper, lower-throughput machines [UBS, 2026-04-29].

**A contradiction on segments.** UBS writes that GLI fell 32% and omics "grew a rapid 48%" in "2025 and Q1 2026," but its own table shows omics falling from Rmb400m (2024) to Rmb279m (2025) and GLI rising from Rmb209m to Rmb254m. The 48% most plausibly refers to Q1 2026 only, and the GLI figures look swapped or mislabelled; treat 2025 segment growth rates with caution. UBS's model also shows net earnings slightly different from the loss to parent: –Rmb596m for 2024 (vs –601m) and –Rmb213m for 2025 (vs –222m).

### Q1 2026: growth from a low base, but a miss

Q1 revenue rose 24.8% year on year to Rmb585m from a weak Q1 2025. The recurring net loss narrowed to Rmb88.5m from Rmb144m, which UBS credits to higher gross margin and cost control. Seq-all grew about 23% (instruments –4%, reagents +23%), GLI rose 10% and omics 48%. Results still "missed our forecasts," against UBS's own numbers rather than consensus and unsized, yet UBS subtitled the note that fundamentals "may have bottomed" [UBS, 2026-04-29].

### H1 2026: sequential improvement and a reagent shift

UBS called H1 "largely in line" [UBS, 2026-08-24]. Revenue was Rmb1.37bn, up 18.9% year on year. Q2 revenue was up 33% on Q1 (implying roughly Rmb785m, derived rather than stated), and losses narrowed from Q1 to Q2. Net loss to parent was Rmb158m (H1 2025: Rmb168m) and the recurring net loss Rmb161m (Rmb203m). UBS credits growth in all segments and regions, gross-margin expansion from a richer reagent mix, and cost control; no H1 gross margin was given.

By segment:

- **Seq-all:** Rmb1.1bn, up 17.6%; regional growth is given above (China +8.5%). Instruments fell 6.4% while reagents rose 16.3%, which UBS calls "an accelerated conversion of the installed base into recurring higher-margin reagent revenues": machines already placed are generating more repeat consumable sales. Clinical customers (hospitals and diagnostic labs) drove growth at +36.6%, while research was broadly flat [MGI mgmt via UBS].
- **GLI:** Rmb116m, up 4.1%, mostly from customized EMEA projects.
- **Omics:** Rmb162m, up 44.9%, on the formal commercial launch of spatial omics, short/long-read/spatial synergies, and demand from large language models (LLMs, AI systems) for multi-omics datasets (combining several molecular data types).

UBS said guidance was "largely unchanged" without quoting it. Management expects industry fundamentals to improve in 2027, possibly to teens-percentage growth, citing industry reports and competitors' guidance [MGI mgmt via UBS, 2026-08-24].

## UBS forecasts and how they changed

### Before 2025 results (January–February 2026)

UBS's pre-results model [UBS, 2026-01-26 and 2026-02-02] had revenue dipping about 0.5% in 2025 to Rmb2,998m, then reaching Rmb3,389m in 2026 (+13%), Rmb3,957m in 2027 (+17%), Rmb4,584m in 2028 (+16%) and Rmb5,272m in 2029 (+15%). EBIT would move from a Rmb284m loss in 2025 (–9.5% margin) to a Rmb91m loss in 2026, then profits of Rmb109m, Rmb293m and Rmb496m in 2027–29 (9.4% margin in 2029). Net earnings were –Rmb126m, then +Rmb66m, Rmb264m, Rmb434m and Rmb623m for 2025–29, with EPS of –0.30, 0.16, 0.63, 1.04 and 1.49. Return on invested capital (ROIC, profit relative to capital employed) would rise from –4.6% in 2025 to 8.1% in 2029 (8.9% in 2022), and net cash from Rmb1,653m to Rmb2,899m. No dividends were forecast.

### The April 2026 cut

After the Q1 miss, UBS cut 2026–28 operating assumptions "to reflect weak market demand and intensified competition" [UBS, 2026-04-29]. It still expects teens revenue growth led by seq-all and omics, net-profit breakeven in 2026, margin expansion from a richer reagent mix and cost cuts, and extra profit from the deals closing in 2026–27. This is UBS's view; no company guidance is quoted.

UBS lowered revenue because of a lower ASP (more low-throughput sequencers, competition), weaker reagent use on soft demand, and losing the US subsidiary after its assumed early-2027 sale. Gross margin fell on weaker pricing and reagent use, partly offset by higher-margin royalties. Operating margins rose slightly for 2026–28 on cost savings and the US exit, though operating profit was cut from 2028. Non-operating income rose in 2026–27 from upfront and milestone payments and the disposal gain, assuming the deals close.

## Analyst views (UBS)

### The thesis in brief

UBS's stance amounts to "good company, fairly priced" [UBS, 2026-04-29]. The bull case is that MGI keeps gaining share in China and abroad through product competitiveness, its overseas network, and capabilities in product and technology integration, ecosystem operations and global commercialisation [UBS, 2026-08-24], with short-read NGS remaining mainstream. Holding UBS back are weak demand, competition squeezing prices and margins, and geopolitics slowing overseas expansion.

MGI underperformed China's SW Medtech index in early 2026 as consensus cut growth and margin forecasts, and UBS concluded its "higher tech barrier and stronger competitive positioning are fairly priced in." It expects consensus to take time to absorb the acquisition and US divestment. After the August rally, the stock trades about 22% above UBS's unchanged target, yet the rating stays Neutral.

### Risks UBS lists

Every note lists the same downside risks: (1) intellectual-property (IP) litigation and geopolitical tensions; (2) technology-roadmap shifts; (3) macro pressure on research and clinical funding; (4) slower adoption of sequencing; (5) tighter life-science regulation; (6) more competition from new entrants. Upside risks are the mirror image. IP litigation and geopolitics heading the list is the closest the notes come to the Illumina–MGI patent history and US–China tensions.

### Short-term scores

In April and August, UBS's "Quantitative Research Review" scored MGI on a 1–5 scale for the next three to six months (shorter than the 12-month rating): industry structure 2 (leaning worse), regulatory environment 2 (leaning tougher), recent trend 3 (little change), next EPS update versus consensus 3 (in line), and risk skew versus UBS's forecast 3 (balanced). No catalysts were expected in the next three months. For context, UBS's global rating mix was 52% Buy, 41% Neutral and 7% Sell at 31 December 2025, and 55%/40%/6% at 30 June 2026.

### What remains unknown

The notes do not size the 2025 impairment, FX gain or tax reversal, the Q1 2026 miss, H1 2026 gross margin, or the nanopore and spatial assets' economics. They give no company guidance figures and never mention Illumina or China's restrictions on it. UBS's above-consensus 2026–27 profits depend on the Swiss Rockets and Complete Genomics deals closing as assumed.
