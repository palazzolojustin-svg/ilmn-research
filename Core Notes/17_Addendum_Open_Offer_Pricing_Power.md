# 17 Addendum: Open Offer contracts and Illumina's pricing power with big clinical customers

## Short answer

Mostly yes for the customers and products they cover, and in one direction only. Signers' inflation-adjusted prices on Illumina's NextSeq and NovaSeq consumables cannot rise. The exception is passing through material cost increases outside Illumina's control (for example tariffs), and only to the extent of that cost. Any better price given to a comparable customer must be refunded to Caris. Illumina can keep up with inflation and pass through cost shocks, but it cannot take real price from these accounts. Its revenue-per-customer levers are volume, mix and new products, not list increases.

## What the contracts say

**Scope.** The Open Offer was made in 2021 to "any U.S. oncology customer" (Illumina terms page). It covers NextSeq and NovaSeq instruments and sequencing consumables, including new products (Caris §1, §5(d)). The term is 12 years from the GRAIL closing, to August 2033.
- **Not covered:** rare-disease, prenatal and research customers, and ex-US customers. This is our inference from the definition.
- **Sign-up deadline:** one research note says the offer can be signed until about August 2027. This is unverified; the terms page shows no expiry.

**Price freeze, Caris §5(c)–(d).** The inflation-adjusted net price "shall not increase". The index is a US Bureau of Labor Statistics lab-instrument price index, so nominal prices can rise only with that inflation.
- **The one carve-out:** costs that "materially increase due to factors beyond Illumina's control", passed through "solely to reflect that cost increase".

**Price per Gb commitment, §5(d).** By 2025 the highest-throughput system's net price per Gb was to be "at least 43% lower" than the March 2021 NovaSeq 6000 S4 price. The baseline was about $4/Gb, so the target is about $2.3/Gb (unverified against the raw exhibit). No remedy for a miss and no measurement were found. The NovaSeq X at roughly $2–2.5/Gb is in that range.

**Most favoured customer, §5(e)/(g).** Prices must be "no less favorable" than those given to an "Equivalent customer". On a breach, Illumina refunds the difference on units already bought.

**Exit and commitments.** There is no purchase commitment. The customer can leave on 90 days' notice; Illumina cannot terminate for convenience.

## Named customers

| Customer | Contract | Can Illumina raise price? |
|---|---|---|
| Caris | Open Offer template, to Aug 2033 | No in real terms; cost pass-through only; MFN refund |
| Natera | Bilateral, 11th amendment (Mar 2026), to ~Aug 2033 | No: real price frozen (§7(k)); a new flow cell's real price per Gb cannot exceed the prior version's (§7(l)); the Section 122 tariff can be invoiced above a redacted threshold, non-retroactively (§7(g)) |
| Guardant | Bilateral, to ~Jan 2033; tiered discount off "then-current list price" | In principle yes, through list; Guardant can leave on 90 days' notice |

| Tempus, Foundation, Exact, BillionToOne, GeneDx | Terms not found | Unknown |

## What Illumina has actually done on price

- **Tariff pricing:** about 1 point of 2025 growth came from tariff-related price actions. The CEO: "Nobody likes a price increase."
- **Cost sharing in 2026:** "sharing some of the increase" in memory-chip and freight costs with customers (Q2'26). Which customers is not disclosed.
- **Annual January list changes:** management reported "very good realization" (2024).
- No evidence was found of price increases applied to Open Offer signers.

## Implications for the model

- **For big US oncology accounts, price per Gb only goes down in real terms.** Under Natera's §7(l), the 35B cannot be priced above the 25B per Gb. The 35B is therefore a price cut for these accounts, never a reset upward.
- **Discounts spread.** Through the MFN clause, a concession won by one large lab can spread to comparable labs.
- **Where Illumina can still push price:** non-oncology clinical (prenatal, rare disease), smaller and ex-US labs, research (where competition caps it anyway), and GRAIL.
- **Revenue growth from big accounts must come from elsewhere:** volume and intensity, new higher-priced products (TruPath $395, 5-base, the MRD kit, assay kits), and cost pass-through.
- **Contracts are not the only cap.** 90-day exits and rival quotes limit pricing even where no contract does.
