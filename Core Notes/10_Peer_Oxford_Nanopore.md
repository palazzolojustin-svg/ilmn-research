## What Oxford Nanopore sells and why it matters for Illumina

Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) is a London-listed FTSE 100 company. Its sequencers pull a single strand of DNA or RNA through a tiny protein pore (a "nanopore") and read the changes in electrical current as each base passes. Illumina's dominant technology works differently: it copies DNA into short fragments and reads them optically, an approach called sequencing-by-synthesis. Nanopore reads can be essentially any length, which analysts call "universal reads" [JPM, 2026-05-13]. Nanopores can also read RNA directly and detect methylation (the chemical tags on DNA that affect gene activity) without extra lab steps, and results arrive in real time [DB, 2026-09-21].

ONT holds more than 2,500 active patents across more than 250 patent families. It is headquartered and manufactures in the UK, and sells through direct sales teams and distributors in North America, Europe and Asia-Pacific [DB, 2026-09-21].

### Products

The product range runs from small to large, with recurring consumables underneath.

- **MinION** is the small portable device. It returned to growth in 2025, largely because of Biopharma customers [Barclays, 2026-01-12], and the Mk1D model "has done okay" [Barclays, 2026-03-25].
- **GridION** is a mid-sized benchtop system. The GridION Dx, ONT's first in vitro diagnostic (IVD) device (a regulated device for testing patient samples), received CE and UKCA approval (the EU and UK regulatory marks) in January 2026. "Q-line" GridION products are "locked down" versions for regulated settings such as clinical labs and drug-manufacturing quality control (QC) [Barclays, 2026-03-25].
- **PromethION** is the high-throughput sequencer and now accounts for more than half of group revenue [Barclays, 2026-09-28]. ONT is focusing on the P2i model; the smaller P2 Solo was discontinued in early 2026.
- **ElysION**, a sample-preparation automation platform, was stopped in 2025–26 because ONT "was not the best provider" of automation, producing a £1.8m inventory write-down [JPM, 2026-03-02; Barclays, 2026-03-11].
- **Flow cells** are the consumable chips holding the nanopores. Consumables were about 71% of revenue in H1 2026 [DB, 2026-09-21]. Management now expects a steady-state mix of 75–80% consumables, perhaps by 2028, having earlier assumed 60:40 consumables to devices [Barclays, 2026-09-08].

### Customers and regions

ONT reports four end markets: **Research** (academic and government labs); **Clinical** (anyone using the technology to diagnose patients, mostly through lab-developed tests, or LDTs, which a single lab designs and runs itself, rather than ONT-branded kits [Barclays, 2026-03-02]); **BioPharma** (drug makers and contract manufacturers); and **Applied Industrial** (for example plasmid sequencing, food safety and synthetic biology). The last three together are "Applied Markets."

In H1 2026 Research was 65% of revenue and Applied 35% (Clinical 15%, Industrial 12%, BioPharma 8%). By region, the Americas (AMR) were about 33%, EMEAI (Europe, Middle East, Africa and India) about 48% and APAC (Asia-Pacific) about 19% [DB, 2026-09-21].

Estimates of exposure to US government research funding differ. The NIH (the US National Institutes of Health, the main US research funder) represented about half of Americas revenue in 2025 [Barclays, 2026-03-25], while Deutsche Bank puts NIH-funded customers at about 10–15% of group revenue [DB, 2026-07-20]. US federally funded customers were 13% of revenue in 2024 [JPM, 2026-01-20]. China was about 8% of revenue at the start of 2026 and 44% of APAC revenue [JPM, 2026-01-20; Barclays, 2026-03-11]; management later described it as 10% of 2025 revenue, falling to about 7% [Barclays, 2026-07-13].

### Market size

In January 2026 outgoing CEO Gordon Sanghera put ONT's total market opportunity at $20–25bn, of which $13–14bn is unique to nanopore [JPM, 2026-01-20]. The new strategy narrowed this to a serviceable market of about $14–16bn across priority applications: about $4.1–5.5bn in BioPharma, about $6bn in selected Clinical uses and about $610–740m in targeted Research [DB, 2026-09-21].

### Competitive position versus Illumina, PacBio, Roche and others

All three brokers frame ONT as more complementary to Illumina than a substitute.

**Illumina.** Deutsche Bank's channel checks found ONT "complementary to short reads in many applications, not a broad substitute," with Illumina the scale and workflow benchmark whose installed base generates durable consumables demand [DB, 2026-09-21]. J.P. Morgan lists Illumina's very large installed base, pricing and accuracy as its first risk to ONT [JPM, 2025-12-08 onward]. ONT management dismisses Illumina's TruPath long-read assay as "still short reads and stitching them together," needing a separate methylation test [Barclays, 2026-03-11].

**PacBio** is the more direct long-read threat in Deutsche Bank's view. Its SPRQ-Nx chemistry cut the listed cost per HiFi genome by about 30%, and expert feedback suggests PacBio may be preferred where high consensus accuracy and predictable output matter. ONT leads on read length, native RNA and DNA analysis, real-time results and flexibility [DB, 2026-09-21]. Earlier, Deutsche Bank said PacBio lacked direct methylation and high-throughput scalability and needed larger instruments [DB, 2026-01-22].

**Roche SBX / Axelios.** Roche's Sequencing by Expansion shares only the nanopore sensing element with ONT; Sanghera argues it is fundamentally sequencing-by-synthesis and short-read [JPM, 2026-01-20]. Axelios reads average 400–600 bases, peaking around 1,000, and J.P. Morgan treats Roche as an "indirect additional competitor" taking share mainly from short-read incumbents [JPM, 2026-05-13]. CFO Nick Keher warned Roche could destabilise short-read pricing [DB, 2026-01-22]. Management says "ONT doesn't need to take a copy," calls Roche "somewhat complementary" [Barclays, 2026-03-11; 2026-09-08], and notes Roche cannot sequence native RNA, which ONT "remains the only player able to" do [Barclays, 2026-03-25].

**Element Biosciences** planned an IVDR (EU diagnostic regulation) product in 2026; Sanghera saw no threat because it is short-read [JPM, 2026-01-20]. **In China** there are 7–8 local competitors [Barclays, 2026-09-28], pricing at about a 30% discount [Barclays, 2026-09-08].

### Patents

ONT's patents have about 6–7 to nearly 20 years of remaining life, and each new pore brings a new patent. Some wet-chemistry manufacturing steps are deliberately unpatented to keep them secret; management says competitors are "not close" to replicating them [Barclays, 2026-09-08] but concedes the gap with Chinese rivals will likely narrow [Barclays, 2026-09-28]. ONT won rulings in Europe and Australia: in Australia a competitor "agreed to infringement," and certain competitor products are excluded from parts of the European market [Barclays, 2026-09-08; 2026-09-28]. J.P. Morgan still lists competition from other electrical long-read sequencers after patent expiry as a risk.

### Pricing per genome

This table, relayed by J.P. Morgan after Roche's Diagnostics Day, compares the consumable cost to sequence one human genome. ONT is the premium-priced option today.

| Platform | Cost per genome |
|---|---|
| ONT, 2 genomes per flow cell (early access) | ~$345 |
| ONT, 4 genomes per flow cell (roadmap) | <$200 |
| Illumina | $200 |
| Roche Axelios | $150 |
| Ultima Genomics (target) | $80–100 |

ONT intends to keep premium pricing, arguing it delivers a "fully loaded genome" (long reads plus methylation) that would cost more as a short-read run plus a separate methylation assay [JPM, 2026-05-13].

## Chronology: results, guidance and management commentary

### Leadership change (August 2025 – March 2026)

In August 2025 ONT announced that co-founder and CEO Gordon Sanghera would step down [Barclays, 2025-12-08]. On 8 December 2025 it named Francis van Parys as successor, effective 2 March 2026. Van Parys spent about 2.5 years as CEO of Radiometer, a Danaher subsidiary with more than $1bn of revenue making acute-care, point-of-care diagnostics (tests run at or near the patient) for blood gas and immunoassay testing. Before that they were President of Biotechnology Integrated Solutions at Cytiva for one year, VP Commercial for Asia-Pacific at Cytiva for 2.5 years, and spent 11 years in regional roles at GE Healthcare: more than 20 years in all leading multi-billion-dollar life science businesses. Sanghera left the Board at close of business on 2 March 2026 and stays as an adviser until early 2027.

Analysts welcomed the choice. J.P. Morgan was "encouraged" by the fit between point-of-care experience and ONT's push into Biopharma and Clinical, and expected the shares to outperform by more than 5% that day [JPM, 2025-12-08]. Barclays called it "an incremental positive," citing regulated-market experience that ONT has been "progressively moving towards" through partnerships with bioMérieux and Cepheid (which, like Radiometer, is Danaher-owned) [Barclays, 2025-12-08]. Deutsche Bank said the background "bodes well" for applied-market growth [DB, 2026-01-22].

ONT said FY25 revenue would be £223–224m, 24% growth at constant currency (excluding exchange-rate effects) versus FY24's £183.2m. That beat guidance of 20–23%, which ONT had steered to the top end of in November. The table shows brokers' pre-update estimates, which were close.

| Benchmark | FY25 revenue estimate |
|---|---|
| Barclays | £222.9m |
| Company-compiled consensus | £220.4m |
| Deutsche Bank's consensus figure | £221m |

Implied H2 revenue of £117.9m was about 3% above Bloomberg consensus [JPM], 2.7% above company consensus of £114.8m [Barclays] and 1% above J.P. Morgan's estimate. H2 growth of about 21% was roughly 7 points slower than H1, which J.P. Morgan blamed on a tough comparison (H2 2024 grew 36%, versus 12% in H1 2024).

By segment, Clinical grew about 60% for the year (J.P. Morgan estimated more than 70% in H2); BioPharma about 30% (52% in H2); Applied Industrial 27% (about 7% in H2 on tougher comparisons); and Research 15% "despite end-market funding pressures" (about 11% in H2). PromethION grew more than 40% and every region grew more than 20%; Americas growth accelerated from about 17% in H1 to above 20% in H2, driven by non-Research customers.

Cash was £302m (versus £403.8m at end-FY24), beating estimates of £280.9m (consensus, per Barclays), £276m (consensus, per Deutsche Bank) and £287m (J.P. Morgan), helped by working capital and the new capex sales model (below).

ONT gave no 2026 guidance but reiterated mid-term targets: revenue growth above 30% a year (compound annual growth rate, CAGR) for FY24–FY27; gross margin (revenue left after the direct cost of making products) above 62% by FY27; operating-cost growth of 3–8% a year; Adjusted EBITDA breakeven in 2027 (EBITDA is operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation, amortisation and one-off items); and positive cash flow in 2028. Deutsche Bank said "some may need convincing" on the acceleration needed, called the shares "unduly punished in H2-25" and named ONT a top UK small-and-mid-cap healthcare pick for 2026 [DB, 2026-01-12].

### J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference and dbAccess (January 2026)

At J.P. Morgan's conference, CFO Nick Keher called Adjusted EBITDA breakeven the "North Star," said costs could be "modulated further if required," and noted underlying H1 2025 gross margin was already 61% [JPM, 2026-01-20].

**The capex model.** In 2025 ONT moved from leasing larger devices (customers paying over time) to customers buying them outright. Most larger-device sales were leases at the start of 2025; ONT exited the year near 100% outright. The shift added 3–4 points to H1 2025 revenue growth and £20m to cash flow.

**2026 headwinds** Keher listed were US federally funded customers, China export restrictions, and completed EMEAI contracts, to be offset by the UK Biobank contract (50,000 genomes). **Tailwinds** were Clinical and BioPharma: Americas H2 2025 revenue rose about 30%, Applied markets now exceed half of Americas revenue, and ONT has hired a former head of sales from a large sequencing peer. Sanghera credited the sales team with Americas share gains, described plans for PromethION at 4 genomes per flow cell (2 already in early access), a GridION Q v2 for clinical and biomanufacturing QC, and PromethION Q at end-2026, and was most excited by infectious-disease clinical use.

At dbAccess UK two days later, Keher acknowledged past problems with "launching things too early." Deutsche Bank's takeaways: Applied could reach about 50% of sales and BioPharma 20–30% in time; clinical IVD products are expected within three years (by 2028); and Deutsche Bank and consensus expected breakeven in FY28, a year after the company target [DB, 2026-01-22].

### FY25 results and the guidance reset (2 March 2026)

FY25 revenue was £223.9m, up 24.2% at constant currency and 22.2% reported, against £222.4m consensus. H2 revenue was £118.3m, about 1% above consensus, with Clinical up 80%, BioPharma 52% and Applied Industrial 8% in the half. The table shows the regional split.

| Region | FY25 revenue | Growth | Main drivers |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMEAI | £100.4m | +26.1% | UK and Europe |
| AMR | £74.9m | +18.7% | US Clinical demand |
| APAC | £48.6m | +20.2% | Singapore population genomics, Japan, China (+15.2%) |

Reported gross margin missed: 58.6% for the year versus 59.1% consensus (Barclays also cites 59.6%), and 59.1% in H2. Excluding the ElysION write-down, H2 was 60.6% and the year 59.4% (consensus 59%) [JPM; DB]. The Adjusted EBITDA loss of £86.7m beat £90.2m consensus, and the H2 loss of £38.4m was 9% better than consensus. H2 adjusted operating costs rose 1% to £137m (R&D down 9% to £45m; SG&A, selling, general and administrative costs, up 6%). Year-end cash was £302.8m versus £294.5m consensus.

**New guidance replaced the mid-term targets:**

- **FY26 revenue growth** of 21–25% at constant currency, versus 27.5% consensus. With a 2% currency headwind, J.P. Morgan got about £270m, 5% below consensus.
- **FY26 gross margin** of about 62% (consensus 60.9%), driven by flow-cell recycling (reusing returned chips) and the pricing model.
- **FY26 operating-cost growth** of 0–5% and an **Adjusted EBITDA loss** of £45–55m.
- **FY27 growth** "broadly similar" to FY26, replacing the >30% CAGR, which Barclays called effectively removing the mid-term growth guide.
- **Unchanged:** EBITDA breakeven in 2027 and positive cash flow in 2028.

J.P. Morgan calculated this implied cutting 2027 consensus revenue about 11% at the midpoint (£325.3m versus £365.2m) and gross profit about 8%. Shares fell 12% on the day versus a 1% fall in the European sector [Barclays, 2026-03-02], having already lost about 22% in the prior month, so J.P. Morgan called the cut "at least somewhat anticipated" and the breakeven reiteration "reassuring."

Management added [Barclays, 2026-03-02] that the low end reflects a subdued US, the P2 Solo discontinuation and China, while the top end needs more P2i conversions; recent Middle East developments are excluded; 2027 growth need only match 2026 for breakeven; 62% margin brings the old target forward a year, with about 64% achievable in 2027; and revenue is phased about 45:55 between halves. Other points: PromethION recycling gains were offset in 2025 by lower MinION recycling; the EBITDA loss improved 26% in FY25 and 32% in H2, despite a £9m currency headwind since 2024; ONT restructured twice in 2025 (January and November); £80m reported inventory is net of provisions and can fall further; one mRNA contract is signed with another hoped for in 2026; and a large drug maker uses ONT for sterility testing.

### March conferences and the "clearing event" thesis

At the Barclays Global Healthcare Conference, Keher and Head of IR Charlotte Parry said the move to outright sales added 350bps to gross margin in H1 2025 and 400bps for the full year (100 basis points = 1 percentage point), with a similar revenue effect; the 2026 margin benefit should be 100–200bps [Barclays, 2026-03-11]. In EMEAI, about $10m of finishing contracts nets against about $10m of 2026 UK Biobank revenue (the full contract is in the low-teens of millions). In APAC, the end of Singapore's PRECISE contract is a $3m H1 2026 headwind (the $6m contract was split roughly evenly between H2 2024 and H1 2025). China export restrictions created a low-single-digit-million-dollar flow-cell backlog; the situation was "getting worse not better," making China APAC's "wild card." The ViruSure biopharma deal is financially small, and drug makers and CDMOs (contract manufacturers) are not working together: pharma wants testing in-house, CDMOs want to sell it to everyone.

On 25 March Barclays called the guidance cut a "clearing event" and moved coverage to Jonathon Unwin [Barclays, 2026-03-25]. It decomposed the 2025 shortfall against the >30% plan: pricing and purchasing changes added about 4 points (underlying growth about 20%); NIH cost 3 points (NIH-funded revenue grew 3% versus 20% planned); China restrictions, linked to NVIDIA chip rules and customer restriction lists, cost 1.5 points; leaving a 5.5-point gap that management attributes partly to a slower market and partly to execution (GridION Q for Biopharma and ElysION underperformed, and China could have been run better).

Management also said better PromethION yield and recycling could lift PromethION gross margin 10 points, implying about 65% for the group exiting 2027; Adjusted EBITDA margin could eventually reach the mid-20s; with no US manufacturing, all US-dollar headwinds hit profit; inventory built under COVID-era supply agreements should fall to £80–90m by 2027; and capitalised R&D is amortised over three years. The amPORE TB test with bioMérieux, launched for research use in November 2025, will not be a big revenue line but proves ONT can build a regulated test. BioPharma contracts are each worth single-digit millions of pounds, cover mRNA vaccine trials of about 1,000 patients, and could lock in revenue for about 20 years if ONT is written into an approved vaccine's filing. Transformational growth is now likelier in 2028 than 2027; BioPharma could still reach 20% of revenue by 2027, though Clinical has grown faster than expected.

### Roche Diagnostics Day and London Calling (May 2026)

**Roche Diagnostics Day (12 May).** Roche set a "summer 2026" Axelios launch and called 100 placements a successful first year. J.P. Morgan compared earlier first-year launches (PacBio Revio 173 in nine months of 2023, Element's Aviti 112, Illumina's NovaSeq X 352) and judged Axelios's effect on ONT's 2027 growth "likely to be limited," even allowing for conservatism. Roche still targets "significant market share" and blockbuster sales, citing positive feedback from "a few hundred customers." J.P. Morgan noted ONT's 99.8% accuracy with its Dorado v5 basecaller [JPM, 2026-05-13].

**London Calling (21 May).** At ONT's annual event, with van Parys, Keher and Chief Scientific Officer Lakmal Jayasinghe, ONT announced [JPM, 2026-05-22]:

- **PromethION Plus** from July 2026, removing the wash-and-reload step and raising yield about 20% (149GB to 177GB per run).
- An upgraded **GridION Q-line** from June, adding the R10.4.1 flow cell, rapid and rapid-PCR barcoding kits, an RNA flow cell and direct-RNA kit RNA004.
- **mRNA QC with Lonza:** sequence confirmation, N1mpU modified-base reads and poly(A) tail length now; 5′ cap and double-stranded RNA detection in development. **Viral contaminant (AVA) testing** is also in development to replace several tests with one platform.
- The **HAC v6.0 basecaller** (software turning electrical signal into DNA letters), raising accuracy from Q22.6 to Q23.5 (about 99.4%), with better insertion/deletion, variant and methylation calling; ONT says the slower "super accuracy" model is no longer needed. It is on GitHub.
- **Peptide sequencing progress:** proteins cut into peptides on DNA handles, detecting modifications such as phosphorylation.
- **New executives:** Davide Manissero (now Radiometer CMO, previously QIAGEN, ECDC and WHO) becomes first Chief Medical Officer on 3 August; Andrew Watson (from Mimecast, previously Healx and Dyson) becomes CIO on 15 June; both follow Chief People Officer Tina St Leger. Sales and marketing move from Keher to van Parys on 1 July.

ONT argued AI depends on data quality, leaving it "uniquely positioned."

### H1 2026 trading update and second guidance adjustment (July 2026)

On 2 July J.P. Morgan forecast H1 revenue of £118.9m (+14.5%), about 5% below a "stale" £125.4m Bloomberg consensus and 2% below the £121.5m implied by guidance, and warned FY26 guidance might be cut to 18–22% on China and the Middle East. It cited peers: in Q1 Illumina's China revenue fell 29%, PacBio's APAC fell 16%, and PacBio cut its FY26 guide from 3–12% to 3–9%.

On 13 July ONT reported H1 revenue of £116.5m, up 12% at constant currency, versus £105.6m in H1 2025 (when growth was 28%). The table shows the size of the miss.

China fell 16% (tighter export controls and a commercial reorganisation) and the Middle East 14% (geopolitical disruption); excluding both, growth was 16%. Barclays then cut its target to 160p and Deutsche Bank to 195p. Deutsche Bank noted Q2 2026 NIH disbursements rose 5% year on year to $11.4bn and its checks showed academic spending improving, and argued the depressed price "could also increasingly attract strategic interest" [DB, 2026-07-20]. Management was comfortable with consensus year-end cash of about £180m [DB, 2026-07-13].

### Post-results management meetings (September 2026)

**Barclays meeting, 8 September.** Management said about 200bps of 2027 gross-margin expansion, not 400bps, suffices for breakeven; Clinical is "a volume game with competitive pricing," while BioPharma margins are far higher. ONT's mRNA-QC moat is reading RNA directly, revealing modifications, degradation and purity that copy-based methods lose; each batch equals one patient, at a $2,000 list price per test. Management narrowed 47 applications to 18 (two Research, eight BioPharma split five QC and three development, eight Clinical). Van Parys sees no need for big cost increases: R&D will focus on enhancing products rather than new platforms, and the generalist, academia-focused sales force needs only refinement. Few global diagnostics firms can license ONT's technology; bioMérieux and Cepheid remain key in infectious disease, which needs CE-marked IVD approval [Barclays, 2026-09-08].

**Barclays investor trip, 28 September.** The 18 applications are 40–45% of revenue; the other 55–60% should grow mid-single digits, and management calls $700m conservative. Realised mRNA prices are about $1,000 against a $2,000–2,500 list, ONT has declined 10-year fixed-price contracts, and "essentially all major players" in mRNA vaccines are evaluating or using ONT. ONT can replace Sanger sequencing, HPLC and mass spectrometry, cut QC timelines by 3–4 weeks, and is already written into BLA filings (regulatory applications for biologic drugs) for some critical quality attributes (CQAs): CQA1 now, CQA2 in January 2027, CQA3 in 2027. The initial cohort is about 25,000 melanoma patients (about 100,000 across the wider set), with 3–4 CQAs per patient. China should keep declining, with no growth in the long-term plan; the Cepheid product is on track for 2027; ONT has had enquiries about AI datasets approaching one million genomes, with UK Biobank data releases due later in 2026. R&D stays flat in sterling, M&A means bolt-ons in bioinformatics or sample prep, and a fuller strategy update is due in February 2027 [Barclays, 2026-09-28].

**Deutsche Bank review, 21 September.** Consumables gross margin is about 75% (above 80% possible) and devices and services about 34% (heading to about 40%). Net cash should stay above about £100m through breakeven. BioPharma and Clinical could be about 65% of 2030 revenue, and about 20 biopharma customers are evaluating ONT. ONT papers reached almost 1,900 in 2025, twice PacBio's and more than 30% of Illumina's (up from 2% in 2017). Deutsche Bank warned that strong device growth came with only 2.7% consumables growth (period not stated, presumably H1 2026): "The central risk is not whether ONT's technology is differentiated, but whether the company can translate that differentiation into scaled, recurring revenue" [DB, 2026-09-21].

## What Oxford Nanopore sells and how it positions itself against Illumina

The earnings-call and conference transcripts add the company's own framing. ONT was founded in Oxford in 2005. Devices include the MinION, GridION and PromethION range (P2 Solo, P2i and the large P24). About 70% of revenue comes from flow cells and kits made in ONT's Didcot, UK factory, which management says has capacity for the next 3–5 years of growth [Q4 2025 call].

Management's central claim is that ONT reads **native DNA and RNA**, the original molecule with no copying or chemical conversion, at lengths from a few hundred bases to millions. That lets it detect methylation (sometimes called the "fifth base") and RNA modifications in the same run, with sample prep in hours rather than days, no need to batch samples, and small, cheap instruments. "We are not just a longread company. It's that native DNA that makes us highly differentiated," Sanghera said, claiming "there is no other company in this space" that sequences direct DNA and RNA [Q4 2025 call]. Illumina's short-read machines, by contrast, read short copied fragments at very high volume and low cost per base.

ONT says it has customers in more than 125 countries, over 1,300 employees, and 20,000 publications in the 10 years since its first products. DNA products launched about 10 years ago and direct RNA about 5 years ago, and revenue has grown at a 28% CAGR since IPO. Research is about two-thirds of revenue [Q4 2025 call].

## March 2026: CFO at the Barclays Global Healthcare Conference [Barclays, 2026-03-11]

In a fireside chat with Barclays' Jon Unwin, Keher went through each market.

### Applied industrial

ONT sees about $1–1.5bn in synthetic biology where it has "a right to win," replacing Sanger sequencing (the older, low-throughput method), helped by the ability to "debatch" (run a few samples on demand). Plasmid sequencer Plasmidsaurus is a customer doing "very well."

### Clinical

The technology is "really of age" in rare disease, infectious disease and oncology. In **rare disease**, ONT claims higher diagnostic yield than short reads by detecting structural variants (large DNA rearrangements); US reimbursement is emerging and customers are moving it from a "reflex test" (follow-up) to frontline. In **infectious disease**, ONT offers metagenomic sequencing (reading everything in a sample) in 3–4 hours, and Cepheid and bioMérieux will take it into IVD kits as "intel inside" where PCR "don't quite do it." ONT's own amPORE TB test has **CE-IVD** status, though "maybe we might have thought about doing that in a different way"; the GridION Dx also has it, while most partners need only a Q-line platform. In **oncology**, St. Jude told ONT that adaptive sampling can replace about $1,000 of AML (acute myeloid leukemia) pathology tests with a $150 test giving "the exact same answers," a "big driver" for ONT. On **methylation**, "we have the gold standard": conventional platforms take a deep but narrow view, while ONT captures the "entire haystack." A New York customer uses methylation to predict transplant failure, with similar logic for cancer recurrence, and the UK Biobank study should spark "a whole new wave of interest."

### Market sizing (Keher's estimates)

NGS spending totals $9–10bn, one-third to one-half clinical. ONT's serviceable market of $20–25bn also counts conventional tests such as PCR; clinical is about $10bn, biopharma $3–4bn, and the "should-win" set $13–14bn. Displacing tests that already work is "maybe a fool's errand"; ONT sits "on top" of existing lab devices, follows "land and expand," and lacks some competitors' ecosystems, "but that is coming."

### Biopharma QC

Plasmids are the "door opener," and "the gold standard will become ONT." In mRNA vaccines, ONT has a QC contract with "one of the leading players," replacing "7 to 8 orthogonal types of tests" such as HPLC, mass spectrometry and gels; "we're being evaluated by all of the players," though "these things take time." Other uses are AAV (a gene-therapy delivery virus) and sterility testing. Volume is per patient for personalised cancer vaccines and per facility (possibly several sites) for sterility.

### Why the >30% target was missed

Unwin noted growth was running 5–10 points below the 2024 target. Keher said that on joining in 2024 the company was aiming at unachievable 2026 breakeven, so it reset to 2027 EBITDA and 2028 cash breakeven, supported by >30% CAGR, >62% margin and 3–8% cost growth. Growth came in at 23% (2024) and 24% (2025) as NIH cuts and China controls hit after guidance was set: "We didn't expect it to be this size of a headwind," and "we could have done better as well." Two 2025 cost cuts show "we do what we say," and 2027 breakeven was "written in stone, and we've kept it in stone." ONT is still "growing a lot faster than all of the competition, undoubted."
