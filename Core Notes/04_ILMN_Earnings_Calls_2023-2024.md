# Illumina Earnings Calls, 2023-2024

*Core Notes section 04. Synthesized from 8 source extracts as of 2026-10-10. Bracketed tags cite the source; full detail is in Working Notes (see INDEX.md).*

2023 was the year Illumina launched its most important new sequencer in a decade, the NovaSeq X, and the year its financial story unraveled. In April the company beat its own forecasts, held its full-year outlook and promised rising margins. By August a new interim CEO was cutting guidance and calling the rollout "more challenging" than expected. By November a permanent CEO was cutting guidance again and warning that 2024 would look like 2023. In February 2024 management guided 2024 to flat revenue. Through all four calls, management kept framing the weakness mainly as a temporary platform transition plus a tough economy, while China was the one market where it openly admitted competition was hurting. The calls also tracked the long struggle over GRAIL, the cancer-screening company that European regulators ordered Illumina to divest.

Some conventions apply throughout. Unless stated otherwise, figures are **non-GAAP** (adjusted to exclude certain accounting items; Illumina's version still includes stock-based compensation). **"Core Illumina"** means Illumina excluding GRAIL. **Consolidated** figures include GRAIL. On every call, Investor Relations head Salli Schwartz reminded listeners that GRAIL must be "held and operated separately and independently from Illumina" under interim measures ordered by the European Commission (EC), which prohibited the acquisition under the EU Merger Regulation.

## Q1 2023 (call held April 25, 2023)

### Setting

The speakers were Francis deSouza (President and CEO), Joydeep Goswami (CFO and Chief Strategy and Corporate Development Officer) and Salli Schwartz (investor relations). The call fell on DNA Day, the 70th anniversary of the discovery of the double helix, in the month of Illumina's 25th anniversary. deSouza noted that only about 4 million genomes have ever been sequenced, "representing only 0.07% of the population," so "we are still at the beginning of the genome era" [Q1 2023 call].

### Results against expectations

Consolidated revenue, meaning Illumina including GRAIL (its cancer-screening subsidiary), was about $1.09 billion. That was down 11% from Q1 2022, or 9% in **constant currency** (stripping out exchange-rate swings, net of hedging), but up 1% from Q4 2022. It beat the top of management's guidance range and beat analyst **consensus** (the average analyst forecast) by about $20.7 million. **Non-GAAP** EPS (adjusted earnings per share that exclude certain one-off items) of $0.08 beat consensus by $0.05 [Seeking Alpha header; Q1 2023 call]. deSouza said both figures beat start-of-year guidance but fell year over year "as expected." The beat came from shipping 67 NovaSeq X instruments, Illumina's new flagship high-throughput sequencer, against an expected 40 to 50.

Management gave four reasons for the year-over-year decline: the expected fade of **COVID surveillance** (sequencing used to track virus variants), COVID-related disruption in China, some high-throughput customers moving to the X, and customers coping with tight capital markets. COVID surveillance revenue fell to about $11 million from $60 million a year earlier, a $49 million headwind.

Non-GAAP net income was $13 million, or $0.08 per diluted share on about 158 million shares, after absorbing GRAIL's $164 million non-GAAP operating loss. The tax rate was 27.3%, versus 17.8% in Q1 2022. Goswami explained that both quarters reflect **R&D capitalization** (a US tax rule forcing R&D costs to be deducted over several years rather than immediately); the dollar impact was the same, but it weighed more on the rate because earnings were lower.

### Core Illumina by product line

Core Illumina (excluding GRAIL) revenue was $1.08 billion, down 12% year over year (10% in constant currency), including about 400 **basis points** of COVID drag (a basis point is a hundredth of a percentage point). The table compares the three product lines with Q1 2022.

| Line | Q1 2023 | Change and drivers |
|---|---|---|
| Sequencing consumables | $692M | −12% y/y, +1% sequentially; about 500 bp COVID headwind |
| Sequencing instruments | $154M | −27% y/y; 300 bp COVID headwind |
| Sequencing service and other | $119M | +7% y/y |

**Consumables** are the reagent kits and flow cells customers must buy for every sequencing run. Beyond COVID, they fell on lower **pull-through** (consumables spending per installed instrument) on the NovaSeq 6000, China disruption, the move to the X, and tight capital markets. Instruments fell mainly because NovaSeq 6000 shipments compared against a record Q1 2022 and fewer mid-throughput machines shipped, especially NextSeq 550s in China. X shipments partly offset this, though Illumina "remain[s] supply constrained in the first year of launch." Demand for the NextSeq 1000/2000 (mid-throughput benchtop sequencers) from new-to-Illumina customers stayed strong. Service grew on contracts for a larger installed base.

Core **gross margin** (revenue minus production cost, as a share of revenue) was 65.2%, down 500 bp. Goswami blamed lower manufacturing volumes, which spread fixed costs over fewer units, and lower instrument margins during the X launch, "which is typical with a new platform introduction," and expected improvement every quarter as X production scaled. Core operating expenses were $514 million, up $9 million on the full-year effect of 2022 hiring, but below plan thanks to cost containment.

Operating cash flow was $10 million and capital expenditures $52 million, so **free cash flow** (operating cash minus capital spending) was −$42 million. Cash and short-term investments were about $1.5 billion. Illumina bought back no shares and used $500 million to repay term notes that matured in March.

### Sequencing activity

Goswami cited run counts on connected high- and mid-throughput instruments, which are "directionally correlated with revenue over time." Total activity rose 6% from Q4 and 3% year over year. Research and applied activity rose 7% sequentially but fell 4% year over year. Clinical activity was "robust," up 7% sequentially and 15% year over year.

### Regions

Illumina introduced a new structure folding Asia-Pacific together with emerging markets in the Middle East, Africa, Turkey and the CIS. It now reports four regions: Americas, Europe, Greater China and AMEA (Asia Pacific, Middle East and Africa). The table shows Q1 results.

| Region | Q1 2023 | Change | Drivers [Goswami] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Americas | $605M | +5% q/q; −6% y/y | Beat on X shipments. Research weaker (COVID fade, delayed recruitment on large projects). NextSeq 1k/2k shipments up more than 20% |
| Europe | $261M | −9% (−4% cc) | Oncology-led clinical growth more than offset by COVID fade and fewer high-throughput instruments due to X supply limits |
| Greater China | $91M | −28% (−23% cc) | COVID disruption and customer liquidity and funding problems; lower consumables and mid-throughput |
| AMEA | $119M | −27% (−23% cc) | A large Japanese research project ended; COVID fade; fewer NovaSeq 6000s; Russia sanctions |

Goswami said Russia sanctions would persist through 2023; the outlook absorbs about $60 million of lost AMEA consumables revenue for the year.

### GRAIL

GRAIL revenue was $20 million, double the prior year and ahead of plan, on adoption of **Galleri** (its blood test that screens for many cancers at once). It fell from Q4, which had included a milestone payment from **MRD** partnerships with drug companies (minimal residual disease testing, which detects cancer traces left after treatment). Operating expenses were $173 million, up $34 million, funding clinical trials and a sales organization.

Galleri had received more than 85,000 orders since launch, about 20,000 in Q1. The 140,000-person NHS-Galleri trial finished enrollment in July 2022 in just over ten months; more than half of participants had returned for their second annual draw, and it was past halfway and on track for retention targets. John Hancock (a Manulife unit) will expand Galleri access to eligible life-insurance customers after a pilot, and Providence will offer it across 52 hospitals and 900 clinics in seven states.

### Guidance: 2023 held, Q2 set, a back-half ramp promised

Goswami held the full-year 2023 outlook and reaffirmed all other guidance from February 7, 2023:
- consolidated revenue growth of 7% to 10%, with Core growth of 6% to 9% including about 200 bp of COVID headwind plus currency headwinds;
- GRAIL revenue of $90 million to $110 million;
- non-GAAP EPS of $1.25 to $1.50;
- consolidated operating margin of about 8% and Core operating margin of about 22%;
- Core sequencing revenue growth of about 8% at the midpoint.

Three offsetting changes sat inside the unchanged range: lost Russia revenue, NovaSeq X shipments raised to more than 330 (from 300), and higher drug-discovery partnership revenue. At the midpoint, instrument growth rose to about 13%, consumables growth fell to about 5.5% (mainly Russia), and service rose on pharma partnerships.

Goswami expected revenue to climb through the year as in 2017, when the NovaSeq 6000 launched and about 54% of revenue fell in the second half (about 26% in Q3, the rest in Q4). The ramp assumed rising quarterly X shipments, consumables growth as X customers start projects in the second half, and easing macro headwinds including a China recovery.

For Q2, management guided revenue of $1.15 to $1.17 billion, about $70 million (670 bp) above Q1 at the midpoint, on more X shipments, consumables, service and GRAIL revenue, partly offset by a seasonal microarray dip. Midpoint EPS was about $0.01, with consolidated operating margin about 1% and Core about 17%, held down by March equity grants and merit raises but expected to improve in the second half.

### Cost cuts and 2024–25 margin targets

Earlier that day, Illumina committed to Core operating margins of 25% in 2024 and 27% in 2025 while funding its innovation roadmap, and announced more than $100 million of annualized cost cuts starting later in 2023, building on November 2022 actions. Goswami listed four sources: reusing modular R&D building blocks such as **XLEAP-SBS chemistry** (the faster, cheaper sequencing chemistry built for the X) and the new flow cell; moving work to lower-cost hubs; streamlining real estate, vendors and IT; and focusing on highly differentiated products.

### Strategy and commercial highlights [deSouza]

deSouza's 2023 priorities were scaling X production, driving **demand elasticity** (customers sequencing more as cost per sample falls), and improving margins. Q1 milestones were the first X shipments, the launch of Illumina Complete Long Read technology, and Illumina Connected Insights, cloud software for interpreting results that helps more labs do comprehensive tumor profiling.

**High-throughput.** Illumina exited Q1 with more than 200 X orders from more than 30 countries. About 40% came from clinical customers, more than expected, and about 15% from customers new to high-throughput. Early runs exceeded 7 terabases, with more than 95% of bases above Q30 (a standard accuracy threshold), "well ahead of published specifications." Elasticity examples included a European institute planning "atlas-scale" multiomics, spatial and single-cell projects; clinical labs moving from targeted panels to exomes and whole genomes; an oncology lab adding **liquid biopsy** (cancer testing from blood), which needs 12 to 15 times more sequencing than solid-tumor testing; and labs entering MRD. Seventeen NovaSeq 6000s also shipped, mostly fleet expansions, more than 80% to clinical customers and about 25% to oncology.

**Mid- and low-throughput.** NextSeq 1k/2k shipments rose 6% as customers expanded fleets or upgraded from the MiSeq and NextSeq 550, with more than 20% to new-to-Illumina customers. Win rates were strong, but "we are seeing some sales cycles lengthen." About 30% of low-throughput shipments went to new customers.

**Clinical.** For the first time, clinical customers made up 50% of sequencing consumables revenue.
- **Oncology** consumables fell 3% year over year but rose 21% sequentially; deSouza warned that profitability pressure at some customers could slow test development.
- **TruSight Oncology 500 (TSO 500)**, Illumina's broad tumor-profiling kit, earned more than $25 million, up 26%, across more than 540 accounts.
- An expanded Myriad Genetics partnership broadens **HRD** testing (homologous recombination deficiency, a DNA-repair defect in ovarian, breast, prostate and pancreatic cancers). TSO 500 HRD (research use only) is available in the US, with Florida Cancer Specialists an early customer, and will be developed as a **companion diagnostic** (a test that selects patients for a specific drug).
- In Europe, seven Italian regions committed €10 million for lung-cancer **NGS** (next-generation sequencing), German insurers began paying for whole-genome sequencing in relapsed pediatric cancer, and the Czech Republic added comprehensive genomic profiling to its fee schedule from January 1.
- **Reproductive health** consumables fell 4% (mainly currency) but rose 14% sequentially. Virginia and Michigan Medicaid began covering **NIPT** (noninvasive prenatal testing from the mother's blood) for all pregnancies, adding about 4.3 million covered lives and more than 70,000 pregnancies a year.
- **Genetic disease testing** had a record quarter, up 7% year over year and 6% sequentially. A large US insurer began covering rare-disease whole-genome sequencing in Managed Medicaid across 16 states (about 3.8 million lives), and Illumina partnered with Henry Ford Health on whole-genome sequencing in cardiovascular care for underserved populations.

**Research and applied** consumables fell 19%, mainly from the COVID fade. Coming launches included the Complete Long Read enrichment assay in Q3 2023 and XLEAP-SBS on the NextSeq 1k/2k in the first half of 2024. deSouza said Illumina was "on track to deliver on our financial commitments for 2023."

### Analyst Q&A

**Puneet Souda (SVB Securities)** asked whether 330 X units could rise, about distractions from the EC, FTC and an activist campaign, and about biotech funding. deSouza said 330 is "more manufacturing bound than demand bound," with clinical demand leading this time, unlike past research-led launches. Q2 was planned at 80 units. "The story of this year is the X," and "the vast, vast, vast majority" of management attention goes to products. Small-biotech exposure is very small. The activist campaign was not directly addressed.

**Dan Brennan (TD Cowen)** asked about replacement ratios, filling X capacity and GRAIL timing. deSouza modeled demand "customer out": of about 1,000 high-throughput customers, roughly 750 own a single NovaSeq 6000 and cannot buy less than one X, implying about 750 units "right off the bat" before elasticity; the other 250 will apply their own ratios. Filling capacity is not a constraint because buyers want elasticity. GRAIL matters "come to a head" in late 2023 or early 2024: the EU jurisdiction appeal and US FTC appeal were pending, the Fifth Circuit had just granted expedited review, and Illumina expected the EC's divestiture order "shortly." Goswami added that Illumina earns money from clinical trials of new tests before launch.

**Dan Arias (Stifel)** asked whether elasticity arrives in the second half. Goswami said yes: installs happen in Q2–Q3, and projects ramp in earnest in Q4.

**Vijay Kumar (Evercore ISI)** asked about Russia and margins. Goswami put Russia at about $60 million for the year and $18 million in Q2. Gross margin dipped on low early X margins (with more units than planned) and seasonally weak Q1 factory absorption. The Q2 17% margin reflects the raise cycle and instrument-heavy mix after the Russia consumables loss. Goswami expected to exit 2023 with gross margin in the high 60s and operating margin "close to the high 20% numbers."

**Tejas Savant (Morgan Stanley)** asked why X units beat but instrument revenue did not, and about NextSeq pricing against rivals near $2 per gigabase. Goswami said fewer NextSeq 550s in China explained it: "We're not seeing any differential with the expected prices nor a decrease in the prices that we track." deSouza said XLEAP will lower NextSeq prices to "maximize the market opportunity" while charging for unique features: onboard computing on FPGAs (programmable chips), storage optimization and ambient shipping without a cold chain. Illumina would "divest promptly" if it lost either GRAIL appeal and keep GRAIL only if it won both and the business case held.

**Julia Qin (J.P. Morgan)** asked about margin risk, manufacturing, R&D savings and China. Goswami held the 22% guide (the transcript says "2022," evidently a slip for 2023). Initial X manufacturing efficiencies arrive by end-2023, with more in years two and three; R&D savings show "in '24 and beyond." On China: "we are not seeing the benefits of any stimulus in China, at least for our business."

**David Westenberg (Piper Sandler)** asked about 6000 decommissioning. Goswami said most customers, especially clinical, keep their 6000s; only 10 to 12 decommissions were expected among early X customers.

**Kyle Mikson (Canaccord)** asked whether Illumina is stuck in boom-bust cycles, citing strong growth after HiSeq X (2014–15) and NovaSeq 6000 (2017–18), followed by single-digit growth in 2016 and a 2019 setback. deSouza said that with half of consumables now clinical, growth increasingly comes from clinical adoption, not just upgrades. Goswami added that transition dips are larger for research customers.

## Q2 2023 (call held August 9, 2023)

### A changed company

The call followed a leadership shake-up. General Counsel Charles "Chuck" Dadswell had been interim CEO for about two months after Francis deSouza left. Chairman John Thompson also left; Stephen MacMillan became Chairman, and Andrew Teno and Scott Ullem joined the Board. CTO Alex Aravanis left to become CEO elsewhere and was replaced by Steve Barnard, "employee number four" and Illumina's first scientist hire. Chief Medical Officer Phil Febbo also departed; Illumina will review its medical organization before seeking a successor. Dadswell said the Board was "pleased with the outstanding candidates" for CEO, internal and external [Q2 2023 call].

### Results: a beat, then a cut

Consolidated revenue was about $1.18 billion, up 8% from Q1 and 1% year over year (3% constant currency), above the top of guidance on X shipments and about $11 million above consensus. Non-GAAP EPS was $0.32 against guidance of about $0.01, beating consensus by $0.30. Net income was $50 million, including GRAIL's $164 million operating loss, helped by cost cuts, better gross margin and higher revenue. The tax rate was 39.3% versus 25.8%, again inflated by R&D capitalization against lower earnings. Diluted shares were about 158 million.

Consumables reflected mid-teens clinical growth (led by oncology and genetic disease) offset by research weakness, which came from about 260 bp of COVID drag, Russia, constrained funding, and customers cutting 6000 purchases before ramping on the X. In instruments, X shipments more than offset fewer 6000s, fewer NextSeq 550s in China and fewer MiSeqs as buyers moved to the NextSeq 1k/2k, whose shipments grew 8% to about 160 units, more than 20% to new customers. Service grew on contracts, partly offset by lower co-development revenue. Gross margin fell on X launch margins, lower volumes, and higher field service and installation costs. Expenses were below plan on accelerated cuts and lower performance pay.

Sequencing activity rose 3% from Q1 and 9% year over year: research flat sequentially and down 1% year over year, clinical up 6% and 23%. Illumina said it would switch to reporting **gigabases** sequenced (billions of DNA letters read) instead of run counts, since X and NextSeq 1k/2k runs produce far more data each.

Every region suffered from tight funding and the X transition, an "anticipated" effect "magnified" by more X deliveries and a slower ramp. Clinical consumables in the Americas, Europe and AMEA grew just under 20%. Operating cash flow was $105 million, capital expenditures $47 million and free cash flow $58 million. Cash was about $1.6 billion, with no buybacks; $750 million of convertible debt was due in August 2023.

On July 12, 2023, the EC fined Illumina €432 million for closing the GRAIL deal while the EC review was pending. Illumina planned to post a guarantee and defer payment while appealing the EU General Court's ruling that the EC had jurisdiction.

Dadswell gave three reasons: a larger-than-expected temporary drop in high-throughput consumables as customers moved to the X; cautious customer purchasing; and in China, a slower recovery and an "increasingly challenged competitive landscape." The Americas and Europe were still expected to grow. The table compares new and April guidance.

| GRAIL revenue | $90M–$110M | Unchanged |
| Core instruments | ~+3% | ~+13% |

Goswami gave four reasons consumables would fall: funding problems delaying projects; a steeper 6000 decline ahead of X moves, worsened because Illumina was shipping more X units; a delayed X consumables ramp; and a slower China recovery. Instrument guidance also reflected capital limits and Chinese mid-throughput competition. Margins fell with revenue and gross margin, partly offset by the accelerated $100 million-plus cost program.

New 2023 pull-through ranges (annual consumables per installed instrument) were: NovaSeq 6000 $800,000–$900,000 (lowered because of the transition); NextSeq 550 $80,000–$130,000 and MiSeq $30,000–$40,000 (both changed); NextSeq 1000/2000 unchanged at $120,000–$170,000; MiniSeq unchanged at $20,000–$25,000. No X figure would be given until the ramp stabilizes.

For Q3, management guided revenue of about $1.14 billion, up about 2% year over year but about 320 bp below Q2 because of lower 6000 consumables, with EPS of $0.10–$0.15, consolidated operating margin about 4% and Core about 19%.

### The NovaSeq X rollout

Dadswell said the rollout "has been a more challenging process than we anticipated." The X is "the most sophisticated platform we have ever launched," with Illumina's most comprehensive software, rolled out at "unprecedented magnitude and pace." Typical new-launch field issues had appeared. Illumina released a planned software update in June (first shipments were in March) and sent technical teams worldwide; onboarding would take longer than planned.

Demand still looked healthy. Orders exceeded 260 since launch, and Q2 shipments of 109 beat the planned 80, bringing the installed base to 176. Twenty percent of buyers had ordered more than one X. Customers were seeking funding for larger **single-cell** and **spatial** experiments (analyzing cells one at a time, or mapping where molecules sit in tissue); population programs were planning **multiomic** work (combining several molecular data types); and large customers were moving from targeted panels and **exomes** (the protein-coding 1–2% of the genome) to whole genomes.

### Other strategic and commercial messages

Clinical reached about 53% of sequencing consumables.
- **Oncology coverage:** Anthem (the second-largest US commercial payer) and Blue Cross Blue Shield of Michigan began covering comprehensive genomic profiling for advanced cancer, adding more than 30 million lives. Switzerland began reimbursing large NGS panels.
- **TSO 500** was on track to exceed $100 million of 2023 revenue. Illumina completed its TSO Comprehensive submission for US **IVD** registration (in vitro diagnostic, an FDA-regulated test).
- **GRAIL:** Galleri passed its 100,000th commercial test, with more than 7,500 prescribers and more than 80 health systems. NHS-Galleri completed second-year visits with 130,000 participants returning (91.3% retention) and began third-year invitations for fall appointments. At ASCO, the Oxford-sponsored SYMPLIFY study showed high specificity, positive predictive value and accuracy in detecting cancer and predicting its origin, supporting primary-care referral decisions. At AACR, GRAIL and AstraZeneca reported 92% MRD detection in relapsed or refractory patients across six blood cancers.
- **NIPT:** five state Medicaid programs (Louisiana, Michigan, North Carolina, Rhode Island, Tennessee) now cover all pregnancies; the Netherlands offers NIPT for all pregnancies; Italy approved broader coverage.
- **Research:** the Alliance for Genomic Discovery (with Nashville Biosciences) now has five founding members (AbbVie, Amgen, AstraZeneca, Bayer, Merck), co-funding whole-genome sequencing of 250,000 samples. Phase one, announced in January, covers 35,000 samples, mostly from people of African ancestry.
- **Innovation:** PrimateAI-3D (an AI that predicts disease-causing mutations) was Science's June cover story, and DRAGEN 4.2 analysis software launched in July. Coming: the 25B flow cell for the X, the Complete Long Reads enrichment assay, XLEAP-SBS on NextSeq 1k/2k, and work in proteomics, multiomics and spatial.
- **Costs:** the $100 million-plus program was accelerated; a late-June 8-K disclosed headcount and real-estate cuts, plus vendor and travel savings.

Dadswell closed: "While the year hasn't progressed the way we expected, we remain focused on execution, innovation, and supporting our customers."

### Analyst Q&A

**Puneet Souda (SVB Leerink)** asked about field problems, elasticity, a market freeze and the CEO search. Dadswell admitted Illumina "underestimated the amount of time it would ultimately take to bring these instruments online." Goswami called the bugs "nothing out of the ordinary": fixes were deploying, not all customers were affected, and "we're not seeing any fundamental change to elasticity." The faster 6000 decline was "the flip side of people really being excited about the X," a temporary gap, not a market stall. The two executives' tones plainly differed.

**Dan Brennan (TD Cowen)** asked about the 25B, China and competition. Dadswell said the 25B was on track for the second half and claimed "probably … the biggest and the best proprietary pipeline in the industry." Goswami said China's hoped-for second-half acceleration was not happening because of the economy and stronger mid- and low-throughput rivals. Outside China, "I don't think the situation is dire as it was in last year," though purchase cycles were lengthening and win rates were high and slightly up. On the X, "the issue is not competition at all"; buyers want onboard DRAGEN, speed, simpler workflow and ambient reagents.

**Dan Arias (Stifel)** asked about 2024 margins. Goswami said more than $100 million of run-rate cost was gone and that 25% next year "is going to seem like a stretch, but we remain very committed."

**Vijay Kumar (Evercore ISI)** asked about the second half and X pull-through. Goswami split the cut: about 25% China, and the remaining 75% roughly half consumables transition and half customer conservatism. Q3 would dip as the 6000 decline hits a "local maxima" before X consumables ramp and China slips; Q4 would be "up significantly" on the first real X consumables and year-end spending. Initial X pull-through assumptions still stood.

**Tejas Savant (Morgan Stanley)** asked about long-term growth, executive exits and pricing. Goswami said mid-teens long-term growth "has not changed," driven by more samples, more analyses per sample and higher sequencing intensity; the open question was X ramp pace. Instrument revenue lagged X units because of weaker mid- and low-throughput. In China, Illumina is "being very surgical about when to use price" but saw prices decline; elsewhere prices were on plan, with price used to speed adoption.

**Sung Ji Nam (Scotiabank)** asked who is cautious and whether customers await the 25B. Goswami said caution spans academic and clinical customers, since many clinical labs are unprofitable and managing cash. "I don't think there's a delay in 25B."

**Rachel Vatnsdal (J.P. Morgan)** asked about Chinese stimulus. Goswami said none is in guidance "and there never was"; first-half stimulus went to industrial sectors Illumina does not serve.

**John Sourbeer (UBS)** asked about China's long-term outlook and long reads. Goswami said China remains important, Illumina's brand there is "actually very positive," and go-to-market and partners would be refined. Long-read interest is "very high," but the jump depends on the enrichment assay, coupled with the 25B, arriving late 2023 and picking up in 2024.

## Q3 2023 (call held November 9, 2023)

### New CEO, "a disappointing result"

Jacob Thaysen had been CEO for a little over six weeks (about 40 days), after more than a decade at Agilent running its Diagnostics and Genomics Group and then its largest unit, Life Science and Applied Markets. Thaysen said: "Illumina will remain the key player, even as others enter the market," citing two and a half decades of infrastructure, offerings that "set the global standard," and innovation. Management called the quarter "a disappointing result" [Q3 2023 call].

Consolidated revenue was about $1.12 billion, flat year over year (+1% constant currency) and down 5% from Q2, missing consensus by about $14 million. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.33 beat consensus by $0.19 and was roughly flat year over year. Management had expected the 6000 consumables drop, but Illumina "placed fewer NovaSeq X instruments than we expected in the quarter, as customers' purchase constraints led to lengthened sales cycles" [Goswami].

Net income was $52 million, including GRAIL's $155 million loss; EPS beat internal plans on cost cuts and better gross margin. On a **GAAP** basis (standard accounting), Illumina lost $754 million, or $4.77 per share, including $821 million of **impairments** (write-downs of acquired asset values) on GRAIL, caused by Illumina's lower market value and a higher **discount rate** (the rate used to value future cash flows). The tax rate was 39.7% versus 43.2%, lower mainly on increased amortization of capitalized R&D. Diluted shares were about 158 million.

### Core results

Core revenue was $1.11 billion, flat reported and in constant currency, absorbing about 7 points of drag: about 3.5 from COVID and Russia and about 3.5 from China. COVID revenue was about $4 million versus $28 million. The table compares Core lines with Q3 2022.

| Line | Q3 2023 | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Consumables | $695M | −4% |
| Instruments | $179M | +10% |
| Service and other | $142M | +15% |

### How the story evolved

In April 2023, management said supply was the only limit on the X and promised 25% Core margins for 2024. By August, the launch itself was a cause of weakness: X buyers stopped buying 6000 reagents before ramping the new machine, and onboarding ran long. By November, X shipment guidance fell from more than 390 back to 330–340 and a special committee took over GRAIL. Illumina actually shipped 352, but guided 2024 flat with about 20% margins, five points below the April target. Throughout, management treated China as the one structural competitive problem and called the rest timing and macro, a claim resting mainly on its own control-group data, which analysts such as Michael Ryskin openly questioned.

Across 2024, the message moved consistently: revenue guidance went down step by step while margin and earnings guidance went up. In Q1 management simply held guidance; by Q4 it guided to a return to growth in 2025, operating margin of about 23% and EPS up about 10%. Three threads run through all four calls:

- **The NovaSeq X transition.** Customers moved from the 6000 to the X, which costs them far less per unit of data, so volume rose sharply but revenue grew far more slowly. Management disclosed ever more transition metrics.
- **Weak instrument sales.** Management blamed tight capital budgets; analysts kept probing competition.
- **Cost discipline.** Savings increasingly supported earnings as sales disappointed.

China was weak for most of 2024, stabilized late in the year, then became a fresh risk when a Chinese government action was announced just before the Q4 call.

## Q2 2024 (call held August 6, 2024)

Seeking Alpha's consolidated header showed EPS of $0.36, missing consensus by $0.54, and revenue of $1.11 billion, down 5.44% and beating by $26.63 million, a different basis from management's Core figures.

### Results against expectations

Core revenue was about $1.1 billion, down 6% year over year (reported and constant currency) but up 3% from Q1 and above guidance. Dhingra credited rising high-throughput consumables as customers ramped on the NovaSeq X Plus (the flagship X model), partly offset by fewer mid-throughput instrument shipments than expected.

- **Consumables: $737 million, flat** against Q2 2023, 2023's strongest consumables quarter. High-throughput shipments grew year over year and sequentially, a "particularly bright spot."
- **Instruments: $116 million, up 5% from Q1 but down 40% year over year,** lapping Q2 2023's pre-order launch shipments and on weaker mid-throughput.
- **Services and other: $143 million, up 7%,** on partnerships and service contracts.

Operating cash flow was $243 million and capital spending $30 million, for free cash flow of $213 million; cash ended at about $994 million.

Gigabase output grew more than 40% year over year and about 10% sequentially, healthy in both research and clinical; Dhingra cautioned that gigabases show demand and utilization rather than predicting near-term revenue. By region [Thaysen]: Americas roughly flat, Europe down 5% (as warned in Q1), AMEA down 8%, Greater China down 35%.

### Strategic messages

Thaysen framed the year as "Job one… to stabilize our own base… And job two is accelerating growth," the focus of the August 13 virtual strategy update covering vision, "R&D vault" technology and a multi-year financial outlook. **Organization.** Everett Cunningham joined as Chief Commercial Officer, leading the combined commercial organization. Portfolio optimization would rationalize less profitable products, with transition support for affected customers.

**Products.** A new DRAGEN version added multi-genome mapping, machine learning and genotyping of difficult genes; Thaysen said bioinformatics now drives customer decisions. A cheaper single-flow-cell NovaSeq X, upgradable to the X Plus, would ship in Q4 (the X Plus had shipped since March 2023, after the series was announced in late 2022). 100- and 200-cycle 25B flow cells for single-cell and proteomics, plus a yield-increasing software update, would also come in Q4. The Fluent BioSciences acquisition brings single-cell technology that labels cells for sequencing and, with Partek, integrates workflows; Thaysen said "Illumina will therefore remain an open NGS platform," committed to existing single-cell partners. Dhingra, three months in, said transformation was "taking hold" after an investor listening tour.

## Q3 2024 (call held November 4, 2024)

### Context: the August strategy update

The August 13 update set long-term targets, reaffirmed here: high-single-digit revenue growth by 2027; 500 bp of operating-margin expansion by 2027, which Dhingra measures from a 21% base; EPS growth of double digits to the teens annually over three years; and revenue growth turning from negative in 2024 to positive in 2025.

### Results against expectations

Revenue was $1.08 billion, down 2% (reported and constant currency), in line with expectations [Dhingra]. Seeking Alpha showed revenue down 3.49%; its revenue-beat figure appears garbled and is not used.

- **Consumables: $741 million, up 7%,** on X uptake.
- **Instruments: $104 million, down 42%,** "slightly behind" expectations because of the launch-quarter comparison and weak mid-throughput.
- **Services and other: $150 million, up 6%.**

Gross margin was 70.5%, up 450 bp, mainly on productivity gains Dhingra called "sustainable," with consumables mix roughly half. Operating expenses were $517 million, roughly flat with Q2 despite absorbing Fluent. Operating margin was 22.6%, versus 22.5% a year earlier and about 20% guided. Other expense was $14 million; the 21% tax rate was helped by one-time credits on filing the prior-year return. Net income was $181 million and EPS $1.14 on about 160 million shares, a $0.26 beat per Seeking Alpha. By region [Thaysen]: Americas down 6%, Europe up 12%, EMEA up 7%, Greater China down 23% (the transcript again lists Europe and EMEA separately).

**Transition.** Dhingra said the transition ran faster than forecast: more than 55% of high-throughput gigabases and more than 35% of consumables revenue were on the X; about 40% of high-throughput clinical gigabases were on the X, with clinical 25B use rising; total gigabases grew more than 40%; and half of consumables revenue could still be on the X by mid-2025. ### Results against expectations

Revenue was $1.1 billion, up about 1% in constant currency, the first year-over-year growth in this series, beating company expectations on higher X shipments. Seeking Alpha instead showed EPS of $0.86, a $0.06 miss, and revenue down 1.60%, beating by $554.68K; the revenue gap is probably reported versus constant currency, the EPS gap unexplained.

- **Consumables: $698 million, up about 2%,** mainly high throughput, with mid-throughput growing against an easy comparison.
- **Instruments: $155 million, down 3%,** much better than earlier quarters as MiSeq i100 sales partly offset weak mid-throughput.
- **Services and other: $151 million, down 1%,** on prior-year partnership timing; service contracts, CDx revenue and informatics grew.

Gross margin was 67.4%, up 270 bp despite a seasonally heavy instrument mix. Operating expenses were $526 million and operating margin 19.7%, up 120 bp.
