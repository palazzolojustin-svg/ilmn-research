# Sell-Side and Independent Research on Illumina

*Core Notes section 08. Synthesized from 31 source extracts as of 2026-10-10. Bracketed tags cite the source; full detail is in Working Notes (see INDEX.md).*

## Key takeaways

This section tracks how professional analysts viewed Illumina from November 2025 to September 2026. A rating is a Buy, Hold or Sell call. A price target is the share price the analyst expects in about 12 months. The sources are five sell-side firms (brokers and banks that publish ratings for investors): J.P. Morgan (JPM), UBS, Barclays, Ameriprise and Freedom Broker. Two independent sources that do not sell Illumina's shares round out the picture: Bloomberg Intelligence (BI) and a Seeking Alpha contributor.

The main story is a stock the Street underestimated. Illumina's results kept beating forecasts, the share price roughly doubled, and most analysts raised their targets to keep up. The central debate never went away: can growth in clinical testing outrun price pressure from new competitors, above all Roche?

**The starting point: margins fixed, growth in doubt.** By early 2026 every source agreed that management's "self-help" had restored profitability. Self-help means cost cuts and other actions within the company's control, as opposed to waiting for demand to recover. Adjusted operating margin (operating profit as a share of revenue, excluding one-off items) reached 23.7% in the fourth quarter of 2025 (4Q25). That was up 400 basis points from a year earlier and above the Street's 22.5% [JPM, 2026-02-06]. A basis point (bp) is one-hundredth of a percentage point. BI said growing earnings on flat revenue "creates a higher floor for the stock" [BI, 2026-01-13].

Growth was the open question. Illumina guided to organic growth (which strips out acquisitions and currency) of only 1–3% for 2026, which UBS said "compares unfavorably" with peers [UBS, 2026-02-05]. The bright spot was clinical consumables, the reagent kits that hospital and diagnostic labs must buy for every sequencing run. They grew 20% a year outside China in 4Q25. Research and academic customers stayed weak, squeezed by US funding cuts and by price cuts that came with the move to the new NovaSeq X sequencer. China, restricted by Beijing's "unreliable entity" list, had shrunk to about half its former size [BI, 2025-10-31].

In February 2026 the analysts split three ways. Ameriprise and Freedom Broker rated the stock Buy, JPM and UBS Neutral, and Barclays Underweight (meaning it expected the stock to trail its peers). JPM cut its target to $120, citing "competitive concerns and pressured pricing dynamics." Barclays held at $110 and warned that a guide relying on second-half growth was arriving just as competitors launched.

**A competitive shock milder than feared.** On 19 February Element Biosciences unveiled its VITARI sequencer. UBS said its consumables cost roughly one-tenth of a NovaSeq X Plus run, for about one-fifth of the output [UBS, 2026-02-19]. On 26 February Roche priced its Axelios 1 at $150 per genome, roughly 25% below the ~$200 JPM estimated for NovaSeq X [JPM, 2026-02-26].

The sources read the Roche news differently, and that split ran through the whole period:

- **UBS** called it "relatively benign." Large Illumina customers already pay similar prices, and Illumina's own roadmap implies about $150 per genome.
- **UBS's experts** pointed to real obstacles to switching: complicated sample preparation, immature software, and clinical doubts about reusing the sensor module 20 times.
- **UBS's estimate of exposure** singled out "counting" applications such as RNA sequencing, at roughly 16% of Illumina's consumables sales [UBS, 2026-02-26].
- **Barclays** saw a "well capitalized competitor" that could force "another round of pricing headwinds in 2027 and beyond" [Barclays, 2026-02-05].

The stock fell about 10% in the two weeks before Roche's pricing, to $120.09 on 18 February, then recovered once the price matched expectations.

The biggest surprise was instrument demand. Most placements went to clinical labs, and many added capacity rather than replacing older machines. Freedom said demand was moving "from stabilization toward growth" [Freedom, 2026-05-01].

JPM made the key call. It upgraded to Overweight on 10 June with a $185 target, arguing that clinical customers are "stickier-than-appreciated." In its survey of 62 customers, many had not heard of Axelios, few planned to buy one within a year, and only about 11% of potential buyers would retire an Illumina instrument [JPM, 2026-06-10]. JPM raised its target to $220 in July and $230 after Q2.

The share price did most of the work in moving the rest of the Street. It went from $126.74 at the end of April to $205.09 on 30 July and about $278 by early October. The FactSet consensus target rose from $136.11 on 1 May to $175.72 on 31 July, yet still sat about 17% below the share price [Ameriprise]. Bloomberg's ratings count shifted from 10 Buy, 11 Hold and 3 Sell in February to 13 Buy, 6 Hold and 3 Sell by late July.

**Where the sources agree.** Nearly every source now accepts four points:

- **Clinical sequencing is a durable growth engine.** Clinical work's share of consumables rose 10 points in two years to about 65%, which "adds ballast" to revenue [BI, 2026-07-31].
- **The NovaSeq X transition is close to complete.** By Q2 it carried about 92% of research volume and 78% of clinical volume, which should end the price erosion the transition caused.
- **Management is executing well.** Even Barclays conceded management is "doing a great job" [Barclays, 2026-07-31].
- **Roche's threat is gradual, not immediate.** Roche "could still chip away where workflow or economic advantages are clear," but disruption "is unlikely to be immediate" [BI, 2026-06-26].

**Where they still disagree.** By midsummer the debate had shifted from the business to the price: few doubted the operating momentum, but sources differed on how much the stock already reflected.

- **The bulls (JPM, Ameriprise).** JPM calls high-single-digit revenue growth and mid-to-high-teens EPS growth "highly achievable" [JPM, 2026-07-31]. Ameriprise kept its Buy but said the case now "increasingly depends on" new instruments converting into consumables sales, which typically takes six to nine months.
- **The valuation skeptics (Freedom, Seeking Alpha).** Freedom downgraded to Hold at $185 on 5 August, saying "much of the upside is now priced in" and guidance was no longer conservative. Stephen Ayers of Seeking Alpha set a fair value of $188, arguing that most of the Q2 margin gain came from government incentives and absent restructuring charges, not operations [Seeking Alpha, 2026-09-08].
- **The bear (Barclays).** Barclays raised its target three times, from $110 to $160, but kept Underweight. It sees a mid-single-digit grower that will need "continued price concessions, bundling, and favorable terms." It "cannot justify" a price-to-earnings multiple of about 35x, the highest in its life-science tools coverage.

**Warning signs worth tracking.** These are mostly opinions rather than settled facts:

- **Clinical growth slowed.** Clinical consumables growth outside China eased to 15% in Q2 from 20%.
- **Cash flow missed.** Free cash flow (cash left after capital spending) was $162 million in Q2, against a Street estimate of $321 million. Management blamed tax-payment timing and inventory built up for scarce components.
- **Price per unit of data may be falling.** Barclays suspected quiet discounting, because NovaSeq X's share of data output rose faster than its share of consumables revenue. BI saw Illumina "in the early stages of trading price for volume" [BI, 2026-05-01].
- **Placements may have been pulled forward** ahead of Axelios, Ayers suggested. Management insisted "this is a very healthy market" [Q2 2026 call].
- **New-product growth is unverified.** Barclays and Ayers both doubted management's claim that multiomics will add 1–2 points of growth in 2027. Multiomics means measuring DNA, RNA and proteins together, and here includes SomaLogic and the BioInsight data unit.

**Data quality caveats.** BI's figure of a 20% fall in Q1 service revenue conflicts with every other source. Barclays' July note calls Axelios an Element product, although it is Roche's. Firms disagree on the 2025 operating margin (23.1% from BI and UBS, 23.6% from Barclays). JPM's survey materials carry conflicting dates. Coverage ends in early September 2026, before the stock's further climb to about $278, so every target cited here except JPM's now sits well below the price.

The share price moved more than the ratings did. It was $145.55 on 12 January 2026, just before the preannouncement [JPM, 2026-01-13], and closed at $133.61 on 5 February, the night of the 4Q25 report, for a market value of about $20.4 billion [Ameriprise; JPM; UBS; Barclays].

## Background: Bloomberg Intelligence's standing view of the business

BI's business profile and M&A note, first written on 13 November 2025 and refreshed on 11 March 2026, frame the long-term debate every later note builds on.

### Position, customers and products

BI calls Illumina's reach in genomic tools (the instruments and consumables used to read DNA) "unparalleled for more than a decade" [BI, 2025-11-13]. Illumina sells to research and clinical customers worldwide with a broadening lineup meant to offer "a solution for every setting." BI says competition is rising from venture-backed Element Biosciences and Ultima Genomics, and that Roche's return to sequencing is "on the horizon."

The customer base is diverse by geography and application, with sequencing spreading into oncology (cancer), agriculture and reproductive health. Most sales are in the US, but overseas demand is growing as more countries fund genomics. Government funding makes up almost half of sales. BI's example of the risk is 2012, when US budget sequestration (automatic across-the-board spending cuts) and a federal shutdown temporarily capped growth. During Covid-19, lab reopenings were expected to set the pace of recovery; instead, 2021 brought record instrument demand.

In March 2026 BI added that growth is shifting toward clinical markets and that consumables now make up 60% of sequencing revenue, driven by NovaSeq X volume and oncology. It gave no prior-year comparison [BI, 2026-03-11].

BI describes the product ladder as follows:

- **NovaSeq X.** The flagship, launched at the 2022 Genomics Forum at Illumina's lowest cost per gigabase (one billion DNA letters read). It has 2.5x the throughput of earlier systems, and BI calls it "a critical catalyst."
- **iSeq and MiSeq.** Small, competitively priced machines for smaller labs.
- **NextSeq.** The mid-throughput line, whose demand drives the push into clinical work.
- **HiSeq X Ten.** Launched in 2014, it broke the $1,000-per-genome barrier.
- **XLEAP-SBS.** An upgrade to SBS (sequencing by synthesis, Illumina's core chemistry) that BI projects, not the company, will push costs below $200 per genome.

### Market opportunity

BI relays Illumina's view that its addressable markets exceed $125 billion across two categories. Illumina meaningfully penetrates only research, with an estimated 34% captured by 2027; that figure comes from the 2022 investor day, so it is dated [BI, 2025-11-13].

Clinical penetration depends on more biomarker profiling (testing patients for specific molecular markers) and on physicians using sequencing-based tests. BI cites liquid biopsy (blood tests that detect cancer from circulating DNA) as an $800 million market where Illumina "hoped to compete via Grail," against Guardant Health, Tempus AI and Exact Sciences. Ameriprise later cited the same $125 billion for next-generation sequencing (NGS), noting only about $10 billion is served today [Ameriprise, 2026-02-06].

### M&A history

BI argues that Illumina's dominance normally rules out large acquisitions, so it buys early-stage technologies that extend its sequencing workflow [BI, 2025-11-13]. BI's view of each deal:

- **Solexa.** The $480 million purchase "may go down as one of the best health-care deals in history." Illumina's market value rose from $1.8 billion at announcement to a peak of $76 billion in 3Q21, almost all of it from sequencing.
- **Helix.** A consumer-genomics venture announced on 18 August 2015 with $100 million from two venture investors, promising individuals "an unprecedented amount of genetic information." After years of weighing on profits it was deconsolidated (removed from Illumina's reported results) in 2019, which BI called "a prudent step."
- **Context.** The research market is maturing, and each instrument generation brings smaller performance gains.
- **PacBio.** The terminated deal would have added long-read technology (reading much longer DNA stretches). Questions about cash use mounted until the deal failed in 2020; the cash then went to Grail [BI, 2026-03-11].
- **Grail.** Formed in 2016 to commercialize a blood-based cancer screen, Grail was acquired in August 2021 without EU approval. After an arduous dispute Illumina was forced to divest, and the spin-off took place on 24 June 2024.

The Grail aftermath, per BI, was a $476 million fine, $35–50 million of incremental spin-off charges, a retained 14.5% stake and a re-established royalty agreement. Illumina "must now devise a new strategy" for cancer screening, and the divestiture plus a new CEO help refocus it on its core.

### Capital allocation

The Grail deal paused buybacks, which resumed modestly in the second half of 2024 [BI, 2025-11-11]. Buybacks historically just offset employee-option dilution: none in 2022 or 2023, and $115 million in 2024. Year-end 2024 cash was $1.2 billion and 2024 free cash flow $695 million. The $1.5 billion repurchase authorization had $684 million left at 3Q25 and $643 million at 4Q25. Year-end 2025 cash reached $1.6 billion on free cash flow of $931 million, but BI says regaining pre-Grail flexibility will take time [BI, 2026-03-11].

### Peers

BI says Illumina's offerings "tower over peers" such as Thermo Fisher. Its operating margins matched peers until 2023, when Grail, the delayed NovaSeq X ramp and the Icahn proxy fight hurt performance. BI's chart titles summarize its view: "Margin Setback a 2023 Hiccup, Ambitious Goals Ahead" and "Amid Choppy Water Post-Pandemic, Stability on the Horizon" [BI, 2026-03-11].

## 3Q25 results and BI's post-quarter outlook (late October to November 2025)

### The quarter

BI records 3Q25 as a beat. Revenue was $1.08 billion, 2% above consensus (the average analyst forecast), and EPS was $1.34, 17 cents above consensus. Core sales (Illumina excluding Grail) were flat. Sequencing instruments and consumables each grew 3%, while sequencing service fell 2%. Core operating margin was 24.5%, up 190 bp year on year. Illumina raised 2025 EPS guidance to $4.65–4.75 from $4.45–4.55.

### BI's thesis at that point

BI said NovaSeq X and its updated chemistry should help Illumina hold its position, while Roche's return "adds a wrinkle" [BI, 2025-10-31]. It called the extended NovaSeq X transition "financially painful," because the machine ramped while demand weakened and competition in mid-throughput (mid-sized) sequencers accelerated. Management's early 2026 view pointed to constrained research customers, with tariffs starting to ease. China sanctions had halved that market's contribution, with no clear timing for a resolution. Illumina was gaining "a small degree of momentum," with a stable revenue outlook and cost cuts delivering margins ahead of expectations. Still, the "specter of Roche's launch in 2026" made a sharp recovery unlikely, though "some green shoots are emerging."

BI judged that sequencing consumables had reached an inflection: NovaSeq X volume growth now offset the price cuts customers get when moving to the new machine. That improved visibility on Illumina's 26% operating-margin target for 2027, especially if sales growth could approach the high-single-digit goal (excluding China). BI expected 2026 end markets to resemble the second half of 2025, with coming multiomics launches as an extra growth vector.

### BI's record of 2025 guidance

At this stage BI cited 2025 guidance of a 0.5–1.5% decline in core revenue. Instruments were guided down 4–6% and consumables up 2.5–3% excluding China (printed as "2.53%," apparently a typo). Consensus was more cautious, at +1.5% for consumables and −11% for instruments. Core operating margin was guided to 22.75–23%, up 145–170 bp from 21.3% in 2024.

BI explained that the hoped-for 2025 turnaround "quickly dimmed." China's unreliable-entity designation (a government listing that restricts a foreign company) and tightening US research budgets forced the guidance cut, and only half of the planned mitigation (pricing, supply-chain moves and cost cuts) was expected to land in 2025.

BI's key-points box adds that 2024 core revenue fell 3% and 2024 core EPS was $4.16. The 2025 gross tariff cost was estimated at $85 million, and 2025 China revenue at $220 million. The box also shows a 2025 revenue decline of "0.5–2.1%," conflicting with the 0.5–1.5% in BI's own prose, and an EPS range of $4.45–4.55 that predates the 3Q raise. For history, core sales grew 40% in 2021 and 1% in 2022, then fell in 2023 and 2024; consensus expected a modest rebound in 2026.

## 4Q25 results and the first 2026 guidance (5–10 February 2026)

### What Illumina reported

Illumina reported full 4Q25 results on 5 February 2026.

**Headline numbers.** Revenue was $1,159 million, up 5.0% reported from $1,104 million a year earlier, 4% in constant currency and 7% excluding China, against consensus of about $1.10 billion [Ameriprise, 2026-02-06; JPM, 2026-02-06]. Barclays shows $1,155 million and a 284 bp beat on cc growth (4.0% versus 1.1% expected) [Barclays, 2026-02-05]. Adjusted EPS was $1.35, above the preannounced $1.27–1.30, the Street's $1.21 (Barclays $1.22), the guidance midpoint of $1.20 and, per Ameriprise, internal guidance of $1.29. It was up 42.1% from core EPS of $0.95 a year earlier [Ameriprise; JPM].

**Margins and costs.** Adjusted operating margin was 23.7%, up 400 bp, against the Street's 22.5% and an implied guide of about 22.8% (also Barclays' estimate) [JPM; Barclays]. Adjusted gross margin fell 40 bp to 67.0%, missing the Street's 68.1% (68.2% in another JPM passage) and Barclays' 68.9%. Tariffs cost 205 bp; excluding them, gross margin rose 165 bp from 3Q. Core operating expenses fell $24 million to $502 million, which JPM credited to multiyear cost programs. The tax rate was 19.5%.

**Instrument placements.** Illumina placed more than 100 NovaSeq X systems, above the ">95" preannounced and its second-highest quarter since launch. The installed base reached about 890 units, and over 60% of placements went to clinical customers [JPM; Ameriprise].

## Competitive shocks before and at AGBT (February 2026)

### Element's VITARI (19 February)

UBS's "Fast Take," "Competitive Announcement Keeps the Pressure on Illumina," compared Element's VITARI with the NovaSeq X Plus [UBS, 2026-02-19]. The extracted text is partly garbled, but the readable comparisons are: an instrument about 40% cheaper than the X Plus's $689,000 list price; consumables about one-tenth as costly per run (roughly $3,000 versus $32,000); about half the cost per gigabase; about one-fifth the output per run (3T versus 16T, apparently terabases); and shipping apparently planned for the second half of 2026. ### Roche prices Axelios 1 (26 February)

At its AGBT workshop, Roche set a $150-per-genome list price for consumables on Axelios 1, which uses its SBX (sequencing by expansion) technology. The price includes synthesis reagents but excludes library prep (the step that prepares DNA samples) and assumes the sensor module, Roche's equivalent of a flow cell, is reused 20 times. Against about $200 for NovaSeq X, that is roughly 25% lower [JPM, 2026-02-26]. Most investors had expected about $150, some as low as $100.

Roche's other figures:
- Duplex mode, which reads both DNA strands for accuracy, runs 16 genomes per run and 64 per day, about $2,400 per run.
- Simplex mode produces more than 40 billion reads per run and about 160 billion per day, at about $0.06 per million reads.
- A ~1.1 Mb cell-free DNA (cfDNA) panel at 2,000x coverage and 96 samples per run would cost $25 per sample.
- Reuse data showed stable performance over more than 20 uses within a week; 15 of 16 modules reached 20 runs, and the product is designed for up to 20 uses.

JPM kept Neutral and $120, again citing "competitive concerns and pressured pricing dynamics."

UBS's "First Read" the same day called the news "relatively benign" and said the "competitive overhang could soften" [UBS, 2026-02-26]. Large Illumina customers likely already pay similar or better prices, and Illumina's roadmap, announced earlier that week, implies about $150 per genome on NovaSeq X Plus if the coming 35B flow cell is priced like the current 25B one (the labels refer to billions of reads). UBS saw more vulnerability in "counting" applications such as RNAseq (measuring gene activity), priced per million reads, where Roche's $0.06 compares with Illumina's $0.68 list on the 25B flow cell (decimals reconstructed from garbled text). UBS roughly estimates counting at about 40% of Illumina's research usage, or about 16% of total sequencing consumables sales.

Early researcher checks were "skeptical" anyone would switch. Researchers saw speed as Axelios' main advantage, which matters little in research. They questioned whether simplex quality suffices for the cell barcodes and UMIs (molecular tags used to count molecules) in 10x Genomics single-cell RNAseq, and saw 20x reuse as a possible clinical issue. UBS kept $135, implying 10.8% upside at $121.87.

### Expert feedback (26 February)

A second UBS "First Read" that evening reported on four experts plus a "Doc Debates" call: one very negative, one very positive and two indifferent, which UBS judged a fair picture of the research community. It repeated that the update "should soften the competitive overhang" [UBS, 2026-02-26].

On the positive side, yields of 40 billion reads per run and 16 genomes per chip (up from a previously disclosed 7) beat expectations. Reuse data showed no carryover between runs, and chips can probably be reused "well more than 20 times," with a life of "months, not weeks." And $150 per genome looks cheap "by a wide margin" against Illumina's per-lane pricing, especially per million reads.

On the negative side, library prep is "very complicated": duplex needs two adapters and two preps, and Roche will not share adapter sequences. Software will require going "through hoops… like in the early days of Illumina." One expert already pays $175 per genome after Illumina discounts, expects the 35B flow cell to reach $150, believes large users pay $125, and had expected Roche at $100. Half of that expert's clinical users accept reuse if validated, but the pathologists call it "a nonstarter," especially for MRD. The summer shipment target "seems like a stretch" because the platform is "nowhere near ready"; the expert estimated six to eight more months.

UBS planned another call on 6 March 2026. At $127.40, its $135 target implied 6.0% upside and an excess return of −2.5%.

## The bull and bear debate as of April 2026

**Bull case** (Ameriprise, Freedom and, in part, BI and UBS). Clinical sequencing is a durable engine, with 20% growth exiting 2025 and a double-digit to mid-teens guide. The NovaSeq X transition is nearly done, which should end research price erosion in 2H26. Cost cuts have restored margins above 23%, with a credible path to 26% in 2027. Net cash is building. Multiomics, SomaLogic and BioInsight add revenue from 2027, and the valuation is far below history.

**Bear case** (Barclays, with JPM's concerns leaning the same way). Organic growth of 1–3% trails peers. The guide relies on a second-half ramp just as Element (2H26) and Roche launch. Research funding remains fragile and China is half its former size. Roche is a well-capitalized rival able to push another round of price cuts in 2027 and beyond, and counting applications (about 16% of consumables) look exposed. At 24–26x 2026 earnings, little upside is left.

**Middle ground.** UBS's channel checks and expert calls suggest Roche's pricing roughly matches what large Illumina customers already pay, and point to workflow, software and clinical-reuse hurdles that could slow switching.

## The Roche debate and J.P. Morgan's upgrade (May–July 2026)

### J.P. Morgan upgrades to Overweight, $185 (10 June)

JPM said Illumina had been "one of the most highly debated stocks in our space" since Roche unveiled SBX, and upgraded on the view that clinical customers are "stickier-than-appreciated" [JPM, 2026-06-10]. The thesis had five parts:

1. **Clinical labs are locked in.** The large US clinical labs certified under CLIA rules have all moved, or are moving, to NovaSeq X. Clinical work drives more than 40% of company revenue and more than 65% of sequencing consumables. Transition pricing headwinds are "largely gone exiting this year," and Gb volume consistently grows at least 30%. Diagnostics executives voiced "general intrigue" about Roche but no urgency to switch, and as reimbursement improves the payoff from switching shrinks, especially for FDA-approved assays.
2. **A long volume runway.** Demand comes from therapy selection (choosing drugs from a tumor's genetics), MRD tests and multi-cancer early detection (MCED, blood tests that screen for several cancers at once). "Sequencing intensity" is also rising: whole-genome sequencing uses about 15x the data of whole-exome sequencing, and single-cell and spatial experiments need 3–15x and 10–20x more data than a whole genome. JPM expects clinical consumables to grow low-to-mid teens annually, with diagnostics test volumes growing from high teens to above 20%.
3. **Axelios looks manageable near term.** Experts flagged possible contamination and carryover from reusable flow cells, higher input-DNA needs and a fragmented analysis software stack. "Pricing alone does not seem to be enough," JPM wrote, while conceding that Illumina's standing "is under more pressure now than in the past."
4. **Upside from research and multiomics.** US academic and government funding could recover in H2 2026 and 2027. Research is about 40% of revenue, including about 25% from US academic and government customers. Multiomics (Fluent, SomaLogic and StrataMap) and BioInsight should add 100–200 bp of growth by 2027.
5. **Valuation.** JPM expects mid-to-high-teens EPS growth and a free-cash-flow yield of about 4–5%. Illumina trades at a 4.1x EV/EBITDA premium to the "Core Tools" group, against a historical premium of about 15x.

**Model.** JPM forecasts HSD organic growth from 2027, resting on ex-China clinical consumables growing at a low-to-mid-teens compound annual growth rate (CAGR) through 2030 and China declining by mid-teens. Gross margin stays in the high 60s because reagent rentals weigh on prices. Revenue is $4,583M in 2026, $4,919M in 2027 and $5,401M in 2028, with EPS of $5.23, $5.98 and $7.01. The DCF uses a 9.1% WACC and 4.5% terminal growth, with terminal value 86% of the total.

**Customer survey.** JPM surveyed 62 customers (30 academic, 32 clinical; about 65% in North America). Customers planned to spend more in 2027 and 2028 than Illumina's long-term growth target implies:

| Group | Instruments 2027 | Instruments 2028 | Consumables 2027 | Consumables 2028 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical | +22.2% | +31.0% | +9.4% | +14.0% |
| Academic | +18.6% | +19.0% | +9.0% | +12.8% |

Other findings:
- **Pricing:** planned volume grew 2.4–5.2 points faster than planned spend, implying expected price cuts far smaller than recent double-digit declines.
- **Illumina's share:** academic labs expect it to rise 1–3 points; mid-sized clinical labs expect it to rise from 50% to 57%.
- **Awareness of Axelios:** 40% were unfamiliar with it. On a 1–10 purchase-likelihood scale, academic labs averaged 6.2 and clinical labs 4.7, with 3 and 4 the most common answers.
- **Timing:** only 8% would buy within 6–12 months; the largest group, 35% and mostly academic, said 12–24 months.
- **Effect on Illumina:** 65% of potential buyers would reduce Illumina use, but only about 11% would retire an instrument.
- **Reagent rental:** 37–39% said an Illumina reagent-rental offer would make them less likely to buy Axelios.
- **Decision factors:** data quality led both reasons to consider Axelios and reasons not to.
- **Recent purchases:** only about 5% bought an Illumina instrument in 2025 or 2026 to date, which JPM linked to tight academic budgets.
- **Lock-in:** one core-facility user said switching "will take Tremendous amount of service, labor, and time."

The survey has caveats. The sample is small, and only 37 respondents knew Axelios. Some slide counts and the NovaSeq 6000 share (16% versus 56%) are inconsistent. The survey deck lists Illumina as "Neutral" at $160.03 on the upgrade date, and JPM's later Q2 note refers to the "May upgrade" and "May survey," conflicting with the 10 June date.

## Seeking Alpha (8 September): Hold, DCF fair value $188

Stephen Ayers argued Illumina is "priced for perfection it hasn't earned yet." The $188 DCF value is 13.8% below the $218.22 price and uses a 9.74% WACC, about 11% revenue growth and free-cash-flow margins rising toward 27–30%. Ayers rated the stock Buy in August 2024 (it has since risen 64%) and moved to Hold in March 2025 over China and Roche concerns.

Ayers' main arguments:
- **True growth is lower:** organic growth is about 4–6.5%.
- **The margin gain is mostly non-operating:** of Q2's $31M rise in operating income, $18M came from government incentives ($22M versus $4M) and $8M from absent restructuring charges, leaving about $5M of real improvement on about $100M of extra revenue.
- **H2 looks steep:** Q4 margin must exceed 25% to hit full-year guidance, and H2 free cash flow must reach about $527M for the roughly $940M implied for the year. Refunds of IEEPA tariffs, which the Supreme Court struck down in February 2026, may help.
- **Buybacks nearly stopped:** purchases fell to 32K shares by June, with none under the April authorization, probably partly to manage cash ahead of a $500M note maturing on 9 September. Illumina issued $300M of 2029 notes in August.
- **Placements may be a pull-forward** ahead of Axelios, though management insisted on the Q2 call that "this is a very healthy market."

Ayers called the "1 to 2 points" of new-product growth a "major unsolved mystery."
