## Why 10x Genomics matters for Illumina

10x Genomics (ticker TXG) makes instruments and reagent kits for two fast-growing areas of biology research. The first is **single-cell analysis** (measuring gene activity cell by cell instead of averaging across ground-up tissue), sold as **Chromium**. The second is **spatial biology** (measuring gene activity while recording where in a tissue each molecule sits), sold as **Xenium** and **Visium** and, from late 2026, a new platform called **Atera**.

10x matters to Illumina in three ways. Neither source summarized here, the Q1 2026 call and the BofA conference, contained any Illumina-specific commentary. The links below are therefore partly background context rather than sourced claims.

### Single cell is a sequencing customer

A Chromium experiment prepares samples that must then be read on a DNA sequencer. Strong single-cell volumes therefore pull through sequencing demand, and sequencing cost limits 10x's largest projects.

CEO Serge Saxonov credited Illumina's competitors, not Illumina, for lowering that cost. He said that with **Ultima** and **Roche**, "the price per read has dropped like hugely," which is "definitely a huge enabler," making mega-scale single-cell projects "absolutely possible." He called it "a massive change in the ecosystem, which is something we absolutely love" [BofA, 2026-05-13]. When BofA analyst Michael Ryskin asked whether whole-genome pricing of $80 to $100 per genome was still a problem, Saxonov said no. Single-cell work can use simplex reads (a simpler format than whole-genome sequencing needs), so its effective cost is "even cheaper than the whole genome price would indicate" [BofA, 2026-05-13].

For Illumina, 10x's growth helps sequencing demand in general, but 10x's CEO publicly credits lower-cost rivals as the enabler.

### Spatial is shifting toward imaging

Spatial products come in two kinds. **Sequencing-based** products such as Visium feed a sequencer. **Imaging-based** products such as Xenium and Atera read molecules directly in tissue under a microscope-like instrument and need no sequencer.

Saxonov said the market is moving toward imaging, "especially Xenium," that Atera should reinforce this, and that "spatial will increasingly become the default approach for analyzing biological samples" [Q1 2026 call]. If so, a growing share of tissue-level gene measurement could bypass sequencers. Atera's roadmap also includes in situ sequencing (reading DNA "base-by-base" inside the tissue to find mutations in place), territory that today requires a separate sequencer.

### Same customers, same budgets

10x sells to the same academic and biopharma labs as Illumina. Its commentary on research funding and instrument budgets is therefore a read-across to Illumina's own instrument demand (see the next section).

## Funding and capital-equipment backdrop

CFO Adam Taich recalled that on the Q1 2025 call, 10x withdrew full-year guidance after "drastic changes to government research funding." Since then, he said, 10x has "consistently executed, improved our operating profile and have grown our cash balance by over $100 million. The business is in a meaningfully stronger position today" [Q1 2026 call]. Saxonov said single-cell volumes grew double digits "despite the fact that the end markets have been just dropping out from under us, especially going back to the first half of last year with the academic kind of disruption" [BofA, 2026-05-13].

Both executives described capex (capital expenditure, meaning spending on instruments rather than consumables) as constrained but stable. Taich said the backdrop is "not meaningfully different," "but I'd be remiss if I didn't say it's still fairly constrained," and Saxonov called it "a challenging capital equipment environment" [Q1 2026 call]. Taich added that "customers are finding CapEx. We've got orders," and, in a partly garbled passage, roughly that "this is the type of launch where folks find money" [Q1 2026 call]. He noted that when guidance was reinstated in February 2026, the capex market had not "tremendously recovered" [BofA, 2026-05-13].

10x timed the Atera launch to customer budget cycles, including fall fiscal year-ends and grant cycles, so customers could earmark Atera funds in 2027 budgets. Existing products keep running on established grants. For 2027, Saxonov said "at the very least, we will have good compares" (easy prior-year comparisons), and if conditions improve "we should see an acceleration to all the other trends" [Q1 2026 call].

Saxonov also argued that the roughly ten-year era when "every single lab" bought its own instrument is not returning, which is why Atera is a "somewhat more centralized system" [BofA, 2026-05-13]. That view bears on Illumina's own instrument-placement strategy.

## AI and mega-projects as a demand driver

Management's central growth argument is that AI models of biology need vastly more of the data 10x produces. Saxonov called AI "a significant and structural tailwind," citing "increasing evidence that scaling laws apply in biology just like they do in other domains" (bigger models trained on more data keep improving). He said "many orders of magnitude more data" are needed and "as AI models improve, we expect an exponential increase in the demand for the kind of data our technologies produce" [Q1 2026 call].

William Blair's Matthew Larew noted AI had been a relatively low share of revenue the prior quarter and asked how large the AI TAM (total addressable market) could be. Saxonov gave no number. He said AI is "really pervasive," that no large project lacks AI as a big driver, "if not the biggest," that even small experiments are designed to feed future models, and that AI "is really lifting all of it," in many ways "by design" [Q1 2026 call]. At BofA he said it is "very much early days": large projects are running and protein-protein-interaction (PPI) models are being published, but each model concludes the next step needs 10x more data. "Everything that we're seeing is in the sort of early stage of that exponential," reflecting AI's "insatiable hunger for data" [BofA, 2026-05-13].

Management named these partnerships from the past year [Q1 2026 call]:
- **Chan Zuckerberg Initiative:** the Billion Cells Project.
- **Arc Institute:** the Virtual Cell Challenge.
- **Xaira Therapeutics:** the largest perturbation dataset ever reported (data on how cells respond when genes are switched off or altered).
- **Bioptimus (April 2026):** an AI biotech running STELA, which aims to profile up to 100,000 patient tissue specimens across three continents as the data backbone for a "world model of biology," starting on Xenium and moving to Atera.
- **Chan Zuckerberg Biohub (week before May 7, 2026):** a $500 million Virtual Biology Initiative to build AI models that simulate cells and tissues in silico (by computer), which Saxonov compared to the Human Genome Project.

Ryskin also raised efforts such as a Million Cell Atlas initiative [BofA, 2026-05-13].

## Atera: the new spatial platform

### What it is and why management calls it transformative

10x launched Atera in April 2026 at AACR, a major cancer research conference, about three weeks before the May 13 BofA event. Saxonov called it "the most significant product introduction in our history" [Q1 2026 call] and "the biggest launch in our company's history," saying it is "reshaping the industry and really opening up Spatial in a huge way" [BofA, 2026-05-13]. He said it is "poised to become the large-scale discovery engine, whether for basic research, AI-driven science, or translational applications" [Q1 2026 call].

His design argument is that biology tools have been fragmented across tissues, cells and molecules, and spatial biology unifies them: "measuring molecules, cells, and tissues together, preserving a full context at massive scale and incredibly high resolution." Researchers previously traded off throughput (samples run), specificity (measuring the right molecule), sensitivity (catching faint signals), scale and performance; Atera was built to remove those trade-offs [Q1 2026 call]. Visium and Xenium face "fundamental throttles," and Atera addresses "every single one of them." "No one really quite expected what we were going to announce" [BofA, 2026-05-13].

The program draws on technology from "almost the very beginning of the company" and formally began around Xenium's launch. Saxonov said the needed technology did not exist about five years earlier, so 10x invented across several fields, guided by years of customer feedback [BofA, 2026-05-13].

### Capabilities

**Whole transcriptome with single-cell-level sensitivity.** Atera measures essentially all expressed genes, whereas Xenium uses targeted panels (pre-selected gene lists). Choosing a panel can be "quite a project" and limits unbiased discovery; with whole transcriptome, "you don't have to choose" [BofA]. Sensitivity matches **Flex Apex**, "the gold standard for single cell analysis" [Q1 2026 call]. Raising plex (genes measured at once) used to cost throughput and sensitivity; Atera delivers all three, "something that no one had thought possible before," and "we actually increased sensitivity in a massive way" [BofA]. This answers skeptics who compared spatial data with FISH (an established gene-labeling method) and missed expected genes: "You see these cells with just massive numbers of transcripts lighting up and it resonates with the biology they expect" [BofA].

**Specificity.** Refined probe chemistry gives trustworthy data, which Saxonov called non-negotiable [Q1 2026 call].

**Throughput.** One instrument can process up to 800 whole-transcriptome 1 cm² samples a year, or more than 3,000 with targeted **Atera Select** panels, plus a "billion-cell-per-year capability" [Q1 2026 call]. That is a "massive increase" over Xenium by sample count or tissue area [BofA]. When Jefferies asked about roughly 3,000 core biopsies a year for drug trials, Saxonov said the counts refer to 1 cm² areas and more samples fit per slide (for example, tissue microarrays), so further scaling is possible.

**Standard glass slides and sample prep.** Atera uses off-the-shelf slides, which "feels like a minor detail" but often enables large translational studies [BofA], distributed collection and access to biobanks (archived tissue collections). A universal sample preparation works across tissue types, including mixed types on one slide [Q1 2026 call].

**Workflow and compute.** Atera builds on the Xenium workflow. Image processing, the most compute-heavy step, runs in real time on onboard GPUs (graphics processors), and customers can analyze data on their own servers or the 10x Cloud [Q1 2026 call].

**Roadmap** [Q1 2026 call; BofA]: full "sample-in, data-out" automation, for which the instrument is "really well suited"; protein multiomics (measuring proteins alongside RNA), since "I don't think anyone has quite got proteins right yet"; in situ, base-by-base sequencing for spatial mutation detection; and software, compute and AI work including multi-sample analysis on the 10x Cloud, which Saxonov called "absolutely game changing."

### Customer response, orders and buyers

Saxonov said early response "has been extraordinary," beating high expectations [Q1 2026 call], and "really phenomenal" [BofA]. One customer called it the largest technology leap of their career; another called it "the holy grail." Conversations focus "on how to incorporate Atera into their research programs, not whether to buy it," often moving quickly to multi-year programs and large cohorts [Q1 2026 call]. Pre-order flow is "incredibly encouraging, right out of the gate, not really anything like we've seen before," but he gave no order count ("It's not our policy"), promising only "some directional kind of indication of the velocity of demand" [BofA].

Interest is "coming from everywhere," with no segment lacking it [Q1 2026 call; BofA]. Demand gives 10x "the luxury of prioritizing" customers who can run at scale immediately: large Xenium users, service providers, core labs (shared facilities serving other researchers) and "evangelizers" who will validate the platform. It will broaden later, including to the "quite intense interest" from labs new to spatial or to 10x spatial that can now "step off from the sidelines" [BofA]. Atera has a "substantially higher ceiling" per instrument than Xenium, and management expects "materially higher utilization rate pretty quickly out of the gate," with no figure given [BofA].

Saxonov said Atera expands the market in three segments [Q1 2026 call]: discovery research (such as whole-organ mapping for the Human Cell Atlas); translational research on clinical cohorts (citing collaborator Carl June's AACR work); and large AI models, "an entirely new market."

### Supply, ramp and margins

Taich expects about **40 Atera units** sold across Q3 and Q4 2026, mostly Q4 [Q1 2026 call]. Saxonov said "supply is going to be the constraint" through 2026 and into 2027 [BofA], and most customers ordering now "probably won't be getting their instruments until well into 2027" [Q1 2026 call].

Asked by BofA about bottlenecks, Saxonov named none, calling Atera "a really sophisticated instrument" that 10x will be "very smart and measured around how we build it and how we test it" [Q1 2026 call]. No 2027 capacity was given. Taich said capacity spending is "not a significant investment relative to sort of where we are in manufacturing capacity" because it leverages Xenium. There is "some dual capability" at the Pleasanton headquarters and the Singapore plant, with final location undecided. 10x is pre-buying long-lead parts, has begun procuring for H1 2027, and feels "really confident" in about 40 units for 2026 [BofA].

On gross margin (revenue minus direct product cost, as a share of revenue), Taich said Atera instrument margins in Q4 2026 should resemble Xenium's, which are "lower than the corporate average for sure," causing "a little bit of margin pressure," especially in Q4. Atera consumable margins are designed to match Chromium's. As the installed base grows at high utilization, Atera will be "margin accretive" (raising company-wide margin), and the mix "should actually get better than where our corporate averages have been" [Q1 2026 call; BofA].

## Effect on Xenium and Visium (cannibalization)

Q1 2026 spatial instrument revenue fell 32% year over year. Taich blamed "fairly significant anticipation" of Atera, not macro: a campaign at AGBT (a February genomics conference), word of mouth and early-access customers under NDA led would-be Xenium buyers to wait [Q1 2026 call]. Saxonov added that 10x signaled a launch in mid-Q1, instrument purchases are "always back-end loaded" (concentrated late in a quarter), and many expected a new Xenium version [BofA]. Ryskin said Q1 spatial instruments were lighter than BofA expected.

Barclays, Leerink and Deutsche Bank pressed on cannibalization. Saxonov told Leerink that Atera "is going to put pressure on both Spatial platforms," and that this is in guidance [Q1 2026 call]. Customers keep running existing systems because of projects, workflows, familiar data, grants and collaborations: "You're always surprised by how sticky things are" [BofA]. Xenium will keep selling at "a much reduced number, but … continued number," helped by long-cycle pipeline deals, with consumables especially resilient [BofA; Taich, Q1 2026 call]. Long term, "things will just consolidate onto Atera," improving margin, turnaround and scale [BofA]. Visium "has its place" for its consistency across large sample sets and will stay supported [Q1 2026 call, to Deutsche Bank]. Migration to Atera would be "an amazingly great thing for us" [Q1 2026 call, to Barclays].

Saxonov gave no Atera price. He said pricing is set to expand the market, since whole transcriptome removes panel customization, throughput allows whole cohorts and standard slides unlock biobanks. He said the "biggest launch" label was intentional and that he is mindful of the company's history with its earlier Single Cell product transition [Q1 2026 call].

Asked by Stifel whether Flex volume could migrate to Atera, Saxonov said it is "not something that we're expecting to be an issue in the near term nor is it anything that we're hearing from customers," because configuration, pricing and workloads are "sufficiently different" [Q1 2026 call]. Apex enables the next order of magnitude of single-cell scale; only at "2 logs of scaling" (about 100 times larger) might some projects become Atera candidates [BofA].

## Chromium single cell: price versus volume

The single-cell story is falling prices offset by rising usage. Saxonov recalled that around 2024 investors debated whether single cell had any market left, while 10x saw "way more volume than people were appreciating." He set three goals [BofA, 2026-05-13]:
1. **Consistent volume growth:** achieved, with double-digit reaction growth for "quite a number of quarters running."
2. **Absorb price declines:** the last couple of quarters reached "that ballpark."
3. **Let pricing equilibrate so volume becomes revenue growth:** "We're getting closer to that point as we think especially towards 2027."

In Q1 2026, single-cell consumables revenue grew 6% year over year while reaction volumes (number of single-cell runs) grew double digits, so revenue per reaction fell. Flex Apex pricing and mix were "similar to what we saw in Q4" [Q1 2026 call], and "the pricing declines were more than made up with the volume growth" [BofA]. Guggenheim's request for the Q2 pricing headwind and Stifel's for volume growth excluding Flex Apex went unanswered; Saxonov noted Apex was just over one quarter into launch with "really large scaling up of volumes."

Growth came from **Flex** (the most popular assay by volume) and **Flex Apex**, Chromium assays that work well on FFPE samples (formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue, the standard preserved clinical form), suiting large translational studies. Products "with more accessible pricing have been unlocking new ways of Single Cell demand" [Q1 2026 call]. Big and AI projects are "unquestionably" incremental and "would not have happened at like previous price points." Single cell is moving from "an esoteric technique where you pick and choose your samples" to one where "the entire cohort runs it"; "Flex and especially Apex has been sort of a game changer" [BofA]. By 2027, Saxonov expects Apex widely adopted and pricing "will have stabilized," so volume growth becomes revenue growth [Q1 2026 call].

## Q1 2026 results

10x reported on May 7, 2026. Revenue was **$150.8 million**, down 2.61% year over year as reported, but up **9%** excluding one-time patent-settlement revenue in Q1 2025 [Q1 2026 call]. Per Seeking Alpha headers, revenue beat consensus by $4.28 million (implying about $146.6 million), and EPS (earnings per share) of **$0.07** beat by $0.13 (implying an expected loss of about $0.06). Taich said Q1 was helped by late-Q4 2025 orders shipped in early January.

The table compares Q1 2026 revenue categories with Q1 2025. Consumables (reagent kits bought for every experiment) track usage; instruments (one-time purchases) track capital budgets. The pattern is strong usage growth alongside an instrument pause ahead of Atera.

| Category | Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025 | Driver cited by management |
|---|---|---|
| Total consumables | +13% | Growth in both single cell and spatial |
| Single-cell consumables | +6% | Double-digit reaction growth, offset by lower price per reaction (Flex Apex) |
| Spatial consumables | +31% | Xenium momentum; double-digit growth "several quarters running" |

| Chromium instruments | −12% | n/a |

Excluding Q1 2025 license and royalty revenue, Americas grew 9%, EMEA 16% and APAC 5% year over year [Q1 2026 call].

Gross margin was 70%, up from 68%, on lower warranty costs and inventory write-downs, partly offset by lower license and royalty revenue; Wolfe Research said this beat its and the Street's expectations. Operating expenses fell 15% year over year on lower outside legal and personnel costs, or 20% excluding a one-time patent-settlement gain in Q1 2025. Cash and investments were $540 million, up $113 million year over year and $16 million from Q4 2025 [Q1 2026 call].

Saxonov called it "quite a strong performance along multiple dimensions" [BofA] and said "spatial biology remains in the very early stages of adoption and the vast majority of the opportunity is still untapped" [Q1 2026 call].

## 2026 guidance and quarterly shape

10x reinstated full-year guidance in February 2026 and maintained it on May 7 [Q1 2026 call]:
- **Revenue** of **$600–625 million**, 0% to 4% growth over 2025 excluding upfront patent-settlement revenue, with the midpoint assuming double-digit growth in single-cell reactions and spatial consumables revenue.
- **Gross margin** in the mid-60s percent despite Q1's 70%. Taich is watching inflation in memory chips and petroleum-based plastics and is "managing that quite well."
- **Operating expenses** roughly flat, with flexibility to keep investing.

The February guide was built "with the Atera launch in mind," including purchase delays; Taich said management deliberately reinstated guidance knowing Atera would launch at AACR [BofA]. Saxonov said results are "very much in concordance" with plan: "We feel very comfortable where we are with the guide, especially after like what we've seen in Q1." Taich: "Nothing is easy," but "we feel really confident about the guide." No change was made at BofA.

## Translational research and clinical diagnostics

Saxonov said translational demand is growing in academia and biopharma [Q1 2026 call]. Translational research turns lab findings into drug targets and biomarkers (measurable signals predicting disease course or treatment response), which matter because many therapies work only in patient subsets. Xenium is robust on clinical samples, and Atera adds larger studies and more information per sample. Biopharma conversations on Apex and Atera are "really encouraging," and he expects biopharma to be "a big driver, especially as we kind of think about next year and beyond." He did not detail feedback on Phase 2 or 3 trial readiness, which Jefferies asked about.

The largest opportunity lies in later-stage work, where biomarkers explain response and toxicity, pointing toward diagnostics. The next step is clinical evidence on large cohorts via two tracks: supporting and partnering on customer studies for later clinical deployment, and 10x's own work in tissue-based spatial profiling in oncology and blood-based single-cell monitoring in autoimmune disease [Q1 2026 call]. At BofA, Saxonov said diagnostics have "come into much sharper focus" as Atera and Chromium can "generate clinical evidence at scale." 10x is "leaning in very intensely," but "it's not necessarily a story quite yet in terms of revenue for the near term," though "there's potential for really massive businesses to grow out of that."

## 2027 setup and open questions

Saxonov framed 2027 three ways [Q1 2026 call]. **Product:** "2027 will be the first full year of Atera, really the year of Atera," with Flex Apex widely adopted and single-cell pricing stabilized. **Market:** exponential AI-driven research and scaling translational cohorts are "set to kick in." **Macro:** good compares at minimum, acceleration if funding improves.

Across both events, management did not disclose Atera price, order counts, 2027 production capacity, specific production bottlenecks, AI revenue share or TAM, single-cell reaction growth excluding Flex Apex, Q2 Flex Apex pricing, or Atera utilization figures.
