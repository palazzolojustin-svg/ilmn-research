# Illumina: Financial History and Guidance

*Core Notes section 02. Synthesized from 24 source extracts as of 2026-10-10. Bracketed tags cite the source; full detail is in Working Notes (see INDEX.md).*

## Overview: two years dominated by GRAIL and a product transition

Illumina's 2022–2023 results are hard to read at face value. Net losses totalled about $5.6 billion over the two years. Most of that came from non-cash and legal charges tied to GRAIL, the cancer-screening company Illumina bought in August 2021, not from the core sequencing business.

The underlying business tells a quieter but still important story. Core Illumina, meaning everything except GRAIL, grew revenue only 1% in 2022 and shrank 3% in 2023. Its gross margin, the share of revenue left after the direct cost of making products, fell from 70.7% in 2021 to 64.4% in 2023. Three forces drove the slowdown:

- **Macroeconomic caution.** Customers held less inventory and delayed lab expansions and capital purchases.
- **A platform changeover.** Customers moved from the NovaSeq 6000 to the NovaSeq X, launched in Q1 2023. That hurt instrument margins and consumables volumes in the meantime.
- **Weakness in China.** The company cited competitive pressure there.

All figures below come from Illumina's SEC filings: the 10-Q quarterly reports for Q1 2022 through Q3 2023, and the 10-K annual reports for FY2022 and FY2023. Illumina's fiscal years end on the Sunday closest to December 31, so FY2022 ended January 1, 2023 and FY2023 ended December 31, 2023.

### Revenue by product type

Illumina sells three kinds of things:

- **Instruments:** the sequencers and array scanners themselves.
- **Consumables:** the reagent kits customers must buy for every run.
- **Services:** maintenance contracts, sequencing services, licensing, and GRAIL's Galleri test.

Sequencing made up about 91% of revenue every year, and microarrays (an older chip-based genotyping technology) about 9%. Services grew every year, while consumables, the recurring engine of the business, fell 4% in 2023. The 2021 instrument split is not given in these sources.

## 2022: flat growth, collapsing margins and the GRAIL charges

### Q1 2022: the last "normal" quarter

Revenue grew 12% to $1,223 million, up from $1,093 million in Q1 2021 [Q1 2022 10-Q]. Products contributed $1,070 million, up 12%:

- Sequencing consumables were $778 million, up 13%, from a larger installed base (the number of Illumina machines in customers' labs).
- Sequencing instruments were $211 million, up 20%, on NovaSeq shipments.
- Microarray consumables were $75 million, down from $79 million.

Service and other revenue was $153 million, including $10 million from GRAIL. By region, the Americas rose 15% to $648 million, EMEA 4% to $316 million and Asia-Pacific 33% to $132 million. Greater China was flat at $127 million.

Gross margin fell 330 basis points (3.3 percentage points) to 66.6%. Core margin held at 69.7% despite higher freight costs. GRAIL posted a $29 million gross loss, driven by $34 million of amortization, the gradual expensing of intangible assets such as technology and brand recorded at acquisition. One extract summary refers to a "30 basis point" decline; the filing's numbers show 330.

R&D rose 64% to $323 million: Core R&D was $238 million (+21%) and GRAIL added $85 million. SG&A (selling, general and administrative expense) fell 18% to $308 million. Q1 2021 had included $105 million of "Continuation Payments," cash advanced to GRAIL during regulatory review. Q1 2022 also benefited from a $49 million gain from cutting the estimated value of the GRAIL earn-out.

Operating income fell 5% to $184 million. Net income fell 41% to $86 million because the effective tax rate jumped to 38.3% from 13.1%. Two factors drove the higher rate:

- $24 million from GRAIL's pre-acquisition losses interacting with GILTI, a US minimum tax on foreign earnings.
- $4 million from a new rule forcing R&D costs to be capitalized and spread over years rather than deducted at once.

Management estimated the R&D rule would add about $103 million to 2022 tax payments [Q1 2022 10-Q], then raised that to about $142 million [Q2 2022 10-Q]. Operating cash flow fell to $172 million from $282 million, and free cash flow to $111 million from $240 million.

### Q2 2022: $609 million of legal accruals

Revenue rose 3% to $1,162 million [Q2 2022 10-Q]. Core consumables grew 5% to $818 million, Core instruments were flat at $193 million, and GRAIL contributed $12 million. The Americas rose to $639 million from $589 million and Asia-Pacific to $97 million, while EMEA fell to $308 million and Greater China to $118 million. Foreign exchange (FX) cut first-half revenue by $33 million.

Gross margin fell to 66.0% from 71.2%. Management cited less fixed-cost leverage (fewer units to spread factory overhead across), higher freight costs and GRAIL's $29 million gross loss, partly offset by a better product mix.

The quarter swung to a $579 million operating loss because of $609 million in legal charges:

- **EU fine accrual, $453 million.** This was 10% of 2021 revenue, the maximum fine for closing GRAIL before EU clearance. The European Commission (EC) issued its Statement of Objections, a formal charge, on July 19, 2022.
- **BGI patent dispute, $156 million.** This estimated past damages owed under a settlement signed July 14, 2022.

The net loss was $535 million, or $(3.40) per share, against a profit of $185 million ($1.26) a year earlier. Core R&D rose 23% to $249 million. Core SG&A fell 18% to $339 million despite a $38 million charge from raising the earn-out estimate.

Leadership changed as well. CFO Sam Samad left, and on June 9, 2022 strategy chief Joydeep Goswami became interim CFO.

### Q3 2022: the $3.9 billion GRAIL write-down

Revenue rose 1% to $1,115 million [Q3 2022 10-Q]. Sequencing consumables were flat at $720 million; management said installed-base growth was offset by "challenging macroeconomic factors on customers." FX cost $27 million, partly offset by $16 million of hedge gains. Sequencing instruments fell 9% to $162 million as fewer NovaSeq 6000s shipped, while Core service revenue rose 11% to $142 million. EMEA fell 7% and Greater China rose 9%. Core gross margin fell to 67.9% from 70.7%; the consolidated figure was 64.3%.

The headline was a **$3,914 million goodwill impairment** on GRAIL. Goodwill is the premium paid over the book value of an acquired company's assets. The triggers were the EU General Court's July 13, 2022 ruling that the EC had jurisdiction, the EC's September 6, 2022 prohibition of the deal, and the fall in Illumina's stock price.

The discount rate used to value GRAIL rose to 22.0% from 16.0%. The discount rate is the annual return investors would demand to fund a risky business, so a higher rate means a lower present value. At the May 2022 test, GRAIL's value had exceeded its book value by $700 million, and Core Illumina's by more than $30 billion.

A $218 million gain from cutting the GRAIL earn-out estimate shrank consolidated SG&A to $146 million, and the BGI settlement netted an $11 million gain. The net loss was $3,816 million, or $(24.26) per share. In Q3 2021 the company had earned $317 million, helped by a one-time $899 million gain on its prior GRAIL stake.

Management issued its first numeric 2022 guidance: revenue "flat to 1% higher" than 2021 [Q3 2022 10-Q].

### Q4 and full-year 2022

Full-year revenue rose 1% to $4,584 million [FY2022 10-K], at the top of guidance. Sequencing consumables rose $26 million to $2,919 million. Instruments fell 3% to $728 million as customers deferred high-throughput purchases while waiting for the NovaSeq X, though NextSeq 1000/2000 shipments rose. Core service grew 6%, and GRAIL reached $55 million.

By region, the Americas reached $2,479 million, of which the US was $2,290 million. The 10-K extract describes the Americas as "down," but the figures show a rise from $2,358 million. EMEA fell to $1,215 million from $1,289 million, Greater China to $472 million from $502 million, and Asia-Pacific rose to $418 million from $377 million. FX cost Core revenue $84 million, offset by $53 million of hedge gains. International sales fell to 50% of revenue from 52%.

Gross margin fell 490 basis points to 64.8%, and GRAIL's gross loss widened to $117 million on $134 million of amortization. Core R&D rose 13% to $1,004 million. Core SG&A fell 33% to $1,003 million, helped by a $205 million earn-out gain and the end of the 2021 Continuation Payments ($245 million). Q4 restructuring charges were $24 million in SG&A and $6 million in R&D, mostly severance and a lease write-down.

The EU fine accrual was raised to $458 million, now 10% of 2022 revenue, bringing total legal charges for the year to $619 million.

The tax provision was $68 million despite a $4,336 million pre-tax loss. The non-deductible impairment added $822 million of tax effect, the fine $96 million, R&D capitalization $87 million and GRAIL's losses $60 million. Operating cash flow was $392 million and capex $286 million. The year included a $180 million cash payment recorded as a licence asset from the BGI deal.

## 2023: decline, restructuring and the move to divest GRAIL

### Q1 2023: NovaSeq X launch hits revenue and margin

Revenue fell 11% to $1,087 million [Q1 2023 10-Q]. Core consumables fell 10% to $770 million. Sequencing consumables dropped $92 million because of "lower NovaSeq 6000 consumables pull-through as some of our high throughput customers transition to NovaSeq X," plus tighter customer inventory. Pull-through is consumables spending per installed instrument. Core instruments fell 27% to $160 million, with the first NovaSeq X shipments only partly offsetting. FX cost $24 million.

Under reorganized sales regions, the Americas fell 5% to $616 million, Europe 9% to $261 million, Greater China 28% to $91 million, and AMEA (Asia-Pacific, Middle East and Africa) 27% to $119 million.

Core gross margin fell to 63.8% from 69.7%. Management said that is "typical with a new platform introduction until we scale manufacturing." The operating loss was $64 million. Interest expense rose to $20 million on the new notes, against $17 million of interest income. A 103.9% effective tax rate produced an $81 million tax benefit, so net income came to $3 million ($0.02 per share). Operating cash flow was only $10 million, largely because of income-tax payments.

Management said "we continue to expect revenue to grow 7% to 10% in 2023" and planned to cut "annualized run rate expenses by more than $100 million beginning later in 2023" [Q1 2023 10-Q].

### Q2 2023: guidance cut and restructuring begins

Revenue rose 1% to $1,176 million, leaving first-half revenue down 5% [Q2 2023 10-Q]. Sequencing consumables fell 1% to $734 million, instruments rose 2% to $193 million on NovaSeq X shipments, and service and other rose 12% to $175 million. Europe rose 11% to $303 million, while AMEA fell 10%. Gross margin was 62.2%, with higher field-service and installation costs adding to earlier pressures.

Restructuring charges totalled $33 million ($25 million of severance and $7 million of asset write-downs), including a plan to exit the i3 campus in San Diego. SG&A also absorbed $25 million of costs from Icahn's proxy contest, a shareholder campaign to replace directors. The net loss was $234 million, or $(1.48) per share, with a $145 million tax charge on an $89 million pre-tax loss.

Full-year 2023 revenue guidance was cut to about 1% growth from 7–10%, citing macroeconomic conditions and competition in China.

On July 12, 2023, the EC imposed its €432 million fine; the accrual stood at $471 million. One extract wrongly says the fine was "substantially accrued in Q2 2022." The accrual began then, but the fine itself was imposed in July 2023.

### Q3 2023: second GRAIL impairment

Revenue was flat at $1,119 million and down 3% year to date [Q3 2023 10-Q]. Greater China fell 26% and AMEA 22%, while the Americas rose 11% to $663 million. Gross margin was 61.1%, helped somewhat by lower freight costs.

A falling share price triggered another $821 million GRAIL charge: $712 million on goodwill and $109 million on in-process R&D, using a 24.0% discount rate. Restructuring added $58 million, mostly i3 campus write-downs. The net loss was $754 million, or $(4.77) per share.

### Full-year 2023 and the outlook for 2024

Revenue fell 2% to $4,504 million [FY2023 10-K], slightly below the roughly 1% growth guided in Q2. Core consumables fell $140 million and Core instruments $23 million, while Core service rose 8% to $626 million and GRAIL reached $93 million.

Core operating income rose 15% to $552 million despite a 64.4% gross margin. SG&A grew 24% as the earn-out gain shrank to $24 million from $205 million; restructuring added $94 million and proxy costs $32 million. The order backlog (firm orders not yet shipped) fell to $653 million from $1,030 million.

For 2024, management guided Core revenue to "remain flat" and operating expenses to "slightly increase."

## Balance sheet, debt, cash flow and buybacks

### Cash and debt

Cash fell from $1,232 million at the start of 2022 to $1,000 million by Q3 2022. It rose to $2,011 million at year-end after Illumina issued $1 billion of notes on December 13, 2022: $500 million at 5.800% due 2025 and $500 million at 5.750% due 2027. It then fell to $1,048 million by end-2023, as Illumina repaid the $500 million 0.550% notes on March 23, 2023 and the $750 million 0% convertible notes on August 15, 2023, both in cash with no shares issued.

End-2023 debt was $1.5 billion of term notes: the 2025 and 2027 notes plus $500 million at 2.550% due 2031. Annual interest expense on debt rose to $74 million from $21 million.

The $750 million revolving credit facility, a standing loan line, was replaced on January 4, 2023 with one running to January 2028. It was never drawn. Its key covenant caps debt at 3.5 times EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization).

### Shareholders' equity

Equity fell from $10,740 million to $6,599 million at end-2022 and to $5,898 million by Q3 2023. The share count stayed around 157–159 million.

### Buybacks

There were no buybacks in 2022 or 2023. Only $15 million remained under the $750 million programme authorized on February 5, 2020, and management stated it had no intention to repurchase shares in fiscal 2022, 2023 or 2024.

### GRAIL-related liabilities

- **Earn-out.** The contingent value rights (CVRs) pay former GRAIL holders 2.5% of the first $1 billion of annual GRAIL revenue and 9% above that, for 12 years. Their fair value fell from $615 million to $412 million in 2022, then to $387 million in 2023.
- **EU fine.** The accrual reached $484 million by end-2023, including interest at 5.5%. In October 2023, Illumina provided guarantees in place of cash while it appealed.
- **Divestment funding.** The EC's October 12, 2023 divestment order requires GRAIL to be funded for 2.5 years at sale.
- **Tax on a sale.** Estimates of Illumina's tax basis in GRAIL were $500 million–$1 billion in the FY2022 and Q2 2023 filings, and zero to $500 million in Q3 2023. A lower basis means a bigger taxable gain on any sale.

### The GRAIL purchase price

Sources state the GRAIL purchase price three ways, and the figures measure different things:

- $4,986 million of stock [Q1 2022 10-Q];
- about $7.1 billion [Q2 2022 and Q3 2022 10-Qs];
- $9,745 million of total accounting consideration, including cash, stock, the earn-out and the prior 12% stake [FY2022 and FY2023 10-Ks].

### December 2023

On December 15, 2023, the Fifth Circuit vacated the FTC's divestiture order and sent it back to the agency. Two days later, on December 17, Illumina announced it would divest GRAIL, aiming to finalize terms by the end of Q2 2024.

## How to read the 2024–2025 numbers

Three accounting features make Illumina's 2024–2025 results hard to compare at face value.

**GRAIL is in some periods and not others.** GRAIL stayed in Illumina's results until its spin-off on June 24, 2024. The spin-off was not treated as a "discontinued operation," so earlier periods were not restated. Illumina therefore reports "consolidated" figures, which include GRAIL until the spin-off, and "Core Illumina" figures, which exclude it. From the second half of 2024 the two are the same.

**GAAP and non-GAAP figures differ a lot.** GAAP results follow official accounting rules. Non-GAAP results are management's adjusted figures, which strip out amortization, impairments, legal swings and investment gains. GAAP results were distorted by a $1.9B GRAIL impairment, a $481M net gain when the EU withdrew its fine, and quarterly revaluations of the GRAIL CVRs.

**Most figures come from filings.** Unless tagged otherwise, figures come from 10-Qs and 10-Ks. Non-GAAP figures and all guidance come from the earnings slide decks [Q2 2025 slides, 2025-07-31; Q3 2025 slides, 2025-10-30; Q4 2025 slides, 2026-02-05].

## 2024: GRAIL exits, the EU fine reverses, buybacks restart

### Q1 2024: flat sales, wider loss

Revenue fell 1% to $1,076M from $1,087M. Core Illumina fell 2% to $1,056M, while GRAIL grew 35% to $27M on sales of its Galleri cancer-screening blood test [Q1 2024 10-Q].

Instrument revenue fell 28% to $114M. Management said the company "entered 2024 with a lower backlog of NovaSeq X instruments" (its flagship high-throughput sequencer) after heavy launch pre-orders in 2023. Mid-throughput shipments also weakened as "capital and cash flow constraints continue to impact our customer's purchasing behavior."

Consumables were flat at $762M: sequencing consumables rose 1% to $691M and microarray consumables fell 9% to $71M. Service and other revenue rose 21% to $200M, helped by strategic partnerships. Regional results were mixed:

| Region | Q1'24 revenue | Change vs Q1'23 |
|---|---|---|
| Americas | $603M | −2% |
| Europe | $279M | +7% |
| Greater China | $78M | −14% |
| Asia-Pacific, Middle East & Africa | $116M | −3% |

GAAP gross margin rose 170 basis points to 62.0%, and Core gross margin reached 65.7% from 63.8%. Management credited a favorable consumables mix and freight and productivity savings, partly offset by lower-margin partnership revenue and higher warranty and field-service costs.

The operating loss widened to $111M from $64M, and the net loss was $126M (−$0.79 per share). Core R&D fell 7% to $241M on lower headcount. Core SG&A rose 17% to $336M, driven by $33M of restructuring charges, a $17M loss on the CVR liability and $7M of interest on the EU fine. Operating cash flow rose to $77M from $10M.

Management stated: "We do not intend to make any share repurchases during fiscal year 2024" [Q1 2024 10-Q]. That statement was later overtaken.

### Q2 2024: the GRAIL spin-off and a $1.9B write-down

Revenue fell 5% to $1,112M, and Core fell 6% to $1,092M. Instruments dropped 39% to $120M, consumables were flat at $807M, and Greater China fell to $75M from $115M. Core gross margin rose to 68.0% from 65.5% [Q2 2024 10-Q].

On June 24, 2024, Illumina distributed 85.5% of GRAIL to its shareholders, one GRAIL share for every six Illumina shares, and kept 14.5%. It contributed $974M of "Disposal Funding," cash required by the EU to cover 2.5 years of GRAIL's operations, and about $968M of cash left its balance sheet as a result. To fund this, Illumina drew a $750M 364-day term loan on June 20 at 6.7%.

GRAIL's goodwill and in-process R&D (IPR&D) were written down by $1,886M ($1,466M goodwill plus $420M IPR&D), cutting GRAIL's carrying value to an estimated $580M. ## 2025: China, tariffs, then a clinical-led recovery

### Q1 2025: new headwinds and a $100M cost program

**China.** On February 4, 2025, China's Ministry of Commerce put Illumina on its "unreliable entities list." On March 4, 2025, Illumina was told it could no longer export sequencing instruments into China, a market that had produced $308M in 2024.

**Tariffs.** In April 2025, management estimated new tariffs would cost about $85M a year, mostly on imports from its Singapore factory. It planned to "partially mitigate" this in 2025 and "more fully" in 2026 through supply-chain, cost and pricing actions.

**Cost program.** Management launched an incremental $100M cost-reduction program for 2025, covering stock compensation, non-labor spending and headcount. It responded to the China loss and uncertainty over NIH funding. The NIH (National Institutes of Health) is the main U.S. funder of academic research customers.

**Results.** Revenue fell 3% to $1,041M (Core down 1.4%). Sequencing consumables were $696M, up from $691M; instruments were $112M; service fell 7% to $161M on lower partnership revenue; and Greater China fell 8% to $72M. Core gross margin held at 65.6% against 65.7%, as management cited "lower product margins, primarily due to reduced pricing" and higher field-service cost.

GAAP operating income was $164M, against a $111M loss a year earlier, and net income was $131M ($0.82 per share). Operating cash flow was $240M and free cash flow $208M. Buybacks were 1.73M shares at $115.74, for $200M. The 10-Q gave no numerical full-year guidance.

### Q2 2025: guidance revised and SomaLogic announced

Revenue was $1,059M, down 3% on a Core basis. Sequencing instruments fell 18% to $96M, and consumables were flat at $740M. Europe grew 7% to $310M, while the Americas fell to $586M and Greater China fell 15% to $63M.

Non-GAAP operating margin rose 160 basis points to 23.8%, and non-GAAP EPS rose 9% to $1.19. GAAP net income was $235M, including about $97–102M of strategic-investment gains.

Free cash flow was $204M. Illumina bought back 4.49M shares at $84.66; the 10-Q gives $384M and the slides $380M. Gross leverage, debt divided by trailing EBITDA, was about 1.7x.

**SomaLogic.** On June 22, 2025, Illumina agreed to buy SomaLogic, a proteomics (protein-measurement) business, from Standard BioTools for $350M in cash, up to $75M in revenue milestones, plus royalties. Illumina would owe a $14.5M termination fee if the deal failed on regulatory grounds. Revenue was flat at $1,084M, while ex-China revenue grew about 2% in constant currency (CC, growth with exchange-rate effects removed). Greater China fell 31% to $52M. ## How to read this part

This part covers results and guidance from the preliminary FY2025 figures, released in January 2026, through Q2 2026, which ended June 28, 2026. It draws on five company documents: the CEO's slides at the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference [JPM, 2026-01-13], the Q1 and Q2 2026 earnings slides [Q1 2026 slides, 2026-04-30; Q2 2026 slides, 2026-07-30], and the matching 10-Qs [Q1 2026 10-Q, 2026-05-01; Q2 2026 10-Q, 2026-07-31]. These contain no analyst questions, ratings, price targets or consensus estimates, so every view below is management's unless marked as an inference.

Three conventions recur. **"Organic" growth** strips out currency movements and acquisitions. **ROW** means everything outside Greater China, which includes Taiwan and Hong Kong. **Non-GAAP figures** exclude items such as amortization of acquired intangibles, acquisition costs, restructuring, and gains or losses on investments. Illumina says non-GAAP operating margin and EPS are the key metrics its board uses to judge and pay management.

### FY27 targets and strategy framing

Management restated three FY27 targets first set out in 2024 [JPM, 2026-01-13]:

1. **High-single-digit ex-China CC revenue growth.** The track record so far is –1% in FY24 and +2% in FY25.
2. **At least 500 basis points of non-GAAP operating margin expansion,** reaching 26% in FY27 on an ex-China basis. With margin at 21% in FY24 and about 23% in FY25, about 300 basis points remain.
3. **Double-digit to teens EPS growth,** with FY27 shown at about 15%.

The deck framed growth around three pillars:

- **Core sequencing and the NovaSeq X transition:** NovaSeq X v1.4 for clinical workflows and Constellation, both in H1'26, plus DRAGEN v4.4 and 5-Base, already on the market.
- **Multiomics,** meaning measuring several biological layers such as DNA, RNA and proteins: Single Cell (H1'25), Connected Multiomics (H2'25), Protein Prep (Q4'25) and Ex-Situ Spatial (H1'26).
- **Data, AI and services,** through BioInsight.

On capital deployment, management listed growth investment; bolt-on acquisitions judged on market expansion, EPS accretion and return on capital, with SomaLogic then expected to close in 1H'26; and anti-dilutive buybacks (repurchases that offset share issuance to employees) plus opportunistic repurchases.

Management also claimed, in footnoted "directional" internal estimates, that clinical applications are less than 20% penetrated, that a genome uses about 15x the data of an exome (more than 120 Gb versus about 8 Gb; Gb, or gigabases, measures sequencing data volume), and that clinical data growth will exceed a 30% compound annual rate.

Excluding China's 1.6-point drag, ROW organic growth was 8.1%. Revenue and EPS both beat the top of April guidance; that is our comparison, as the deck does not characterize a beat. ## SomaLogic: what it cost and how it is accounted for

Illumina closed the purchase of SomaLogic and related assets from Standard BioTools on January 30, 2026. The total price was $460M: $382M in cash plus $81M of contingent consideration, less $3M for settling a prior relationship.

The contingent payments were milestones of up to $75M and a 2% royalty on SOMAmer-based library-prep kit sales for 10 years, through January 2036. A $25M milestone for 2025 was met and paid in April 2026, and up to $50M was tied to 2026 revenue. The remaining contingent liability was $37M at June 28. On July 27, 2026, Illumina paid $30M to buy out the 2026 milestone and the royalty early.

The purchase price was allocated as follows:

- **Goodwill of $171M,** tax-deductible.
- **Intangibles of $186M:** developed technology $158M (9-year life), trade name $16M (7-year) and customer relationships $12M (8-year).
- **Inventory of $57M,** of which $22M is long-term because its manufacturing takes more than a year. This was a new accounting policy for Illumina; non-current inventory was $26M by June.
- **Other items:** $19M of cash, $13M of receivables, $19M of property and $14M of lease assets, less liabilities.

Deal costs of $7M were expensed in Q1.

On a pro forma basis, as if SomaLogic had been owned since the start of 2025, Q1'26 revenue would have been $1,099M. Q2'26 revenue would have been $1,159M with net income of $192M, against $1,076M and $241M in Q2'25. First-half revenue would have been $2,258M with net income of $333M, against $2,136M and $318M.

Together with a separate $50M patent-portfolio purchase (14-year life), net intangibles rose to $430M in Q1 and stood at $410M in Q2, up from $210M at year-end; gross intangibles rose from $724M to $961M. Expected amortization is $78M in 2027, $76M in 2028, $46M in 2029, $40M in 2030 and $131M thereafter. The remainder-of-2026 figure was $59M in the Q1 filing and $39M in the Q2 filing.

## GRAIL residual exposure

Illumina still holds GRAIL stock and owes the CVRs described above. Payments remain small, though GRAIL's covered revenue is growing, which management credits to sales of the Galleri test. In Q1'26 Illumina paid $410K on $44M of covered revenue in Q4'25, against $360K on $38M a year earlier. Over two quarters, payments were $794K on $84M, against $660K on $70M.

The CVR liability was $35M in March and $40M in June, against $54M at year-end. It is valued with a Monte Carlo simulation (a statistical model of many possible outcomes) using a 3% revenue risk premium. The two filings describe the market-cap averaging window differently, as 25-day versus 60-day.

## Headwinds management flagged

**Tariffs.** Tariffs in place since April 2025 hit mainly imports from the Singapore plant. In February 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that IEEPA, an emergency-powers law, does not authorize tariffs, and in March 2026 the Court of International Trade ordered customs refunds to be processed in phases. Illumina is pursuing refunds, but recoveries so far are immaterial, and "timing and amount... remain uncertain."

**Cost pressures.** Freight and memory-chip costs are rising in 2026.

**Demand risks.** These include NIH research funding cuts, ongoing China restrictions and armed conflict.

**ERP rollout.** The ERP upgrade, begun in Q3 2025, is due in H1 2027, and management warned it could affect internal controls.

**GRAIL litigation.**

- A Delaware derivative suit (brought on the company's behalf against its directors) had a motion-to-dismiss hearing on February 13, 2026; the motion is still pending.
- A federal securities class action awaits a ruling on the motion to dismiss, with no hearing set.
- In the California state class action, class certification was granted May 15, 2026, and Illumina's motion for judgment on the pleadings was denied June 26, 2026. Illumina sought appellate review on July 28, 2026, and a hearing on amending its answer is set for October 30, 2026.
- The board again deferred shareholder litigation demands on February 4, 2026.
