# Illumina: Financial History and Guidance

*Core Notes section 02. Synthesized from 26 source extracts as of 2026-10-10. Bracketed tags cite the source; full detail is in Working Notes (see INDEX.md).*

## The two years in one paragraph

From 2022 through 2023, Illumina's core sequencing business stopped growing and its profitability fell, while its acquisition of GRAIL produced very large accounting charges. Core revenue went from $4.52 billion in 2021 to $4.55 billion in 2022, then down to $4.44 billion in 2023. Gross margin (the share of revenue left after the direct cost of making products) fell from 69.7% in 2021 to 60.9% in 2023.

GRAIL is the cancer-screening company Illumina bought in August 2021. It drove reported net losses of $4.4 billion in 2022 and $1.2 billion in 2023, mostly through goodwill impairments (write-downs of the premium paid in an acquisition) and an EU fine.

Management's guidance moved in one direction, down:

- 2022 started with "revenue will increase" and ended at "flat to +1%."
- 2023 started at +7–10% growth and was cut to about +1%; revenue actually fell 2%.
- For 2024, the outlook was flat core revenue.

All figures below come from Illumina's SEC filings (10-Qs for quarterly reports, 10-Ks for annual reports). Earnings-call guidance (which often includes EPS ranges) is not in these sources.

### Segment view: Core Illumina vs. GRAIL

The core business stayed profitable throughout, but its operating income was depressed in 2022 by $619 million of legal charges booked in that segment. GRAIL's revenue grew quickly from a tiny base, while its losses were dominated by impairments.

### Q1 2022: the last growth quarter

Revenue rose 12% to $1,223 million [Q1 2022 10-Q]. The drivers were:

- **Sequencing consumables** (the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run) rose 13% to $778 million on a larger installed base of machines.
- **Sequencing instruments** rose 20% to $211 million on NovaSeq shipments.
- **Microarray consumables** fell to $75 million.
- **Service and other** revenue grew 3% to $153 million.

By region, Americas rose 15% to $648 million, EMEA 4% to $316 million, and Asia-Pacific 33% to $132 million. Greater China was flat at $127 million.

Gross margin fell 330 basis points (3.3 percentage points) to 66.6%. Core margin held at 69.7%, with higher freight costs the main drag. GRAIL lost $29 million at the gross level, including $34 million of amortization.

On operating costs:

- **R&D** jumped 64% to $323 million, of which $85 million was GRAIL's.
- **SG&A** fell 18% to $308 million. The prior year had included $105 million of Continuation Payments, and this quarter included a $49 million gain from revaluing the GRAIL earnout lower.
- **GRAIL** lost $172 million at the operating level.

Net income fell 41% to $86 million. The effective tax rate (taxes as a share of pre-tax profit) jumped to 38.3% from 13.1%. Part of that was $24 million from GRAIL's pre-acquisition losses interacting with GILTI (a US minimum tax on foreign earnings) and $4 million from Section 174. Section 174 is a 2017 tax-law rule that, starting in 2022, forces companies to spread R&D deductions over several years instead of deducting them immediately. Management estimated this would add about $103 million to 2022 tax payments [Q1 2022 10-Q].

Operating cash flow fell 39% to $172 million, and free cash flow was $111 million versus $240 million a year earlier.

### Q2 2022: legal charges swing results to a loss

Revenue grew just 3% to $1,162 million. Core consumables rose 5% to $818 million, and core instruments were flat at $193 million. Foreign exchange was a $33 million headwind in the first half.

Gross margin fell to 66.0% from 71.2%. The reasons given were:

- less fixed-cost leverage (fixed factory costs spread over fewer units);
- freight;
- GRAIL's $29 million gross loss.

Favorable product mix partly offset these.

The quarter's $579 million operating loss came from $609 million of legal contingencies [Q2 2022 10-Q]:

- **EU fine accrual: $453 million.** This was 10% of 2021 revenue, the maximum, booked after the European Commission's July 2022 objection to Illumina closing GRAIL without clearance.
- **BGI settlement: $156 million.** This was the estimated past-damages share of a $325 million settlement with BGI and its affiliate Complete Genomics, signed July 14 and paid July 25, 2022.

Core SG&A fell 18% to $339 million despite a $38 million charge from revaluing the GRAIL earnout upward. The effective tax rate was 16.0%. Section 174 added $23 million to tax expense, and management raised its full-year estimate of the Section 174 impact to about $142 million.

On June 9, 2022, CFO Sam Samad left. Joydeep Goswami, the chief strategy officer, became interim CFO.

### Q3 2022: the $3.9 billion GRAIL impairment

Revenue of $1,115 million was up 1% [Q3 2022 10-Q]. By product:

- Sequencing consumables were flat at $720 million, which management blamed on "challenging macroeconomic factors on customers." Foreign exchange cost $27 million, partly offset by $16 million of hedge gains.
- Instruments fell 9% to $162 million as NovaSeq 6000 shipments dropped.
- Core service revenue rose 11% to $142 million.

By region, EMEA fell 7% to $290 million while Greater China grew 9% to $133 million. Core gross margin fell to 67.9% from 70.7%, again because of fixed-cost deleverage and freight.

The headline charge was a $3,914 million goodwill impairment on GRAIL. Two events triggered it: the EU General Court's July 13 ruling that the Commission had jurisdiction over the deal, and the Commission's September 6 Prohibition Decision, together with a sharp fall in Illumina's share price. The discount rate used to value GRAIL rose from 16.0% at the May test to 22.0%. GRAIL goodwill was left at $2,178 million, and core fair value exceeded its carrying value by more than $30 billion.

SG&A collapsed to $146 million because of a $218 million gain from revaluing the earnout. Net loss was $3,816 million. Management guided 2022 revenue to "flat to 1% higher."

### Q4 / FY2022: flat year, refinancing done

Full-year revenue rose 1% to $4,584 million [FY2022 10-K]:

- **Consumables** were $3,225 million (70% of revenue). Sequencing consumables rose 1% to $2,919 million.
- **Instruments** fell 3% to $728 million because customers deferred purchases ahead of the NovaSeq X, Illumina's next-generation high-throughput sequencer due in 2023.
- **Services** rose to $631 million.
- **By technology,** sequencing was 91% of revenue and arrays 9%.

The legal charge for the year totaled $619 million:

- **EU fine accrual.** It was raised to $458 million, 10% of 2022 revenue.
- **BGI settlement.** It was finalized as a $145 million net loss: $150 million for past damages, less a $5 million gain. Of the $325 million payment, $180 million was capitalized as a license to BGI's two-channel sequencing patents, amortized over 6.5 years at about $28 million a year. The earlier Q2 estimate of the license value was $177 million.

Q4 restructuring charges were $6 million in R&D and $24 million in SG&A. The earnout gain for the year was $205 million. The effective tax rate was −1.6%, because the impairment was non-deductible ($822 million tax effect), as was the fine ($96 million). Section 174 added $87 million.

### Q1 2023: the NovaSeq X air pocket

Revenue fell 11% to $1,087 million, and core revenue fell 12% to $1,076 million [Q1 2023 10-Q]:

- **Sequencing consumables** fell by $92 million. Management cited "lower NovaSeq 6000 consumables pull-through" as big customers moved to NovaSeq X. Pull-through means consumables bought per installed machine. Tighter customer inventories also contributed.
- **Instruments** fell 27% to $160 million even with the first NovaSeq X shipments.
- **Regions.** Greater China fell 28% to $91 million and the new Asia-Pacific/Middle East/Africa (AMEA) region fell 27% to $119 million.

Core gross margin dropped to 63.8% from 69.7%. Management said this reflected lower volumes and lower early NovaSeq X instrument margins, "typical with a new platform introduction."

Several below-the-line items stood out:

- **Earnout.** The gain was only $1 million, versus $49 million a year earlier, which raised SG&A to $378 million.
- **Taxes.** The effective tax rate was 103.9%, including $49 million from Section 174 and $44 million from GRAIL-related GILTI effects. A tax benefit turned a $78 million pre-tax loss into $3 million of net income.
- **Cash.** Operating cash flow was just $10 million.

Management reiterated 7–10% growth for 2023 and planned more than $100 million of annualized cost cuts starting later in 2023.

### Q2 2023: guidance cut to ~1%

Revenue was $1,176 million, up 1%, and first-half revenue was down 5% [Q2 2023 10-Q]:

- Sequencing consumables were $734 million, down 1%.
- Instruments were $193 million, up 2% on NovaSeq X.
- Service rose 12% to $175 million.

Gross margin was 62.2%, also hurt by higher field-service and installation costs.

Several costs pushed SG&A to $450 million:

- $33 million of restructuring, including $25 million of severance and $7 million of impairments;
- $25 million of proxy contest costs (a shareholder fight over board seats).

Management cut the 2023 outlook to about 1% growth, citing macro conditions, customer inventory discipline and Chinese competition.

The effective tax rate was −163.8%: $145 million of tax expense on an $89 million pre-tax loss. Interim tax accounting switched to a year-to-date method. The SEC opened an investigation into the GRAIL deal in July 2023.

### Q3 2023: second GRAIL write-down

Revenue was flat at $1,119 million. Americas rose 11% to $663 million, while Greater China fell 26% to $98 million and AMEA fell 22% to $98 million [Q3 2023 10-Q]. Gross margin was 61.1%, helped somewhat by lower freight.

The falling share price triggered a further GRAIL charge of $821 million at a 24.0% discount rate:

- **$712 million** of goodwill impairment;
- **$109 million** of IPR&D impairment (in-process R&D, the value of unfinished research projects).

This cut GRAIL goodwill to $1,466 million. Core fair value exceeded its carrying value by more than $20 billion.

Restructuring charges reached $58 million, mostly impairments from exiting the i3 campus in San Diego. Core R&D fell 6% as cost cuts took hold. Jacob Thaysen became CEO, with an offer letter dated August 31, 2023.

### FY2023 close

Revenue fell 2% to $4,504 million [FY2023 10-K]:

- **Sequencing consumables** were $2,790 million, down from $2,919 million.
- **Sequencing instruments** fell 3% to $685 million.
- **Service** rose to $717 million (15.9% of revenue).
- **International** revenue fell to 48% of the total from 50%.
- **By region:** Americas $2,521 million (US $2,359 million), Europe $1,140 million, Greater China $384 million and AMEA $459 million.

On costs:

- **SG&A** rose 24% to $1,612 million: earnout gains shrank to $24 million from $205 million, restructuring charges rose by $94 million, and the proxy contest cost $32 million.
- **R&D** rose 2% to $1,354 million.
- **Impairments** totaled $827 million, including a further $6 million IPR&D charge in Q4.
- **Taxes.** The effective tax rate was −3.9%. Singapore tax holidays, which began expiring in 2023, cut the tax bill by $75 million, worth $0.47 per share.

On December 17, 2023, Illumina said it would divest GRAIL, aiming to settle terms by the end of Q2 2024.

## What drove margins and costs

The margin story changed over the period:

- **2022.** The causes were freight inflation and fixed-cost deleverage as instrument volumes fell, partly offset by a richer consumables mix. The 2021 comparison was also flattered by patent settlement revenue that carried no cost.
- **2023.** The causes were lower factory volumes, the NovaSeq X ramp (new instruments carry thin margins until production scales) and higher service and installation costs, partly offset by lower freight [FY2023 10-K].

GRAIL's roughly $134 million a year of amortization also weighed on the consolidated figure.

Operating costs were noisy for three reasons:

- **The GRAIL earnout.** Formally these are contingent value rights, or CVRs: payments to former GRAIL holders of 2.5% of GRAIL's first $1 billion of annual revenue and 9% above that, for 12 years. Each quarterly revaluation runs through SG&A. The liability's fair value went from $615 million at the start of 2022 to $412 million at end-2022 and $387 million at end-2023. In between it ranged from $330 million to $605 million. Actual payments were trivial: about $803,000 in 2023.
- **Liability-linked compensation.** GRAIL employees hold cash-based equity awards worth $292 million at end-2023, rising and falling with GRAIL's value.
- **Restructuring.** Charges ran from Q4 2022 through 2023, including $100 million of lease and facility impairments in 2023 from exiting i3 and part of the Foster City campus.

Share-based compensation was $366 million in 2022 and $380 million in 2023.

## Interest, other income and taxes

**Interest.** Expense fell from $61 million in 2021 to $26 million in 2022, mainly because an accounting change (ASU 2020-06, adopted January 3, 2022) ended the non-cash interest charge on the convertible notes. Interest on debt then rose to $74 million in 2023 after the December 2022 refinancing at higher rates. Interest income rose from zero to $11 million in 2022 and $47 million in the first nine months of 2023 on higher money-market yields.

**Other income.** It swung from $1,068 million in 2021 (the GRAIL stake gain) to $(142) million in 2022, mainly because of $122 million of losses on strategic investments.

**Tax rates.** Reported effective tax rates were not meaningful through this period because of non-deductible impairments and fines, Section 174 and GRAIL's pre-acquisition losses. At end-2023, federal net operating losses (past losses that can shelter future profit) were $860 million.

## Cash flow, debt and buybacks

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow fell from $545 million in 2021 to $392 million in 2022, then recovered to $478 million in 2023. Capex peaked at $286 million in 2022, mainly on facilities. Other 2022 investing outflows were the $180 million BGI license and an $85 million acquisition.

**Refinancing.** Illumina faced $1.25 billion of maturities in 2023: $500 million of 0.55% term notes due March 2023 and $750 million of 0% convertible notes due August 2023. Convertible notes are bonds holders can swap for shares; these converted at $457.77 per share, far above the share price. Management handled this in three steps:

1. **New bonds.** On December 13, 2022, it issued $500 million of 5.80% notes due December 2025 and $500 million of 5.75% notes due December 2027, raising $991 million net.
2. **Repayments.** It repaid the 2023 term notes on March 23, 2023 and the convertibles in cash on August 15, 2023, issuing no shares.
3. **Credit line.** On January 4, 2023, a new $750 million revolving credit facility (a standing bank credit line, undrawn throughout) replaced the March 2021 facility. The new one runs to January 2028, with debt capped at 3.5 times EBITDA.

The result: cash went from $1,232 million (end-2021) to $2,011 million (end-2022) and then to $1,048 million (end-2023). Debt at end-2023 was $1.5 billion of term notes, including $500 million of 2.55% notes due 2031.

**Buybacks.** Illumina repurchased no shares in 2021, 2022 or 2023. Its last buyback was $735 million in 2020, and only $15 million remained under the $750 million authorization from February 2020. Management said it would not repurchase shares in 2022, 2023 or 2024. Shares outstanding drifted from 157.1 million in April 2022 to 158.9 million in February 2024.

**Balance sheet.** Several lines show the strain:

- **Equity** fell from $10,740 million to $6,599 million (end-2022) and $5,898 million (Q3 2023).
- **Goodwill** fell from $7,113 million to $3,239 million and then $2,545 million (the Q3 2023 10-Q says $2,527 million).
- **Inventory** rose from $431 million to $568 million to $615 million (Q3 2023).
- **Backlog** (firm unshipped orders) fell from $1,030 million at end-2022 to $653 million at end-2023, a forward signal of weak instrument demand.

**The EU fine.** It was set at €432 million on July 12, 2023. Illumina provided guarantees in October 2023 instead of paying cash while it appealed, and the fine accrues interest at 5.5%. The accrual stood at $484 million at year-end.

## Source inconsistencies to note

The filings and extracts disagree on a few points:

- **GRAIL deal value.** It is cited as $4,986 million (stock only), about $7.1 billion, and $9.745 billion (total consideration including cash, CVRs and the prior stake).
- **Base for the fine accrual.** The FY2022 10-K uses 2022 revenue of $4,584 million, while the Q1 2023 10-Q cites $4,578 million.
- **GRAIL tax basis.** It was estimated at $500 million–$1 billion through mid-2023, but at "zero to $500 million" in the Q3 2023 10-Q.
- **CVR fair value at Q3 2022.** The extract reports $387 million in one place and implies higher elsewhere.
- **Extract error.** The Q1 2022 extract's "30 bp" consolidated margin decline is a typo for 330 bp.

## The arc of 2024–2025 in brief

Between early 2024 and the end of 2025, Illumina's reported numbers swung wildly while the underlying business barely moved. Revenue for the ongoing company, which the filings call "Core Illumina" (Illumina excluding GRAIL), was $4,332M in 2024 and $4,343M in 2025, essentially flat [FY2025 10-K].

The big swings came from one-off items. GRAIL, the cancer-detection company Illumina bought in 2021, generated huge write-downs in 2024 before it was spun off on June 24, 2024. That year also included a $481M gain when the European Commission (EC) withdrew its fine. In 2025, a cleaner company returned to profit, with GAAP net income of $850M. It did so despite three new headwinds: China's sanctions, US tariffs that began in April 2025, and cuts to NIH (US National Institutes of Health) research funding.

Two definitions recur throughout:

- **GAAP** figures follow standard accounting rules.
- **Non-GAAP** figures are management's adjusted numbers. They exclude amortization (the gradual write-off of acquired intangible assets), restructuring and other one-off items.

## 2024: the GRAIL year

### Q1 2024: flat sales, wider losses

Revenue was $1,076M, down 1% from $1,087M in Q1 2023 [Q1 2024 10-Q].

- **Instruments:** revenue fell 28% to $114M. Management blamed a smaller NovaSeq X backlog after the 2023 launch pre-orders, plus "capital and cash flow constraints" at customers buying mid-throughput machines.
- **Consumables:** flat at $762M.
- **Services:** grew 21% to $200M on strategic partnerships. GRAIL contributed $27M (+35%, from its Galleri blood test).
- **Regions:** Europe grew 7%. Greater China fell 14% to $78M, which management attributed to "competitive challenges" and the macro environment.
- **Gross margin:** rose 170 bps to 62.0% (a basis point, or bp, is 0.01 percentage point). Core margin was 65.7%, helped by consumables mix, freight savings and productivity.

The operating loss widened to $111M from $64M. The causes were $36M of restructuring charges, a $17M loss on the GRAIL CVR liability and $7M of interest accruing on the EC fine. CVRs (contingent value rights) are IOUs issued to former GRAIL holders that pay 2.5% of GRAIL revenue up to $1B a year and 9% above that, through August 2033. GRAIL itself lost $227M at the operating line.

Net loss was $126M ($0.79 per share). The tax rate was -15.3%, driven by R&D capitalization rules and GRAIL loss effects. Operating cash flow was $77M, capex $36M, and cash ended at $1,108M. Management said it did "not intend to make any share repurchases during fiscal year 2024" [Q1 2024 10-Q].

### Q2 2024: spin-off and a $1.9B write-down

Revenue fell 5% to $1,112M. Core revenue was down 6% to $1,092M, instruments fell 39% to $120M, and Greater China dropped to $75M from $115M [Q2 2024 10-Q]. Core gross margin rose to 68.0%.

The GAAP figures were dominated by GRAIL:

- **Impairments:** a $1,466M goodwill impairment and a $420M IPR&D impairment (in-process R&D, an intangible asset for unfinished research), totaling $1.9B.
- **Valuation basis:** bankers' valuations of GRAIL had fallen from $3–4B to $400–770M. Illumina used a fair value of $580M and a 46.5% discount rate.
- **CVR gain:** a $271M gain on the CVR liability, which fell from $387M to $131M.
- **Retained stake loss:** a $328M unrealized loss on the 14.5% GRAIL stake Illumina kept. It was booked at $397M and was worth $74M by quarter-end.

The quarter's net loss was $1,988M ($12.48 per share).

Illumina contributed $974M of "Disposal Funding" to GRAIL, enough to cover 2.5 years of operations. It financed this partly with a $750M 364-day term loan at 6.7%, drawn June 20. Equity fell to $1,436M from $5,745M at year-end 2023.

### Q3 2024: EC fine withdrawn, buybacks restart

Revenue was $1,080M (-3%), all of it Core [Q3 2024 10-Q].

- Sequencing consumables grew 8% to $741M, driven by NovaSeq X.
- Instruments fell 42% to $104M.
- Greater China fell 24% to $75M, while Europe grew 12%.
- Gross margin reached 68.9%, up 410 bps.

On September 3, 2024, the EU Court of Justice ruled that the EC had lacked jurisdiction. On September 6, the EC withdrew all of its GRAIL decisions, including the €432M fine. That produced a $481M net gain ($489M in operating expense, less an $8M currency loss). As a result, GAAP operating income was $741M and net income $705M ($4.42 per share).

On September 9, Illumina issued $500M of 4.650% notes due September 2026 and repaid the term loan ($761M including interest, with a $5M extinguishment loss). In August 2024 the board authorized a new $1.5B buyback. Illumina bought 770k shares at $127.71 ($99M) in the quarter.

### FY2024 in full

For the full year, revenue was $4,372M (-3%) and Core revenue $4,332M (-2%) [FY2024 10-K]. Sequencing instruments fell $203M (-29%), while sequencing consumables rose $59M.

- **Gross margin:** 65.4% against 60.9% in 2023, an improvement of 450 bps. The filing's summary says 490 bps, but 450 bps is the arithmetic. Core gross margin was 67.1%, up 270 bps.
- **Operating and net result:** an operating loss of $833M and a net loss of $1,223M (-$7.69 per share). Core Illumina earned $1,473M at the operating line.
- **Tax:** a -3.8% tax rate, including $308M of tax effect from non-deductible goodwill.
- **Cash flow:** operating cash flow of $837M.
- **Restructuring:** $62M for the year, bringing the cumulative total to $214M.

In the 10-K, management said it expected to focus on "returning to revenue growth" in 2025. It also warned that China's February 4, 2025 decision to add Illumina to its Unreliable Entities List put the $308M of Greater China revenue at risk.

## 2025: profits return amid China, tariffs and NIH cuts

Revenue was $1,041M (-3%). The decline reflects the loss of $27M of GRAIL revenue and a $12M drop in partnership services [Q1 2025 10-Q].

- **China:** on March 4, 2025, China banned imports of Illumina sequencers.
- **Cost response:** management launched a $100M cost-reduction program for 2025 to offset China and NIH uncertainty.
- **Tariffs:** management estimated the cost of the April 2025 tariffs at "approximately $85 million" and aimed to partly mitigate it in 2025 and "more fully" in 2026.

The 10-Q gave no numerical guidance. The original February 2025 FY25 guidance is not in these sources, which is a gap.

## Where this part picks up

This part covers Illumina's financial results from the preliminary fiscal 2025 numbers, released in January 2026, through the third quarter of 2026. It also covers how 2026 guidance moved during the year. Two terms recur throughout:

- **Non-GAAP** figures are the company's adjusted numbers. They strip out items such as amortization of acquired intangibles, acquisition costs, restructuring, and gains or losses on investments.
- **Organic growth** removes the effects of currency and acquisitions.

Illumina uses three further conventions:

- **ROW organic growth** ("rest of world") also excludes **Greater China**, which in Illumina's usage includes Taiwan and Hong Kong.
- **Constant currency (CC)** growth removes only exchange-rate effects.
- **Recurring revenue** is consumables plus service and other revenue. Consumables are the reagent kits customers must buy for every sequencing run. Recurring revenue was about 90% of the total in FY25 [JPM, 2026-01-13].

Illumina says non-GAAP operating margin and EPS are the metrics its board uses to judge and pay management. Its performance stock units are tied to operating margin, EPS (resumed in 2026) and relative total shareholder return [JPM, 2026-01-13; Q1 2026 10-Q].

### Q3 2026 (reported October 29) and the tariff refund

In February 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) did not authorize the tariffs imposed from April 2025. In March, the Court of International Trade ordered U.S. Customs to process refunds. Illumina's largest tariff exposure is imports from its Singapore plant [Q1 and Q3 2026 10-Qs].

Illumina booked the refund in Q3 [Q3 2026 10-Q]:

- **Cost recovery:** a $79M reduction to cost of revenue.
- **Customer credits:** a $37M reduction to revenue, with a matching liability, for refunds it will pass to customers. Of this, $28M was in sequencing consumables, $4M in instruments, $3M in service and $2M in microarray. By region, $28M was in the U.S. and Canada and $9M in EMEALA.
- **Net effect:** a $42M benefit to gross profit.
- **Cash:** $32M had been received by quarter-end, with $47M still receivable.

These items lifted GAAP gross margin to 73.5% and GAAP operating margin to 27.0%. Excluding them, non-GAAP gross margin was 69.4% (up 20 bps) and operating margin was 25.0% [Q3 2026 deck]. Management described gross margin excluding the benefit as "relatively flat." A simple recalculation that adds back only the $42M gives about 69.8% against 67.6%, but the non-GAAP basis supports management's description.

Other Q3 results:

- **GAAP revenue** rose 4% to $1,129M. On a GAAP basis, instruments fell 5% to $105M and service rose 17% to $183M.
- **Ex-refund sequencing figures:** consumables were $792M (up 6% organically), instruments $106M (down 1%) and service $158M (up 8%).
- **GAAP net income** rose 47% to $220M, helped by a 16.4% tax rate. The Q3'25 rate was 31.8%, which included the $42M tax valuation allowance.
- **Venture funds** lost $31M.

For the nine months, revenue was $3,380M (up 6%), GAAP operating margin 22.5% against 19.0%, and non-GAAP operating margin 23.1% against 22.9%. Non-GAAP EPS was $3.90 against $3.49.

## SomaLogic and the GRAIL stake

Illumina closed the SomaLogic acquisition from Standard BioTools on January 30, 2026, for $460M [Q1 2026 10-Q]. SomaLogic measures proteins, and the purchase price comprised:

- $382M of cash.
- $81M of contingent consideration, meaning future payments tied to performance.
- Less $3M for settling a preexisting relationship.

The purchase price was allocated to:

- **Goodwill** of $171M (the premium over the fair value of assets), expected to be tax-deductible.
- **Intangibles** of $186M: developed technology $158M over 9 years, trade name $16M over 7 years and customer relationships $12M over 8 years.
- **Inventory** of $57M, of which $22M is classed as long-term under a new accounting policy.

Separately, Illumina bought a $50M patent portfolio, amortized over 14 years. Intangible amortization is expected to be $78M in 2027 and $76M in 2028 [Q3 2026 10-Q]. Transaction costs were reported as $7M in Q1 but as about $6M year-to-date in the Q3 filing.

The earnout was settled in two steps:

- **2025 milestone:** Illumina paid $25M in Q2 (the Q1 filing dates the payment to April).
- **Buyout:** on July 24, 2026, Illumina agreed to pay $30.04M for the remaining 2026 milestone of up to $50M, a 2% royalty, a 5% Single-SOMAmer royalty and a license. No earnout obligations remain.

By our arithmetic from the filing's pro forma tables, SomaLogic brought in about $24M of revenue in Q3'25.

Illumina sold its remaining **GRAIL shares** in 2026, for $206M of proceeds year-to-date. The stake fell from $215M at year-end to $66M after Q1, $88M after Q2 and $2M after Q3.

Illumina also owes **contingent value rights (CVRs)**, payments to former GRAIL holders. They equal 2.5% of GRAIL's annual revenue up to $1B and 9% above that, through August 2033. Payments were $1.2M year-to-date. Revenue subject to the CVRs was $129M for Q4'25 through Q2'26, against $106M a year earlier. The CVR liability moved from $54M at year-end to $35M after Q1, $40M after Q2 and $36M after Q3. The revenue risk premium used to value it fell from 3% to 2%. Illumina also has a $1M indemnity accrued for a GRAIL award worth up to $78M.
